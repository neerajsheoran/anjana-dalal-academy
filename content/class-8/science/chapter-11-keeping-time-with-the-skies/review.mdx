## Key Concepts

- The Moon produces **no light of its own** -- it shines by reflecting sunlight
- At any moment, exactly **one half** of the Moon is lit by the Sun; the other half is dark
- **Phases of the Moon** are the changing shapes of the visible sunlit portion as the Moon orbits Earth
- **Krishna Paksha** (waning/shrinking phase): Full Moon → Waning Gibbous → Third-Quarter Half → Waning Crescent → Amavasya (New Moon)
- **Shukla Paksha** (waxing/growing phase): Amavasya → Waxing Crescent → First-Quarter Half → Waxing Gibbous → Full Moon
- One complete phase cycle (Purnima to Purnima) takes about **29.5 days**
- Phases are NOT caused by Earth's shadow -- that is a **lunar eclipse** (a rare event)
- Waning Moon is best seen around **sunrise**; waxing Moon around **sunset**
- Moon rises approximately **50 minutes later** each day
- Three celestial cycles measure time: **day** (24 hours, Earth's spin), **month** (~29.5 days, Moon's phases), **year** (~365.25 days, Earth's orbit around Sun)
- **Lunar calendar**: 12 lunar months ≈ 354 days (11 days shorter than solar year -- seasons drift)
- **Solar calendar** (e.g., Gregorian): keeps seasons fixed to months; leap year every 4 years
- **Luni-solar calendar**: follows Moon for months but adds **Adhika Maasa** (extra month) every 2-3 years to stay aligned with seasons
- Most traditional Indian calendars are **luni-solar**
- Indian festivals (Diwali, Holi, Eid) are linked to lunar phases and shift dates in the Gregorian calendar yearly
- **Indian National Calendar (Saka calendar)**: solar, 365 days, begins on 22 March
- **Uttarayan**: Sun's apparent northward movement (~Dec-Jun); **Dakshinayan**: southward (~Jun-Dec)
- **Artificial satellites** orbit Earth for communication, navigation, weather forecasting, disaster response, and science
- **ISRO** satellites: Cartosat (mapping), AstroSat (astronomy), Chandrayaan (Moon), Aditya L1 (Sun), Mangalyaan (Mars)
- **Space debris** from old satellites is a growing hazard

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Phases of the Moon** | Changing shapes of the Moon's visible sunlit portion during its orbit |
| **Krishna Paksha** | Waning fortnight -- Full Moon to New Moon (decreasing brightness) |
| **Shukla Paksha** | Waxing fortnight -- New Moon to Full Moon (increasing brightness) |
| **Amavasya** | New Moon -- sunlit half faces entirely away from Earth |
| **Purnima** | Full Moon -- entire sunlit face is visible from Earth |
| **Lunar calendar** | Calendar based on the Moon's phase cycle |
| **Solar calendar** | Calendar based on Earth's orbit around the Sun (keeps seasons fixed) |
| **Luni-solar calendar** | Calendar that tracks months by Moon but adds an extra month periodically to match seasons |
| **Adhika Maasa** | Extra/bonus month inserted in luni-solar calendars every 2-3 years |
| **Artificial satellite** | Human-made object placed in orbit around Earth |

## Remember These

- Moon phases are caused by **changing viewing angle** of the sunlit half, NOT by Earth's shadow
- One phase cycle = **29.5 days** (frequently tested)
- Lunar year (354 days) is **11 days shorter** than solar year (365.25 days) -- this is why festivals shift dates
- **Saka calendar** starts on **22 March** -- know this for exams
- India's Mangalyaan reached Mars on its **very first attempt**
