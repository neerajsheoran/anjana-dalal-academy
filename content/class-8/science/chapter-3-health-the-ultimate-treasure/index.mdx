## Health: Far More Than the Absence of Illness

When someone asks "Are you healthy?", most of us think about whether we have a fever or a cold. But true health goes much deeper than simply not being sick. It includes feeling energetic in your body, calm and focused in your mind, and connected with the people around you.

The **World Health Organization (WHO)** captures this beautifully: health is "a state of complete **physical**, **mental**, and **social** well-being, and not merely the absence of disease."

A truly healthy person:
- Has the energy and strength to carry out daily tasks with ease
- Handles stressful or challenging situations without being overwhelmed
- Maintains caring and supportive relationships with family, friends, and community

> **Key idea:** Health has three interconnected dimensions -- physical well-being (a strong body), mental well-being (a balanced mind), and social well-being (meaningful connections with others). Neglecting any one dimension weakens the others.

### Wisdom from Ayurveda

India's ancient healing system **Ayurveda** recognised this holistic view of health thousands of years ago. It teaches that wellness arises from harmony between body, mind, and environment. Practices like **dinacharya** (a structured daily routine including early rising, exercise, and balanced meals) and **ritucharya** (adjusting diet and activities according to the season) are designed to maintain this harmony. Eating fresh, seasonal food suited to your individual **prakriti** (body type), combined with physical activity, cleanliness, sound sleep, and a peaceful mind, forms the Ayurvedic path to lasting health.

## Pathways to Staying Healthy

Maintaining good health is an active, everyday effort. Here are the essential areas to focus on:

### Cultivating a Healthy Lifestyle

**Habits that strengthen health:**
- Eat a **balanced diet** rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and pulses
- Avoid excessive consumption of processed, fried, or sugary foods and beverages
- Stay **physically active** -- play outdoors, walk to school, swim, cycle, or run
- **Limit time spent on screens** and make time for nature and fresh air
- Ensure **adequate sleep** every night so your body and mind can repair themselves
- Practise **yoga** or breathing exercises like **pranayama** regularly
- Stay away from tobacco, alcohol, and habit-forming substances

**Habits that weaken health:**
- Spending hours every day on phones, tablets, or computers
- Replacing home-cooked meals with packaged snacks and fast food
- Going to bed very late or getting fewer hours of sleep than your body needs
- Missing meals, particularly the morning meal

### Keeping Your Surroundings Clean

A clean environment is a silent guardian of your health:
- Pure air and safe drinking water are basic requirements for a healthy body
- Pollution from vehicle exhaust and industrial smoke can trigger coughing, breathing difficulties, and conditions like asthma
- The **Air Quality Index (AQI)** is a tool that measures how clean or polluted the air is on any given day
- Maintaining hygiene at home, school, and in public spaces reduces exposure to disease-causing organisms

> **Key idea:** Your health is shaped by two forces: your **lifestyle** (the choices you make each day) and your **environment** (the conditions around you). Both must be cared for.

## Recognising When Something Is Wrong

When your body is not functioning properly, it sends you warning signals in two forms:

- **Symptoms** -- What you feel internally, such as a headache, nausea, exhaustion, or dizziness. These are personal experiences that only you can describe.
- **Signs** -- What can be observed or measured from the outside, such as a high temperature, skin rashes, swollen glands, or elevated blood pressure. A doctor can detect and quantify these.

Symptoms and signs together help medical professionals piece together what might be wrong and decide on the right treatment.

## Understanding Diseases

A **disease** is any condition that disrupts the normal functioning of the body or mind. It occurs when one or more organs or systems in the body stop working as they should.

Diseases fall into two broad categories:

### 1. Communicable Diseases

These are caused by **pathogens** -- disease-causing agents such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasitic worms, or protozoa. The defining feature is that they can **pass from one person to another**.

**Routes of transmission:**
- **Airborne** -- An infected person coughs or sneezes, releasing tiny droplets carrying pathogens (examples: common cold, tuberculosis, chickenpox)
- **Contaminated food and water** -- Consuming water or food harbouring pathogens (examples: cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A)
- **Physical contact** -- Touching an infected person or sharing personal belongings (examples: ringworm, conjunctivitis)
- **Insect vectors** -- Organisms like mosquitoes and houseflies that transport pathogens from one host to another (examples: malaria, dengue)

### 2. Non-Communicable Diseases

These are **not caused by pathogens** and **cannot spread** between people. They arise from factors such as genetics, lifestyle choices, dietary patterns, and environmental exposure.

Examples include cancer, diabetes, asthma, heart disease, obesity, and hypertension (high blood pressure).

> **Key idea:** In recent decades, non-communicable diseases have overtaken communicable ones as the leading cause of death in India. Diabetes, heart attacks, and cancers are rising sharply, driven by shifts towards processed diets, sedentary work, and high stress levels.

## Common Communicable Diseases at a Glance

### Airborne Diseases

| Disease | Pathogen Type | Key Symptoms | How to Prevent |
|---------|--------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Common Cold and Influenza** | Virus | Blocked nose, sore throat, fever, body pain | Regular handwashing, avoiding shared utensils, covering mouth while coughing |
| **Chickenpox** | Virus | Mild fever, itchy rash, fluid-filled blisters | Isolating the patient, vaccination |
| **Measles** | Virus | High fever, sore throat, reddish spots on skin | Good hygiene, vaccination |
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | Bacteria | Persistent cough, fever, fatigue, night sweats, loss of appetite | Avoiding close prolonged contact with TB patients, BCG vaccination |

### Waterborne and Foodborne Diseases

| Disease | Pathogen Type | Key Symptoms | How to Prevent |
|---------|--------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Hepatitis A** | Virus | Tiredness, fever, loss of appetite, yellowing of eyes and skin | Drinking purified or boiled water, vaccination |
| **Cholera** | Bacteria | Severe watery diarrhoea, dehydration | Hygiene, clean drinking water, properly cooked food, vaccination |
| **Typhoid** | Bacteria | Persistent fever, headache, stomach pain, diarrhoea | Hand hygiene, safe water, thoroughly cooked food, vaccination |
| **Ascariasis** | Parasitic worms | Worms visible in stool, poor appetite, weight loss, weakness | Washing hands before meals, drinking clean water, properly cooked food |

### Vector-Borne Diseases

| Disease | Pathogen Type | Insect Vector | Key Symptoms | How to Prevent |
|---------|--------------|--------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Malaria** | Protozoa | Mosquito | Recurring high fever with chills and sweating | Mosquito nets, repellents, eliminating stagnant water |
| **Dengue** | Virus | Mosquito | Sudden high fever, severe headache, muscle and joint pain | Preventing mosquito breeding, using nets and repellents |

### Everyday Precautions Against Communicable Diseases

1. Maintain personal and household cleanliness
2. Wash hands thoroughly with soap before eating and after using the toilet
3. Cover your mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing
4. Use a mask in crowded or poorly ventilated spaces
5. Do not share towels, combs, or drinking cups
6. Ensure food is properly cooked and water is safe to drink
7. Rest at home when feeling unwell to avoid spreading illness

## Non-Communicable Diseases

### Deficiency Diseases

When the body lacks a specific nutrient over a prolonged period:
- **Scurvy** -- results from insufficient Vitamin C (bleeding gums, weakness)
- **Anaemia** -- results from insufficient iron (fatigue, pale skin, dizziness)
- **Goitre** -- results from insufficient iodine (swelling in the neck region)

### Lifestyle-Related Diseases

These develop gradually from unhealthy habits sustained over years:

- **Diabetes** -- India ranks among the countries with the highest number of diabetes cases globally. It arises from hormonal imbalances worsened by poor eating habits, insufficient physical activity, and excess body weight.
- **Obesity** -- Results from consuming more calories than the body uses, compounded by lack of exercise
- **Hypertension** (High blood pressure) -- Connected to chronic stress, salty or fatty diets, and inactivity
- **Heart disease** -- Linked to smoking, unhealthy diets, lack of exercise, and prolonged stress

> **Key idea:** The encouraging truth is that most lifestyle-related diseases are largely preventable. Regular physical activity, a wholesome diet, and healthy daily habits can dramatically reduce your risk.

## Defending Against Disease

### Your Body's Defence: The Immune System

Your body comes equipped with a remarkable defence force called the **immune system**. It identifies, attacks, and destroys pathogens that try to invade your body. The natural capacity of your body to fight infections is known as **immunity**.

Some people fall ill frequently while others rarely do, even when exposed to the same germs. The difference often lies in the strength and readiness of their immune system.

### Vaccines: Preparing the Immune System in Advance

A **vaccine** is a preparation that trains your immune system to recognise and combat a specific pathogen before you ever encounter the real disease. This results in **acquired immunity** -- protection built through deliberate exposure to a harmless version of the pathogen.

**The mechanism of vaccination:**
1. A vaccine contains dead, weakened, or fragmentary parts of a pathogen
2. When introduced into the body, it does not cause disease
3. However, the immune system treats it as a real threat and builds specific defences
4. If the actual pathogen enters the body later, the immune system recognises it instantly and neutralises it swiftly

**Vaccines prevent disease; they do not cure it.** They must be given before infection occurs.

### The Story of the First Vaccine

**Smallpox** was once among the deadliest diseases known to humanity. In the 1790s, English physician **Edward Jenner** noticed something curious: milkmaids who had contracted **cowpox** -- a mild illness caught from cows -- never seemed to develop the far more dangerous smallpox. Jenner tested this idea by inoculating a young boy with cowpox material. The boy developed mild symptoms but recovered quickly. When later exposed to actual smallpox, the boy remained perfectly healthy -- he was immune! This experiment gave the world its first **vaccine** (from *vacca*, the Latin word for cow). Through global vaccination drives, smallpox was eventually **eradicated entirely** by 1979.

### India's Early Tradition of Inoculation

Centuries before Jenner's work, healers in India practised **variolation** -- a technique where material from a smallpox blister was deliberately introduced into a scratch on a healthy person's skin. This caused a mild infection that built lifelong immunity. The practitioners who carried out this procedure were known as **teekedaars**.

### India as a Vaccine Superpower

Today, India is one of the world's largest producers and exporters of vaccines. During the **COVID-19 pandemic**, Indian pharmaceutical companies manufactured billions of doses, playing a critical role in the global fight against the virus.

## Treating Disease

### Antibiotics

When the immune system cannot overcome a bacterial infection on its own, doctors prescribe **antibiotics** -- medicines specifically designed to kill bacteria or halt their growth.

**Essential facts about antibiotics:**
- They are effective **only against bacteria**, not against viruses, fungi, or protozoa
- They target structures unique to bacterial cells, leaving human cells unharmed
- Taking antibiotics without a doctor's prescription is dangerous and irresponsible
- The full prescribed course must always be completed, even if you feel better midway

### The Accidental Discovery of Penicillin

In 1928, Scottish scientist **Alexander Fleming** noticed something unexpected. A petri dish containing bacteria had been accidentally contaminated by mould. Around the mould, the bacteria had stopped growing -- the mould was producing a substance that killed bacteria. Fleming named this substance **penicillin**, and it became the world's first antibiotic, saving countless millions of lives.

### The Growing Threat of Antibiotic Resistance

**Antibiotic resistance** is one of the most alarming health challenges of our time. It occurs when bacteria that were once killed by antibiotics evolve to survive them.

**How resistance develops:**
1. Taking antibiotics when they are not needed (for example, for a viral cold)
2. Stopping the antibiotic course halfway, allowing the strongest bacteria to survive and multiply
3. Excessive use of antibiotics in livestock farming

**How resistant bacteria spread:**
- From person to person through contact
- Through contaminated food, water, and soil

> **Key idea:** Using antibiotics responsibly -- only when prescribed, at the correct dosage, and for the full duration -- is essential to keep these life-saving medicines effective for future generations.

### India's Traditional Healing Systems

India is home to several ancient medical traditions:
- **Ayurveda**
- **Siddha**
- **Unani**

These systems employ natural ingredients -- herbs, roots, minerals, and oils -- to treat ailments and promote recovery. They emphasise prevention through balanced living, seasonal diet, and mental calm. While valuable for many conditions and general wellness, they may not be sufficient for all diseases at every stage, and modern medical consultation is recommended alongside traditional practices.

## Key Takeaways

- **Health** encompasses complete physical, mental, and social well-being -- not merely the absence of illness.
- A **disease** disrupts the normal functioning of body or mind.
- **Symptoms** are what you feel (pain, fatigue); **signs** are what can be observed or measured (fever, rash).
- **Communicable diseases** are caused by pathogens and can spread from person to person through air, water, food, contact, or insect vectors.
- **Non-communicable diseases** like diabetes and heart disease are linked to lifestyle, diet, and environment and cannot be transmitted between people.
- The **immune system** is your body's built-in defence against pathogens.
- **Vaccines** train the immune system to fight specific diseases. They are preventive, not curative.
- **Antibiotics** are effective only against bacterial infections, not viral ones.
- **Antibiotic resistance** -- caused by misuse and overuse of antibiotics -- is a growing global threat.
- The foundations of good health are simple: eat well, stay active, sleep enough, maintain hygiene, and nurture positive relationships.
