## Key Concepts

- Health is **complete physical, mental, and social well-being** -- not merely the absence of disease (WHO definition)
- **Symptoms** are what you feel (headache, nausea); **signs** are what can be observed/measured (fever, rash)
- Diseases are of two types: **communicable** (spread by pathogens) and **non-communicable** (cannot spread)
- **Communicable diseases** spread through: air, contaminated food/water, physical contact, or insect vectors
- **Pathogens** include bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasitic worms, and protozoa
- Common airborne diseases: cold, influenza, chickenpox, measles, tuberculosis
- Common waterborne diseases: cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, ascariasis
- Vector-borne diseases: **malaria** (protozoa, mosquito), **dengue** (virus, mosquito)
- **Non-communicable diseases**: diabetes, obesity, hypertension, heart disease, cancer -- linked to lifestyle
- **Deficiency diseases**: scurvy (Vitamin C), anaemia (iron), goitre (iodine)
- The **immune system** identifies, attacks, and destroys pathogens; its strength is called **immunity**
- **Vaccines** train the immune system using dead/weakened pathogens -- they are **preventive, not curative**
- **Edward Jenner** developed the first vaccine (smallpox) using cowpox material in the 1790s
- Smallpox was **completely eradicated** by 1979 through global vaccination
- **Antibiotics** work only against **bacteria**, NOT viruses
- **Alexander Fleming** accidentally discovered **penicillin** (first antibiotic) in 1928
- **Antibiotic resistance** develops from misuse: taking antibiotics for viral infections, stopping the course early, overuse in livestock
- India's traditional healing systems: **Ayurveda**, **Siddha**, **Unani**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Disease** | Any condition that disrupts normal functioning of body or mind |
| **Communicable disease** | Disease caused by pathogens that can spread person to person |
| **Non-communicable disease** | Disease that cannot spread; caused by genetics, lifestyle, or environment |
| **Pathogen** | Disease-causing agent (bacteria, virus, fungus, protozoa, parasitic worm) |
| **Vector** | Organism (like mosquito) that carries pathogens from one host to another |
| **Immunity** | The body's natural capacity to fight infections |
| **Vaccine** | Preparation of dead/weakened pathogens that trains the immune system |
| **Antibiotic** | Medicine that kills bacteria or stops their growth |
| **Antibiotic resistance** | When bacteria evolve to survive antibiotics that once killed them |

## Remember These

- Vaccines **prevent** disease -- they must be given **before** infection, not after
- Antibiotics are effective **only against bacteria**, never against viruses (no antibiotics for common cold!)
- Always complete the **full course** of antibiotics even if you feel better midway
- Non-communicable diseases have **overtaken** communicable diseases as the leading cause of death in India
- India practised **variolation** (inoculation by teekedaars) centuries before Jenner's vaccine
