## Key Concepts

- A conductor carrying electric current creates a **magnetic field** around it -- the **magnetic effect of electric current**
- **Hans Christian Oersted** (1820) discovered that electric current deflects a compass needle
- An **electromagnet** is a coil of wire carrying current; it behaves as a magnet only when current flows
- Adding an **iron core** inside the coil greatly strengthens the electromagnet
- Electromagnet strength increases with more **wire turns** and stronger **current**
- Electromagnet **poles can be reversed** by reversing current direction
- Electromagnets are used in: electric bells, motors, loudspeakers, magnetic door locks, lifting cranes
- **Earth's magnetic field** is generated by flowing liquid iron in the outer core
- Earth's magnetic field helps in **navigation** (compass, migratory animals) and **protection** from solar wind
- Current flowing through a conductor produces **heat** -- the **heating effect of electric current**
- **Resistance** is the opposition a material offers to current flow; higher resistance = more heat
- Heat produced depends on: **material**, **thickness**, **length** of wire, **current strength**, and **duration**
- **Nichrome** (nickel-chromium alloy) has high resistance and is used in heating elements
- Appliances using heating effect: room heaters, electric stoves, kettles, irons, immersion rods, incandescent bulbs
- A **Voltaic (Galvanic) cell** converts chemical energy to electrical energy using two different metal electrodes and a liquid electrolyte
- **Luigi Galvani** vs **Alessandro Volta**: Galvani thought electricity was from the frog; Volta proved it came from the metals
- **Dry cells** use a paste electrolyte (zinc casing = negative terminal, carbon rod = positive terminal); single-use
- **Rechargeable batteries** (Li-ion, lead-acid) can be recharged hundreds of times but degrade over time
- Used batteries must be disposed of at **e-waste collection centres** -- never in household garbage

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Magnetic effect of current** | A current-carrying conductor creates a magnetic field around it |
| **Electromagnet** | A temporary magnet made by passing current through a coil of wire |
| **Heating effect of current** | Current flowing through a conductor converts electrical energy into heat |
| **Resistance** | Opposition offered by a material to the flow of electric current |
| **Electrode** | A metal plate that conducts electricity in a cell |
| **Electrolyte** | A liquid or paste that enables chemical reactions to produce current |
| **Dry cell** | A portable cell with a paste electrolyte (non-spillable); single-use |
| **Rechargeable battery** | A battery whose chemical reaction can be reversed by feeding electricity back in |

## Remember These

- Electromagnets can be **switched on/off**, **strengthened/weakened**, and have their **poles reversed** -- permanent magnets cannot
- The heating effect depends on **five factors**: material, thickness, length, current, and duration
- Antibiotics work only on bacteria; similarly, the **magnetic effect** only exists **while current flows** -- switch off and it vanishes
- Dry cells are **single-use**; rechargeable batteries **degrade** over hundreds of cycles
- Overloaded circuits cause **overheating** -- always use correctly rated wires and plugs
