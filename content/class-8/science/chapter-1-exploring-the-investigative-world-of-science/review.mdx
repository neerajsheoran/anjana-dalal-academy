## Key Concepts

- Science is a **way of thinking** and investigating, not just memorising facts
- **Scientific investigation** involves framing questions, planning experiments, recording observations, building explanations, and generating new questions
- A **well-framed question** is the starting point of every investigation
- A **controlled experiment** (fair test) changes **only one variable** at a time while keeping all others constant
- **Variables** are things you can change or control in an experiment
- **Outcomes** are the things you observe or measure
- **Observations** must be recorded honestly and thoroughly in a notebook
- Grade 8 science spans microorganisms, health, electricity, forces, pressure, matter, solutions, light, Moon phases, ecosystems, and Earth science
- Everyday events like cooking samosas involve scientific principles (heat transfer, starch changes, moisture escape)
- The **samosa investigation** demonstrates how kitchen activities can be studied using the scientific method
- Changing oil temperature while keeping dough thickness, size, and filling the same is an example of a controlled experiment
- Science is present everywhere -- from frying food to the orbit of the Moon
- New questions arise from every investigation, pushing understanding further
- Curiosity, careful observation, and honest recording are the foundations of scientific thinking

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Scientific investigation** | The process of asking questions, designing experiments, gathering evidence, and building explanations |
| **Variable** | A factor in an experiment that can be changed or controlled |
| **Outcome** | The result you observe or measure in an experiment |
| **Controlled experiment (fair test)** | An experiment where only one variable is changed at a time while all others are kept constant |
| **Observation** | Carefully noticing and recording what happens during an experiment |

## Remember These

- Always change **only one variable** at a time in a fair test -- this is the fundamental rule of experimenting
- A good investigation starts with a **sharp question** based on careful observation of ordinary events
- Science is about **actively investigating**, not passively memorising answers
- Record observations **during** the experiment, not from memory afterwards
