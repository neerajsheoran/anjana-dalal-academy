## Key Concepts

- All matter is made of incredibly tiny **constituent particles** invisible to the naked eye
- Crushing a substance into smaller pieces is a **physical change** -- particles remain the same substance
- When salt dissolves in water, its particles fit into **interparticle spaces** between water particles
- **Acharya Kanad** proposed the concept of the smallest indivisible particle (**Parmanu**) -- one of the earliest atomic theories
- **Interparticle forces of attraction** hold particles together; their strength determines the state of matter
- **Solids**: particles very close, very strong attractions, fixed shape and volume, particles only vibrate
- **Liquids**: particles slightly farther apart, moderate attractions, definite volume but take container's shape, particles slide past each other
- **Gases**: particles far apart, negligible attractions, no fixed shape or volume, particles move freely in all directions
- **Melting point**: temperature at which a solid turns into a liquid (ice = 0°C, iron = 1538°C)
- **Boiling point**: temperature at which a liquid turns into vapour with bubbles forming throughout
- **Evaporation** happens at **any temperature**, only at the surface, and proceeds slowly (unlike boiling)
- Liquids and gases can both flow -- together called **fluids**
- Gases can be **compressed** easily (large interparticle spaces); liquids and solids cannot
- **Thermal energy** (heat) makes particles move faster; more heat = particles break free from attractions
- Sugar dissolving in water: final volume is slightly less than expected because sugar particles occupy interparticle spaces
- Potassium permanganate spreading in water shows that liquid particles are in **continuous motion**
- Particles spread **faster in hot water** than cold water (more thermal energy = faster movement)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Constituent particles** | The smallest building blocks that make up a substance |
| **Interparticle spaces** | Tiny gaps between particles of matter |
| **Interparticle forces of attraction** | Invisible forces that pull particles toward each other |
| **Melting point** | Temperature at which a solid becomes a liquid at normal pressure |
| **Boiling point** | Temperature at which a liquid converts to vapour with bubbles forming throughout |
| **Evaporation** | Slow conversion of liquid to vapour at any temperature, occurring only at the surface |
| **Fluids** | Substances that can flow -- both liquids and gases |
| **Thermal energy** | Heat energy that governs how fast particles move |
| **Parmanu** | Ancient Indian concept of the smallest indivisible particle (by Acharya Kanad) |

## Remember These

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|----------|-------|--------|-----|
| **Shape** | Fixed | Takes container's shape | No fixed shape |
| **Volume** | Fixed | Fixed | Fills available space |
| **Compressibility** | Nearly zero | Nearly zero | Easily compressed |
| **Particle movement** | Vibrate in place | Slide around | Dart freely |
| **Interparticle attraction** | Very strong | Moderate | Almost zero |

- **Evaporation ≠ Boiling**: evaporation happens at any temperature and only at the surface; boiling happens at a specific temperature with bubbles throughout
- Spaces between particles are **empty gaps**, not filled with air
- At the most fundamental level, constituent particles are **atoms** and **molecules**
