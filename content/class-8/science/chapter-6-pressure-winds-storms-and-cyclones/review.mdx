## Key Concepts

- **Pressure = Force / Area**; SI unit is **pascal (Pa)** or N/m²
- Same force on smaller area = **more pressure** (sharp knife cuts easily); larger area = **less pressure** (camel hooves spread weight)
- Liquid pressure increases with **depth** and acts in **all directions**
- Overhead water tanks are placed high to create greater water pressure at taps
- Dam walls are thicker at the **bottom** because water pressure is greatest there
- **Atmospheric pressure** is the pressure exerted by the weight of air around us
- We don't feel atmospheric pressure because body fluids push outward with equal force
- **Suction cups** work because atmospheric pressure outside is greater than reduced pressure behind the cup
- Wind flows from **high-pressure** to **low-pressure** regions
- Greater pressure difference = **faster wind**
- **Sea breeze** (day): land heats faster → warm air rises → cooler sea air moves inland
- **Land breeze** (night): land cools faster → sea air is warmer and rises → land air moves to sea
- **Fast-moving wind creates low pressure** in its path (explains tin roofs flying off in storms)
- **Storms** form when warm, moist air rises, cools, condenses into clouds, and causes heavy rain and wind
- **Lightning** is a massive discharge of static electricity built up inside storm clouds (collisions of ice crystals and water droplets)
- **Thunder** is caused by lightning superheating air, which expands explosively
- **Cyclones** are giant spinning storm systems over warm tropical oceans
- Cyclone structure: calm **eye** at the centre, surrounded by the most violent winds
- Cyclones cause: ferocious winds, storm surges (3-12 m walls of seawater), flooding, saltwater contamination
- Cyclones weaken after crossing the coastline (lose warm ocean fuel)
- **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** tracks cyclones using satellites and radar
- **Lightning conductor**: metal rod from building top to ground, safely channels lightning charge into the earth

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Pressure** | Force applied per unit area (P = F/A) |
| **Pascal (Pa)** | SI unit of pressure; equal to 1 N/m² |
| **Atmospheric pressure** | Pressure exerted by the weight of air in the atmosphere |
| **Sea breeze** | Daytime wind blowing from sea to land |
| **Land breeze** | Night-time wind blowing from land to sea |
| **Thunderstorm** | Storm with lightning, thunder, heavy rain, and strong winds |
| **Lightning** | Sudden discharge of static electricity from storm clouds |
| **Cyclone** | Large spinning storm system formed over warm tropical oceans |
| **Eye of the cyclone** | Calm, cloud-free centre of a cyclone |
| **Storm surge** | Wall of seawater pushed inland by cyclone winds |
| **Lightning conductor** | Metal rod on a building that channels lightning safely into the ground |

## Remember These

- **Pressure = Force ÷ Area** -- this formula is frequently tested
- During storms, **open windows on opposite sides** to equalise pressure and prevent the roof from flying off
- Cyclones get energy from **warm ocean water** and weaken once they hit land
- Regional Indian storms: **Kalboishakhi** (Bengal), **Bordoisila** (Assam), **Mango showers** (South India)
- Lightning safety: crouch low outdoors, avoid tall/metal objects, get out of water immediately
