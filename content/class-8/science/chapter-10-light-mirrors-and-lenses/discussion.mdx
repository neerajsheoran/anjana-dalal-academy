## Discovering Curved Mirrors

**Meera:** Yaar, I was helping my Daadi polish the brass puja thali yesterday and something weird happened. When I looked at the inside, my face was huge and upside down. The back showed me tiny and the right way up. Same thali!

**Arjun:** Oh, that is just because of the curve. Inside curves inward — that is a concave shape. Outside bulges outward — that is convex. They bend light in opposite ways.

**Meera:** So one shape, two totally different reflections. That is wild.

**Arjun:** And we use both shapes all the time without realising. The rear-view mirrors on cars in Mumbai? Convex. That is why they squeeze a wider chunk of the road into a small mirror.

**Meera:** Wait, I have seen that tiny warning on autos — "Objects in mirror are closer than they appear." Is that because the convex shape shrinks everything?

**Arjun:** Exactly. Your brain sees a small image and assumes the bike behind is far away. It is actually much closer. Auto drivers have to factor that in every second.

**Meera:** And concave? Where do we see those?

**Arjun:** Inside a torch, behind the bulb. That curved silver bit gathers all the light from the bulb and throws it forward as a focused beam. Dentists also use tiny concave mirrors to get a magnified view of your teeth.

## The Laws That Light Follows

**Meera:** Our teacher said the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. But does that rule only work for flat mirrors?

**Arjun:** No yaar, it works for every mirror. Flat, concave, convex — doesn't matter. The trick is you have to draw the normal exactly at the spot where the light hits.

**Meera:** Ohh, so on a curved mirror, the normal points in slightly different directions at different spots?

**Arjun:** Right. And that is why parallel rays end up meeting at one point in a concave mirror — each ray follows the law at its own spot, but the normals are angled to make them all converge.

**Meera:** That is how a concave mirror can set paper on fire! The sunlight all meets at one tiny hot spot.

**Arjun:** Same principle is behind those huge solar cookers in Rajasthan villages. The Brahma Kumaris ashram in Mount Abu has one of the world's biggest solar kitchens — thousands of rotis a day from just sunlight and curved reflectors.

**Meera:** So one bit of physics powers both a torch and a community kitchen. That's actually beautiful.

## Understanding Lenses

**Meera:** Mirrors bounce light back. But lenses let it through and bend it on the way. A convex lens is fat in the middle, thin at the edges — it brings light together.

**Arjun:** And concave is the reverse. Thin in the middle, thick at the edges, spreads light apart. Your own eye has a natural convex lens, you know. It bends the light from whatever you are looking at and focuses it on the retina.

**Meera:** Daadi's reading glasses — those are convex too?

**Arjun:** Yes, magnifying glasses are convex. And every camera on every phone in Mumbai uses a stack of tiny convex and concave lenses together to take a sharp photo.

**Meera:** I tried something cool yesterday. I put a water drop on a glass tile and looked at the newspaper underneath. The letters became BIG.

**Arjun:** That water drop was curved on both sides — basically a tiny convex lens. There was a Dutch guy, Leeuwenhoek, in the 1600s. He ground tiny glass beads, used them as lenses, and discovered bacteria. Whole worlds nobody had ever seen.

**Meera:** Can a convex lens also start a fire?

**Arjun:** Yaar, easily. Same idea as the concave mirror — gather sunlight into one bright dot. The mirror does it by reflecting, the lens does it by bending light as it passes through. Either way, that tiny spot gets hot enough to burn paper.

## How Spectacles Correct Vision

**Meera:** Yaar, my Daadi wears glasses only for reading. But Arjun's chacha wears them all the time because he can't see distant things clearly. Why are their glasses different?

**Arjun:** Daadi is farsighted — her eye lens cannot bend light enough for nearby things. So her glasses are convex, adding extra bending power for reading.

**Meera:** And chacha is nearsighted — his eye lens bends light too much, so far things look blurry?

**Arjun:** Right. His glasses have concave lenses that gently spread the light before it enters his eyes, correcting the over-bending. We learned this when my cousin went to the ophthalmologist last month.

**Meera:** So many people in India need glasses but cannot afford a check-up.

**Arjun:** Aravind Eye Hospital in Madurai and LV Prasad in Hyderabad do incredible work — lakhs of free or affordable surgeries every year. Just understanding optics has literally helped millions see better.

**Meera:** I noticed Daadi's reading glasses have a faint line across the middle. She looks down through the lower part to read, straight ahead for distance.

**Arjun:** Bifocals — two lens powers in one frame. Benjamin Franklin invented those because he was tired of swapping between two pairs. These days they make progressive lenses too — same idea but without the visible line.

## Our Eyes — Nature's Finest Lens

**Meera:** Arjun, the lens in our eye can actually change its shape! Thicker for close things, thinner for distant things. No camera can do that.

**Arjun:** It is called accommodation. Tiny muscles squeeze or relax the lens. A camera lens has to physically slide forward or backward to focus. Our eye just reshapes itself instantly. Pretty incredible piece of biology.

**Meera:** Speaking of focusing — India has that giant radio telescope near Pune, right? Does that use mirrors or lenses?

**Arjun:** GMRT — Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope. Those big parabolic dishes work like concave mirrors, except they focus radio waves from distant galaxies, not visible light. All the weak signals get gathered at one focal point.

**Meera:** And ISRO's Aditya-L1 — that one studies the Sun, doesn't it?

**Arjun:** Yes! It is parked at a special spot in space where it can watch the Sun's corona and solar storms without atmosphere getting in the way. Same physics — bending and focusing light — but applied to a telescope millions of kilometres long in concept.

**Meera:** So from Daadi's reading glasses to Aditya-L1, it all comes back to how we bend and focus light.

**Arjun:** Master reflection and refraction and you basically hold the key to half the technology around you. Phones, cars, eyeglasses, telescopes — all of it.
