## Key Concepts

- **Spherical mirrors** have curved reflecting surfaces cut from a hollow sphere
- **Concave mirror**: curves inward (like inside of a bowl); **convex mirror**: curves outward (like back of a spoon)
- Concave mirror images: **close object** = upright, magnified; **far object** = inverted, can be magnified or reduced
- Convex mirror images: **always upright** and **always smaller** than the object
- **Laws of reflection**: (1) angle of incidence = angle of reflection; (2) incident ray, normal, and reflected ray lie in the same plane
- These laws apply to ALL mirrors -- flat, concave, and convex
- **Concave mirror converges** parallel light to a point; **convex mirror diverges** parallel light outward
- Concave mirror uses: torches, headlamps, dentist mirrors, telescopes, solar cookers
- Convex mirror uses: rear-view mirrors, blind-corner mirrors, security mirrors
- Rear-view mirrors show "Objects in mirror are closer than they appear" because convex mirrors make things look smaller
- A **lens** is transparent material with curved surfaces that bends light passing through it
- **Convex lens**: thicker at centre, thinner at edges; **converges** light (converging lens)
- **Concave lens**: thinner at centre, thicker at edges; **diverges** light (diverging lens)
- Convex lens images: **close** = upright, magnified; **far** = inverted, can be magnified or reduced
- Concave lens images: **always upright** and **always smaller**
- A convex lens can focus sunlight to a point hot enough to burn paper
- Spectacles: **convex lens** corrects farsightedness; **concave lens** corrects nearsightedness
- Lenses are used in cameras, telescopes, microscopes, and the human eye
- The human eye has a **convex lens** that can change its curvature to focus at different distances
- A water drop acts as a tiny natural convex lens

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Concave mirror** | Spherical mirror with an inward-curving reflecting surface |
| **Convex mirror** | Spherical mirror with an outward-curving reflecting surface |
| **Incident ray** | Light beam travelling toward the mirror/lens |
| **Reflected ray** | Light beam bouncing away from the mirror |
| **Normal** | Imaginary line perpendicular to the surface at the point of contact |
| **Angle of incidence** | Angle between the incident ray and the normal |
| **Angle of reflection** | Angle between the reflected ray and the normal |
| **Convex lens** | Lens thicker at centre, converges light |
| **Concave lens** | Lens thinner at centre, diverges light |

## Remember These

- **Concave mirror and convex lens both CONVERGE light** -- don't confuse them!
- **Convex mirror and concave lens both DIVERGE light**
- Convex mirrors always give **upright, smaller** images -- that's why they're used as rear-view mirrors (wide field of view)
- Concave lenses always give **upright, smaller** images
- Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection -- this law works for ALL types of mirrors
