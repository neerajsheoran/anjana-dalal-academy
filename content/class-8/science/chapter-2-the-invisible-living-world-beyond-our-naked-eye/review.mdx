## Key Concepts

- Objects smaller than ~0.1 mm are invisible to the naked eye and require a **microscope**
- **Robert Hooke** (1665) observed dead cork cells under a microscope and coined the term **cell**
- **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** was the first to observe living microorganisms; called the **Father of Microbiology**
- The **cell** is the basic unit of life -- every living organism is made of one or more cells
- Every cell has three essential parts: **cell membrane**, **cytoplasm**, and **nucleus**
- Plant cells additionally have a **cell wall**, **chloroplasts** (for photosynthesis), and a **large vacuole**
- Cell shapes are adapted to their functions (spindle-shaped muscle cells, branching nerve cells, flat cheek cells)
- Levels of organisation: **Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism**
- **Unicellular organisms** (e.g., Amoeba, bacteria) perform all life functions with a single cell
- Four groups of microorganisms: **Protozoa**, **Bacteria**, **Fungi**, **Algae**
- **Viruses** are not made of cells; they can only reproduce inside a living host cell
- **Decomposers** (bacteria and fungi) break down dead matter and recycle nutrients into soil
- **Yeast** (fungus) causes bread dough to rise by producing CO2 through fermentation
- **Lactobacillus** converts milk into curd by fermenting lactose into lactic acid
- **Rhizobium** bacteria in legume root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen into usable compounds
- **Microalgae** produce more than half of Earth's oxygen
- **Prokaryotic** cells (bacteria) lack a membrane-bound nucleus; **eukaryotic** cells have a true nucleus
- **Electron microscopes** can magnify up to 10 lakh times (1,000,000x)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cell** | The smallest unit of life; the basic building block of all organisms |
| **Cell membrane** | Thin, flexible boundary that controls what enters and leaves the cell |
| **Cytoplasm** | Gel-like material inside the cell where most chemical activities occur |
| **Nucleus** | Command centre of the cell; directs growth, repair, and reproduction |
| **Cell wall** | Rigid outer layer in plant cells providing stiffness and support |
| **Chloroplasts** | Green structures in plant cells containing chlorophyll for photosynthesis |
| **Microorganisms (microbes)** | Living beings too small to see without a microscope |
| **Virus** | Non-cellular packet of genetic material in a protein coat; reproduces only inside a host cell |
| **Fermentation** | Process where microbes break down sugars, producing gases or acids |
| **Prokaryotic** | Cells without a membrane-bound nucleus (e.g., bacteria) |
| **Eukaryotic** | Cells with a true, membrane-enclosed nucleus |

## Remember These

- Plant cells have **cell wall + chloroplasts + large vacuole** -- animal cells do NOT
- Viruses are **not considered truly alive** because they cannot reproduce on their own
- **Lactobacillus** needs warmth to work -- curd sets faster in summer than winter
- **Rhizobium** in legume roots is why farmers practise **crop rotation** -- legumes replenish soil nitrogen naturally
- The ostrich egg yolk is the **largest known single cell** (~170 mm)
