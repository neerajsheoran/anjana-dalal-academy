## Key Concepts

- A **mixture** combines two or more substances without chemical reaction; each component retains its properties
- **Non-uniform mixtures**: components visible (e.g., chaat, salad)
- **Uniform mixtures**: components evenly blended, indistinguishable (e.g., nimbu paani, air)
- **Alloys** are uniform mixtures of metals: stainless steel (iron + chromium + nickel), brass (copper + zinc), bronze (copper + tin)
- **Air** is a uniform mixture: ~78% nitrogen, plus oxygen, argon, CO2, and water vapour
- Lime water turns **milky** when exposed to air -- proves air contains CO2
- A **pure substance** (scientific definition) contains only one type of particle; cannot be separated by physical methods
- Pure substances are either **elements** or **compounds**
- An **element** cannot be broken down further; made of one type of **atom** (118 elements known)
- Elements classified as **metals** (gold, iron, copper), **non-metals** (carbon, oxygen, sulfur), **metalloids** (silicon, boron)
- Only **2 elements** are liquid at room temperature: **mercury** and **bromine**
- Only **11 elements** are gaseous at room temperature
- A **compound** forms when atoms of 2+ elements bond chemically in a **fixed ratio**
- Compound properties are **entirely different** from its component elements
- Water (H2O): hydrogen is flammable, oxygen supports combustion, but water **puts out fires**
- Table salt: sodium (reactive metal) + chlorine (toxic gas) = harmless white crystals
- Compounds can only be separated by **chemical methods** (electrolysis, strong heat), NOT physical methods
- **Electrolysis of water** produces hydrogen (2 parts) and oxygen (1 part)
- Heating sugar decomposes it into carbon (black residue) and water
- **Iron + sulfur mixture**: magnet picks up iron, HCl produces hydrogen gas
- **Iron sulfide compound**: magnet has no effect, HCl produces hydrogen sulfide (rotten egg smell)
- **Minerals** are naturally occurring solids with fixed composition; most are compounds, some are native elements

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Mixture** | Combination of substances without chemical reaction; components retain their properties |
| **Pure substance** | Contains only one type of particle; cannot be separated physically |
| **Element** | Simplest pure substance; cannot be broken down further; made of one type of atom |
| **Atom** | The smallest particle of an element that retains its identity |
| **Molecule** | Two or more atoms bonded together |
| **Compound** | Substance formed when atoms of 2+ elements bond chemically in a fixed ratio |
| **Alloy** | Uniform mixture of metals (or metals + non-metals) |
| **Mineral** | Naturally occurring solid substance with a fixed chemical composition |

## Remember These

- In a **mixture**, components keep their properties and can be separated physically; in a **compound**, properties are entirely new and separation requires chemical methods
- "Pure" in everyday language (unadulterated) differs from "pure" in science (single type of particle)
- Iron-sulfur experiment: mixture responds to magnet and gives H2 with acid; compound does NOT respond to magnet and gives H2S (rotten egg smell) with acid
- Only 2 liquid elements at room temperature: **mercury** (metal) and **bromine** (non-metal)
- Light, sound, heat, and electricity are NOT matter -- they have no mass or volume
