## Key Concepts

- A **habitat** is an organism's natural home, made of **biotic** (living) and **abiotic** (non-living) components
- **Population** = all individuals of one species in a particular area; **community** = all populations sharing a habitat
- Every organism matters -- removing even one species triggers a **chain of effects** through the ecosystem
- An **ecosystem** = all biotic + abiotic components interacting in an area
- Ecosystems can be **terrestrial** (forests, deserts) or **aquatic** (ponds, oceans)
- Artificial ecosystems (fish tanks, rice paddies, parks) need regular human intervention
- **Producers**: green plants that make food via photosynthesis
- **Consumers**: herbivores (eat plants), carnivores (eat animals), omnivores (eat both)
- **Decomposers**: bacteria and fungi that break down dead matter and return nutrients to soil
- Without decomposers, nutrients would remain locked in dead matter and soil would become barren
- A **food chain** traces energy flow from one organism to the next; each step is a **trophic level**
- A **food web** is interconnected food chains showing the realistic feeding relationships
- Types of interactions: **competition**, **mutualism** (both benefit), **commensalism** (one benefits, other unaffected), **parasitism** (one benefits, other harmed)
- Ecosystems maintain a **dynamic balance** -- populations fluctuate but the system stays stable
- India's frog leg export ban: frog decline → pest surge → excessive pesticide use → soil/water poisoning
- Ecosystem services: clean air, water, timber, medicines, storm barriers, climate stability
- **Sundarbans**: world's largest mangrove forest, UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987)
- Threats: deforestation, pollution, overexploitation, invasive species, monoculture, chemical overload
- **Green Revolution** boosted food production but caused soil degradation, groundwater contamination, pest resistance
- **Organic/natural farming** and ancient practices like **Vrikshayurveda** work with nature, not against it

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Habitat** | Natural home of an organism with its biotic and abiotic components |
| **Biotic** | Living components of an environment |
| **Abiotic** | Non-living components (sunlight, water, air, soil, temperature) |
| **Population** | All individuals of one species in a given area |
| **Community** | Collection of different populations in the same habitat |
| **Ecosystem** | System formed by interactions between biotic and abiotic components |
| **Producer** | Organism that makes its own food (green plants) |
| **Consumer** | Organism that eats other organisms for food |
| **Decomposer** | Organism that breaks down dead matter (bacteria, fungi) |
| **Food chain** | Linear pathway showing energy transfer from one organism to the next |
| **Food web** | Network of interconnected food chains |
| **Mutualism** | Both organisms benefit from the relationship |
| **Commensalism** | One organism benefits, the other is unaffected |
| **Parasitism** | One organism benefits at the expense of the other |

## Remember These

- **Producer → Herbivore → Carnivore → Top Carnivore** -- always remember energy flows in one direction
- Without **decomposers**, the nutrient cycle stops and the entire ecosystem collapses
- India's frog export example shows how removing one species cascades through the whole ecosystem
- **Cuscuta (amarbel)** is a common exam example of a **parasite**
- Protected areas: Kaziranga (rhino), Periyar (elephants/tigers), Sundarbans (Bengal tiger), Keibul Lamjao (only floating national park)
