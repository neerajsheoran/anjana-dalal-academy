## Key Concepts

- A **force** is any push or pull acting on an object; SI unit is **newton (N)**
- A force requires at least **two interacting objects** -- one applying, one receiving
- Effects of force: change **speed**, change **direction**, change **shape**, or a combination
- **Balanced forces** produce no change in motion (e.g., book on a table -- gravity down, table pushes up)
- Forces are classified as **contact forces** (require touch) and **non-contact forces** (act across space)
- **Muscular force** is the force generated by contraction and relaxation of muscles
- **Friction** is the force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact
- Friction arises from microscopic bumps and grooves on surfaces interlocking
- Rough surfaces produce **more friction** than smooth surfaces
- Friction is essential (walking, writing, gripping) but wastes energy in machines
- **Streamlined shapes** reduce air/water friction (racing cars, bullet trains, jets)
- **Magnetic force**: like poles repel, unlike poles attract; acts without contact
- **Electrostatic force**: like charges repel, unlike charges attract; acts without contact
- Rubbing builds **static electric charges** on objects
- **Gravitational force** (gravity) always pulls objects toward Earth's centre; it is always attractive
- Ball thrown up: speed decreases (going up) → momentarily zero (at peak) → speed increases (coming down)
- **Weight** = gravitational force on an object (measured in newtons with a spring balance)
- **Mass** = quantity of matter (measured in kg); stays the same everywhere
- Weight of 1 kg on Earth ≈ 10 N; on Moon ≈ 1.6 N (about 1/6th)
- **Buoyant force (upthrust)** is the upward push a liquid exerts on a submerged object
- Object sinks if gravity > buoyant force; floats if they are equal
- **Archimedes' Principle**: upward force = weight of liquid displaced by the object
- **Pumice** is a volcanic rock light enough to float on water

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Force** | A push or pull acting on an object |
| **Newton (N)** | SI unit of force |
| **Contact force** | Force that acts only when objects physically touch |
| **Non-contact force** | Force that acts across space without physical touch |
| **Friction** | Force that resists motion between two surfaces in contact |
| **Gravitational force** | Earth's pull on every object toward its centre |
| **Weight** | The gravitational force acting on an object (in newtons) |
| **Mass** | The quantity of matter in an object (in kilograms) |
| **Buoyant force** | Upward push exerted by a liquid on a submerged object |

## Remember These

- **Mass is constant** everywhere; **weight changes** with location (Moon, Mars, etc.)
- Friction always acts **opposite** to the direction of motion
- Gravity is always **attractive** -- it only pulls, never pushes
- An object floats when **buoyant force = gravitational force**; sinks when gravity is greater
- Weight of 1 kg: Earth = 10 N, Moon = 1.6 N, Jupiter = 25.4 N
