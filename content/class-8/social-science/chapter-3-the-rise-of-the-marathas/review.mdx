## In One Paragraph

The **Marathas** are a Marathi-speaking people of the Deccan, whose rise as a sovereign political power was the work of **Chhatrapati Shivaji** (1630–1680). Born to Shahji Bhonsle and Jijabai, he began capturing forts at age 16 with a vision he called **Swarajya** — self-rule. Using the hills and forests of the **Western Ghats**, hundreds of hill forts, and **guerrilla warfare**, he defeated Bijapuri general **Afzal Khan** at Pratapgad, raided the Mughal noble **Shaista Khan**, sacked **Surat**, founded the **Maratha navy** (1657), escaped Aurangzeb's prison hidden in a basket of sweets (1666), and was crowned **Chhatrapati at Raigad** in 1674. After his death his son **Sambhaji** continued the war; Aurangzeb spent twenty-six years trying to crush the Marathas and failed, dying in 1707 with the Maratha resistance — led by Tarabai — *expanding*, not collapsing. The 18th century brought the **Peshwa-led confederacy** — with the **Scindias, Holkars, Gaekwads, and Bhonsles of Nagpur** controlling vast regions. The Marathas briefly held **Delhi (1771)**, suffered a great defeat at **Panipat in 1761**, but recovered. They became the **largest pan-Indian power before the British took over**. Three **Anglo-Maratha wars (1775–1818)** finally ended their independence — meaning the British took India from the Marathas more than from any other power. The Maratha legacy: a centralised but humane administration with eight ministers (**Ashta Pradhan Mandal**), a daring navy, a cultural revival of Sanskrit and Marathi, the women rulers **Tarabai** and **Ahilyabai Holkar**, the southern bridge at **Thanjavur** (Carnatic music and Bharatanatyam took modern form there), and above all the idea of **Swarajya** that two centuries later became the rallying cry of India's freedom movement.

## Key Takeaways

- The **Marathas** are a Marathi-speaking people of the Deccan; before Shivaji, they served Deccan sultans without a kingdom of their own.
- **Chhatrapati Shivaji** (1630–1680), son of **Shahji Bhonsle** and **Jijabai**, founded the Maratha kingdom; his vision was **Swarajya** — Indian self-rule.
- **Bhakti saints** like **Dnyaneshwar, Namdev, Tukaram, and Ramdas** gave Maratha society a strong cultural foundation in the centuries before Shivaji.
- Shivaji built power around **hill forts in the Western Ghats** and **guerrilla warfare**; he killed **Afzal Khan** at Pratapgad and raided **Shaista Khan** in Pune.
- He sacked **Surat** twice, founded the **Maratha navy** (1657), and escaped from house arrest at **Agra** by hiding in baskets of sweets (1666).
- He was crowned **Chhatrapati at Raigad in 1674** with full Vedic rites; minted coins in his name in **Devanagari** script; started his own **Rajyabhisheka shaka** era.
- His **Ashta Pradhan Mandal** — eight ministers (Peshwa, Amatya, Sachiv, Mantri, Senapati, Sumant, Nyayadhish, Panditrao) — was a model of centralised, salaried administration; he abolished hereditary jagirs.
- After Shivaji, **Sambhaji** was tortured and killed by Aurangzeb (1689); **Rajaram** fled to **Gingee**; queen **Tarabai** led the northward Maratha expansion; Aurangzeb died in 1707 having failed to subdue the Marathas.
- The 18th-century **Maratha Confederacy** was led by the **Peshwas** at Pune, with the **Scindias (Gwalior), Holkars (Indore), Gaekwads (Baroda), and Bhonsles (Nagpur)** as major regional chiefs.
- **Peshwa Bajirao I** was a brilliant cavalry commander; under **Nanasaheb Peshwa** the Marathas reached their territorial peak — from Attock and Peshawar in the northwest to the deep south.
- The Marathas suffered a great defeat at the **Third Battle of Panipat (1761)** against Ahmad Shah Abdali but recovered under **Madhavrao I**; **Mahadji Shinde (Scindia)** recaptured **Delhi in 1771**.
- The **Anglo-Maratha wars** (1775–1818) ended Maratha power; the British took India from the Marathas more than from the Mughals.
- The Marathas levied **chauth (25%)** and **sardeshmukhi (10%)** from territories not directly under them, in exchange for protection.
- The **Maratha navy** under **Kanhoji Angre** was one of the few Asian forces to seriously contest European sea power; coastal forts included **Sindhudurg and Vijaydurg**.
- **Tarabai** was the architect of the northward expansion; **Ahilyabai Holkar** of Indore ruled Malwa wisely for 30 years and rebuilt **Kashi Vishwanath**, **Somnath**, and hundreds of other temples across India.
- The Marathas under **Ekoji** and later **Serfoji II** ruled **Thanjavur**, where modern **Carnatic music** and **Bharatanatyam** took shape; Serfoji II ran the first **Indian-owned printing press**.
- Shivaji's **Swarajya** ideal inspired the **freedom movement** two centuries later — **Tilak's** "Swarajya is my birthright" echoes directly from this chapter.

## Key Terms

- **Swarajya** — "self-rule"; Shivaji's vision of Indian political, economic, and cultural independence.
- **Chhatrapati** — "Lord of the Umbrella"; the sovereign title taken by Shivaji at his coronation in 1674.
- **Peshwa** — "prime minister" (Persian); the hereditary chief minister at Pune who in the 18th century became the effective head of the Maratha state.
- **Ashta Pradhan Mandal** — Shivaji's council of eight ministers.
- **Jagir** — a land grant from a ruler to a noble or soldier; Shivaji abolished hereditary jagirs for officials.
- **Chauth** — a 25% tax demanded by the Marathas from territories not directly governed by them, in exchange for protection.
- **Sardeshmukhi** — an additional 10% tax beyond chauth, based on the older Maratha claim of overlordship.
- **Bargir / Shiledar** — two classes of Maratha cavalry; bargirs were equipped by the state, shiledars by themselves.
- **Wagh nakh** — "tiger's claw"; a small concealable claw-shaped weapon Shivaji used to kill Afzal Khan.
- **Modi** — a cursive form of Devanagari used as the everyday handwriting of the Maratha administration.
- **Maratha Confederacy** — the 18th-century alliance of Maratha houses (Peshwas, Scindias, Holkars, Gaekwads, Bhonsles of Nagpur) under the nominal authority of the Chhatrapati.
- **Dakshina-digvijaya** — Shivaji's southern campaign of 1677 that extended Maratha influence into Tamil Nadu and Karnataka.
