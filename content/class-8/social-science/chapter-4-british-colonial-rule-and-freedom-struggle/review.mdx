## In One Paragraph

The **Colonial Era in India** is the story of how a London-based trading company — the **English East India Company** — turned itself into the ruler of a Subcontinent, and how Indians eventually pushed it out. In **1498**, **Vasco da Gama** reached Kerala and the Portuguese, Dutch, French, and British set up trading posts along India's coasts. The British outmanoeuvred the rest. After winning the **Battle of Plassey in 1757** through Mir Jafar's betrayal of **Siraj-ud-Daulah**, **Robert Clive** seized control of Bengal, and the Company began extracting wealth through brutal taxation. The result was famine — **10 million dead in 1770–72**, perhaps **50–100 million dead** across the entire colonial period. Indian industries were deliberately destroyed; India's share of world GDP fell from **about 25 per cent in 1700 to under 5 per cent in 1947**. Tools of conquest included the **Doctrine of Lapse**, **Subsidiary Alliance**, **Macaulay's 1835 education Minute**, and railways and infrastructure paid for by Indian taxes. Indians resisted from the start — Rani Abbakka, the Sannyasi-Fakir rebellion, the Santhals, the Indigo Revolt — and in **1857**, the **Great Rebellion** led by Mangal Pandey, Bahadur Shah Zafar, Rani Lakshmibai, and Begum Hazrat Mahal nearly toppled the Company. It was crushed; the British Crown took over in **1858**; but the seed of freedom had been planted. Through the Indian National Congress, **Gandhi's** non-violent satyagraha, and the sacrifices of countless freedom fighters, India finally won independence on **15 August 1947** — leaving behind one of the largest wealth transfers in human history (an estimated **45 trillion US dollars** drained) and a country trying ever since to rebuild what was taken.

## Key Takeaways

- **Colonialism** is when one country takes control of another, ruling it and extracting its wealth — Europe's age of colonialism lasted from the 15th century to the mid-20th
- Before the Europeans came, **India produced about a quarter of world GDP** and was one of the two richest economies on earth
- **1498:** Vasco da Gama reached Kappad in Kerala; the Portuguese built Goa as their colonial capital and ran the brutal **Goa Inquisition (1560–1812)**
- The **Dutch** were defeated by King Marthanda Varma at the **Battle of Colachel (1741)**; the **French** were limited to Pondicherry after the **Carnatic Wars (1746–63)**
- The **English East India Company** had a royal charter from Queen Elizabeth I allowing it to keep a **private army** — its trading posts at Surat, Madras, Bombay, and Calcutta became platforms for conquest
- **Divide and rule** — the British exploited rivalries between Indian rulers and divisions within Indian society
- **Battle of Plassey, 1757** — Robert Clive defeated **Siraj-ud-Daulah** of Bengal after **Mir Jafar's** betrayal; Mir Jafar's name is still a synonym for traitor in India today
- The **Doctrine of Lapse** (Lord Dalhousie) and the **Subsidiary Alliance** system (Lord Wellesley) allowed the Company to swallow princely states without overt war
- **Famines** under British rule killed between **50 million and 100 million** Indians — including 10 million in Bengal in 1770–72 and 8 million in the Great Famine of 1876–78
- The **drain of wealth** from India to Britain is estimated at about **45 trillion US dollars** in today's value — possibly the largest sustained wealth transfer in history
- The British deliberately **deindustrialised India** — Indian textiles, iron, and other manufactures were ruined by rigged trade rules; India's share of world GDP collapsed from ~25% (1700) to <5% (1947)
- **Macaulay's Minute (1835)** redesigned Indian education to create Indians "English in taste, in opinions, in morals, and in intellect" — destroying traditional pathshalas and madrasas
- **Indians paid for their own subjugation** — railways, telegraph, British military expeditions, even British salaries were funded by Indian taxes
- Early resistance came from the **Sannyasi-Fakir rebellion**, the **Vellore Mutiny (1806)**, the **Kol Uprising (1831–32)**, the **Santhal Rebellion (1855–56)**, and the **Indigo Revolt (1859–62)**
- The **Great Rebellion of 1857** — sparked by **Mangal Pandey** at Barrackpore — saw **Bahadur Shah Zafar** proclaimed Emperor, and was led by **Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi, Tatya Tope, Nana Saheb, and Begum Hazrat Mahal**; it failed but planted the seed of the freedom struggle
- After 1857 the British Crown took over directly (the **British Raj**); the freedom struggle then gradually built up through the **Indian National Congress (1885)**, the **Swadeshi movement**, and **Gandhi's** non-violent satyagraha, culminating in **Independence on 15 August 1947**

## Key Terms

- **Colonialism** — the system in which one country takes control of another, settling it, ruling it, and extracting wealth and resources
- **East India Company** — the English (later British) trading company chartered in 1600 that ruled large parts of India until 1858
- **Sepoy** — an Indian soldier serving in a European-officered army (especially the East India Company's army)
- **Plassey** — the 1757 battle in Bengal in which Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah, marking the beginning of British political power in India
- **Doctrine of Lapse** — the policy under which the Company annexed any princely state whose ruler died without a natural male heir, ignoring the Hindu tradition of adoption
- **Subsidiary Alliance** — the system in which Indian rulers accepted British "protection," British troops, and a British Resident, in exchange for surrendering real power
- **Drain of Wealth** — the theory, made famous by **Dadabhai Naoroji**, that British rule was systematically transferring Indian wealth to Britain
- **Deindustrialisation** — the deliberate destruction of India's existing industries (especially textiles) through rigged British trade policy
- **Macaulay's Minute (1835)** — Thomas B. Macaulay's policy paper that reshaped Indian education to favour English over Indian languages and learning
- **Great Rebellion of 1857 / First War of Independence** — the massive uprising against the East India Company that began at Meerut and spread across northern and central India
- **British Raj** — the period of direct rule by the British Crown over India, from 1858 to 1947
- **Satyagraha** — Gandhi's method of non-violent resistance to injustice, literally "truth-force," which became the central strategy of India's freedom movement
