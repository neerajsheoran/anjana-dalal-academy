## In One Paragraph

Every good or service you use — from a roti at home to the phone in your pocket — is made by combining **four factors of production**: **land** (natural resources like soil, water, forests, minerals, sunlight), **labour** (the physical and mental effort of people), **capital** (money plus human-made things like machines, tools, buildings, vehicles, software), and **entrepreneurship** (the spark that imagines a business, takes the risk, and brings the other three factors together). The quality of labour depends on **human capital** — the skills, knowledge, and dedication a worker brings — which is built by **education, training, healthcare, and culture**. **Technology** is a powerful enabler that lets the same factors produce more output. Different products use these factors in different proportions: handicrafts and farming are **labour-intensive**, while semiconductor chips and satellites are **capital-intensive**. The factors flow through global **supply chains** that can be disrupted (as during COVID-19). Indian examples — Ratna's roadside restaurant, the **Amul** dairy cooperative, **J.R.D. Tata**, India becoming the world's **second-largest mobile phone manufacturer** — show all four factors at work. Producers also carry **responsibilities** toward natural resources and workers, which is why India became the **first country** to mandate **Corporate Social Responsibility** by law in 2014.

## Key Takeaways

- The resources used in any production process are called **factors of production**.
- There are **four factors**: land, labour, capital, and entrepreneurship.
- **Land** in economics means **all natural resources** — soil, water, forests, minerals, oil, sunlight — not just plots of ground.
- **Labour** is the **physical and mental effort** humans contribute to production.
- **Human capital** is the **skill, knowledge, and expertise** that makes labour productive. It is built by **education, training, healthcare, and culture**.
- **Capital** is **money plus human-made things** used in production — machines, tools, vehicles, buildings, software.
- Most small businesses begin with **personal savings, family, and friends**; larger ones borrow from banks or raise money from the **stock market** by selling shares and paying **dividends**.
- An **entrepreneur** identifies a problem, takes risk, combines the other three factors, makes key decisions, and serves society. **J.R.D. Tata** is a model Indian example.
- **Technology** is not a fifth factor but a **facilitator** — it lets the same inputs produce more output (UPI, drones, GPS, online learning platforms like SWAYAM).
- Industries can be **labour-intensive** (handicrafts, farming, construction) or **capital-intensive** (chips, satellites, petrochemicals).
- Factors flow through **supply chains** — networks across regions and countries that can be disrupted (as during COVID-19).
- **Amul** is the textbook example of all four factors at work — village dairy farmers, modern plants, marketing, and visionary leadership.
- India enjoys a **demographic dividend** — 65% of Indians are under 35 — which becomes growth only if matched by education, healthcare, and skilling.
- Producers have **responsibilities** to use natural resources sustainably and treat workers fairly. India made **CSR** legally mandatory in **2014** — a world first.

## Key Terms

- **Factors of production** — the four inputs (land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship) used to make goods and services
- **Land** — all natural resources used in production: soil, water, forests, minerals, sunlight, etc.
- **Labour** — the physical and mental effort humans put into production
- **Human capital** — the skills, knowledge, dedication, and ability of workers; built by education, training, health, and culture
- **Capital** — money plus all human-made things used in production (machines, tools, buildings, vehicles, software)
- **Entrepreneurship** — the activity of starting and running a business: spotting a problem, taking risk, combining factors, deciding direction
- **Interest** — money paid by a borrower to a lender for using the lender's money over time
- **Dividend** — a share of profit paid by a company to its shareholders
- **Productivity** — the amount of output produced in a given time
- **Demographic dividend** — the economic benefit a country gets when most of its population is young and working-age
- **Supply chain** — the network of people, organisations, resources, activities, and technology involved in making and selling a product
- **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)** — the duty of a business to contribute to society and the environment; made legally mandatory in India in 2014
