## In One Paragraph

A **natural resource** is any material or substance found in Nature that humans can reach, can afford, and feel right about using. Trees in a forest are part of Nature; a wooden chair is the resource. We can sort resources by **what they do for us** — life-sustaining (air, water, soil), material (wood, marble, iron), or energy (coal, sunlight, wind) — or by whether Nature replaces them as fast as we use them. **Renewable resources** (sunlight, wind, river water, well-managed forests) keep coming back if we respect Nature's rhythm; **non-renewable resources** (coal, oil, metals) form over millions of years and run out once used. Resources are unevenly distributed across India — coal in the east, oil in Mumbai High and Assam, iron in Bailadila and Keonjhar — which shapes industry, trade and sometimes conflict (like the Kaveri waters dispute). Having lots of resources doesn't automatically make a country rich (the **resource curse**); turning raw materials into finished goods does. Over-using groundwater in Punjab, polluting rivers with industrial waste, burning fossil fuels — all push Nature past her ability to restore and regenerate. The way forward is **stewardship**: stretching non-renewables, protecting renewables, and remembering the Bhagavad Gita's idea of **lokasangraha** — acting for the welfare of all.

## Key Takeaways

- A part of Nature becomes a **resource** only when it is **technologically accessible**, **economically feasible** and **culturally acceptable** to use.
- Resources can be sorted by use — **life-essential, material, energy** — or by **renewable vs non-renewable**.
- **Renewable resources** (sunlight, wind, river water, forests, soil) regenerate naturally — but only if we don't overuse them.
- **Non-renewable resources** (coal, petroleum, natural gas, iron, copper, gold) form over geological time and cannot be replaced once used.
- Nature works in **cycles** with no waste; **restoration** heals damage, **regeneration** creates new life.
- Resources are **unevenly distributed** across India and the world — coal in Damodar Valley, oil in Mumbai High and Assam, iron in Bailadila.
- Resource distribution shapes **industries, townships, trade routes** and sometimes **conflicts** (Kaveri waters, Brahmaputra sharing).
- The **resource curse** or **paradox of plenty**: countries rich in raw resources may still grow slowly if they don't develop industries to add value.
- India's **Green Revolution** in Punjab fed the country but left **80% of Punjab's groundwater overexploited**.
- **Cement production** is one of the most polluting industries; traditional materials like **mud, stone and lime** are being revived (Auroville, Jaisalmer Fort).
- **Sikkim** became India's first **100% organic state** in 2016 — farmer incomes rose by 20%, biodiversity returned.
- India co-founded the **International Solar Alliance** with France in 2015; the **Bhadla Solar Park** in Rajasthan is among the world's largest.
- Resource access is **unequal within India** — some neighbourhoods have piped water, others rely on tankers; pollution often hits the poorest hardest.
- **Stewardship** means letting renewables stay renewable and stretching non-renewables until we can replace them — the Bhagavad Gita calls this **lokasangraha**.

## Key Terms

- **Natural resource** — Any material or substance found in Nature that is technologically accessible, economically feasible, and culturally acceptable to use.
- **Exploitation** — In this chapter, the extraction, utilisation and consumption of natural resources (not a negative word here).
- **Renewable resource** — A resource Nature replaces at roughly the rate we use it, like sunlight, wind, river water or a sustainably managed forest.
- **Non-renewable resource** — A resource formed over geological time that runs out once used, like coal, petroleum or metals.
- **Restoration** — Nature's ability to return to a healthy state after damage (a forest re-growing after a fire).
- **Regeneration** — Nature's ability to create new life and the conditions for life to thrive.
- **Ecosystem services** — The benefits humans get from natural processes (clean water from a forest, pollinated crops from bees).
- **Resource curse / paradox of plenty** — The puzzle that countries rich in raw natural resources often grow more slowly than resource-poor countries unless they build industries to add value.
- **Green Revolution** — The 1960s shift in Indian agriculture to high-yield wheat and rice with chemical fertilisers and heavy irrigation; made India food-secure but stressed groundwater and soils.
- **Overexploited groundwater** — A region where water is being pumped from below the ground faster than rain can refill it.
- **Stewardship** — Caring for resources on behalf of others, including future generations.
- **Lokasangraha** — A concept from the **Bhagavad Gita** meaning acting for the welfare of all, transcending personal desire.
- **International Solar Alliance (ISA)** — A coalition of sunshine-rich countries co-founded by India and France in 2015 to promote solar power.
- **Vrikshayurveda** — An ancient Indian botanical science covering plant care, soil management, seed treatment and sustainable cultivation.
