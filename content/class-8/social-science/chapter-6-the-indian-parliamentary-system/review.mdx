## In One Paragraph

India is a **parliamentary democracy** where the people elect representatives who make laws and choose the government. The **Parliament** has three parts — the **President**, the directly elected **Lok Sabha** (up to 550, currently 543 seats) and the indirectly elected **Rajya Sabha** (up to 245 seats). This **bicameral** structure was chosen so that the population is represented through the Lok Sabha and the states through the Rajya Sabha. Parliament does four big jobs: it upholds the Constitution, makes laws, holds the executive accountable, and approves and monitors government spending. A proposed law begins as a **bill**, is examined by a **Standing Committee**, debated clause by clause, passed by both houses, given **Presidential assent**, and finally notified in the Gazette as an Act. The **executive** — the **President, Vice President, and the Council of Ministers led by the Prime Minister** — implements these laws. The Council is **collectively responsible** to the Lok Sabha, which keeps it accountable through **Question Hour, Zero Hour, Standing Committees, the Public Accounts Committee, debates,** and the ultimate weapon: the **no-confidence motion**. The same parliamentary structure is mirrored at the state level through **Vidhan Sabhas, Vidhan Parishads, Governors,** and **Chief Ministers**. The whole arrangement is held together by the **separation of powers and checks and balances** between legislature, executive, and judiciary — designed so that no single organ becomes too powerful and the citizen's rights and the federal spirit are always protected.

## Key Takeaways

- India chose the **parliamentary system** over the presidential one because of familiarity, Indian republican traditions (mahajanapadas, panchayats), and the need for an executive that can be removed if it loses Parliament's confidence.
- **Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha.** A two-house legislature is called **bicameral**.
- The **Lok Sabha** has up to 550 seats (543 elected today) and is directly elected for a 5-year term; the **Speaker** presides over it.
- The **Rajya Sabha** has up to 245 members (233 elected by State Assemblies plus 12 nominated by the President), is a permanent body where one-third retire every two years, and is chaired by the **Vice President of India**.
- Parliament's four functions: **constitutional, lawmaking, executive accountability, financial accountability**.
- A bill becomes law through introduction, committee review, clause-by-clause debate, vote in both houses, **Presidential assent**, and Gazette notification — illustrated by the journey of the **RTE Act, 2009**.
- A **Money Bill** can only originate in the Lok Sabha and only with the **prior recommendation of the President**.
- The **Union Executive** is the **President, Vice President, and Council of Ministers** led by the **Prime Minister** — the de facto executive authority.
- The Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible** to the Lok Sabha; if it loses a no-confidence vote, the whole government falls.
- Tools to keep the executive accountable: **Question Hour, Zero Hour, Standing Committees, the Public Accounts Committee,** and the **no-confidence motion**.
- The annual **Union Budget** must be passed before the government can spend any money; the **CAG** and **Public Accounts Committee** check whether it was spent properly.
- The structure is mirrored at the state level: **Governor** (nominal), **Chief Minister** (real), **Vidhan Sabha** (always), **Vidhan Parishad** (in 6 states), and **MLAs** instead of MPs.
- Power is divided into the **Union List, State List, and Concurrent List**, with the Union law prevailing on the Concurrent List.
- **Separation of powers** and **checks and balances** keep the legislature, executive, and judiciary in their lanes; the judiciary is the **custodian of the Constitution**.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Bicameral** | A legislature with two houses (e.g., Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha at the Centre). |
| **Lok Sabha** | The House of the People; up to 550 directly elected members; 5-year term; the Lower House. |
| **Rajya Sabha** | The Council of States; up to 245 indirectly elected/nominated members; permanent body; the Upper House. |
| **Speaker** | The presiding officer of the Lok Sabha, elected by its own members. |
| **Bill** | A draft of a proposed law that must be approved by Parliament before becoming an Act. |
| **Money Bill** | A bill dealing with taxation, borrowing, or government spending; can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha with the President's prior recommendation. |
| **Question Hour** | The first hour of a parliamentary sitting (typically 11 am-12 noon) when MPs question ministers on government actions. |
| **Zero Hour** | The period after Question Hour when MPs can raise matters of urgent public importance without prior notice. |
| **Standing Committee** | A permanent committee of MPs from different parties that scrutinises government work and examines bills. |
| **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** | The powerful committee, traditionally chaired by an Opposition MP, that examines whether government money was spent as Parliament authorised. |
| **Council of Ministers** | The team of ministers headed by the Prime Minister, collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. |
| **Collective Responsibility** | The principle that the entire Council of Ministers stands or falls together; if it loses Lok Sabha confidence, the whole government resigns. |
