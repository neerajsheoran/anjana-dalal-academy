## In One Paragraph

Between the **13th and 17th centuries**, India's political map was reshaped again and again by waves of dynasties from beyond the Hindu Kush and from within the Subcontinent itself. The **Delhi Sultanate** (1206–1526), ruled by five Turkic-Afghan dynasties — **Mamluks, Khiljis, Tughlaqs, Sayyids, and Lodis** — expanded through plunder, taxes, slave trade, and the iqta system, but faced constant succession violence, Mongol invasions, and **Timur's** devastating attack of 1398. Across India, regional powers resisted: the **Eastern Gangas** of Kalinga under **Narasimhadeva I**, the **Hoysalas** and later the **Vijayanagara Empire** in the south (1336, peaking under **Krishnadevaraya** before its destruction at **Talikota** in 1565), the **Musunuri Nayakas** in Telugu lands, **Rana Kumbha** of Mewar, and the breakaway Bahmani and Deccan Sultanates. In **1526**, **Babur** ended the Sultanate at the **First Battle of Panipat** and founded the **Mughal Empire**, which expanded greatly under **Akbar** (whose reforms included abolishing jizya, promoting sulh-i-kul, the mansabdari system, and Todar Mal's revenue settlement) and reached its peak — and breaking strain — under **Aurangzeb**. Resistance ran throughout: **Maharana Pratap** and the Rajputs, **Rani Durgavati** of the Gonds, the **Ahoms** under **Lachit Borphukan** at **Saraighat** in 1671, and the **Sikhs** from Guru Nanak to **Guru Gobind Singh's Khalsa**. Through it all, Indian agriculture, crafts, hundi-based trade, and temple economies kept the Subcontinent one of the wealthiest regions of the world.

## Key Takeaways

- The **Delhi Sultanate** (1206–1526) was ruled by five successive Turkic-Afghan dynasties — Mamluks, Khiljis, Tughlaqs, Sayyids, Lodis — with an average sultan's reign of just **nine years** due to constant violent successions
- **Ala-ud-din Khilji** repelled the Mongols and pushed his armies — through his slave-general **Malik Kafur** — deep into south India, sacking temple cities like Srirangam and Madurai
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** stretched the Sultanate to its greatest extent but his failed schemes — shifting capital to **Daulatabad** and introducing **token currency** — caused enormous suffering and economic collapse
- **Timur's** invasion in **1398** destroyed Delhi and broke the Sultanate's power; the **Lodis** were its last dynasty
- Resistance to the Sultanate came from the **Eastern Gangas** under **Narasimhadeva I** (who built the **Konark Sun Temple**), the **Hoysalas**, the **Musunuri Nayakas**, and **Rana Kumbha** of Mewar (builder of **Kumbhalgarh Fort**)
- The **Vijayanagara Empire** was founded in **1336** by **Harihara and Bukka**, peaked under **Krishnadevaraya** (poet of *Amuktamalyada*), and was destroyed at the **Battle of Talikota** in **1565** by the coalition of five **Deccan Sultanates** (Bijapur, Golconda, Berar, Ahmednagar, Bidar)
- The **Mughal Empire** began with **Babur's** victory over Ibrahim Lodi at the **First Battle of Panipat (1526)** using gunpowder artillery
- **Akbar** (1556–1605) abolished the **jizya**, promoted **sulh-i-kul** (peace with all), made marriage alliances with Rajputs, established a house of translation at **Fatehpur Sikri**, and built the **mansabdari** administrative system documented in Abul Fazl's **Ain-i-Akbari**
- **Aurangzeb** (1658–1707) pushed the Mughal Empire to its greatest size but drained it in 25 years of Deccan wars; he reimposed the **jizya** and ordered destruction of temples
- Mughal resistance included **Maharana Pratap** (guerrilla war from the Aravallis after **Haldighati 1576**, aided by **Bhil** archers), **Rani Durgavati** of the Gonds, the **Ahoms** under **Lachit Borphukan** at **Saraighat (1671)**, and the **Sikhs** — culminating in **Guru Tegh Bahadur's** martyrdom and **Guru Gobind Singh's** founding of the **Khalsa** in **1699**
- The Sultanate ran on the **iqta system**; the Mughals improved this through the **mansabdari/jagirdari system**, twelve provincial **subahs**, and **Todar Mal's** land revenue survey
- India remained one of the world's wealthiest regions through this period — sustained by agriculture, textiles, ports like **Surat, Calicut, Masulipatnam, Hooghly**, the **hundi** credit system, and temple-based local economies — even as common peasants and labourers often suffered
- Through constant political churning, the people of the Subcontinent showed remarkable **resilience**, blending indigenous and foreign elements into shared cultural traditions in art, music, language, and architecture

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Sultanate** | A territory ruled by a Sultan — a title adopted by some Muslim rulers |
| **Iqta** | A territory assigned to a noble (iqtadar) under the Delhi Sultanate to collect taxes; non-hereditary |
| **Jizya** | A tax imposed on non-Muslim subjects in exchange for protection; abolished by Akbar, reimposed by Aurangzeb |
| **Jauhar** | The act of Rajput women jumping into mass fires to avoid capture and dishonour after a fort fell |
| **Mansabdari** | Akbar's system of ranking officers (mansabdars) who were required to maintain a set number of troops and animals for the empire |
| **Jagir** | A land grant assigned to a mansabdar in lieu of salary; hence mansabdars were also called jagirdars |
| **Sulh-i-kul** | "Peace with all" — Akbar's doctrine of tolerance toward all faiths |
| **Hundi** | A written instruction to make a payment, used by merchants to transfer funds across regions without carrying currency — an early form of banking |
| **Guerrilla warfare** | Surprise attacks and ambushes by small groups using knowledge of difficult terrain to defeat larger armies |
| **Iconoclasm** | The rejection or destruction of religious images considered idolatrous |
| **Paik system** | The Ahom system requiring every able-bodied man to give labour or military service to the state in exchange for land rights |
| **Khalsa** | The martial brotherhood founded by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699, committed to justice, equality, and defence of the Sikh faith |

## Remember These

- The **average sultan ruled only nine years** — violence was built into the system; Mughal emperors averaged **27 years** because succession (though still violent) gave the winner a long reign
- **First Battle of Panipat (1526) — Babur** vs. **Ibrahim Lodi**: the gunpowder battle that founded the Mughal Empire
- **Second Battle of Panipat (1556) — Akbar's** forces vs. **Hemu**: secured the Mughal throne
- **Battle of Haldighati (1576) — Akbar** vs. **Maharana Pratap**: Pratap retreated and then waged years of guerrilla war
- **Battle of Talikota (1565)** — Deccan Sultanates destroy Vijayanagara
- **Battle of Saraighat (1671)** — Ahoms under **Lachit Borphukan** defeat Aurangzeb's army on the Brahmaputra
- **Konark Sun Temple** = Narasimhadeva I (Eastern Ganga). **Kumbhalgarh** = Rana Kumbha. **Hampi** = Vijayanagara. **Fatehpur Sikri**, **Humayun's Tomb**, **Red Fort**, **Taj Mahal** = Mughal architectural peak
