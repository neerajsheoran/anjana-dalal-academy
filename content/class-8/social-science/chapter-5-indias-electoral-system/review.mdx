## In One Paragraph

India practises **universal adult franchise** — every citizen aged 18 or older has the right to vote, with one vote per person of equal value, regardless of caste, gender, religion, education or wealth. This principle is enshrined in **Article 326** of the Constitution and was extended to all adult Indians from the very first general election of **1951–52** under Chief Election Commissioner **Sukumar Sen** — a bold choice given that literacy was only 14 per cent at independence. Elections are run by the **Election Commission of India (ECI)**, an independent constitutional body set up in 1950, headed by a Chief Election Commissioner and two Election Commissioners. The ECI conducts elections to the **Lok Sabha**, the **Rajya Sabha**, state assemblies, and the offices of the **President** and **Vice President**, enforces the **Model Code of Conduct**, and oversees voter rolls. Voters cast their ballot on **Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs)** backed by **VVPAT** paper slips for verification, and may choose **NOTA** to reject all candidates. India uses the **First-Past-the-Post** system for direct elections, while the President, Vice President and Rajya Sabha members are chosen indirectly through electoral colleges using the **single transferable vote**. Reformers like **T. N. Seshan** made the ECI a feared, neutral institution. Today the system faces challenges — money power, criminalisation of politics, voter apathy and digital misinformation — making aware, responsible voting more important than ever.

## Key Takeaways

- **Universal adult franchise** = every citizen aged 18+ has one vote of equal value, written into **Article 326** of the Constitution
- India adopted universal franchise from **day one (1951–52)** — bold, given 14% literacy rate at Independence
- The **voting age was lowered from 21 to 18 in 1988**
- About **980 million voters**, **543 Lok Sabha constituencies**, **over 1 million polling stations** in the 2024 general election
- The **Election Commission of India (ECI)**, set up in **1950**, is an independent constitutional body that runs elections
- ECI is headed by the **Chief Election Commissioner + 2 Election Commissioners**; **Sukumar Sen** was the first CEC
- Local body (panchayat, municipal) elections are run by **State Election Commissions**, not the ECI directly
- **EVMs** (rolled out fully by 2004) replaced paper ballots; **VVPAT** machines print a paper slip after every vote for verification
- **NOTA** ("None of the Above") lets voters reject all candidates without staying home
- **84 Lok Sabha seats** are reserved for SCs and **47 for STs**
- India uses **First-Past-the-Post** — most votes wins, even without 50%
- **Rajya Sabha** has 245 members: 233 elected indirectly by MLAs, 12 nominated by the President; it is the **Permanent House**
- The **President** is elected by an Electoral College of elected MPs + elected MLAs (Delhi & Puducherry too); the **Vice President** by elected + nominated MPs of both Houses; both via the **single transferable vote**
- The **Model Code of Conduct (MCC)** governs election conduct — adopted in Kerala in 1960, enforced nationally by ECI from 1991
- **T. N. Seshan** (CEC from 1990) transformed Indian elections — voter IDs, spending limits, neutral enforcement

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Franchise / Suffrage** | The right to vote granted by the constitution or government |
| **Universal Adult Franchise** | Right to vote for every adult citizen, irrespective of caste, gender, religion, education or wealth |
| **Constituency** | A geographical area whose voters elect a representative to a legislative body |
| **Electoral Roll** | The official list of registered voters in a constituency |
| **Direct Election** | Voters directly choose their representative (e.g., Lok Sabha, MLAs) |
| **Indirect Election** | Elected representatives or an Electoral College choose someone on the voters' behalf (e.g., Rajya Sabha, President) |
| **First-Past-the-Post (FPTP)** | The candidate with the most votes wins, even without securing 50% |
| **EVM** | Electronic Voting Machine — records votes electronically |
| **VVPAT** | Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail — prints a paper slip so voters can verify their EVM vote |
| **NOTA** | None Of The Above — option to reject all candidates |
| **Model Code of Conduct** | ECI-enforced rules for parties and candidates during elections |
| **Secret Ballot** | Voting kept private so no one can know whom you voted for |

## Remember These

- Universal franchise means **one vote, one value, every adult citizen** — and no one can vote on your behalf
- The voting age changed from **21 to 18 in 1988**
- ECI was set up in **1950**, first general election held in **1951–52**, first CEC was **Sukumar Sen**
- **EVMs + VVPAT** = electronic vote with a paper backup
- **First-Past-the-Post**: most votes wins, even below 50% (like Gurmat's 12/33 in the class election)
- President is elected by **elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi & Puducherry)** — *nominated* members are excluded
- **Rajya Sabha is never dissolved** — one-third retires every two years
- **T. N. Seshan** transformed Indian elections in the 1990s — a favourite exam name
