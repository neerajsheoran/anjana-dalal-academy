## Key Concepts

- The square built on the **diagonal** of a square has exactly **double** the area
- Connecting the **midpoints** of a square's sides creates a tilted inner square with **half** the area
- In a right triangle, the longest side (opposite the right angle) is called the **hypotenuse**
- **Baudhayana-Pythagoras Theorem:** In a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c: **a² + b² = c²**
- Baudhyana stated this in the **Shulba-Sutra** (~800 BCE), predating Pythagoras by ~300 years
- For an isosceles right triangle with legs a: hypotenuse = **a x sqrt(2)**, and c² = 2a²
- **sqrt(2) is irrational** -- it cannot be expressed as a fraction; its decimal never ends or repeats
- sqrt(2) ≈ 1.41421356...
- **Pythagorean triples** are whole-number sets (a, b, c) satisfying a² + b² = c²
- Common triples: (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (8,15,17), (7,24,25)
- If (a,b,c) is a triple, then (ka, kb, kc) is also a triple for any positive k
- **Converse:** If a² + b² = c² for three sides, the triangle must be right-angled
- The theorem is used to find distances, heights, and verify right angles

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Hypotenuse | The longest side of a right triangle, opposite the right angle |
| Pythagorean triple | Three whole numbers (a, b, c) where a² + b² = c² |
| Irrational number | A number that cannot be expressed as a fraction; its decimal is infinite and non-repeating |
| Converse | The reverse statement: if a² + b² = c², the triangle is right-angled |
| Shulba-Sutra | Ancient Indian geometric text by Baudhyana (~800 BCE) |

## Key Formulas

| Formula | Use |
|---------|-----|
| a² + b² = c² | Finding the missing side of a right triangle |
| c = a x sqrt(2) | Hypotenuse of an isosceles right triangle with equal legs a |

## Remember These

- Doubling the side of a square gives **4 times** the area, not double -- use the diagonal for double area
- Always identify which side is the hypotenuse (the **longest** side, opposite the right angle) before applying the theorem
- The converse is equally important: use it to **test** whether a triangle is right-angled (e.g., 9² + 12² = 15² confirms a right triangle)
- sqrt(2) cannot be a fraction because that leads to a contradiction with even/odd prime factor counts
