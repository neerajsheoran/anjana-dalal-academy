## 3.1 Proportionality -- A Quick Recap

When two or more related quantities change by the **same factor**, we call the relationship **proportional**.

**Example:** Dosa batter calls for rice and urad dal in the ratio **3 : 1**. Lakshmi uses 9 cups of rice and 3 cups of dal (9 : 3), while Kamala uses 6 cups of rice and 2 cups of dal (6 : 2). Will their dosas taste the same?

Check by **cross multiplication**: 9 x 2 = 18 and 3 x 6 = 18. The products match, so the ratios are proportional. The dosas will taste identical!

Two ratios a : b and c : d are proportional when:
- **a x d = b x c** (cross multiplication), or equivalently
- **a/b = c/d**

## 3.2 Ratios in Maps

Maps use a **Representative Fraction (RF)** to express the scale. For example, **1 : 40,00,000** means 1 cm on the map stands for 40,00,000 cm (= 40 km) on the ground.

**How to read a map scale:**
1. Measure the distance between two locations on the map with a ruler (in cm).
2. Multiply by the scale factor to obtain the real geographical distance.

**Example:** If Mumbai and Pune are 3.75 cm apart on a map scaled at 1 : 40,00,000:
- Actual distance = 3.75 x 40 = **150 km** (approximately)

## 3.3 Ratios with More than 2 Terms

Ratios can involve **several terms** when comparing multiple quantities at once.

**Example:** Lakshmi's sambar masala uses:
- 6 spoons coriander seeds : 3 red chillies : 2 spoons chana dal : 1 spoon pepper
- Ratio: **6 : 3 : 2 : 1**

If Kamala has only 1 red chilli (one-third of Lakshmi's 3), she must scale everything by 1/3:
- **2 : 1 : 2/3 : 1/3**

The ratios are proportional: 6 : 3 : 2 : 1 :: 2 : 1 : 2/3 : 1/3

When multi-term ratios are proportional, a : b : c : d :: p : q : r : s, then:

**a/p = b/q = c/r = d/s**

### Example: Blending Colours

To make orange paint, mix Yellow : Red : White = **3 : 2 : 5**.

If you have 15 litres of white paint:
- White = 5 parts = 15 litres, so 1 part = 3 litres
- Yellow = 3 parts = **9 litres**
- Red = 2 parts = **6 litres**
- Total orange paint = 9 + 6 + 15 = **30 litres**

### Example: Concrete Mixture

For laying a strong foundation, cement : sand : aggregate = **1 : 2 : 4**.

With 5 bags of cement, multiply each term by 5:
- Cement : Sand : Aggregate = 5 : 10 : 20
- Total material = 5 + 10 + 20 = **35 bags**

## 3.4 Dividing a Whole in a Given Ratio

To split a quantity **x** in the ratio **a : b : c**:

1. Add all terms: a + b + c
2. Each unit part = x / (a + b + c)
3. The shares are: x x a/(a+b+c), x x b/(a+b+c), x x c/(a+b+c)

### Example: Concrete Mixture

Mix cement, sand, and aggregate in 1 : 2 : 4. Need 140 units of concrete.

- Total parts = 1 + 2 + 4 = 7
- Multiplier = 140 / 7 = 20
- Cement = 1 x 20 = **20 units**
- Sand = 2 x 20 = **40 units**
- Aggregate = 4 x 20 = **80 units**

### Example: Blending Colours

Yellow : Red : White = 3 : 2 : 5. Need 60 mL of orange paint.

- Total parts = 3 + 2 + 5 = 10
- Yellow = 60 x 3/10 = **18 mL**
- Red = 60 x 2/10 = **12 mL**
- White = 60 x 5/10 = **30 mL**

### Example: Angles of a Triangle

Construct a triangle with angles in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4.

Sum of angles = 180 degrees. Total parts = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9.
- Angle A = 180 x 2/9 = **40 degrees**
- Angle B = 180 x 3/9 = **60 degrees**
- Angle C = 180 x 4/9 = **80 degrees**

> **Key idea:** To split any quantity into a given ratio, first find the total number of parts, then distribute proportionally. This technique works regardless of how many terms the ratio has.

## 3.5 Pie Charts

A **pie chart** displays different proportions of a whole as slices of a circle. The full circle represents 360 degrees and the entire quantity (100%).

### Building a Pie Chart

**Step 1:** Express each quantity as a fraction of the total.

**Step 2:** Convert each fraction to degrees (multiply by 360).

**Step 3:** Draw the corresponding sectors using a protractor.

![Example pie chart showing grade distribution of students with five colour-coded slices for grades A through E](pie-chart-grade-distribution.png)

**Example:** A school of 50 students has the following grade distribution:
- A: 15 students, B: 10 students, C: 12 students, D: 8 students, E: 5 students

The sector angles:
- A: (15/50) x 360 = **108 degrees**
- B: (10/50) x 360 = **72 degrees**
- C: (12/50) x 360 = **86.4 degrees**
- D: (8/50) x 360 = **57.6 degrees**
- E: (5/50) x 360 = **36 degrees**

Total: 108 + 72 + 86.4 + 57.6 + 36 = 360 degrees (check!)

> **Key idea:** In a pie chart, each slice's angle is proportional to the quantity it represents. The angles must always sum to 360 degrees.

## 3.6 Direct and Inverse Proportion

### Direct Proportion

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when increasing one causes the other to increase by the same factor (and decreasing one decreases the other similarly).

**Examples:**
- More cups of rice require more cups of dal (at the same ratio)
- More kilometres driven require more litres of petrol (at the same rate)
- More tickets bought mean higher total cost (at the same price per ticket)

If x and y are directly proportional: **x/y = constant (k)**

### Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when increasing one causes the other to decrease by the same factor.

**Examples:**
- More painters finishing a house means fewer days needed
- Faster speed means shorter travel time for the same distance
- More taps filling a tank means less time to fill it

If x and y are inversely proportional: **x x y = constant (k)**

### Example: Labourers and Time

If 8 labourers can harvest a field in 12 days, how many days will 24 labourers need?

Labourers and days are inversely proportional:
- 8 x 12 = 24 x d
- d = 96/24 = **4 days**

### Example: Speed and Time

A bus takes 5 hours at 48 km/h to reach a town. How long at 60 km/h?

Speed and time are inversely proportional:
- 48 x 5 = 60 x t
- t = 240/60 = **4 hours**

> **Key idea:** In **direct proportion**, both quantities rise or fall together (their ratio stays constant). In **inverse proportion**, one rises as the other falls (their product stays constant).

## Summary

- **Proportional ratios** share the same simplest form. Cross multiplication confirms proportionality.
- **Map scales** express real distances as ratios on paper.
- Ratios can have **multiple terms** (a : b : c : d) for comparing several quantities simultaneously.
- To **divide a quantity** in a ratio, compute the total parts and distribute proportionally.
- **Pie charts** represent proportions as circular sectors (total = 360 degrees).
- **Direct proportion:** x/y = constant (both quantities move in the same direction).
- **Inverse proportion:** x x y = constant (one quantity rises as the other falls).
