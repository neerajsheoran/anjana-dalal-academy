## 1.1 Fractions as Percentages

Walk into any Diwali sale and you will see banners screaming "Flat 40% Off!" or hear someone say "She got 91% in her boards." But what does the **%** symbol really stand for?

The word **per cent** traces back to the Latin **per centum**, meaning "for every hundred."

So **30%** means 30 out of every 100 -- like 30 rupees out of 100 rupees, or 30 correct answers out of 100 questions.

**Percentages are simply fractions whose denominator is 100.**

- 10% = 10/100 = 1/10
- 25% = 25/100 = 1/4
- 50% = 50/100 = 1/2
- 80% = 80/100 = 4/5

---

### Expressing Fractions as Percentages

**Example 1:** Express 4/5 as a percentage.

**Method 1:** Convert to an equivalent fraction with denominator 100.
- 4/5 = (4 x 20)/(5 x 20) = 80/100 = **80%**

**Method 2:** Multiply the fraction by 100.
- 4/5 x 100 = 80. So 4/5 = **80%**

**Example 2:** Express 3/8 as a percentage.
- 3/8 x 100 = 37.5. So 3/8 = **37.5%**

> **Key idea:** To turn a fraction into a percentage, multiply it by 100. To turn a percentage back into a fraction, write it over 100 and simplify.

**Example 3:** Express 36% as a fraction.
- 36% = 36/100 = 18/50 = **9/25**

---

### Why Percentages Matter

Suppose a bakery makes two types of cake. Butter makes up 7/26 of Cake A and 11/38 of Cake B. Which cake is richer in butter?

Comparing fractions directly is awkward. But as percentages:
- Cake A: 7/26 x 100 = **26.92%**
- Cake B: 11/38 x 100 = **28.95%**

Cake B is slightly richer in butter!

**Percentages are powerful because:**
- They create a **uniform base** (100) for easy comparison
- They connect naturally to our **decimal system**: 42% = 0.42
- 100 strikes the right balance -- detailed enough for precision, simple enough for intuition

### Historical Note

The concept of "per hundred" appears as early as the 4th century BCE in **Kautilya's Arthashastra**, where interest rates on loans were stated per hundred. Roman tax collectors also levied centesima -- a 1/100 tax on auction sales. The modern **%** symbol evolved from abbreviations used in Italian merchant manuscripts during the 15th century.

### Percentages in Everyday Life

- The Earth's atmosphere is roughly **78% nitrogen** and **21% oxygen**
- Cow's milk is about **87% water** by weight
- Nearly **71%** of Earth's surface is covered by water
- India's voter turnout in the 2024 general elections was about 65%

## 1.2 Percentage of Some Quantity

**Example:** Kavita buys a 150 g packet of namkeen that is 18% fat. How much fat is she consuming?

18% fat means 18 g fat per 100 g of namkeen.

**Using proportion:** 18 : 100 :: x : 150
- x = (18 x 150) / 100 = **27 g**

**Using the unitary method:**
- 100 g contains 18 g fat
- 1 g contains 18/100 g fat
- 150 g contains (18/100) x 150 = **27 g fat**

**Quick formula:** To find z% of a quantity Q:

**z% of Q = (z/100) x Q**

**Example:** 45% of 80 kg = (45/100) x 80 = **36 kg**

> **Key idea:** Watch out when comparing percentages! 20% of 200 g (= 40 g) can be more than 30% of 120 g (= 36 g). Percentages are relative -- the **whole** matters just as much as the rate.

## 1.3 Profit and Loss

When a shopkeeper buys goods at one price and sells at another:

- **Cost Price (CP):** The amount paid to buy the item
- **Selling Price (SP):** The amount received when selling it
- **Profit:** SP - CP (when SP > CP)
- **Loss:** CP - SP (when CP > SP)

### Profit and Loss Percentages

These are always calculated as a percentage of the **cost price**:

- **Profit %** = (Profit / CP) x 100
- **Loss %** = (Loss / CP) x 100

**Example:** Sunita bought a table for Rs 4500 and sold it for Rs 5175.
- Profit = 5175 - 4500 = Rs 675
- Profit % = (675/4500) x 100 = **15%**

**Example:** A trader bought a watch for Rs 1200 and sold it for Rs 1020.
- Loss = 1200 - 1020 = Rs 180
- Loss % = (180/1200) x 100 = **15%**

### Finding SP from CP and Profit/Loss %

- **SP = CP x (1 + Profit%/100)** when there is profit
- **SP = CP x (1 - Loss%/100)** when there is loss

**Example:** CP = Rs 1500, Profit = 25%.
- SP = 1500 x (1 + 25/100) = 1500 x 1.25 = **Rs 1875**

## 1.4 Simple Interest

When you deposit money in a bank or borrow from a lender, **interest** is the fee charged for using that money.

- **Principal (P):** The original amount deposited or borrowed
- **Rate of interest (R):** The annual percentage charged
- **Time (T):** The number of years
- **Interest (I):** The extra amount earned or paid

### Simple Interest Formula

In **simple interest**, the calculation uses only the original principal each year:

**Simple Interest (SI) = (P x R x T) / 100**

**Total Amount = Principal + Interest = P + (P x R x T) / 100**

**Example:** Deepak deposits Rs 8000 in a bank at 6% per annum for 4 years.
- SI = (8000 x 6 x 4) / 100 = **Rs 1920**
- Total amount after 4 years = 8000 + 1920 = **Rs 9920**

**Example:** Fatima borrows Rs 15,000 at 9% per annum for 3 years.
- SI = (15000 x 9 x 3) / 100 = **Rs 4050**
- She must repay 15000 + 4050 = **Rs 19,050**

> **Key idea:** With simple interest, the interest earned each year stays the same (it does not grow on itself). The formula SI = PRT/100 is proportional reasoning applied directly.

## 1.5 Discount

A **discount** is a reduction offered on the displayed price of an item.

- **Marked Price (MP):** The price printed on the tag (also called MRP)
- **Discount:** The amount reduced from the marked price
- **Selling Price:** MP - Discount

**Discount % = (Discount / MP) x 100**

**Example:** A kurta has a marked price of Rs 1200. The store offers a 20% discount.
- Discount = 20% of 1200 = (20/100) x 1200 = Rs 240
- Selling Price = 1200 - 240 = **Rs 960**

---

## 1.6 Tax (GST)

**GST (Goods and Services Tax)** is a tax levied on the selling price of goods and services in India.

- **Bill amount = Selling Price + GST**
- **GST amount = (GST% / 100) x Selling Price**

**Example:** A laptop costs Rs 45,000 and attracts 18% GST.
- GST = (18/100) x 45000 = Rs 8100
- Total bill = 45000 + 8100 = **Rs 53,100**

---

## Summary

- **Percentage** means "per hundred." z% = z/100.
- To convert a fraction to a percentage, multiply by 100.
- To find z% of a quantity Q: (z/100) x Q.
- **Profit %** = (Profit/CP) x 100; **Loss %** = (Loss/CP) x 100.
- **Simple Interest** = (P x R x T) / 100.
- **Discount** = (Discount%/100) x Marked Price.
- **GST** is added to the selling price to arrive at the final bill.
- Percentages are fractions wearing a convenient disguise -- they simplify comparison by anchoring everything to a common denominator of 100.
