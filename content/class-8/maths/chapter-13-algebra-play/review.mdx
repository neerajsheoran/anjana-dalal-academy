## Key Concepts

- "Think of a number" tricks work because the **variable cancels out**, leaving a fixed result
- To design a trick ending in answer k: ensure the algebra simplifies to k regardless of the starting number
- In **number pyramids**, each cell = sum of the two cells directly below it
- A 3-cell base pyramid summit = **p + 2q + r** (middle cell counted twice)
- A 4-cell base pyramid summit = **p + 3q + 3r + s** (coefficients are Pascal's Triangle: 1, 3, 3, 1)
- In a **calendar 2x2 block** starting at d: sum = **4d + 16**, so d = (sum - 16)/4
- **Algebra grids** are systems of equations in disguise -- solve by substitution
- To maximise a product from digits in __ __ x __: place the **largest digit** as the single-digit multiplier
- Reversing a 2-digit number (10a + b) and subtracting: difference = **9(a - b)** -- always divisible by 9
- Reversing a 3-digit number (100a + 10b + c) and subtracting: difference = **99(a - c)** -- always divisible by 99
- Sum of a 2-digit number and its reverse = **11(a + b)** -- always divisible by 11
- Expressing numbers using **place value** (10a + b) reveals hidden divisibility patterns
- Linear equations can model age puzzles, sum-difference problems, and more

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Number pyramid | A triangle of numbers where each cell equals the sum of the two below |
| Pascal's Triangle | Array where each number is the sum of the two above; gives pyramid coefficients |
| Place value form | Writing a number as 10a + b (2-digit) or 100a + 10b + c (3-digit) |
| Substitution | Replacing a known variable's value into another equation to solve for unknowns |

## Remember These

- In number pyramids, the **middle base cell** contributes most to the summit (coefficient 2 in a 3-row pyramid)
- When reversing and subtracting a 3-digit number, the **middle digit cancels out** completely
- The birthday trick works by encoding month in the hundreds place and day in the units place using algebra: 100M + D
- For largest product from 3 digits: biggest digit goes alone, remaining two form the two-digit number in descending order
