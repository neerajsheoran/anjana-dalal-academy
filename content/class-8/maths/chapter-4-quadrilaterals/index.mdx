## What is a Quadrilateral?

A **quadrilateral** is a closed figure bounded by exactly four straight sides. The name derives from Latin: **quadri** (four) + **latus** (side).

Every quadrilateral has four **angles** formed where its sides meet. In this chapter, we investigate different families of quadrilaterals — rectangles, squares, parallelograms, and rhombuses — and uncover the properties that make each special.

---

## 4.1 Rectangles and Squares

### Defining a Rectangle

A **rectangle** is a quadrilateral where:
- Every angle measures exactly **90 degrees**
- Opposite sides have **equal length**

### The Bamboo Strip Experiment

Imagine a craftsman making a photo frame. He has two thin bamboo strips. He wants to join them so that threading a string through the four endpoints creates a rectangle.

If one strip is 10 cm long, what should the other strip measure? Where should they be connected?

The strips become the **diagonals** of the rectangle. Through careful geometric reasoning, we discover:

![Rectangle ABCD with diagonals intersecting at point O, showing equal opposite sides and right angles at all corners](rectangle-properties-diagonals.png)

### Properties of a Rectangle

**Property 1:** Every angle of a rectangle measures **90 degrees**.

**Property 2:** Opposite sides of a rectangle have **equal** length.

**Property 3:** Opposite sides of a rectangle are **parallel**.

**Property 4:** The diagonals of a rectangle are **equal in length** and **bisect each other** (they cross at their midpoints).

> **Key idea:** Two bamboo strips will form a rectangle if and only if they have **equal length** and are **joined at their midpoints**. The angle between them does not matter — any angle will do!

### Why the Diagonals Must Be Equal (Deduction 1)

In rectangle PQRS:
- PQ = SR (opposite sides)
- Angle QPR = Angle SRP = 90 degrees
- PR is shared by both triangles

By the **SAS congruence condition**, triangle PQR is congruent to triangle SRP. Therefore, diagonal QR = diagonal PS.

### Why the Diagonals Bisect Each Other (Deduction 2)

Using the **AAS congruence condition**, we can prove that the two triangles formed at the intersection point are congruent. This gives equal segments on each diagonal, confirming that the intersection point is the midpoint of both.

### Why All Angles Must Be 90 Degrees

When two equal diagonals bisect each other at any angle x, the four angles of the resulting quadrilateral always compute to:

90 - x/2 + x/2 = **90 degrees** each

Regardless of the diagonal angle, the quadrilateral is always a rectangle!

## Squares: Rectangles with Equal Sides

A **square** is a rectangle where all four sides are also equal.

**Square:** A quadrilateral with all angles equal to 90 degrees and all sides equal.

Since every square satisfies the definition of a rectangle, **every square is a rectangle** — but not every rectangle is a square.

### Properties of a Square

A square inherits every property of a rectangle, plus gains additional ones:

**Property 1:** All four sides are **equal**.

**Property 2:** Opposite sides are **parallel**.

**Property 3:** All angles measure **90 degrees**.

**Property 4:** Diagonals are **equal in length** and **bisect each other at 90 degrees**.

**Property 5:** The diagonals **bisect the vertex angles** (splitting each 90-degree angle into two 45-degree angles).

> **Key idea:** For the craftsman to make a square instead of a rectangle, the two strips must be equal in length, joined at their midpoints, and placed **perpendicular** to each other (at 90 degrees).

## 4.2 Angles in a Quadrilateral

### The Angle Sum Property

Draw any quadrilateral and connect one pair of opposite corners with a diagonal. The diagonal splits the quadrilateral into **two triangles**. Since each triangle has angles summing to 180 degrees:

**The sum of all angles in any quadrilateral = 180 + 180 = 360 degrees.**

> **Key idea:** The four angles of any quadrilateral always add up to **360 degrees**. This explains why if three angles of a quadrilateral are each 90 degrees, the fourth must also be 90 degrees (since 90 + 90 + 90 + fourth = 360, giving fourth = 90).

## 4.3 Parallelograms

### What is a Parallelogram?

A **parallelogram** is a quadrilateral where both pairs of opposite sides are **parallel**.

Every rectangle qualifies as a parallelogram (it has parallel opposite sides), but not every parallelogram is a rectangle — a parallelogram may have angles other than 90 degrees.

### Constructing a Parallelogram

**Example:** Draw a parallelogram with sides 6 cm and 3 cm, and an angle of 40 degrees between them.

1. Draw PQ = 6 cm and PS = 3 cm with a 40-degree angle at P.
2. Through S, draw a line parallel to PQ.
3. Through Q, draw a line parallel to PS.
4. Label the intersection as R. PQRS is the parallelogram.

![Parallelogram with marked equal opposite sides, equal opposite angles, and diagonals bisecting each other](parallelogram-properties.png)

### Properties of a Parallelogram

**Property 1:** Opposite sides are **equal** (PQ = SR, PS = QR).

**Property 2:** Opposite sides are **parallel**.

**Property 3:** Adjacent angles are **supplementary** (add to 180 degrees), and opposite angles are **equal**.

**Property 4:** The diagonals **bisect each other** (but they are NOT necessarily equal in length).

### Determining All Angles

If one angle of a parallelogram measures x degrees:
- The **adjacent** angle = 180 - x degrees
- The **opposite** angle = x degrees

**Example:** If one angle is 40 degrees, the four angles are: 40, 140, 40, 140.

> **Key idea:** In a parallelogram, opposite angles are always equal, and adjacent angles always sum to 180 degrees. This follows from the co-interior angles property of parallel lines.

### Proving Opposite Sides Are Equal (Deduction 7)

In parallelogram PQRS, draw diagonal PR:
- Alternate angles are equal (since PQ is parallel to SR)
- Alternate angles are also equal (since PS is parallel to QR)
- PR is shared

By **AAS congruence**, triangle PQR is congruent to triangle RPS. Therefore PQ = SR and QR = PS.

### Proving Diagonals Bisect Each Other (Deduction 8)

Using alternate angles and equal opposite sides, we prove that the two triangles formed at the intersection point are congruent. This yields equal half-diagonals, confirming the diagonals bisect each other.

## 4.4 Rhombus: The Equal-Sided Quadrilateral

### What is a Rhombus?

A **rhombus** is a quadrilateral where **all four sides have equal length**.

A square is a special rhombus (with all right angles), but a rhombus can have any angle measure.

### Building a Rhombus

1. Draw two sides PS and PQ of equal length, with any chosen angle (say 60 degrees) between them.
2. Using a compass, locate point R at the same distance from both Q and S.
3. Connect QR and SR to complete the rhombus.

### Finding the Angles

If one angle of a rhombus is 60 degrees:
- Since a rhombus is a parallelogram (opposite sides are parallel), adjacent angles sum to 180 degrees.
- The four angles are: **60, 120, 60, 120 degrees**.

### Every Rhombus is a Parallelogram

Because all sides are equal, the diagonal creates two congruent triangles (by SSS). The equal alternate angles prove opposite sides are parallel, confirming every rhombus is a parallelogram.

### Properties of a Rhombus

**Property 1:** All sides are **equal**.

**Property 2:** Opposite sides are **parallel**.

**Property 3:** Adjacent angles sum to **180 degrees**; opposite angles are **equal**.

**Property 4:** Diagonals **bisect each other**.

**Property 5:** Diagonals **bisect the vertex angles**.

**Property 6:** Diagonals meet at **90 degrees** (right angles).

> **Key idea:** The diagonals of a rhombus have special properties — they bisect each other **at right angles** and they bisect the vertex angles. These properties make the rhombus extremely useful in geometric constructions and design.

## The Quadrilateral Family Tree

Different quadrilateral types are related like members of a family:

- Every **square** is a **rectangle** (all angles 90 degrees)
- Every **square** is a **rhombus** (all sides equal)
- Every **rectangle** is a **parallelogram** (opposite sides parallel)
- Every **rhombus** is a **parallelogram** (opposite sides parallel)

This can be visualised as a Venn diagram:

![Venn diagram showing the relationship between quadrilaterals: parallelograms containing rectangles and rhombuses, with squares at their intersection](quadrilateral-venn-diagram.png)

- **Parallelogram** forms the outermost set
- Within it: **Rectangle** and **Rhombus** overlap
- Their overlap region is the **Square**

## 4.5 Exploring Quadrilaterals Further

### Making Quadrilaterals from Triangles

**Activity 1:** Take two equilateral triangles with side 6 cm each. Join them along one edge. The result is a **rhombus** (all sides 6 cm).

**Activity 2:** Take two isosceles triangles with sides 6, 6, and 4 cm. Different joining methods produce different quadrilaterals:
- Joining along the **equal sides** (6 cm) gives a parallelogram
- Joining along the **unequal side** (4 cm) gives a different quadrilateral

### Trapezium

A **trapezium** is a quadrilateral with exactly **one pair of parallel sides**. The parallel sides are called **bases**, and the non-parallel sides are called **legs**.

A trapezium where the legs are equal in length is called an **isosceles trapezium**.

### Kite

A **kite** is a quadrilateral with **two pairs of consecutive sides** that are equal, but opposite sides are not equal.

Properties of a kite:
- The diagonals are **perpendicular** (meet at 90 degrees)
- One diagonal bisects the other
- One pair of opposite angles is equal

## Summary of Properties

| Property | Rectangle | Square | Parallelogram | Rhombus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All angles 90 degrees | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Opposite sides equal | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| All sides equal | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| Opposite sides parallel | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Diagonals equal | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Diagonals bisect each other | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Diagonals perpendicular | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| Diagonals bisect angles | No | Yes | No | Yes |

> **Key idea:** The sum of angles in any quadrilateral is **360 degrees**. Different types of quadrilaterals possess different special properties involving their sides, angles, and diagonals. Mastering these properties is essential for construction problems and geometric reasoning.
