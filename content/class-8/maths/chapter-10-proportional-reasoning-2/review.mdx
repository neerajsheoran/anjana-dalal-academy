## Key Concepts

- Two ratios a : b and c : d are proportional when **a x d = b x c** (cross multiplication)
- **Map scales** express real distances as ratios (e.g., 1 : 40,00,000 means 1 cm = 40 km)
- Ratios can have **multiple terms**: a : b : c : d -- proportional if a/p = b/q = c/r = d/s
- To **divide a quantity x** in ratio a : b : c: total parts = a+b+c, each share = x x (part)/(total parts)
- **Pie charts** display proportions as slices of a circle; total = 360 degrees
- Sector angle = (quantity / total) x 360 degrees
- **Direct proportion:** when one quantity increases, the other increases by the same factor; x/y = constant (k)
- **Inverse proportion:** when one quantity increases, the other decreases by the same factor; x x y = constant (k)
- Direct proportion examples: cost and quantity, distance and petrol, cups and ingredients
- Inverse proportion examples: workers and time, speed and travel time, taps and fill time
- Multi-term ratios scale the same way: multiply or divide all terms by the same factor

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Representative Fraction (RF) | The scale of a map expressed as a ratio (e.g., 1 : 40,00,000) |
| Pie chart | A circular chart where each slice's angle is proportional to the quantity it represents |
| Direct proportion | Two quantities where x/y = constant (both increase or decrease together) |
| Inverse proportion | Two quantities where x x y = constant (one increases as the other decreases) |
| Sector angle | The angle of a slice in a pie chart |

## Key Formulas

| Formula | Use |
|---------|-----|
| Sector angle = (value/total) x 360° | Building a pie chart |
| Share = total x (part / sum of all parts) | Dividing a quantity in a given ratio |
| x/y = k | Direct proportion relationship |
| x x y = k | Inverse proportion relationship |

## Remember These

- In a pie chart, all sector angles must add up to exactly **360 degrees**
- Direct proportion: **ratio stays constant** (e.g., double the rice, double the dal)
- Inverse proportion: **product stays constant** (e.g., double the workers, half the time)
- When dividing in a ratio, always find the **total number of parts** first, then calculate each share
- Do not confuse direct and inverse: more speed means LESS time (inverse), not more
