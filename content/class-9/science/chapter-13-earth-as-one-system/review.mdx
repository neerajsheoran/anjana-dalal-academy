## Key Concepts

- Earth is one connected system of four interacting **spheres**
- **Geosphere** = solid Earth (crust, mantle, outer and inner core)
  - Crust: 5–70 km thick, where we live
  - Mantle: hot semi-solid rock beneath crust
  - Outer core: liquid iron/nickel; generates Earth's magnetic field
  - Inner core: solid iron/nickel under extreme pressure
- **Hydrosphere** = all of Earth's water
  - 97% in oceans
  - Most of the remaining 3% in ice caps/glaciers
  - Only a tiny fraction is usable freshwater
- **Atmosphere** = layer of gases
  - 78% N₂, 21% O₂, 0.9% argon, 0.04% CO₂ (rising), trace others
  - Layers: troposphere → stratosphere → mesosphere → thermosphere → exosphere
  - Troposphere (0-12 km) is where all weather happens
  - Stratosphere contains the ozone layer
- **Biosphere** = thin layer where life exists; from soil microbes to deep ocean fish
- The Sun provides nearly all of Earth's energy; Earth's interior contributes some heat
- Matter cycles between spheres in great loops:
  - **Water cycle**: ocean → vapour → clouds → rain → rivers → ocean
  - **Carbon cycle**: atmospheric CO₂ ↔ plants ↔ animals ↔ fossil fuels ↔ atmosphere
  - **Nitrogen cycle**: atmospheric N₂ → soil bacteria → plants → animals → decomposition → N₂
- **Greenhouse effect** is natural and essential — keeps Earth at ~15°C average instead of -18°C
- **Greenhouse gases** (CO₂, CH₄, H₂O, N₂O) absorb infrared (heat) radiation from the Earth and re-emit it, warming the atmosphere
- Human activities have raised atmospheric CO₂ from 280 ppm (1750) to over 420 ppm (today)
- Main human sources of extra CO₂: burning fossil fuels, deforestation, cement production
- Effects of climate change: Himalayan glaciers melting, sea levels rising, monsoon patterns shifting, more extreme heat waves, more frequent severe weather, biodiversity stress
- India is the world's 3rd-largest emitter overall but has low per-person emissions
- India's clean energy push: solar power (International Solar Alliance), wind, electric vehicles, target of 500 GW renewable capacity by 2030

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Earth system | Earth viewed as one interconnected system of spheres |
| Geosphere | The solid Earth — crust, mantle, core |
| Hydrosphere | All of Earth's water in any form |
| Atmosphere | The layer of gases surrounding Earth |
| Biosphere | The thin zone where life exists |
| Water cycle | Continuous circulation of water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff |
| Carbon cycle | Movement of carbon between atmosphere, plants, animals, fossil fuels, and Earth's interior |
| Nitrogen cycle | Movement of nitrogen between atmosphere, bacteria, plants, animals, and back |
| Greenhouse effect | Natural process by which atmospheric gases trap heat and warm the planet |
| Greenhouse gas | A gas (like CO₂, methane) that absorbs infrared radiation and warms the atmosphere |
| Climate change | Long-term changes in global climate, currently driven by human activity |
| Renewable energy | Energy from sources that don't run out (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal) |

## Remember These

- **Four spheres**: geosphere (rock), hydrosphere (water), atmosphere (gases), biosphere (life)
- The **Sun powers almost everything** that happens on Earth's surface
- **Natural greenhouse effect = good** (keeps Earth warm enough for life)
- **Enhanced greenhouse effect = problem** (extra CO₂ from human activity warming Earth faster than ecosystems can adapt)
- CO₂ has risen from **280 ppm to 420 ppm** in just 270 years
- India's **per-capita CO₂ emissions** are much lower than developed countries, even though our total emissions are large
- India founded the **International Solar Alliance** in 2015
- **Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Kochi** — all coastal Indian cities — face rising flood risk from climate change
