## Key Concepts

- **Law of conservation of mass** (Lavoisier): Mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction
- **Law of constant proportions** (Proust): A pure compound always has its elements in the same fixed ratio by mass
- A **molecule** is the smallest unit of a substance that can exist independently and retain its properties
- **Atomicity** = number of atoms in one molecule of an element
  - Monatomic: noble gases (He, Ne, Ar)
  - Diatomic: H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂
  - Triatomic: O₃ (ozone)
- A **compound** is two or more different elements bonded in a fixed ratio
- Properties of a compound are very different from its constituent elements (hydrogen and oxygen → water)
- **Ions** are charged atoms or groups of atoms
  - **Cations** (positive) form by LOSING electrons (e.g., Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Al³⁺)
  - **Anions** (negative) form by GAINING electrons (e.g., Cl⁻, O²⁻)
- Ionic compounds are electrically neutral overall (charges balance)
- Writing formulas: criss-cross — use the magnitude of each ion's charge as the subscript of the other
- Atomic mass: H=1, C=12, N=14, O=16, Na=23, Mg=24, S=32, Cl=35.5, Ca=40, Fe=56
- **Atomic mass unit (u)** = 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom
- **Molecular mass** = sum of atomic masses of all atoms in the molecule
- **1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles** (Avogadro's number)
- 1 mole of atoms / molecules / ions = 6.022 × 10²³ of them
- **The mass of 1 mole of a substance in grams = its atomic/molecular mass in u**
- So 18 g of water = 1 mole of water = 6.022 × 10²³ water molecules

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Molecule | Smallest particle of a substance that exists independently and retains the substance's properties |
| Atomicity | Number of atoms in one molecule of an element |
| Compound | Substance made of two or more different elements bonded in fixed ratio |
| Ion | A charged atom or group of atoms |
| Cation | Positively charged ion (lost electrons) |
| Anion | Negatively charged ion (gained electrons) |
| Atomic mass unit (u) | 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Atomic mass | Mass of an atom in atomic mass units |
| Molecular mass | Sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule |
| Mole | The chemist's counting unit; 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles |
| Avogadro's number | 6.022 × 10²³ (the number of particles in one mole) |
| Molar mass | Mass of 1 mole of a substance in grams (numerically equal to atomic/molecular mass in u) |

## Remember These

- **The two laws of chemical combination** (conservation of mass; constant proportions) gave the first scientific evidence for atoms
- **Diatomic molecules** to memorise: H₂, O₂, N₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂
- **Common molecular masses**: H₂O = 18, CO₂ = 44, CH₄ = 16, NH₃ = 17, H₂SO₄ = 98, glucose = 180
- **A mole is just a count** — like a dozen (12) or a gross (144), only much bigger (6 × 10²³)
- The brilliance of the mole: **molar mass in grams = molecular mass in u**, letting chemists count atoms by weighing
- Indian fertiliser industry (urea, DAP) and pharmaceutical industry (paracetamol, aspirin) all rely on these calculations daily
