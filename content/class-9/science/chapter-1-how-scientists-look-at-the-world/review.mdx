## Key Concepts

- Science is not just a body of facts -- it is a **way of thinking** that combines careful observation, reasoning, and openness to being wrong
- A **model** is a simplified picture of a real system that keeps only what matters for the question being asked
- Building a model requires deciding what to **include** and what to deliberately **ignore**
- Scientists across the world use **agreed words, symbols, and units** so that knowledge can be shared without confusion
- **SI units** are the international standard (kg, m, s, K) and prevent costly errors like the Air Canada fuel mishap
- Common symbols: **m** (mass), **v** (velocity), **F** (force), **I** (current), **c** (speed of light)
- A **law** describes a regular pattern in nature -- it tells us *what* happens
- A **theory** explains *why* a law works, based on careful evidence -- not a guess
- A **principle** is a broad idea that applies across many situations (e.g., conservation of energy)
- **Prediction** is one of the most powerful tools of science -- when predictions fail, scientists update their models
- Scientific thinking helps you separate real information from "viral" claims by asking: what evidence? what mechanism? has it been tested?
- **Estimation** -- getting a "good enough" answer with simple reasoning -- is a daily scientific skill
- Every theory has **limits** -- the willingness to be revised is a strength, not a weakness, of science
- Real problems usually need ideas from **multiple branches** of science (physics, chemistry, biology, earth science) together
- Science is a **human activity** shaped by curiosity, creativity, collaboration, and honesty
- Indian scientists -- Aryabhata, Sushruta, C.V. Raman, Meghnad Saha, Vikram Sarabhai, Tessy Thomas -- have always been part of the scientific story

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Model | A simplified representation of a system that focuses only on the details relevant to the question |
| SI units | The internationally agreed system of measurement units (Système International) |
| Law (scientific) | A statement that describes a regular pattern in nature, often as a mathematical relationship |
| Theory | A well-supported explanation of *why* a pattern occurs, based on evidence and testing |
| Principle | A broad guiding idea that applies across many specific situations |
| Prediction | A reasoned expectation of what will happen, based on a model, law, or theory |
| Estimation | The skill of getting a reasonable, approximate answer with simple reasoning |
| Hypothesis | A testable idea about how something works, proposed before evidence is gathered |

## Remember These

- A **theory** in science is **not a guess** -- it is a thoroughly tested explanation, often surviving decades of scrutiny
- Models simplify on purpose -- the choice of what to ignore is as important as what to include
- Standard units allow a vegetable seller in Coimbatore and a scientist in Geneva to mean the same thing by "one kilogram"
- A good prediction is **reasoned**, not guessed -- it follows from a model or law and identifies what could be measured to check it
- **Estimation** answers questions like "is this number even possible?" before going for precision
- The same chapter you are reading now uses physics (the IMD example), chemistry (the unit mishap), and biology (the COVID mask science) together
- The greatest strength of science is its **openness to being corrected by nature**, not its certainty
