## Understanding Force

A **force** is simply a push or a pull. You cannot see a force itself, but its **effects** are unmistakable.

**What a force can do:**
- Set a stationary cricket ball **rolling**
- Bring a speeding bicycle to a **halt**
- Make a car go **faster** or **slower**
- Alter the **direction** of a hockey ball mid-play
- **Deform** a lump of clay when squeezed

---

## Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

### Balanced Forces

When two forces of **equal strength** push on an object from **opposite sides**, they cancel out. The net force is zero. The object stays exactly as it was -- at rest or moving steadily.

### Unbalanced Forces

When forces on an object are **unequal**, there is a leftover (net) force. The object **accelerates** -- it speeds up, slows down, or changes direction -- whichever way the stronger force points.

![Balanced forces on a box (equal push from both sides, no movement) vs unbalanced forces (net force causes acceleration)](balanced-vs-unbalanced-forces.png)

> **Key idea:** Only an unbalanced force can change how an object moves. Balanced forces leave its motion untouched.

**Illustration:** Two children push a heavy almirah across a room. Friction pushes back. If their combined push equals friction, the almirah stays put (balanced). If they push harder than friction, it slides (unbalanced).

## Newton's First Law of Motion -- The Law of Inertia

**Statement:** Every object continues in its state of rest, or of uniform motion along a straight line, unless an unbalanced external force compels it to change that state.

### What Is Inertia?

**Inertia** is an object's natural reluctance to have its motion disturbed. Every object has it.

- A parked scooter **resists being pushed** into motion
- A rolling marble **resists being stopped**

**Mass is the measure of inertia.** The heavier something is, the harder it is to start moving or to stop.

### Inertia in Daily Life

- A bus **lurches forward** from a stop -- passengers stumble backward because their bodies, at rest, resist the sudden motion
- A bus **brakes sharply** -- passengers pitch forward because their moving bodies resist the sudden stop
- Shake a mango tree vigorously -- ripe **mangoes drop** because the branch moves but the fruit resists the change
- Place a ₹10 coin on a piece of cardboard resting over a glass; flick the cardboard away -- the **coin drops straight into the glass** because it resists horizontal motion
- Jump off a slowly moving tonga -- you tend to **stumble forward** because your feet stop but your upper body keeps going

> **Key idea:** A loaded goods truck has far more inertia than an empty one. That is why it takes a much bigger force to get it moving -- or to bring it to a stop.

## Newton's Second Law of Motion

**Statement:** The net force acting on an object equals the product of its mass and its acceleration.

**F = m × a**

Equivalently: the rate at which an object's momentum changes is directly proportional to the applied unbalanced force, and the change happens in the direction of that force.

### Momentum

**Momentum** is how much "motion content" an object carries -- the product of its mass and velocity.

**p = m × v**

- **SI unit:** kg m/s (kg m s⁻¹)
- Momentum is a **vector** (it has direction)
- A heavy object moving slowly can pack more momentum than a light one moving fast

**Example:** A loaded truck crawling at 20 km/h on a highway may carry far more momentum than a hatchback zipping past at 80 km/h, simply because the truck's mass is enormous.

### Deriving F = ma

Rate of change of momentum = Force

**F = (mv − mu) / t = m(v − u) / t = ma**

- **SI unit of force:** Newton (N)
- **1 Newton** = the force needed to accelerate a 1 kg object by 1 m/s²
- 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻²

> **Key idea:** F = ma reveals two things at once. For a given force, a heavier object accelerates less. For a given mass, a larger force produces a larger acceleration.

## Newton's Third Law of Motion

**Statement:** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Whenever object A pushes or pulls object B (**action**), object B pushes or pulls object A back with the **same force** in the **opposite direction** (**reaction**).

**Crucial point:** Action and reaction act on **two different objects**. Because they target different bodies, they never cancel each other.

### Everyday Examples

- **Walking:** Your foot pushes the ground backward (action); the ground pushes your foot forward (reaction)
- **Firing a rifle:** The bullet flies forward (action); the rifle kicks backward -- the recoil (reaction)
- **Swimming:** Hands push water backward (action); water pushes the swimmer forward (reaction)
- **Rocket launch:** Exhaust gases blast downward (action); the rocket is thrust upward (reaction)
- **Rowing a boat:** The oar pushes water backward (action); the water pushes the boat forward (reaction)

![Newton's third law illustrated with a rocket: action force pushes gases downward, reaction force pushes rocket upward](newtons-third-law-rocket.png)

> **Key idea:** Forces always appear in pairs -- one cannot exist without the other. The pair are equal in size, opposite in direction, and always act on separate objects.

## Conservation of Momentum

**Law of Conservation of Momentum:** When no external unbalanced force acts on a system of objects, the total momentum of the system before an event equals the total momentum after the event.

![Two objects before and after collision showing conservation of total momentum](conservation-of-momentum-collision.png)

**Total momentum before = Total momentum after**

**m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂**

Where:
- m₁, m₂ = masses of the two objects
- u₁, u₂ = velocities before the event
- v₁, v₂ = velocities after the event

### How a Gun Recoils

Before firing, both gun and bullet sit still -- total momentum = 0.

After firing, the bullet (mass m) shoots forward at velocity v, and the gun (mass M) lurches backward at velocity V.

Conservation gives: 0 = mv + MV

So: **V = −mv / M**

The minus sign confirms the gun moves opposite to the bullet. Since the gun is much heavier (M >> m), its recoil velocity V is much smaller than the bullet's velocity v.

> **Key idea:** The heavier the gun, the gentler the recoil. Military weapons are designed heavy and fitted with shock absorbers for exactly this reason.

## Summary of Newton's Three Laws

| Law | What It Says | Core Concept |
|---|---|---|
| **First Law** | An object at rest or in uniform straight-line motion stays that way unless forced to change | Inertia |
| **Second Law** | F = ma -- force equals mass times acceleration | Relationship between force, mass, and acceleration |
| **Third Law** | Every action has an equal and opposite reaction | Action-reaction pairs on different bodies |

---

## Key Formulae

- **Momentum:** p = mv
- **Force:** F = ma
- **Acceleration:** a = (v − u) / t
- **Second Law (momentum form):** F = m(v − u) / t
- **Conservation of momentum:** m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
- **1 Newton** = 1 kg m/s²
- **1 kgf** = 9.8 N

---

## Key Terms to Remember

- **Force** -- A push or pull that can alter an object's state of motion or shape
- **Inertia** -- The natural tendency to resist any change in motion
- **Momentum** -- Mass multiplied by velocity (p = mv)
- **Newton** -- The SI unit of force
- **Balanced forces** -- Equal and opposite forces whose net effect is zero
- **Unbalanced forces** -- Forces that do not cancel, causing acceleration
- **Action and reaction** -- Equal, opposite forces acting on different objects
- **Conservation of momentum** -- Total momentum stays constant when no external force acts
