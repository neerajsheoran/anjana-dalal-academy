## Key Concepts

- **Biodiversity** = total variety of living organisms on Earth
- Scientists have catalogued about 1.7 million species; total estimates range from 5 million to 100 million
- India holds about 8% of all known species in only 2.4% of the world's land area
- **Classification** = grouping organisms by similarities; biology's filing system
- Hierarchy of classification (broad → specific): **Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**
- **Binomial nomenclature** = the two-word scientific name; genus + species (e.g., *Homo sapiens*)
- Developed by **Carolus Linnaeus**, Swedish botanist, in the 1700s
- All living things are grouped into **five kingdoms**:
  - **Monera** — bacteria; prokaryotic; unicellular
  - **Protista** — single-celled eukaryotes (amoeba, paramecium, malaria parasite)
  - **Fungi** — yeast, mushrooms, moulds; cell walls of chitin; heterotrophic decomposers
  - **Plantae** — multicellular plants; cell walls of cellulose; autotrophic via photosynthesis
  - **Animalia** — multicellular animals; no cell walls; heterotrophic; show locomotion
- **Plant divisions** (from simple to complex): algae → bryophytes → pteridophytes → gymnosperms → angiosperms
- Angiosperms (flowering plants) include **monocots** (1 cotyledon, parallel veins) and **dicots** (2 cotyledons, net veins)
- **Animal phyla** include: porifera (sponges), cnidaria/coelenterata (jellyfish, hydra), platyhelminthes (flatworms), nematoda (roundworms), annelida (segmented worms), **arthropoda (LARGEST phylum — insects, spiders, crustaceans)**, mollusca (snails, octopus), echinodermata (starfish), chordata (animals with backbone)
- **Vertebrate classes**: Pisces (fish), Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves (birds), Mammalia
- India's biodiversity hotspots: **Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma region, Sundarbans**
- Threats to biodiversity: habitat loss, pollution, climate change, poaching, invasive species
- Protection: national parks (Kaziranga, Bandipur, Corbett), wildlife sanctuaries, Project Tiger (1973), Project Elephant (1992)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Biodiversity | Total variety of living organisms on Earth |
| Classification | Grouping organisms based on similarities and differences |
| Taxonomy | The scientific study of classification and naming |
| Binomial nomenclature | The two-word scientific naming system (genus + species) |
| Kingdom | The broadest level of classification |
| Species | The most specific level — a group of organisms that can reproduce together |
| Monera | Kingdom of prokaryotic unicellular organisms (bacteria) |
| Protista | Kingdom of single-celled eukaryotes |
| Fungi | Kingdom of organisms with chitin cell walls; decomposers |
| Plantae | Kingdom of multicellular autotrophic organisms |
| Animalia | Kingdom of multicellular heterotrophic organisms with locomotion |
| Vertebrate | Animal with a backbone |
| Invertebrate | Animal without a backbone |
| Endangered species | Species at high risk of extinction |
| Hotspot | Region with very high biodiversity and high threat to it |

## Remember These

- The hierarchy of classification: **King Philip Came Over For Good Soup** (Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species)
- All scientific names follow **binomial nomenclature** (two words, both italicised, species lowercase)
- **Arthropoda is the largest phylum** — about 80% of all known animal species
- **Five kingdoms** of life: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia
- **Plant cell walls = cellulose; fungi cell walls = chitin; animal cells = no wall**
- India has only **2.4% of land area but 8% of all known species**
- Project Tiger (1973) has helped raise tiger numbers from ~1,800 to over 3,000
- The four major hotspots in/near India: **Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Sundarbans**
