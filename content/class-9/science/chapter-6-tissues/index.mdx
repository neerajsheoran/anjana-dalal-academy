## What Exactly Is a Tissue?

A single cell can do a lot on its own -- but in a multicellular organism, millions of cells must cooperate. When cells that share a similar structure group together to carry out one particular job, we call that group a **tissue**.

> **Key idea:** A tissue is a collection of cells with a common design, working as a team to perform a specific function.

**Familiar examples of tissues:** blood, bone, muscle, the veins of a leaf

In a **unicellular organism** like Amoeba, one cell handles everything -- eating, moving, reproducing. In a **multicellular organism**, there is **division of labour** -- separate teams of cells handle separate tasks, just as a cricket team has different players for batting, bowling, and keeping wicket.

---

## Why Plant and Animal Tissues Differ

Plants and animals lead very different lives, and their tissues reflect this:

- **Plants stay rooted in one place** -- they need plenty of **supportive tissue** (much of it made from dead cells that cost no energy to maintain)
- **Animals are always on the move** -- they rely on **living, energy-consuming tissues** like muscles and nerves
- **Plant growth** is restricted to specific zones (tips, sides, nodes) -- **animal growth** occurs more evenly across the body
- Animal organs tend to be more complex and specialised than plant organs

## Plant Tissues

Plant tissues fall into two broad groups:
1. **Meristematic tissue** (cells that keep dividing)
2. **Permanent tissue** (cells that have stopped dividing)

### Meristematic Tissue

**Meristematic tissue** is made up of cells that are constantly dividing. A plant grows taller, thicker, or longer only where meristematic cells are found.

**What meristematic cells look like:**
- Packed with dense **cytoplasm**
- Covered by thin **cellulose walls**
- Contain large, prominent **nuclei**
- Have **no vacuoles** -- they are too busy dividing to store anything

**Where they sit in the plant:**

![Plant diagram showing locations of apical meristem at tips, lateral meristem along sides, and intercalary meristem at nodes](types-of-meristematic-tissue-location.png)

- **Apical meristem** -- at the **tips** of roots and shoots; responsible for making the plant **longer**
- **Lateral meristem (cambium)** -- running along the **sides** of stems and roots; responsible for making them **thicker** (this is why a neem tree trunk grows wider year after year)
- **Intercalary meristem** -- found near the **nodes** (joints) of certain plants; helps grasses sprout back after being grazed or mowed

> **Key idea:** Growth in plants happens only where meristematic tissue is present -- at specific hotspots, not everywhere.

---

### Permanent Tissue

Once meristematic cells finish dividing, they settle into a fixed shape, size, and role. This settling down is called **differentiation**, and the resulting cells form **permanent tissue**.

Permanent tissue comes in two forms:
1. **Simple permanent tissue** (one type of cell)
2. **Complex permanent tissue** (several types of cells working together)

---

### Simple Permanent Tissue

#### 1. Parenchyma

- The most **widespread** simple tissue in plants
- Cells are **living**, with **thin walls** and **large intercellular spaces**
- **Loosely packed**, giving them a spongy texture
- **Role:** Stores food -- the soft, fleshy part of a potato is mostly parenchyma
- **Chlorenchyma:** When parenchyma cells contain chlorophyll, they carry out photosynthesis (as in leaves)
- **Aerenchyma:** In water plants like lotus, parenchyma develops **large air pockets** that help the plant float

#### 2. Collenchyma

- Cells are **living** and slightly elongated, with **unevenly thickened corners**
- Very little space between cells
- **Role:** Gives the plant **flexibility** -- allows stems and leaf stalks to bend in the wind without snapping
- Found just beneath the outer skin of young stems and in **leaf stalks (petioles)**

#### 3. Sclerenchyma

- Cells are **dead**, long, and narrow
- Walls are **heavily reinforced with lignin**, sometimes so thick that the cell interior is nearly sealed shut
- **Role:** Provides **hardness and rigidity**
- Found in mature stems, around vascular bundles, in leaf veins, and in the tough outer shells of seeds
- A familiar example: the **hard shell of a coconut** is sclerenchyma

![Comparison of parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma cells showing their distinct cell wall structures](simple-permanent-tissues-comparison.png)

> **Key idea:** Parenchyma stores, collenchyma bends, sclerenchyma strengthens.

#### Epidermis

- The **outermost single layer** of cells blanketing the entire plant
- Cells fit together **without gaps**, forming a continuous protective sheet
- **Role:** Shields against water loss, physical damage, and fungal attack
- Aerial surfaces often secrete a **waxy coating (cuticle)** to cut down evaporation
- Leaves contain **stomata** -- tiny pores flanked by two **kidney-shaped guard cells** that open and close to regulate gas exchange and water vapour loss

![Stomatal pore with two kidney-shaped guard cells on the leaf epidermis](stomata-guard-cells-diagram.png)

- Root epidermis sprouts **root hairs** -- fine extensions that hugely increase the area for absorbing water
- Desert plants like cactus have an **extra-thick cuticle** to conserve every drop of moisture

#### Cork

- In older plants, the epidermis is gradually replaced by **cork**, produced by a secondary meristem in the bark region
- Cork cells are **dead**, tightly pressed together with **no gaps** between them
- Their walls contain **suberin**, a waxy substance that makes them **waterproof** and gas-proof
- **Role:** Guards the mature plant against water loss and infection

### Complex Permanent Tissue

Complex tissues contain **more than one cell type**, all collaborating for a shared purpose. The two key complex tissues are **xylem** and **phloem**, which together form the plant's **vascular bundle** -- its internal transport system.

#### Xylem

Xylem carries **water and dissolved minerals** upward from the roots to every part of the plant.

**Made up of:**
- **Tracheids** -- long, dead cells with thickened walls; act as narrow water pipes
- **Vessels** -- wider, tubular dead cells; carry water more efficiently
- **Xylem parenchyma** -- the only living cells in xylem; store food
- **Xylem fibres** -- dead cells that lend mechanical support

> **Key idea:** Apart from xylem parenchyma, all xylem components are dead. Their rigid, hollow structure makes them perfect water-conducting channels.

#### Phloem

Phloem transports **food** (mainly sugars produced during photosynthesis) from the leaves to every other part of the plant. This transport happens in **both directions** -- upward and downward.

**Made up of:**
- **Sieve tubes** -- living tubular cells with perforated end-walls (sieve plates) that let food solutions flow through
- **Companion cells** -- living cells that power the sieve tubes
- **Phloem parenchyma** -- store food
- **Phloem fibres** -- the only dead cells in phloem; provide support

> **Key idea:** Except for phloem fibres, all phloem cells are alive. Xylem moves water up; phloem moves food in every direction.

## Animal Tissues

Animal tissues are classified into four major types:
1. **Epithelial tissue**
2. **Connective tissue**
3. **Muscular tissue**
4. **Nervous tissue**

---

### 1. Epithelial Tissue

Epithelial tissue forms the **outer covering** or **inner lining** of organs and body surfaces. It is the body's first line of defence.

**Hallmarks:**
- Cells sit **closely packed** with almost no space between them
- They form a **continuous, unbroken sheet**
- The sheet rests on a thin, non-cellular **basement membrane**

**Varieties of epithelial tissue:**

| Type | Cell Shape | Where Found | What It Does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Squamous** | Flat and tile-like | Blood vessel walls, lung air sacs, inner cheek lining | Allows substances to pass through easily |
| **Stratified squamous** | Multiple stacked layers of flat cells | Skin | Withstands wear and tear |
| **Cuboidal** | Cube-shaped | Kidney tubules, salivary gland ducts | Provides support and assists secretion |
| **Columnar** | Tall, pillar-shaped | Inner wall of the intestine | Absorbs nutrients, secretes mucus |
| **Ciliated columnar** | Columnar with tiny hair-like cilia on top | Windpipe, bronchi | Sweeps mucus and trapped particles upward |
| **Glandular** | Modified columnar | Glands (e.g., sweat glands, salivary glands) | Produces and releases substances |

---

### 2. Connective Tissue

Connective tissue **links, supports, and cushions** different organs and tissues. Its defining feature is that cells are scattered within a **matrix** -- a non-living material that fills the space between them.

**Types of connective tissue:**

| Type | Nature of Matrix | Primary Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Blood** | Liquid (plasma) | Ferries oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and wastes around the body |
| **Bone** | Rock-hard, rich in calcium salts | Forms the skeleton; protects vital organs |
| **Cartilage** | Firm yet flexible | Smoothens joints; shapes the nose, ear, and windpipe |
| **Tendon** | Very strong, limited stretch | Fastens **muscle to bone** |
| **Ligament** | Strong and elastic | Fastens **bone to bone** |
| **Areolar tissue** | Loose, gel-like | Fills gaps between skin and muscles; cushions internal organs |
| **Adipose tissue** | Packed with fat-storing cells | Stores energy as fat; insulates the body |

> **Key idea:** Blood is classified as connective tissue because it has cells (RBCs, WBCs, platelets) floating in a fluid matrix (plasma).

---

### 3. Muscular Tissue

Muscular tissue is built from long cells called **muscle fibres** that house special **contractile proteins**. These proteins can shorten and lengthen, letting muscles **contract and relax** to produce movement.

**Three kinds of muscle:**

![Three types of muscle fibres: striated (skeletal), smooth (unstriated), and cardiac muscle under microscope](three-types-of-muscle-tissue.png)

| Feature | Striated (Skeletal) | Smooth (Unstriated) | Cardiac |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cell shape** | Long, cylindrical, unbranched | Spindle-shaped, tapered ends | Cylindrical, branched |
| **Nuclei** | Many per cell | One per cell | One per cell |
| **Striations (bands)** | Present | Absent | Present |
| **Voluntary or involuntary** | Voluntary (you decide to move) | Involuntary (works on its own) | Involuntary |
| **Location** | Attached to bones (arms, legs) | Walls of stomach, intestines, blood vessels | Heart wall only |
| **Job** | Moves limbs and body parts | Pushes food along the gut, controls blood vessel width | Pumps blood rhythmically for a lifetime |

> **Key idea:** You consciously control skeletal muscles (like lifting your hand). Smooth muscles and cardiac muscles work automatically -- you never have to think about digesting food or beating your heart.

---

### 4. Nervous Tissue

Nervous tissue specialises in **detecting stimuli** and **transmitting electrical signals** at high speed throughout the body.

**The nerve cell (neuron):**

![Labeled diagram of a neuron showing cell body, dendrites, axon, myelin sheath, and nerve endings](neuron-structure-labeled.png)

- **Cell body** -- contains the nucleus and most of the cytoplasm
- **Dendrites** -- short, tree-like branches that pick up incoming signals
- **Axon** -- a single, very long fibre that carries signals away from the cell body
- **Nerve endings** -- tips of the axon that connect to the next neuron or to a muscle

A single neuron can be **up to one metre long**. Bundles of nerve fibres wrapped in connective tissue form a **nerve**.

A **nerve impulse** is the rapid electrical message that races along a neuron. Signals jump from the nerve ending of one neuron to the dendrite of the next, creating a lightning-fast communication chain.

> **Key idea:** Nervous tissue and muscular tissue work hand in hand -- neurons detect what is happening and send signals; muscles carry out the response. Touch a hot tawa, and within a fraction of a second your hand pulls back.

## Key Terms to Remember

- **Tissue** -- A group of structurally similar cells performing one function
- **Meristematic tissue** -- Ever-dividing plant tissue found at growing points
- **Permanent tissue** -- Plant tissue whose cells have differentiated and stopped dividing
- **Parenchyma** -- Living, thin-walled cells that store food
- **Collenchyma** -- Living cells with thickened corners that provide flexibility
- **Sclerenchyma** -- Dead, lignified cells that provide rigidity
- **Xylem** -- Complex tissue that transports water and minerals upward
- **Phloem** -- Complex tissue that transports food in all directions
- **Epithelial tissue** -- Covers body surfaces and lines cavities
- **Connective tissue** -- Connects, supports, and binds body parts
- **Muscular tissue** -- Contracts and relaxes to produce movement
- **Nervous tissue** -- Detects stimuli and transmits impulses
- **Stomata** -- Leaf pores for gas exchange, bordered by guard cells
- **Lignin** -- The hardening substance deposited in sclerenchyma walls
- **Suberin** -- The waterproofing substance in cork cell walls
