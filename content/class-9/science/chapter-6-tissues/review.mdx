## Key Concepts

- A **tissue** is a group of cells with similar structure working together to perform a specific function
- Plants have **meristematic tissue** (dividing) and **permanent tissue** (differentiated, non-dividing)
- **Meristematic cells**: dense cytoplasm, thin walls, large nuclei, no vacuoles
- **Apical meristem** (root/shoot tips) -- increases length; **lateral meristem** (sides) -- increases girth; **intercalary meristem** (nodes) -- helps grasses regrow
- **Differentiation** = process by which meristematic cells become permanent tissue
- **Parenchyma**: living, thin-walled, loosely packed; stores food; **chlorenchyma** (with chlorophyll) does photosynthesis; **aerenchyma** (with air spaces) helps aquatic plants float
- **Collenchyma**: living, unevenly thickened corners; provides flexibility to stems and leaf stalks
- **Sclerenchyma**: dead, walls thickened with **lignin**; provides hardness and rigidity (e.g., coconut shell)
- **Epidermis**: outermost protective layer; secretes waxy **cuticle** to reduce water loss; **stomata** (pores with guard cells) for gas exchange
- **Cork** replaces epidermis in old plants; dead cells with **suberin** (waterproof); no intercellular spaces
- **Xylem** transports water and minerals **upward**; components: tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma, xylem fibres (mostly dead cells)
- **Phloem** transports food in **all directions**; components: sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma, phloem fibres (mostly living cells)
- Animal tissues: **epithelial**, **connective**, **muscular**, **nervous**
- **Epithelial tissue**: covers body surfaces and lines organs; closely packed cells on a basement membrane
- Types: squamous (flat), cuboidal (cube), columnar (pillar), ciliated columnar (with cilia), glandular (secretory)
- **Connective tissue** has cells in a **matrix**; includes blood (liquid matrix), bone (hard), cartilage (flexible), tendon, ligament, areolar, adipose
- **Tendon** connects muscle to bone; **ligament** connects bone to bone
- **Striated/skeletal muscle**: voluntary, attached to bones, multinucleated, has striations
- **Smooth/unstriated muscle**: involuntary, in walls of organs (stomach, intestines), spindle-shaped
- **Cardiac muscle**: involuntary, branched, found only in the heart, rhythmic contractions for life
- **Nervous tissue**: neurons detect stimuli and transmit electrical impulses; parts -- cell body, dendrites, axon, nerve endings

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tissue | Group of similar cells performing a specific function |
| Meristematic tissue | Plant tissue with actively dividing cells |
| Permanent tissue | Plant tissue with differentiated, non-dividing cells |
| Differentiation | Process by which cells take on a fixed shape, size, and function |
| Xylem | Complex tissue transporting water and minerals upward |
| Phloem | Complex tissue transporting food in all directions |
| Stomata | Tiny pores on leaves for gas exchange, bordered by guard cells |
| Lignin | Hardening substance in sclerenchyma cell walls |
| Suberin | Waterproofing substance in cork cell walls |
| Neuron | Nerve cell that transmits electrical impulses |

## Remember These

- **"Parenchyma stores, collenchyma bends, sclerenchyma strengthens"** -- quick memory aid for simple tissues
- In xylem, all components are dead **except** xylem parenchyma; in phloem, all are living **except** phloem fibres
- **Blood is a connective tissue** -- cells (RBCs, WBCs, platelets) float in a liquid matrix (plasma)
- Plants use mostly **dead cells** for support (cheap, no energy cost); animals rely on **living cells** (muscles, nerves) because they move
- Cardiac muscle never fatigues -- it contracts rhythmically throughout life without rest
