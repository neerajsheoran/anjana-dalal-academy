## Key Concepts

- Atoms contain three sub-atomic particles: **electrons** (e-, negative), **protons** (p+, positive), **neutrons** (n, neutral)
- **J.J. Thomson** discovered the electron; **E. Goldstein** discovered canal rays leading to the proton; **James Chadwick** discovered the neutron
- **Thomson's model** (Watermelon model): atom is a ball of positive charge with electrons embedded in it -- could not explain Rutherford's results
- **Rutherford's alpha-particle scattering experiment**: most alpha particles passed straight through gold foil; a few deflected; very few bounced back
- Rutherford concluded: atom is mostly **empty space** with a tiny, dense, positively charged **nucleus** at the centre; electrons orbit the nucleus
- **Flaw of Rutherford's model**: orbiting electrons should radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus, but atoms are stable
- **Bohr's model**: electrons revolve in fixed **orbits/shells** (K, L, M, N...); they do not lose energy while in a shell; energy is absorbed/released only when jumping between shells
- **Maximum electrons per shell**: 2n² (K=2, L=8, M=18, N=32), but outermost shell holds max **8 electrons**
- Electrons fill **innermost shell first** before moving outward
- **Valence electrons** = electrons in outermost shell; **valency** = combining capacity
- Valency rules: 1-3 outer electrons --> valency = that number; 5-7 --> valency = 8 minus that number; 4 --> valency = 4; 8 (or 2 for He) --> valency = 0
- **Atomic number (Z)** = number of protons = number of electrons (in neutral atom)
- **Mass number (A)** = protons + neutrons; **neutrons = A - Z**
- **Isotopes**: same element, same Z, different A (different neutron count); same chemical properties, different physical properties
- **Isobars**: different elements, different Z, same A
- Average atomic mass is a **weighted average** of isotope masses based on their natural abundance

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Electron | Negatively charged sub-atomic particle; mass ~1/1840 u |
| Proton | Positively charged sub-atomic particle; mass = 1 u; found in nucleus |
| Neutron | Neutral sub-atomic particle; mass = 1 u; found in nucleus |
| Nucleus | Tiny, dense, positively charged centre of an atom |
| Shell / Energy level | Fixed orbit around the nucleus where electrons reside |
| Valence electrons | Electrons in the outermost shell |
| Valency | Combining capacity of an element |
| Atomic number (Z) | Number of protons in the nucleus |
| Mass number (A) | Total number of protons + neutrons |
| Isotopes | Atoms of same element, same Z, different A |
| Isobars | Atoms of different elements, different Z, same A |
| Nucleons | Collective term for protons and neutrons |

| Iodine-131 | Thyroid diagnosis and treatment |

## Remember These

- Chlorine's average atomic mass = 35.5 u because Cl-35 (75%) and Cl-37 (25%) both exist in nature
- Hydrogen isotopes: **Protium** (0 neutrons), **Deuterium** (1 neutron), **Tritium** (2 neutrons)
- Potassium (Z=19) has configuration 2, 8, 8, 1 (not 2, 8, 9) because outermost shell cannot exceed 8
- Protons and neutrons together account for nearly **all** the mass of an atom; electron mass is negligible
- Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar) have completely filled outermost shells and valency = 0
