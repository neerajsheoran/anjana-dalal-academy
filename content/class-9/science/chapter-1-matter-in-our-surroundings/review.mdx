## Key Concepts

- **Matter** is anything that has mass and occupies space; emotions and thoughts are not matter
- Matter is made of extremely tiny **particles** with spaces between them
- Particles are always in **motion**; higher temperature means faster movement
- **Diffusion** is the spontaneous intermixing of particles; it is fastest in gases, slowest in solids
- Particles attract each other with a **force of attraction** that varies by substance
- **Solids** have fixed shape, fixed volume, tightly packed particles that only vibrate
- **Liquids** have fixed volume but no fixed shape; particles slide past each other
- **Gases** have no fixed shape or volume; particles move randomly at high speed and are highly compressible
- **Melting point** is the temperature at which a solid becomes a liquid (ice: 0 °C / 273 K)
- **Boiling point** is the temperature at which a liquid boils (water: 100 °C / 373 K)
- During a change of state, temperature remains constant -- energy goes into breaking/forming bonds
- **Latent heat of fusion** = heat to convert 1 kg solid to liquid at melting point (no temperature change)
- **Latent heat of vaporisation** = heat to convert 1 kg liquid to gas at boiling point (no temperature change)
- **Sublimation** = solid directly to gas (e.g., camphor, naphthalene, dry ice); reverse is **deposition**
- **Evaporation** is a surface phenomenon; occurs at any temperature below boiling point
- Evaporation causes **cooling** because escaping particles carry energy away
- Evaporation rate increases with higher temperature, larger surface area, lower humidity, and higher wind speed
- Gases can be **liquefied** by applying high pressure and lowering temperature

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Matter | Anything that has mass and occupies space |
| Diffusion | Spontaneous intermixing of particles of different substances |
| Melting point | Temperature at which a solid converts to a liquid at atmospheric pressure |
| Boiling point | Temperature at which a liquid starts boiling at atmospheric pressure |
| Latent heat of fusion | Heat needed to convert 1 kg of solid to liquid at its melting point, with no temperature change |
| Latent heat of vaporisation | Heat needed to convert 1 kg of liquid to gas at its boiling point, with no temperature change |
| Sublimation | Direct change from solid to gas without passing through the liquid state |
| Deposition | Direct change from gas to solid without passing through the liquid state |
| Evaporation | Conversion of liquid to vapour at any temperature below the boiling point (surface phenomenon) |
| Humidity | Amount of water vapour present in the air |

## Remember These

- **Celsius to Kelvin:** add 273; **Kelvin to Celsius:** subtract 273; SI unit of temperature is **Kelvin (K)**
- Steam at 100 °C causes worse burns than water at 100 °C because steam carries extra **latent heat of vaporisation**
- Boiling is a **bulk phenomenon** (happens throughout the liquid); evaporation is a **surface phenomenon** (only surface particles escape)
- A sponge appears compressible but it is a solid -- squeezing only pushes out trapped air
- Naphthalene balls disappear from cupboards by **sublimation**, not melting
