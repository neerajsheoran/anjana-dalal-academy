## Key Concepts

- A **force** is a push or a pull. SI unit: **newton (N)**
- Forces are vectors — they have magnitude AND direction
- **Balanced forces** = net force is zero → no change in motion
- **Unbalanced forces** = there is a net force → object accelerates in the direction of the net force
- **Newton's first law of motion**: An object continues at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted on by an unbalanced force (the law of inertia)
- **Inertia** = the tendency of an object to resist any change in its motion
- More mass → more inertia. A truck is harder to start, harder to stop, than a bicycle
- Three types of inertia: rest, motion, direction
- **Newton's second law of motion**: F = m × a (Force = mass × acceleration)
- One newton (1 N) is defined as the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s²
- **Newton's third law of motion**: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction
- Action and reaction act on DIFFERENT objects — they never cancel each other
- **Friction** opposes the relative motion of two surfaces in contact
- Friction is useful for walking, writing, braking, gripping; wasteful in engines (causes wear and heat)
- Friction can be reduced (lubricants, ball bearings, smoothing) or increased (rough soles, tyre treads)
- **Momentum** = mass × velocity (symbol p, unit kg·m/s)
- Total momentum is **conserved** in any interaction without external forces — what is gained by one object is lost by another

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Force | A push or a pull; measured in newtons |
| Newton | SI unit of force; 1 N gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s² |
| Balanced forces | Forces that cancel out — no net force, no acceleration |
| Unbalanced forces | Forces that do not cancel — produce a net force and acceleration |
| Inertia | Tendency to resist change in motion |
| Newton's first law | An object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless an unbalanced force acts on it |
| Newton's second law | F = m × a (force equals mass times acceleration) |
| Newton's third law | For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction (on a different object) |
| Friction | Force opposing relative motion of two surfaces in contact |
| Momentum | Mass × velocity; conserved when no external force acts |
| Conservation of momentum | Total momentum before = total momentum after, in any interaction without external forces |

## Remember These

- **Action and reaction act on different objects** — they do NOT cancel
- **More mass = more inertia** = harder to start, harder to stop
- **A turning car is accelerating** even at constant speed (direction changes)
- **F = ma** — bigger force OR smaller mass gives bigger acceleration
- **Without friction, you could not walk** — your foot would just slip backward
- **Airbags reduce force** by stretching the time over which your momentum changes
- **Rocket propulsion** is Newton's third law: gases push down, rocket pushes up
