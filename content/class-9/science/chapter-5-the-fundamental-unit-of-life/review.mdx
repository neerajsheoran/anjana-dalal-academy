## Key Concepts

- **Robert Hooke** (1665) first observed cells in cork; the term "cell" comes from Latin "cella" (small room)
- **Cell theory** (Schleiden & Schwann): all living organisms are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life
- **Virchow** added: every new cell arises from a pre-existing cell
- **Unicellular organisms** (Amoeba, Paramecium, bacteria) -- one cell does everything
- **Multicellular organisms** (humans, plants) -- division of labour among specialised cell groups
- Every cell has three essential parts: **plasma membrane**, **nucleus**, **cytoplasm**
- **Plasma membrane** is made of lipids and proteins; it is **selectively permeable**
- **Diffusion**: movement of molecules from high to low concentration (e.g., gas exchange -- O2 in, CO2 out)
- **Osmosis**: movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from high to low water concentration
- In **hypotonic** solution, cell swells; in **isotonic**, no change; in **hypertonic**, cell shrinks
- **Endocytosis**: cell engulfs food particles by wrapping its membrane around them (Amoeba)
- **Cell wall** (plants only) is made of **cellulose**; provides rigidity and prevents bursting; freely permeable
- **Plasmolysis**: shrinking of cell contents away from the cell wall in a hypertonic solution (only in living cells)
- **Nucleus** is the control centre; contains **chromosomes** made of **DNA** wrapped around proteins
- **DNA** carries hereditary information; functional segments of DNA are **genes**
- **Prokaryotic cells** (bacteria): no membrane-bound nucleus, no membrane-bound organelles
- **Eukaryotic cells** (plants, animals, fungi): true nucleus with nuclear membrane, membrane-bound organelles
- **Mitochondria** = powerhouse; produce **ATP**; have their own DNA; double membrane with **cristae**
- **Chloroplasts** (plants only) contain **chlorophyll** for **photosynthesis**; have own DNA
- **Lysosomes** = suicide bags; contain digestive enzymes; break down foreign material and worn-out organelles
- **Golgi apparatus** = packaging and dispatch; modifies, sorts, and ships proteins
- **ER**: Rough ER (with ribosomes) makes proteins; Smooth ER makes lipids and detoxifies
- **Vacuoles**: large central vacuole in plant cells (maintains turgidity); small/absent in animal cells
- **Mitosis** = 2 identical daughter cells (for growth/repair); **Meiosis** = 4 cells with half chromosomes (for gametes)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Cell | Smallest structural and functional unit of life |
| Plasma membrane | Selectively permeable outer boundary of a cell (lipid + protein) |
| Cell wall | Rigid cellulose layer outside the plasma membrane in plant cells |
| Osmosis | Movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane |
| Plasmolysis | Shrinkage of cell contents from cell wall in a hypertonic solution |
| Nucleus | Control centre of the cell; houses DNA |
| Chromosomes | Structures made of DNA + protein; carry genetic information |
| Genes | Functional units of heredity on DNA |
| Prokaryotic | Cell lacking a membrane-bound nucleus (e.g., bacteria) |
| Eukaryotic | Cell with a true membrane-bound nucleus |
| ATP | Adenosine triphosphate; energy currency of the cell |

## Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

| Feature | Plant Cell | Animal Cell |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Cell wall | Present (cellulose) | Absent |
| Plastids | Present | Absent |
| Vacuoles | One large central vacuole | Small or absent |
| Centrioles | Usually absent | Present |
| Shape | Rectangular, rigid | Rounded or irregular |

## Remember These

- Lysosomes are called **"suicide bags"** because they burst and digest the entire cell if it is damaged
- Mitochondria and plastids have their own **DNA and ribosomes** -- they can make some of their own proteins
- **Chromatin** (spread-out DNA threads) condenses into visible **chromosomes** just before cell division
- Osmosis explains why raisins swell in water and eggs shrink in salt solution
