## Key Concepts

- **Law of Conservation of Mass**: Total mass before a reaction = total mass after (mass is neither created nor destroyed)
- **Law of Constant Proportions**: A compound always contains the same elements in a fixed ratio by mass (e.g., water is always H:O = 1:8 by mass)
- **Dalton's Atomic Theory**: All matter is made of indivisible atoms; atoms of the same element are identical; atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios
- An **atom** is the smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical properties
- **1 atomic mass unit (u)** = 1/12th of the mass of one carbon-12 atom
- Element symbols: first letter uppercase, second letter (if any) lowercase; some from Latin names (Fe, Au, Ag, Na, K, Cu, Sn)
- A **molecule** is the smallest unit of a substance, made of two or more atoms bonded together
- **Atomicity** = number of atoms in one molecule of an element (H2 = diatomic, O3 = triatomic, P4 = tetra-atomic, S8 = octa-atomic)
- **Ions** are charged atoms/groups: **cation** (+), **anion** (-)
- **Polyatomic ions**: NH4+, OH-, NO3-, CO3 2-, SO4 2-, PO4 3-
- **Valency** = combining capacity of an element
- **Criss-cross method**: swap valencies to get subscripts in a chemical formula
- **Molecular mass** = sum of atomic masses of all atoms in one molecule
- **Formula unit mass** = sum of atomic masses in one formula unit (for ionic compounds)
- Brackets are used in formulas when a polyatomic ion appears more than once, e.g., Mg(OH)2

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Atom | Smallest unit of an element that keeps its chemical identity |
| Molecule | Smallest independent unit of a substance; two or more atoms bonded together |
| Ion | An atom or group of atoms with an electrical charge |
| Cation | Positively charged ion (lost electrons) |
| Anion | Negatively charged ion (gained electrons) |
| Valency | Combining capacity of an element |
| Atomic mass unit (u) | 1/12th of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Molecular mass | Sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule |
| Chemical formula | Shorthand showing which elements and how many atoms make up a compound |
| IUPAC | International body that approves element names and symbols |

## Remember These

- CO (carbon monoxide) is not the same as Co (cobalt) -- capitalisation matters
- Water (H2O): molecular mass = 2(1) + 16 = **18 u**; CaCO3: formula unit mass = 40 + 12 + 48 = **100 u**
- Ionic compounds form crystal lattices, not individual molecules -- use "formula unit mass" instead of "molecular mass"
- In any compound formula, total positive charges must equal total negative charges (electrically neutral)
