## Key Concepts

- **Sound** is a form of energy that travels as vibrations through a medium
- All sounds are produced by **vibrating objects** (drums, vocal cords, strings, bells)
- Sound travels as a **longitudinal wave** — particles vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation
- A sound wave consists of regions of **compression** (high pressure) and **rarefaction** (low pressure)
- Sound CANNOT travel through vacuum — it needs a medium
- Speed of sound varies with medium:
  - **Air at 20°C**: ~343 m/s
  - **Water**: ~1500 m/s
  - **Steel**: ~6000 m/s
- Sound travels FASTER in solids than in liquids than in gases (particles are closer in solids)
- Sound also travels slightly faster as temperature rises
- **Frequency** = number of vibrations per second. SI unit: **hertz (Hz)**
- Frequency determines **pitch** — higher frequency = higher pitch
- **Amplitude** = maximum displacement of particles from rest
- Amplitude determines **loudness** — bigger amplitude = louder sound
- **Loudness** is measured in **decibels (dB)** — decibels are logarithmic
- Human hearing range: **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)**
- **Infrasound** = below 20 Hz (elephants, whales)
- **Ultrasound** = above 20,000 Hz (bats, dolphins, used in medicine)
- An **echo** is sound reflected off a surface; needs at least 17 m between source and reflector for the human ear to distinguish
- **Reverberation** = multiple reflections producing a sustained sound (e.g., in large halls)
- **SONAR** uses sound reflection to detect underwater objects (submarine navigation, fish detection)
- **Ultrasound applications**: medical imaging (pregnancy scans), cleaning, lithotripsy (breaking kidney stones)
- Human ear has three parts:
  - **Outer ear**: pinna and ear canal collect sound
  - **Middle ear**: eardrum + three tiny bones (hammer, anvil, stirrup) amplify
  - **Inner ear**: cochlea converts vibrations to electrical signals via the auditory nerve

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Sound | Energy that travels as vibration through a medium |
| Longitudinal wave | A wave where particles vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation |
| Compression | A region of high pressure in a sound wave |
| Rarefaction | A region of low pressure in a sound wave |
| Frequency | Number of vibrations per second (Hz) |
| Pitch | How high or low a sound is (determined by frequency) |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement of particles from rest |
| Loudness | How loud a sound is (determined by amplitude) |
| Decibel (dB) | Unit of loudness (logarithmic) |
| Echo | A distinct reflected sound |
| Reverberation | Multiple overlapping reflections producing a sustained sound |
| SONAR | Sound Navigation and Ranging — uses sound to detect underwater objects |
| Ultrasound | Sound above 20,000 Hz (humans cannot hear) |
| Infrasound | Sound below 20 Hz (humans cannot hear) |
| Cochlea | The fluid-filled inner ear structure that converts sound vibrations into nerve signals |

## Remember These

- **No sound in vacuum** — without a medium, vibrations cannot travel
- **Speed of sound**: solids > liquids > gases (because particles are closer)
- **Frequency → pitch**, **Amplitude → loudness**
- Human range: **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz**
- For a clear echo, the reflecting surface needs to be at least **17 metres away**
- **Ultrasound saves lives** — pregnancy scans, lithotripsy, dental cleaning
- The **three smallest bones in the human body** (hammer, anvil, stirrup) are in the middle ear
- Volume above **90 dB** can damage hearing if prolonged
