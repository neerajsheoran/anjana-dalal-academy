## Key Concepts

- The **cell** is the smallest unit of life — all living organisms are made of one or more cells
- **Unicellular** organisms (amoeba, yeast, bacteria) are made of just one cell; **multicellular** organisms (plants, animals) are made of many
- **Robert Hooke** discovered cells in 1665 by examining a slice of cork under his own microscope
- The **limit of resolution of the human eye** is about 0.1 mm; cells are smaller, so we need microscopes
- A **light microscope** uses lenses + visible light; an **electron microscope** uses a beam of electrons and reveals far finer detail
- The **cell membrane (plasma membrane)** is a selectively permeable boundary controlling what enters and leaves
- **Osmosis** = movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from higher to lower water concentration
- **Diffusion** = movement of any particles from higher to lower concentration (does not need a membrane)
- The **nucleus** controls cell activities and contains the **DNA**
- **Mitochondria** are the power plants — they produce ATP (the cell's energy currency)
- **Ribosomes** sit on the endoplasmic reticulum and build **proteins** from instructions in the DNA
- The **Golgi apparatus** packages and ships proteins to their destinations
- **Lysosomes** are the recycling crew — they break down old cell parts and digest invaders
- **Plant cells** have a **cell wall** (cellulose), a large **central vacuole**, and **chloroplasts** (for photosynthesis)
- **Animal cells** have NO cell wall, NO chloroplasts, and only small vacuoles
- **Mitosis** = cell division producing two identical daughter cells (growth and repair)
- **Meiosis** = special cell division producing sex cells with half the chromosomes (variety in reproduction)
- Organisation hierarchy: **Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Cell | The smallest structural and functional unit of life |
| Cell membrane | Selectively permeable boundary that surrounds every cell |
| Osmosis | Diffusion of water through a selectively permeable membrane |
| Organelle | A structure inside a cell with a specific function (nucleus, mitochondrion, etc.) |
| Nucleus | The cell's control centre, contains DNA |
| Mitochondrion | Organelle that produces energy (ATP) from glucose and oxygen |
| Chloroplast | Green organelle in plant cells where photosynthesis happens |
| Cell wall | Rigid cellulose layer outside the cell membrane in plant cells |
| Vacuole | Sac inside a cell that stores water and other substances |
| Mitosis | Cell division producing two identical daughter cells |
| Meiosis | Cell division producing sex cells with half the chromosomes |
| Unicellular | An organism made of a single cell (e.g., amoeba) |
| Multicellular | An organism made of many cells (e.g., humans, banyan trees) |

## Remember These

- **Plant cell vs animal cell** — only plant cells have cell wall, large vacuole, and chloroplasts
- **Osmosis only moves water** — solutes like salt and sugar cannot cross the membrane
- **Mitosis = identical copies**, **Meiosis = half the chromosomes**
- A potato in salt water shrinks because water LEAVES the cells (osmosis)
- Robert Hooke saw cells in cork; he called them "cells" because they reminded him of monks' rooms
- Indian researchers at Birbal Sahni Institute (Lucknow) study thermophilic bacteria in Ladakh's hot springs as a clue to early life
