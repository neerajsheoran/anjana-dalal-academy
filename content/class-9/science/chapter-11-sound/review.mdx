## Key Concepts

- Sound is produced by **vibrating objects**; no vibration = no sound
- Sound is a **longitudinal wave**: particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel
- Alternating **compressions** (high pressure/density) and **rarefactions** (low pressure/density) form a sound wave
- Sound **needs a medium** (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel; it **cannot travel through a vacuum**
- **Wavelength (lambda)** = distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions; SI unit: metre
- **Frequency (f)** = number of oscillations per second; SI unit: Hertz (Hz); f = 1/T
- **Time period (T)** = time for one complete oscillation; T = 1/f; SI unit: second
- **Amplitude** = maximum displacement from rest position; determines **loudness**
- **Speed of sound**: v = lambda x f
- Sound is fastest in **solids** (~5960 m/s in steel), moderate in **liquids** (~1531 m/s in water), slowest in **gases** (~346 m/s in air)
- Speed of sound in air increases with **temperature**
- **Loudness** depends on amplitude; measured in **decibels (dB)**; sounds > 80 dB can damage hearing
- **Pitch** depends on frequency; high frequency = shrill (high pitch); low frequency = deep (low pitch)
- **Timbre/quality** distinguishes sounds of same pitch and loudness from different sources
- Human **audible range**: **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz**
- **Infrasound**: < 20 Hz (used by elephants); **Ultrasound**: > 20,000 Hz (used by bats, dolphins)
- **Echo**: reflected sound heard after at least 0.1 s delay; minimum distance to reflecting surface ~ **17.2 m**
- **Reverberation**: prolonged sound from multiple reflections in enclosed spaces
- **SONAR** uses ultrasound for underwater detection; distance = v x t / 2

## Key Formulae

| Quantity | Formula | SI Unit |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Speed of sound | v = lambda x f | m/s |
| Frequency | f = 1/T | Hz |
| Time period | T = 1/f | s |
| Echo distance | d = v x t / 2 | m |

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Compression | High-pressure, high-density region in a longitudinal wave |
| Rarefaction | Low-pressure, low-density region in a longitudinal wave |
| Wavelength | Distance between two consecutive compressions (or rarefactions) |
| Frequency | Number of complete oscillations per second (Hz) |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement of a particle from its rest position |
| Loudness | Perceived volume; depends on amplitude; measured in dB |
| Pitch | Perceived shrillness/deepness; depends on frequency |
| Timbre | Tonal quality that distinguishes different sources playing the same note |
| Echo | Reflected sound heard distinctly after a time delay |
| Reverberation | Prolonged persistence of sound due to repeated reflections |
| Ultrasound | Sound frequency above 20,000 Hz |
| Infrasound | Sound frequency below 20 Hz |
| SONAR | Sound Navigation And Ranging; uses ultrasound for underwater detection |

## Applications of Ultrasound

- **Ultrasonography** -- imaging internal organs and monitoring pregnancy
- **Echocardiography** -- examining the heart
- **Lithotripsy** -- shattering kidney stones
- **Industrial cleaning** and **flaw detection** in metals

## The Human Ear

- **Outer ear (pinna)** collects sound --> **eardrum** vibrates --> **middle ear bones** (hammer, anvil, stirrup) amplify --> **cochlea** (inner ear) converts to electrical signals --> **auditory nerve** --> brain

## Remember These

- Sound is a **longitudinal** wave (not transverse); light is transverse
- For a clear echo, reflecting surface must be at least **17.2 m** away (at ~344 m/s in air)
- Speed of sound in air at room temperature is approximately **344 m/s** (not 330 -- that is at 0 °C)
- In SONAR, divide by 2 because sound travels to the object **and back**
- Hearing a train sooner through the rail than through air proves sound travels faster in solids
