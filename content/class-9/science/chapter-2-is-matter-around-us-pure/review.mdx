## Key Concepts

- A **pure substance** in chemistry contains only one type of particle (e.g., distilled water, copper wire)
- A **mixture** combines two or more pure substances without a chemical reaction; components keep their properties
- **Homogeneous mixtures** have uniform composition throughout (e.g., saltwater, air, alloys)
- **Heterogeneous mixtures** have visibly distinct parts (e.g., soil, oil in water, sand in water)
- A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture; **solute** dissolves in the **solvent**
- Solution particles are smaller than **1 nm**, do not scatter light, cannot be filtered, and are stable
- **Alloys** are solid solutions of metals (e.g., brass = Cu + Zn, bronze = Cu + Sn, stainless steel = Fe + Cr + C)
- A **saturated solution** has dissolved the maximum solute at a given temperature; **solubility** generally increases with temperature
- A **suspension** is heterogeneous, with particles > 100 nm that scatter light, settle over time, and can be filtered
- A **colloid** has particles between 1 nm and 100 nm; appears uniform but is heterogeneous; shows **Tyndall effect**; stable; cannot be filtered but separated by centrifugation
- **Tyndall effect** = scattering of light by colloidal particles (e.g., sunbeams in a dusty room, fog in headlights)
- Colloid components: **dispersed phase** (like solute) + **dispersion medium** (like solvent)
- **Elements** cannot be broken down further chemically; **metals**, **non-metals**, **metalloids**
- **Compounds** form when elements chemically combine in a fixed ratio; properties differ from constituent elements
- Physical changes produce no new substance; chemical changes produce new substances

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Pure substance | Made of a single type of particle (element or compound) |
| Mixture | Two or more pure substances combined without chemical bonding |
| Solution | Homogeneous mixture with particle size < 1 nm |
| Suspension | Heterogeneous mixture with large visible particles that settle over time |
| Colloid | Mixture with particle size 1-100 nm; stable; shows Tyndall effect |
| Tyndall effect | Scattering of a light beam by colloidal particles |
| Solubility | Maximum mass of solute that dissolves in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature |
| Element | Substance that cannot be decomposed by chemical means |
| Compound | Two or more elements chemically bonded in a fixed proportion |
| Alloy | Homogeneous solid mixture of metals (or metal + non-metal) |

## Separation Techniques at a Glance

| Method | Principle / Use Case |
|--------|---------------------|
| Evaporation | Recover dissolved solid from liquid (salt from seawater) |
| Filtration | Remove undissolved solid from liquid |
| Centrifugation | Spin to separate denser particles (cream from milk) |
| Sublimation | Separate a sublimable substance (ammonium chloride from salt) |
| Separating funnel | Separate immiscible liquids (oil and water) |
| Chromatography | Separate dissolved substances moving at different speeds |
| Simple distillation | Separate miscible liquids with boiling points > 25 °C apart |
| Fractional distillation | Separate miscible liquids with close boiling points |

## Remember These

- **Mercury** is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature; **bromine** is the only non-metal liquid at room temperature
- Concentration (mass %) = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) x 100
- Mixtures can be separated by physical methods; compounds require chemical or electrochemical methods
- Mixing iron and sulphur gives a mixture (iron is still magnetic); heating them together forms iron sulphide, a compound with entirely new properties
