## Key Concepts

- **Reproduction** is the biological process by which living things produce new individuals of their own kind
- No individual is immortal — reproduction is how a species continues across generations
- Two main strategies: **asexual reproduction** (one parent) and **sexual reproduction** (two parents)
- **Asexual reproduction**: offspring are nearly identical clones of the parent; fast, but no genetic variety
- **Sexual reproduction**: offspring inherit a mix of two parents' genes; slower, but produces variety needed for adaptation
- Methods of asexual reproduction:
  - **Binary fission** — bacteria, amoeba (parent divides into two)
  - **Budding** — yeast, hydra (a small bud grows from the parent)
  - **Fragmentation** — Spirogyra (body breaks into pieces, each becomes new individual)
  - **Regeneration** — planaria, starfish (lost body parts regrow)
  - **Vegetative propagation** — plants (cuttings, layering, grafting, tubers, bulbs)
- Indian agriculture relies heavily on vegetative propagation (bananas, sugarcane, mango varieties)
- A flower's parts:
  - **Petals** (attract pollinators), **sepals** (protect bud)
  - **Stamens** (male) — anther (pollen) + filament
  - **Pistil/carpel** (female) — stigma + style + ovary (contains ovules)
- **Pollination** = transfer of pollen from anther to stigma; can be self or cross
- Pollinators: bees, butterflies, beetles, birds, bats, wind
- **Fertilisation** = fusion of male and female gametes (in plants: pollen and egg cell)
- After fertilisation: ovule → seed; ovary → fruit
- **Germination** = sprouting of a seed under suitable conditions (water, warmth, oxygen)
- In humans:
  - **Female system**: ovaries (produce eggs), fallopian tubes (where fertilisation occurs), uterus (where embryo develops), vagina
  - **Male system**: testes (in scrotum, produce sperm), sperm ducts, penis
- **Zygote** = fertilised egg; develops into embryo, foetus, then baby over ~9 months
- The **placenta** transfers nutrition and waste between mother and developing baby
- **Adolescence** = transition between childhood and adulthood (ages ~10-19)
- **Puberty** = biological maturation; driven by **oestrogen** (girls) and **testosterone** (boys)
- **Menstruation** = monthly shedding of uterine lining if no pregnancy occurs
- **Reproductive health** includes preventing STIs, managing reproductive cancers, family planning

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Reproduction | The biological process by which new individuals are produced |
| Asexual reproduction | Reproduction involving only one parent; offspring are clones |
| Sexual reproduction | Reproduction involving two parents and the mixing of their genes |
| Binary fission | Asexual reproduction where one cell divides into two |
| Budding | Asexual reproduction where a small bud grows from the parent |
| Fragmentation | Asexual reproduction where the body breaks into pieces |
| Vegetative propagation | Asexual reproduction in plants from non-reproductive parts |
| Pollination | Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma |
| Fertilisation | Fusion of male and female gametes |
| Zygote | A fertilised egg |
| Placenta | Organ connecting mother and baby for nutrition exchange |
| Puberty | The biological maturation that makes a person capable of reproducing |
| Menstruation | The monthly shedding of the uterine lining |

## Remember These

- Asexual = one parent, identical offspring; sexual = two parents, mixed offspring
- **Pollination is NOT the same as fertilisation** — pollination comes first; fertilisation needs the pollen tube to reach the ovule
- The **ovary becomes the fruit**; the **ovule becomes the seed**
- In humans, sperm are produced in **testes** (scrotum), eggs in **ovaries**
- Fertilisation usually happens in the **fallopian tube**; development happens in the **uterus**
- **Puberty hormones**: oestrogen (girls), testosterone (boys)
- **Menstruation is healthy and normal** — a sign that the reproductive system is functioning
- HPV vaccine prevents cervical cancer — given to adolescent girls under National Immunisation Programme
- Indian banana plantations use **vegetative propagation/tissue culture** — same chapter, industrial scale
