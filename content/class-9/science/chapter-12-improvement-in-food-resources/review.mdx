## Key Concepts

- India's food security depends on producing more from the same land using scientific methods
- **Green Revolution** boosted grain production; **White Revolution (Operation Flood)** made India the top milk producer
- Crops are classified as: **cereals** (carbohydrates), **pulses** (proteins), **oilseeds** (fats), **vegetables & fruits** (vitamins/minerals), **fodder** (animal feed)
- **Kharif crops** (June-October, monsoon): paddy, soyabean, maize, cotton, bajra, moong
- **Rabi crops** (November-April, winter): wheat, chana, peas, mustard, barley
- Three pillars of crop improvement: **variety improvement**, **production management**, **protection management**
- **Hybridisation** = crossing plants with different traits; types: intervarietal, interspecific, intergeneric
- Goals of variety improvement: higher yield, better quality, disease resistance, drought/salinity tolerance, faster maturity, broad adaptability
- Plants need **16 essential nutrients**: from air (C, O), water (H, O), soil -- **macronutrients** (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) and **micronutrients** (Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl)
- **Manure** = organic (compost, vermi-compost, green manure); improves soil structure, adds humus
- **Fertilisers** = chemical (urea, DAP, MOP); targeted nutrients in high concentration; overuse harms soil
- **Organic farming** uses manure, bio-fertilisers, and natural pest control instead of chemicals
- Modern irrigation: **drip irrigation** (water to root zone, saves up to 70%) and **sprinkler system**
- **Mixed cropping**: two+ crops together for risk reduction; **inter-cropping**: alternating rows of different crops; **crop rotation**: changing crops season by season (legumes fix nitrogen)
- Crop threats: **weeds** (steal nutrients), **insect pests** (chewing/sucking/boring), **diseases** (fungi/bacteria/viruses)
- Storage losses prevented by: drying grain below 14% moisture, fumigation, neem leaves, proper ventilation
- **Cattle farming**: indigenous breeds (Sahiwal, Gir) for disease resistance; exotic breeds (Jersey, Holstein) for high milk; **cross-breeding** combines both strengths
- Cattle feed: **roughage** (fodder, hay) + **concentrates** (grains, oilseed cakes, supplements)
- **Poultry**: layers (eggs) vs broilers (meat); cross-breeding improves yield and disease resistance
- **Fish production**: capture fishing (from natural sources) vs culture fishing/aquaculture (in ponds)
- **Composite fish culture**: 5-6 species in one pond occupying different feeding zones (no competition)
- **Apiculture** (bee-keeping) for honey and beeswax; **Apis mellifera** (Italian bee) preferred commercially

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Green Revolution | Major increase in food grain production using modern agriculture |
| White Revolution | Dramatic rise in India's milk output via Operation Flood |
| Hybridisation | Crossing genetically different plants to combine desirable traits |
| Macronutrients | Elements needed by plants in large amounts (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) |
| Micronutrients | Elements needed in trace amounts (Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl) |
| Manure | Organic material from decomposed plant/animal waste |
| Fertiliser | Factory-made chemical providing specific nutrients |
| Mixed cropping | Growing two or more crops together on the same field |
| Inter-cropping | Growing different crops in alternating rows in a pattern |
| Crop rotation | Changing crops on the same field across seasons |
| Composite fish culture | Rearing multiple complementary fish species in one pond |
| Apiculture | Scientific rearing of honeybees for honey and beeswax |

## Manure vs Fertiliser

| Feature | Manure | Fertiliser |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Origin | Natural (decomposed organic waste) | Factory-manufactured |
| Nutrients | Broad but low concentration | Specific (N, P, K) in high concentration |
| Humus | Adds humus, improves soil | No humus added |
| Long-term effect | Builds soil health | Overuse degrades soil |
| Cost | Low | Higher |

## Remember These

- Legumes in **crop rotation** naturally fix nitrogen in the soil, reducing the need for fertilisers
- **Composite fish culture** works because different species feed at different levels (surface, middle, bottom) and eat different food
- **Drip irrigation** is the most water-efficient method -- delivers water directly to roots
- Proper grain storage requires moisture below **14%** to prevent fungal growth and insect damage
- Bee-keeping provides a **dual benefit**: honey production + crop pollination
