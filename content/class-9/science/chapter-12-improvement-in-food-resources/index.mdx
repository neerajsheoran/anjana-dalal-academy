## Introduction

Feeding more than 1.4 billion people is one of the greatest challenges India faces. Every year the country needs hundreds of millions of tonnes of food grains, along with pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, milk, eggs, fish, and meat. Because the amount of farmable land cannot be expanded easily -- forests and wetlands must be protected -- the focus must shift to **producing more from the same land** and managing livestock and fisheries more efficiently.

**Landmark programmes that transformed Indian food production:**
- **Green Revolution** -- dramatically raised wheat and rice output through high-yielding seeds, better irrigation, and modern techniques
- **White Revolution (Operation Flood)** -- turned India into the world's largest milk producer

> **Key idea:** Boosting food output must go hand-in-hand with protecting the environment. Sustainable farming practices ensure that soil, water, and biodiversity are preserved for the future.

**Food security** requires both producing enough food and making it accessible to every citizen. Scientific management of farming, combined with mixed livelihood approaches -- integrating crops with dairy, poultry, fisheries, and bee-keeping -- helps raise both production and rural incomes.

## Improvement in Crop Yields

### Major Crop Categories

| Category | Common Examples | Nutritional Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Cereals** | Wheat, rice, bajra, jowar, ragi | Carbohydrates for energy |
| **Pulses** | Arhar (tur), moong, urad, chana, masoor | Proteins |
| **Oilseeds** | Groundnut, mustard, soyabean, sesame, sunflower | Fats and oils |
| **Vegetables & fruits** | Spinach, tomato, mango, banana, guava | Vitamins, minerals, dietary fibre |
| **Fodder crops** | Berseem, lucerne, napier grass | Feed for cattle and buffaloes |

### Crop Seasons in India

- **Kharif season** (June to October -- the monsoon months): Paddy, soyabean, maize, cotton, bajra, moong, urad
- **Rabi season** (November to April -- the cool, dry months): Wheat, chana, peas, mustard, linseed, barley

### Three Pillars of Crop Improvement

1. **Crop variety improvement** -- developing better seeds
2. **Crop production management** -- providing the right nutrients, water, and growing patterns
3. **Crop protection management** -- shielding the crop from pests, diseases, and weeds

## Crop Variety Improvement

Scientists develop superior crop varieties mainly through **hybridisation** (crossing plants with different genetic traits) and **genetic modification** (inserting specific useful genes into a plant).

**Types of hybridisation:**
- **Intervarietal** -- crossing two distinct varieties within the same species (e.g., a drought-resistant wheat variety with a high-yielding one)
- **Interspecific** -- crossing two different species of the same genus
- **Intergeneric** -- crossing plants from entirely different genera

### Goals of Variety Improvement

- **Greater yield** -- harvesting more grain or fruit per hectare
- **Better quality** -- higher protein in pulses, improved oil content in mustard, superior taste in Basmati rice
- **Resistance to living threats (biotic)** -- protection against fungal diseases, insect attacks, and nematodes
- **Tolerance of non-living stresses (abiotic)** -- ability to withstand drought, salinity, waterlogging, frost, or extreme heat
- **Faster maturity** -- a crop that ripens quickly allows farmers to sow a second or even third crop on the same field within the year
- **Broad adaptability** -- performing well across varied climates and soil types
- **Useful physical traits** -- shorter stems in wheat and rice (so the plant channels more nutrients into grain), taller stems in fodder crops (more biomass for animals)

> **Key idea:** The ultimate aim is to breed varieties that combine high yield, disease resistance, climate resilience, and good quality.

## Crop Production Management

### Nutrient Management

Plants require **16 essential nutrient elements** drawn from air, water, and soil.

| Source | Nutrients Provided |
|---|---|
| Air | Carbon, oxygen |
| Water | Hydrogen, oxygen |
| Soil -- Macronutrients (needed in bulk) | Nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium, sulphur |
| Soil -- Micronutrients (needed in traces) | Iron, manganese, boron, zinc, copper, molybdenum, chlorine |

#### Manure

**Manure** is prepared from decomposed plant residues and animal dung. It enriches the soil with organic matter (humus), improving its texture and water-holding ability.

**Varieties of manure:**
- **Compost** -- farm and household waste allowed to decompose in a pit; **vermi-compost** uses earthworms to speed up the process and produce nutrient-rich castings
- **Green manure** -- fast-growing plants like dhaincha or sunhemp are cultivated and then ploughed into the field before the main crop is sown

#### Fertilisers

**Fertilisers** are factory-made chemicals that deliver concentrated doses of specific nutrients.

**Common examples:** Urea (supplies nitrogen), DAP -- di-ammonium phosphate (supplies nitrogen and phosphorus), Muriate of Potash (supplies potassium)

### Manure versus Fertilisers

| Aspect | Manure | Fertiliser |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Natural decomposition of organic waste | Factory-manufactured chemicals |
| Nutrient range | Broad spectrum but in modest amounts | Targeted nutrients (N, P, K) in high concentration |
| Humus content | Adds humus, improving soil structure | Does not add humus |
| Long-term impact | Builds soil health over time | Overuse can degrade soil and pollute waterways |
| Cost | Low, often prepared on-farm | Higher, purchased from market |

> **Key idea:** Organic farming relies on manure, bio-fertilisers (nitrogen-fixing bacteria, mycorrhizae), and natural pest deterrents instead of synthetic chemicals. It protects soil health and supports environmental balance.

### Irrigation

Adequate water supply is crucial for seed germination, nutrient uptake, and photosynthesis.

**Traditional sources:** Tube wells, open wells, canal networks, village tanks, river lift schemes

**Water-efficient modern methods:**

![Drip irrigation system delivering water directly to plant roots through pipes and emitters](drip-irrigation-system.png)

- **Drip irrigation** -- water trickles through a network of pipes directly to each plant's root zone, slashing water waste by up to 70 percent compared to flood irrigation
- **Sprinkler system** -- water is sprayed over the crop in a rain-like pattern, suitable for uneven terrain

### Cropping Patterns

- **Mixed cropping** -- sowing two or more crops together on the same plot (e.g., wheat + mustard). If one crop fails, the other provides a safety net.
- **Inter-cropping** -- growing two or more crops in alternating rows following a definite pattern (e.g., maize rows alternating with urad rows). Each crop draws different nutrients, reducing competition.
- **Crop rotation** -- changing crops season by season on the same land (e.g., paddy in kharif followed by wheat in rabi, with a legume like moong in between). Legumes fix nitrogen, naturally replenishing soil fertility.

## Crop Protection Management

Even a healthy, well-nourished crop faces threats from **weeds**, **insect pests**, and **diseases**.

### Weeds

**Weeds** are uninvited plants that steal water, nutrients, sunlight, and space from the crop.

**Control strategies:** Hand-weeding, application of selective weedicides, proper seed cleaning before sowing, timely planting, and cropping techniques like inter-cropping that leave little room for weeds to establish.

### Insect Pests

Insects damage crops in three main ways:
- **Chewing** -- caterpillars and beetles bite off roots, leaves, or stems
- **Sucking** -- aphids and leafhoppers pierce plant tissue and drain sap
- **Boring** -- stem borers tunnel into stems and fruits, weakening the plant from inside

**Control strategies:** Chemical pesticides (used carefully to avoid harming beneficial insects), biological control (introducing natural predators such as ladybird beetles that eat aphids), crop rotation, and planting pest-resistant varieties.

### Diseases

Pathogens -- **fungi, bacteria, and viruses** -- cause plant diseases. They spread through wind, contaminated water, infected soil, and insect carriers.

**Control strategies:** Using disease-resistant seed varieties, treating seeds with fungicides before sowing, practising crop rotation, and applying targeted sprays when infection is detected.

### Safe Storage After Harvest

Proper storage prevents food grain losses caused by:
- **Living agents** -- grain-boring insects, rodents, fungal moulds, mites
- **Non-living factors** -- excess moisture, high temperature

**Preventive steps:** Thorough drying of grain (moisture below 14 percent), cleaning and sorting, fumigation of storage spaces, use of natural repellents like dried neem leaves, and modern storage in steel silos or well-ventilated godowns.

## Animal Husbandry

### Cattle Farming

Cattle serve two main purposes: **milk production** (dairy animals) and **farm labour** (draught animals for ploughing and transport).

**Breed categories:**
- **Indigenous (desi) breeds** (e.g., Sahiwal, Gir, Red Sindhi, Tharparkar) -- naturally resistant to tropical diseases and heat
- **Exotic (foreign) breeds** (e.g., Jersey, Holstein-Friesian, Brown Swiss) -- bred for exceptionally high milk output

**Cross-breeding** merges the strengths of both -- creating animals that yield more milk while still coping with Indian climatic conditions and local diseases.

**Feeding cattle:**
- **Roughage** -- bulky, fibre-rich feed such as green fodder, hay, straw, and silage
- **Concentrates** -- energy-and-protein-rich feed such as crushed grains, oilseed cakes, bran, and mineral-vitamin supplements

Regular **vaccination** and prompt **veterinary care** protect cattle from viral, bacterial, and parasitic diseases (external parasites like ticks, internal ones like liver flukes and roundworms).

### Poultry Farming

**Poultry** farming focuses on rearing chickens for **eggs** (layer breeds) and **meat** (broiler breeds).

**Key management practices:** Hygienic housing with proper ventilation and temperature control, nutritionally balanced feed (with adequate protein for layers and high-energy feed for broilers), and scheduled vaccinations.

**Indian breeds:** Aseel, Kadaknath, Busra
**Exotic breeds:** White Leghorn, Rhode Island Red, Plymouth Rock

Cross-breeding indigenous birds with exotic varieties produces offspring that lay more eggs, grow faster, and resist local diseases better.

### Fish Production

Fish is an important and affordable source of protein for millions of Indians, especially along the coastline.

**Two approaches:**
- **Capture fishing** -- harvesting fish from rivers, lakes, estuaries, and the open sea
- **Culture fishing (aquaculture)** -- raising fish in purpose-built ponds, tanks, or enclosures under controlled conditions

**Mariculture** -- culturing fish and shellfish in coastal seawater enclosures

**Composite fish culture** -- a clever system where 5-6 different fish species coexist in one pond. Each species occupies a different feeding zone (surface, mid-water, or bottom) and eats different food, so they do not compete. For example, catla feeds at the surface, rohu in the middle column, and mrigal and common carp forage at the bottom.

### Bee-Keeping (Apiculture)

**Apiculture** is the scientific rearing of honeybees for **honey** and **beeswax**.

**Commonly managed bee species:**
- **Apis cerana indica** (Indian honeybee)
- **Apis dorsata** (rock bee -- builds large open combs)
- **Apis florea** (little bee -- small colonies)
- **Apis mellifera** (Italian bee -- gentle temperament, high honey yield, commercially preferred)

**What determines good honey production:**
- Placing hives close to abundant flowering plants (near mustard fields in Haryana, litchi orchards in Bihar, or eucalyptus plantations in Tamil Nadu)
- The type of flowers available shapes the colour, flavour, and aroma of the honey
- Careful colony management -- regular inspection, disease control, timely harvesting

> **Key idea:** Strengthening food resources calls for a combined approach -- better crop varieties, efficient water and nutrient management, sustainable pest control, and scientific animal and fish husbandry. Bee-keeping adds the bonus of crop pollination alongside honey production.

## Key Terms to Remember

- **Green Revolution** -- Surge in food grain production through modern agricultural science
- **White Revolution** -- Dramatic increase in India's milk output via Operation Flood
- **Hybridisation** -- Crossing genetically different plants to combine desirable traits
- **Kharif crops** -- Monsoon-season crops sown from June to October
- **Rabi crops** -- Winter-season crops sown from November to April
- **Macronutrients** -- Elements plants need in large quantities (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S)
- **Micronutrients** -- Elements needed in very small amounts (Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl)
- **Manure** -- Organic material from decomposed waste that enriches soil
- **Fertiliser** -- Industrially produced chemical nutrients for crops
- **Mixed cropping** -- Growing multiple crops together on the same land
- **Inter-cropping** -- Growing different crops in a fixed row arrangement
- **Crop rotation** -- Alternating crops on the same field across seasons
- **Composite fish culture** -- Rearing several complementary fish species in one pond
- **Apiculture** -- The practice of keeping bees for honey and beeswax
