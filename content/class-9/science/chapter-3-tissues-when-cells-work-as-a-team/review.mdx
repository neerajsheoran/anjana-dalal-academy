## Key Concepts

- A **tissue** is a group of cells of similar structure that work together for a specific function
- Tissues exist because multicellular organisms benefit from **division of labour** at the cellular level
- **Plant tissues** are two kinds: **meristematic** (dividing, drives growth) and **permanent** (specialised)
- **Apical meristem** → at root + shoot tips → increases length
- **Lateral meristem (cambium)** → along sides of stem and root → increases thickness (girth)
- **Intercalary meristem** → at the base of leaves or above nodes (in grasses) → allows regrowth after cutting/grazing
- Simple permanent plant tissues:
  - **Parenchyma** — soft cells that store food and do photosynthesis (when green)
  - **Collenchyma** — flexible support (young stems, leaf stalks)
  - **Sclerenchyma** — rigid, dead at maturity (coconut husk, gritty bits of pear)
- Complex permanent plant tissues:
  - **Xylem** — transports water and minerals UPWARD (often dead cells, hollow tubes)
  - **Phloem** — transports food (sugars) to all parts (made of living cells)
- **Epidermis** — protective outer layer, often coated with waxy cuticle; gas exchange happens through stomata
- **Animal tissues** are four families:
  - **Epithelial** — coverings and linings (squamous, cuboidal, columnar, ciliated)
  - **Connective** — blood, bone, cartilage, adipose (fat), ligaments, tendons
  - **Muscular** — skeletal (voluntary), smooth (involuntary), cardiac (heart-only)
  - **Nervous** — neurons that carry electrical signals
- **Ligaments** connect bone to bone; **tendons** connect muscle to bone
- A **neuron** has a cell body, branched dendrites (receive signals), and a long axon (sends signals)
- Plants and animals have different tissues because plants are rooted + autotrophic while animals move + heterotrophic

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tissue | A group of cells of similar structure working together for a specific function |
| Meristem | Plant tissue made of actively dividing cells; drives growth |
| Parenchyma | Simple plant tissue with soft, thin-walled cells; stores food |
| Collenchyma | Simple plant tissue providing flexible support |
| Sclerenchyma | Simple plant tissue providing rigid strength; usually dead at maturity |
| Xylem | Complex plant tissue that transports water and minerals upward |
| Phloem | Complex plant tissue that transports food from leaves to other parts |
| Epithelial tissue | Animal tissue forming coverings and linings |
| Connective tissue | Animal tissue that connects, supports, or fills (includes blood, bone, cartilage) |
| Muscular tissue | Animal tissue specialised for contraction and movement |
| Nervous tissue | Animal tissue specialised for transmitting electrical signals |
| Neuron | A single nerve cell |
| Ligament | Connective tissue that joins bone to bone |
| Tendon | Connective tissue that joins muscle to bone |

## Remember These

- **Apical meristem grows length, lateral meristem grows girth, intercalary regrows**
- **Xylem carries water UP, phloem carries food EVERYWHERE**
- Wood of a tree is mostly **old xylem** (dead cells form the bulk of mature wood)
- **Blood is a connective tissue** — it has cells suspended in a liquid matrix
- The three muscle types: **skeletal (voluntary), smooth (involuntary), cardiac (heart only)**
- **Tendon → muscle to bone**; **Ligament → bone to bone**
