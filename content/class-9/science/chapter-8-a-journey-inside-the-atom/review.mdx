## Key Concepts

- All matter is made of tiny particles called **atoms**
- Ancient idea of "indivisible particles": Indian sage **Kanada** (paramanu) and Greek **Democritus** (atomos), c. 600 BCE
- **Dalton's atomic theory (1808)**: atoms are indivisible particles; atoms of an element are identical; atoms combine in fixed ratios to form compounds
- **J.J. Thomson (1897)** discovered the electron via gas discharge tubes; proposed the "plum pudding" atomic model
- **Rutherford's gold foil experiment (1911)**: showed the atom is mostly empty space with a tiny dense positive nucleus at the centre
- **Bohr (1913)**: electrons orbit in specific shells/energy levels around the nucleus
- **Chadwick (1932)**: discovered the neutron
- Three subatomic particles in every atom:
  - **Proton (+1)** — in nucleus, mass 1 unit
  - **Neutron (0)** — in nucleus, mass 1 unit
  - **Electron (−1)** — in shells around nucleus, mass ~1/1836 of a proton
- **Atomic number (Z)** = number of protons. Defines the element. Hydrogen Z=1, Carbon Z=6, Oxygen Z=8
- **Mass number (A)** = protons + neutrons. Number of neutrons = A − Z
- In a neutral atom: number of electrons = number of protons
- Element symbols: H, He, C, O, N, Na (sodium, from Latin natrium), Fe (iron, ferrum), Au (gold, aurum), Ag (silver, argentum)
- Electrons fill shells from inside out. Maximum per shell: **2n²** (n is the shell number)
- The outermost shell holds at most **8 electrons** (octet rule)
- **Valency** = number of electrons an atom gains, loses, or shares to achieve a stable outer shell
- **Noble gases** (He, Ne, Ar) already have full outer shells → valency = 0 → chemically inert
- **Isotopes** = atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons
- Example isotopes: ¹H, ²H (deuterium), ³H (tritium); ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C (radioactive, used in carbon dating)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Atom | The smallest particle of an element that retains its chemical properties |
| Nucleus | The dense centre of an atom containing protons and neutrons |
| Proton | Positively charged subatomic particle in the nucleus |
| Neutron | Neutral subatomic particle in the nucleus |
| Electron | Negatively charged subatomic particle orbiting the nucleus |
| Atomic number (Z) | Number of protons in the nucleus; defines the element |
| Mass number (A) | Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus |
| Shell | An allowed orbital region where electrons exist around the nucleus |
| Valency | The combining capacity of an atom; how many electrons it gains, loses, or shares |
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons |

## Remember These

- **Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus**; electrons are around it
- **Atomic number Z = number of protons** — never changes for a given element
- In a neutral atom, **# of electrons = # of protons**
- **Mass of an electron is about 1/1836 of a proton** (essentially negligible for the total mass of an atom)
- Maximum electrons in a shell = **2n²** (1st shell = 2, 2nd = 8, 3rd = 18, 4th = 32)
- Outermost shell holds at most 8 (octet rule)
- **Valency** = electrons needed to lose OR gain to complete outer shell, whichever is smaller
- **Noble gases have valency 0** because their outer shell is already full
- **Isotopes have same Z, different A** — same chemical behaviour, different mass
- ¹⁴C is the radioactive isotope used to date organic remains in archaeology
