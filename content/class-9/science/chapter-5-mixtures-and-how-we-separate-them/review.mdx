## Key Concepts

- A **mixture** is a combination of two or more substances physically combined; components can usually be separated again
- **Homogeneous mixtures (solutions)** have a uniform composition throughout (sugar water, air, brass)
- **Heterogeneous mixtures** have visibly different components (sand in water, oil and water, concrete)
- In a solution, the dissolving substance is the **solute**; the dissolving medium is the **solvent**
- **Saturated solution** = no more solute can dissolve at that temperature; **unsaturated** = can still dissolve more
- Solubility usually increases with temperature (hot water dissolves more sugar than cold)
- **Mass percentage of solute** = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × 100%
- **Suspensions** = heterogeneous mixtures with visible particles that settle over time (muddy water)
- **Colloids** = particles small enough not to settle but large enough to scatter light (milk, fog, smoke)
- **Tyndall effect** = scattering of a light beam by particles in a colloid or suspension. True solutions do NOT show the Tyndall effect
- Separation techniques:
  - **Filtration** → insoluble solid from liquid (tea leaves from chai)
  - **Evaporation** → dissolved solid from solution (salt from sea water)
  - **Crystallisation** → pure crystals of a solid from impure solution
  - **Distillation** → miscible liquids with different boiling points (water from ink)
  - **Fractional distillation** → multiple miscible liquids with close boiling points (crude oil refining)
  - **Sublimation** → sublimable solid from non-sublimable solid (ammonium chloride from salt)
  - **Separating funnel** → two immiscible liquids (oil from water)
  - **Chromatography** → coloured components of a mixture (separating dyes in ink)
  - **Centrifugation** → denser particles from liquid (cream from milk, plasma from blood)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Mixture | Two or more substances physically combined; components are separable |
| Solution | A homogeneous mixture of two or more substances |
| Solute | The substance that dissolves in a solution |
| Solvent | The substance that does the dissolving |
| Saturated solution | A solution that cannot dissolve more solute at the given temperature |
| Suspension | A heterogeneous mixture with visible particles that settle over time |
| Colloid | A mixture with particles small enough to stay suspended but large enough to scatter light |
| Tyndall effect | Scattering of a light beam by colloidal particles, making the beam visible |
| Distillation | Separating miscible liquids by boiling and condensing |
| Chromatography | Separating components based on differential movement through a medium |

## Remember These

- **A solution is homogeneous** (uniform) — a suspension is heterogeneous
- **Tyndall effect** shines in colloids and suspensions, NOT in true solutions
- **Filter paper traps solids that are larger than its pore size** — useless for dissolved solute
- **Crystallisation gives PURER crystals than simple evaporation** because impurities stay in the solution
- **Sea salt production** uses evaporation; **crude oil refining** uses fractional distillation
- Indian women have been using **separating funnels (in spirit)** for centuries — skimming oil off curry, separating ghee from milk solids, decanting clear water from settled mud
