## Scientific Concept of Work

In our everyday conversations, studying for an exam, cooking dinner, or even thinking hard feels like "work." But physics defines **work** in a very precise way.

**Work is done only when a force makes an object move along the direction in which the force acts.**

For work to happen, two things must occur together:
1. A **force** must be applied to the object
2. The object must **shift position** (undergo displacement) along the force's line of action

> **Key idea:** When force and displacement point the same way, the work done is positive. When they oppose each other (like friction slowing a sliding crate), the work is negative. If there is zero displacement, or if the force is at right angles to the displacement, absolutely no work is done.

### Illustrations

**Work IS done:**
- Aarav pushes a heavy almirah across the room (force and displacement both horizontal)
- Meena lifts a bucket of water from the well (force upward, displacement upward)
- A pair of oxen dragging a plough through a field (force in the direction the plough moves)

**Work is NOT done:**
- A watchman pushing against a locked iron gate that refuses to budge (no displacement)
- Priya carrying a pot of water on her head while walking along a flat road (the upward force she exerts is perpendicular to the horizontal displacement)
- The Moon orbiting Earth in a near-circular path (gravitational force points towards the centre, but displacement at each instant is perpendicular to it)

---

## Formula for Work

**W = F x s**

Where:
- W = work done
- F = applied force (in the direction of displacement)
- s = displacement of the object

- **SI unit of work:** Joule (J)
- **1 Joule** = 1 Newton x 1 metre = 1 N m
- When a force of 1 N shifts an object by 1 m in the force's direction, exactly 1 J of work is performed

**When the force is applied at an angle to the displacement:**

W = F x s x cos(angle)

- Angle = 0 degrees: W = Fs (full work, force and displacement aligned)
- Angle = 90 degrees: W = 0 (no work, force perpendicular to displacement)
- Angle = 180 degrees: W = -Fs (negative work, as when friction opposes sliding)

## Energy

**Energy** is what gives an object the ability to perform work. Any body possessing energy can push, pull, or otherwise act on another body and thereby do work on it.

- **SI unit of energy:** Joule (J)
- Energy and work share the same unit because they are deeply linked

> **Key idea:** Doing work is really a way of transferring energy. The body that exerts the force loses energy, while the body that gets displaced gains energy.

---

## Forms of Energy

### Kinetic Energy

**Kinetic energy** is the energy a body carries because it is **moving**.

**KE = (1/2) x m x v squared**

Where:
- m = mass of the body
- v = its speed

**Important observations:**
- A parked scooter has zero kinetic energy
- Kinetic energy grows with both mass and speed
- Because KE depends on the **square** of speed, doubling the speed makes the kinetic energy **four times** larger -- this is why speeding on Indian highways is so dangerous

**Example:** A flowing river, a spinning ceiling fan, a bowler's delivery in cricket -- all possess kinetic energy.

### Potential Energy

**Potential energy** is the energy a body stores because of its **position or arrangement**.

#### Gravitational Potential Energy

Any object raised above the ground holds gravitational potential energy, ready to be released if it falls.

**PE = m x g x h**

Where:
- m = mass of the object
- g = acceleration due to gravity (9.8 m/s squared)
- h = vertical height above the chosen reference level

**Example:** A ripe mango hanging high on a tree, water held behind the Tehri Dam in Uttarakhand, a roller coaster car paused at the summit of the track.

> **Key idea:** Potential energy depends on the reference level you pick. If you choose the ground as reference, PE at ground level is zero.

## Law of Conservation of Energy

**Energy can neither appear from nothing nor vanish into nothing. It only changes from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system always stays the same.**

![Energy transformation during free fall: potential energy converting to kinetic energy at different heights](energy-conservation-falling-object.png)

### Illustration: A Falling Coconut

When a coconut sits on the tree at height h:
- PE = mgh, KE = 0
- Total energy = mgh

As it falls and reaches some intermediate height h':
- PE = mgh', KE = (1/2)mv squared
- Total energy = PE + KE = mgh (unchanged)

The instant it reaches the ground:
- PE = 0, KE = (1/2)mv squared = mgh
- Total energy = mgh (still the same)

Potential energy steadily transforms into kinetic energy on the way down, but the **sum never changes**.

### Everyday Energy Transformations

| Situation | Energy Change |
|---|---|
| Wood burning in a chulha | Chemical energy to heat and light energy |
| LED bulb glowing | Electrical energy to light energy (with very little heat) |
| Plants making food through photosynthesis | Solar (light) energy to chemical energy |
| Bhakra Nangal Dam generating electricity | Gravitational PE to kinetic energy to electrical energy |
| Mobile phone battery powering the screen | Chemical energy to electrical energy to light energy |
| Solar panels on a rooftop in Jaipur | Light energy to electrical energy |

## Rate of Doing Work: Power

**Power** measures how quickly work gets done (or how rapidly energy is transferred).

**Power = Work done / Time taken**

**P = W / t**

- **SI unit of power:** Watt (W)
- **1 Watt** = 1 Joule per second = 1 J/s
- **1 kilowatt (kW)** = 1000 W

### Average Power

When work is done unevenly over several intervals:

**Average power = Total work done / Total time taken**

### Commercial Unit of Energy

Electricity boards across India charge households based on the **kilowatt hour (kWh)**.

**1 kWh = 1 kW x 1 hour = 1000 W x 3600 s = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 x 10 to the power 6 J**

One kWh is commonly called **1 unit** of electricity on your bijli bill.

> **Key idea:** Power indicates speed of work. A stronger motor finishes the same job in less time. The watt is named in honour of James Watt, the Scottish engineer who improved the steam engine.

## Key Terms to Remember

- **Work** -- Product of force and displacement in the direction of force
- **Energy** -- The ability to perform work
- **Kinetic energy** -- Energy arising from motion
- **Potential energy** -- Energy stored due to position or configuration
- **Law of conservation of energy** -- Total energy remains constant; it only transforms between forms
- **Power** -- How fast work is done (work per unit time)
- **Watt** -- SI unit of power (1 J/s)
- **Kilowatt hour (kWh)** -- Commercial unit of energy used in electricity billing (3.6 x 10^6 J)
