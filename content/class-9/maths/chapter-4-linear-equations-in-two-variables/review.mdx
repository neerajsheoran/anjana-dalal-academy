## Key Concepts

- A **linear equation in two variables** has the form **ax + by + c = 0** (a, b not both zero)
- "Linear" means every variable has power **1** only -- no x², xy, or √x
- A linear equation in one variable (ax + b = 0) is a special case where b-coefficient = 0
- A solution is an **ordered pair (x, y)** that satisfies the equation
- A linear equation in two variables has **infinitely many** solutions
- To find solutions: pick a value for one variable, solve for the other
- The **graph** of a linear equation in two variables is always a **straight line**
- Every point on the line is a solution; every solution is a point on the line
- To draw the graph: find at least **two** solutions, plot them, draw a line through them
- **y = mx** passes through the origin
- **x = a** (constant) is a **vertical line** parallel to y-axis
- **y = b** (constant) is a **horizontal line** parallel to x-axis
- The x-axis has equation **y = 0**; the y-axis has equation **x = 0**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Linear equation in two variables** | ax + by + c = 0 where a, b are not both zero |
| **Solution** | An ordered pair (x, y) that makes the equation true |
| **Graph of the equation** | The straight line formed by plotting all solutions |

## Remember These

- A linear equation in two variables has **infinitely many** solutions, not just one
- Two points are enough to draw the line, but a third point is a good cross-check
- x = 5 is a vertical line (not a point) -- it passes through (5, 0), (5, 1), (5, -3), etc.
- y = -3 is a horizontal line -- it passes through (0, -3), (2, -3), (-4, -3), etc.
- Do not confuse "linear equation in one variable" (unique solution) with "two variables" (infinite solutions)
