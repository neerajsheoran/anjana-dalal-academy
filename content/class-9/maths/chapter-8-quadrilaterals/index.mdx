## Introduction

A **quadrilateral** is any closed shape bounded by four straight sides, possessing four vertices and four interior angles. From the rectangular screen of your phone to the diamond-shaped kite flying over a maidan, quadrilaterals are everywhere. In this chapter, we focus on a particularly important family -- the **parallelogram** -- and examine its properties, the conditions that turn a general quadrilateral into a parallelogram, and a powerful result called the **Mid-Point Theorem**.

---

## Properties of a Parallelogram

A **parallelogram** is a quadrilateral whose opposite sides run parallel to each other.

### Theorem 8.1: A diagonal splits a parallelogram into two congruent triangles

Draw diagonal PR in parallelogram PQRS. Because PQ || SR and PS || QR, the alternate interior angles formed with the diagonal are equal. Combined with the shared side PR, we get triangle PQR congruent to triangle RSP by ASA.

![Parallelogram ABCD with diagonal AC drawn, showing the two congruent triangles formed and alternate interior angles marked](parallelogram-diagonal-congruent-triangles.png)

### Theorem 8.2: Opposite sides are equal

> **Key idea:** In every parallelogram, **opposite sides are equal in length**. (PQ = SR and PS = QR)

### Theorem 8.3 (Converse): Equal opposite sides guarantee a parallelogram

If both pairs of opposite sides of a quadrilateral are equal, the quadrilateral must be a parallelogram.

### Theorem 8.4: Opposite angles are equal

In a parallelogram, **opposite angles are equal**. (angle P = angle R and angle Q = angle S)

### Theorem 8.5 (Converse): Equal opposite angles guarantee a parallelogram

### Theorem 8.6: Diagonals bisect each other

The diagonals of a parallelogram **cut each other into equal halves**. If diagonals PR and QS meet at point O, then PO = OR and QO = OS.

### Theorem 8.7 (Converse): Mutually bisecting diagonals guarantee a parallelogram

> **Key idea:** A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if **any one** of the following holds: (1) both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, (2) both pairs of opposite sides are equal, (3) both pairs of opposite angles are equal, (4) diagonals bisect each other, (5) one pair of opposite sides is both equal and parallel.

## Special Parallelograms

### Rectangle

A **rectangle** is a parallelogram with at least one right angle. Since adjacent angles in a parallelogram are supplementary and opposite angles are equal, one right angle forces **all four angles to be 90 degrees**.

Special diagonal property: the diagonals of a rectangle are **equal in length**.

### Rhombus

A **rhombus** is a parallelogram with all four sides of equal length. Think of a tilted square.

Special diagonal property: the diagonals of a rhombus are **perpendicular** to each other.

### Square

A **square** is a parallelogram that qualifies as both a rectangle and a rhombus. All sides are equal and all angles are 90 degrees. Its diagonals are equal in length, bisect each other, and cross at right angles.

## The Mid-Point Theorem

> **Theorem 8.9 (Mid-Point Theorem):** The segment connecting the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is **parallel** to the third side and measures **half** its length.

If M and N are the midpoints of sides AB and AC in triangle ABC, then:

![Triangle ABC with midpoints D and E on sides AB and AC, showing DE parallel to BC and DE = half of BC](mid-point-theorem-diagram.png)

* MN is parallel to BC
* MN = (1/2) BC

> **Theorem 8.10 (Converse):** A line drawn through the midpoint of one side of a triangle, running parallel to a second side, will bisect the third side.

If M is the midpoint of AB and a line through M runs parallel to BC, that line meets AC at its midpoint.

**Illustration:** In triangle PQR, if X and Y are midpoints of PQ and PR respectively, and QR = 14 cm, then XY = 7 cm and XY || QR.

## Summary

1. A **parallelogram** has opposite sides that are both parallel and equal, opposite angles that are equal, and diagonals that bisect one another.
2. Each diagonal divides a parallelogram into two congruent triangles.
3. A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if: opposite sides are equal, or opposite angles are equal, or diagonals bisect each other, or one pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel.
4. **Rectangle:** Every angle is 90 degrees; diagonals are equal.
5. **Rhombus:** All sides are equal; diagonals are perpendicular.
6. **Square:** All sides equal and all angles 90 degrees; diagonals are equal and perpendicular.
7. **Mid-Point Theorem:** The segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to and half the length of the third side.
