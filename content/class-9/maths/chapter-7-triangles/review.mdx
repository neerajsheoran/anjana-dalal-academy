## Key Concepts

- Two triangles are **congruent** if all corresponding sides and angles are equal
- The **order of vertices** matters: △ABC ≅ △DEF means A↔D, B↔E, C↔F
- **CPCT** (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles) is used after proving congruence
- **Five congruence criteria:** SAS, ASA, AAS, SSS, RHS
- **SAS:** Two sides and the **included** angle are equal
- **ASA:** Two angles and the **included** side are equal
- **AAS:** Two angles and a **non-included** side are equal
- **SSS:** All three sides are equal
- **RHS:** Right angle + hypotenuse + one side (for right triangles only)
- **SSA is NOT a valid** congruence criterion
- **Isosceles triangle theorem:** Equal sides → equal base angles (opposite to equal sides)
- **Converse:** Equal angles → equal sides opposite to them
- In an **equilateral triangle**, each angle = **60°**
- The side opposite the **larger angle** is longer; the angle opposite the **longer side** is larger
- **Triangle inequality:** Sum of any two sides > third side

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Congruent triangles** | Triangles with identical shape and size |
| **CPCT** | Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are equal |
| **Isosceles triangle** | A triangle with at least two equal sides |
| **Equilateral triangle** | A triangle with all three sides equal |
| **Triangle inequality** | Sum of any two sides is always greater than the third side |

## Congruence Rules at a Glance

| Rule | What You Need |
|------|---------------|
| SAS | 2 sides + included angle |
| ASA | 2 angles + included side |
| AAS | 2 angles + any one side |
| SSS | All 3 sides |
| RHS | Right angle + hypotenuse + one side |

## Remember These

- **SSA does not work** -- two sides and a non-included angle can give different triangles
- In SAS, the angle **must be between** the two sides -- a very common exam error
- To check if three lengths form a triangle: verify that the **smallest two sides sum > largest side**
- RHS applies **only** to right-angled triangles -- do not use it for other triangles
- After proving congruence, always write CPCT to justify that specific parts are equal
