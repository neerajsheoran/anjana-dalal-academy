## Introduction

You already know the basic triangle area rule: **Area = (1/2) x base x height**. It works perfectly when you can measure the height. But what if you are standing at a triangular plot of farmland near Nashik and all you have is a measuring tape? You can easily walk along the three sides and record their lengths, but measuring the perpendicular height on uneven ground is impractical. **Heron's formula** solves this problem -- it computes the area using nothing but the three side lengths.

**Heron** (sometimes called **Hero**) was a mathematician and inventor from Alexandria, Egypt, who lived around 10 CE -- 75 CE. Beyond this formula, he is remembered for designing one of the earliest known steam-powered devices.

---

## The Formula

For a triangle whose sides measure **a**, **b**, and **c**, first compute the **semi-perimeter**:

**s = (a + b + c) / 2**

Then the area is:

**Area = sqrt( s (s - a) (s - b) (s - c) )**

![Scalene triangle with sides a, b, c labeled and the semi-perimeter formula s = (a+b+c)/2 shown](herons-formula-triangle-labeled.png)

> **Why it matters:** Heron's formula shines for scalene triangles where no obvious base-height pair is available. It also has a built-in validity check: if any of (s - a), (s - b), or (s - c) turns out negative, the three lengths cannot form a real triangle.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: A right-angled triangle

A triangular garden has sides 9 m, 40 m, and 41 m. Find its area.

s = (9 + 40 + 41) / 2 = 90 / 2 = 45 m

s - a = 45 - 9 = 36, s - b = 45 - 40 = 5, s - c = 45 - 41 = 4

Area = sqrt(45 x 36 x 5 x 4) = sqrt(32400) = **180 m²**

Cross-check: 9² + 40² = 81 + 1600 = 1681 = 41², so this is a right triangle with legs 9 and 40. Area = (1/2) x 9 x 40 = 180 m². The answers agree.

### Example 2: An equilateral triangle

A rangoli border is shaped as an equilateral triangle with each side 12 cm. Find the area.

s = (12 + 12 + 12) / 2 = 18 cm

Area = sqrt(18 x 6 x 6 x 6) = sqrt(3888) = 36sqrt(3) cm²

> **Shortcut for equilateral triangles:** Area = (sqrt(3) / 4) x a². For a = 12: Area = (sqrt(3) / 4) x 144 = 36sqrt(3) cm². Memorise this -- it saves valuable exam time.

### Example 3: Two sides and the perimeter known

A triangle has sides 9 cm and 14 cm, and its perimeter is 36 cm. Find the area.

Third side = 36 - 9 - 14 = 13 cm

s = 36 / 2 = 18 cm

Area = sqrt(18 x 9 x 4 x 5) = sqrt(3240) = 18sqrt(10) cm²

### Example 4: Sides in a given ratio

The boundary of a triangular park has sides in the ratio 5 : 7 : 8 and a perimeter of 200 m. Find its area.

5k + 7k + 8k = 200 => 20k = 200 => k = 10

Sides: 50 m, 70 m, 80 m

s = 200 / 2 = 100 m

Area = sqrt(100 x 50 x 30 x 20) = sqrt(3000000) = 1000sqrt(3) m²

## Extending to Quadrilaterals

Any quadrilateral can be split into two triangles by drawing a diagonal. Compute each triangle's area with Heron's formula and add them together.

![Quadrilateral ABCD divided into two triangles by diagonal AC, with all side lengths labeled](quadrilateral-divided-by-diagonal.png)

**Example:** A plot PQRS has sides PQ = 7 m, QR = 24 m, RS = 20 m, SP = 15 m, and diagonal PR = 25 m.

**Triangle PQR:** sides 7, 24, 25. s = 28.
Area = sqrt(28 x 21 x 4 x 3) = sqrt(7056) = 84 m²

**Triangle PRS:** sides 25, 20, 15. s = 30.
Area = sqrt(30 x 5 x 10 x 15) = sqrt(22500) = 150 m²

Total area of PQRS = 84 + 150 = **234 m²**

> **General principle:** Any polygon can be triangulated using diagonals. A pentagon splits into three triangles, a hexagon into four, and so on. Land surveyors across India routinely use this technique -- measure the sides and one or more diagonals, then compute areas triangle by triangle.

---

## Summary

1. **Heron's formula:** Area = sqrt(s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)), where s = (a + b + c) / 2.
2. The formula needs only side lengths -- no height measurement required.
3. For an equilateral triangle with side a: Area = (sqrt(3) / 4) x a².
4. For quadrilaterals and larger polygons, divide into triangles using diagonals and apply the formula to each piece.
