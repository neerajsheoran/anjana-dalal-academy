## Key Concepts

- The **Cartesian plane** is formed by two perpendicular number lines: the **x-axis** (horizontal) and the **y-axis** (vertical)
- The point where the axes meet is the **origin O (0, 0)**
- The axes divide the plane into **four quadrants** numbered I to IV (anticlockwise from upper-right)
- Every point is written as an **ordered pair (x, y)** -- x-coordinate always comes first
- **x-coordinate (abscissa):** horizontal distance from the y-axis
- **y-coordinate (ordinate):** vertical distance from the x-axis
- Points on the x-axis have the form **(x, 0)**; points on the y-axis have the form **(0, y)**
- **(x, y) ≠ (y, x)** unless x = y -- order matters
- To plot (a, b): start at origin, move a units horizontally, then b units vertically
- To read coordinates: drop perpendiculars to both axes from the point

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cartesian plane** | The plane formed by x-axis and y-axis together |
| **Origin** | The intersection point of the axes, coordinates (0, 0) |
| **Abscissa** | The x-coordinate of a point |
| **Ordinate** | The y-coordinate of a point |
| **Quadrant** | One of four regions created by the axes |

## Quadrant Sign Convention

| Quadrant | x | y | Sign |
|----------|---|---|------|
| I | + | + | (+, +) |
| II | - | + | (-, +) |
| III | - | - | (-, -) |
| IV | + | - | (+, -) |

## Remember These

- The origin (0, 0) does not belong to any quadrant -- it lies on both axes
- Points on the axes do not belong to any quadrant either
- (3, 5) and (5, 3) are **different** points -- a very common exam mistake
- Quadrants go **anticlockwise**: I (upper-right), II (upper-left), III (lower-left), IV (lower-right)
