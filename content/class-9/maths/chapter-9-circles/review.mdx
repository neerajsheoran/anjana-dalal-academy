## Key Concepts

- A **circle** is the set of all points equidistant from a fixed centre point
- **Radius** = distance from centre to circumference; **Diameter** = 2r (longest chord)
- A **chord** is a line segment with both endpoints on the circle
- **Arc:** curved part of circumference; a chord creates a **major arc** and a **minor arc**
- **Segment:** region between a chord and its arc (major and minor segments)
- **Sector:** region between two radii and the arc between them (pizza-slice shape)
- Equal chords subtend **equal angles** at the centre (and vice versa)
- A **perpendicular from the centre** to a chord **bisects** the chord
- A line from the centre to the **midpoint** of a chord is **perpendicular** to the chord
- **Equal chords** are **equidistant** from the centre (and vice versa)
- The **central angle** is **twice** the inscribed angle for the same arc
- All angles in the **same segment** are equal
- Angle in a **semicircle** = **90°**
- In a **cyclic quadrilateral**, opposite angles are **supplementary** (sum = 180°)
- If opposite angles of a quadrilateral sum to 180°, it is cyclic

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Chord** | Line segment with both endpoints on the circle |
| **Arc** | A continuous curved portion of the circumference |
| **Segment** | Region between a chord and its arc |
| **Sector** | Region between two radii and the included arc |
| **Central angle** | Angle subtended by a chord/arc at the centre |
| **Inscribed angle** | Angle subtended by a chord/arc at a point on the circumference |
| **Cyclic quadrilateral** | A quadrilateral with all four vertices on a circle |
| **Concyclic points** | Points that all lie on a single circle |

## Remember These

- Diameter is the **longest chord** -- this fact is often tested
- Central angle = **2 ×** inscribed angle -- the most important theorem in this chapter
- Angle in a semicircle is always **90°** -- useful shortcut in constructions and proofs
- For cyclic quadrilaterals: opposite angles sum to 180° -- if they do not, the quadrilateral is **not** cyclic
- The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord -- and the converse is also true (both directions are tested)
