## Introduction

Walk into any Indian kitchen and you will spot 3D shapes everywhere: a cylindrical steel dabba for storing dal, a conical funnel for pouring oil, a spherical ladoo on a plate. Knowing how to calculate surface areas and volumes of such objects is not just classroom mathematics -- it helps engineers design water tanks, architects plan domes, and sweet-shop owners figure out packaging. In this chapter, we study formulas for **cones**, **spheres**, and **hemispheres**, while revising **cylinders** from earlier classes.

---

## Surface Area of a Right Circular Cone

A **right circular cone** is the shape you get when you spin a right triangle around one of its perpendicular sides. Its key measurements are:

* **r** = radius of the circular base
* **h** = vertical height (centre of base to tip)
* **l** = slant height (edge of base circle to tip, measured along the surface)

These three are connected by the Pythagoras theorem: **l² = r² + h²**, giving **l = sqrt(r² + h²)**.

![Right circular cone with radius r, height h, and slant height l labeled, showing the right triangle relationship](cone-dimensions-labeled.png)

**Curved (Lateral) Surface Area = pi x r x l**

**Total Surface Area = pi x r x l + pi x r² = pi x r (l + r)**

> **Where does CSA = pi r l come from?** If you slit a cone along its slant height and flatten it, you get a sector of a circle whose radius is l and whose arc length equals the base circumference 2 pi r. The sector area works out to (1/2) x arc x radius = (1/2) x 2 pi r x l = pi r l.

**Example:** A conical tent has height 24 m and base radius 7 m. How much canvas is needed for its curved surface?

l = sqrt(24² + 7²) = sqrt(576 + 49) = sqrt(625) = 25 m

CSA = (22/7) x 7 x 25 = 550 m²

## Surface Area of a Sphere

A **sphere** is the collection of all points in space that are the same distance (the radius) from a fixed centre point. Think of a cricket ball or a globe.

![Sphere and hemisphere with radius labeled, showing the curved surface and the flat circular base of the hemisphere](sphere-hemisphere-diagram.png)

**Surface Area of a Sphere = 4 pi r²**

**Curved Surface Area of a Hemisphere = 2 pi r²**

**Total Surface Area of a Hemisphere = 3 pi r²** (dome + flat circular base)

> **Archimedes' insight:** The surface area of a sphere exactly equals the curved surface area of the cylinder that snugly wraps around it (same radius, height = diameter). Archimedes regarded this as his finest discovery -- he even had a sphere-inside-a-cylinder engraved on his tombstone.

**Example:** Find the surface area of a lemon modelled as a sphere of radius 3.5 cm (use pi = 22/7).

Surface area = 4 x (22/7) x 3.5² = 4 x (22/7) x 12.25 = 4 x 22 x 1.75 = 154 cm²

## Volume of a Right Circular Cone

**Volume = (1/3) x pi x r² x h**

A cone holds exactly **one-third** the volume of a cylinder with the identical base and height. You can verify this at home: fill a conical paper cup with water and pour it into a matching cylinder -- it takes precisely three cups to fill the cylinder.

![Three cones fitting inside a cylinder with the same base and height, illustrating the 1/3 volume relationship](cone-cylinder-volume-relationship.png)

**Example:** A conical vessel has radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find its capacity (use pi = 22/7).

Volume = (1/3) x (22/7) x 25 x 12 = (1/3) x (6600/7) = 6600/21 ≈ 314.3 cm³

---

## Volume of a Sphere

**Volume of a Sphere = (4/3) x pi x r³**

**Volume of a Hemisphere = (2/3) x pi x r³**

**Example:** A decorative brass sphere has diameter 42 cm. Find its volume (use pi = 22/7).

Radius = 21 cm

Volume = (4/3) x (22/7) x 21³ = (4/3) x (22/7) x 9261 = 38808 cm³

## Cylinder Formulas (Revision)

**Curved Surface Area = 2 pi r h**

**Total Surface Area = 2 pi r (h + r)**

**Volume = pi r² h**

---

## Formula Reference Table

| Solid          | Curved / Lateral SA     | Total SA           | Volume             |
| -------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| **Cylinder**   | 2 pi r h                | 2 pi r (h + r)     | pi r² h            |
| **Cone**       | pi r l                  | pi r (l + r)       | (1/3) pi r² h      |
| **Sphere**     | 4 pi r²                 | 4 pi r²            | (4/3) pi r³        |
| **Hemisphere** | 2 pi r²                 | 3 pi r²            | (2/3) pi r³        |

Where l = sqrt(r² + h²) for the cone.

---

## Summary

1. **Cone:** CSA = pi r l, TSA = pi r (l + r), Volume = (1/3) pi r² h, with l = sqrt(r² + h²).
2. **Sphere:** Surface Area = 4 pi r², Volume = (4/3) pi r³.
3. **Hemisphere:** CSA = 2 pi r², TSA = 3 pi r², Volume = (2/3) pi r³.
4. A cone's volume is one-third of the cylinder with the same base and height.
5. A sphere's volume equals two-thirds of the circumscribing cylinder (where h = 2r).
