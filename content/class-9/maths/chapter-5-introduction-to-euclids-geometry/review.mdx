## Key Concepts

- **Euclid** (325-265 BCE) organised geometry into a logical framework in his work **Elements** (13 volumes)
- In modern geometry, **point**, **line**, and **plane** are accepted as **undefined terms**
- **Axioms** (common notions) are universal self-evident truths used across all of mathematics
- **Postulates** are assumptions specific to geometry
- **Theorems** are statements proved logically from axioms, postulates, and definitions
- Euclid listed **7 axioms** and **5 postulates**
- A solid has 3 dimensions → surface has 2 → line has 1 → point has 0
- **Postulate 1:** A straight line can be drawn from any point to any other point (unique line through 2 points)
- **Postulate 2:** A line segment can be extended indefinitely in both directions
- **Postulate 3:** A circle can be drawn with any centre and any radius
- **Postulate 4:** All right angles are equal
- **Postulate 5 (Parallel Postulate):** If interior angles on one side of a transversal sum to less than 180°, the lines meet on that side
- Two distinct lines can have **at most one** point in common
- A line segment has exactly **one** midpoint

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Axiom** | A universal self-evident truth accepted without proof |
| **Postulate** | A geometry-specific assumption accepted without proof |
| **Theorem** | A statement proved using definitions, axioms, and postulates |
| **Undefined terms** | Point, line, and plane -- accepted without formal definition |
| **Corollary** | A result that follows directly from a theorem |

## Euclid's 7 Axioms (Summary)

| # | Axiom |
|---|-------|
| 1 | Things equal to the same thing are equal to each other |
| 2 | Equals added to equals give equals |
| 3 | Equals subtracted from equals give equals |
| 4 | Things that coincide are equal (superposition) |
| 5 | The whole is greater than the part |
| 6 | Doubles of the same thing are equal |
| 7 | Halves of the same thing are equal |

## Remember These

- **Axiom vs Postulate:** axioms are universal, postulates are geometry-specific -- exams often test this distinction
- The 5th postulate is about **parallel lines** -- if interior angles on one side = 180°, the lines never meet (they are parallel)
- Two distinct lines cannot share **more than one** common point -- proved by contradiction using Postulate 1
- Euclid's definitions (like "a point has no part") are considered incomplete today; modern math uses undefined terms instead
