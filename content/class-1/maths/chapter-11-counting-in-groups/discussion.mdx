## Counting in Groups (Repeated Addition)

**Child:** Maa, today teacher said we can count faster by putting things in groups. But I like counting one by one!

**Mother:** One by one is fine, beta, but groups are so much quicker! Let me show you. How many eyes do your 3 teddy bears have?

**Child:** Let me count... 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Six eyes!

**Mother:** Now try it with groups. Each teddy has 2 eyes. So we say 2 + 2 + 2 = 6. Same answer, but much faster, right?

**Child:** Oh yes! I did not have to count one by one!

**Mother:** That is the power of **groups**. When every group has the same number, just keep adding that number. Easy!

**Child:** Like chapatis, Maa! If you make 3 chapatis for each person and there are 4 of us, that is 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12 chapatis!

**Mother:** Wah, beta! You are already thinking in groups!

## Understanding Times

**Child:** Maa, teacher used a new word today — "times". She said 3 times 4 is 12. What does "times" mean?

**Mother:** "Times" just tells you how many groups to make. When teacher says 3 times 4, she means — make 3 groups, each with 4 things. Then add them up.

**Child:** So 3 times 4 means 4 + 4 + 4?

**Mother:** Bilkul sahi! And what is 4 + 4 + 4?

**Child:** 12!

**Mother:** Perfect. Let us try another one. 2 times 5?

**Child:** That means 2 groups of 5. So 5 + 5 = 10!

**Mother:** And 4 times 2?

**Child:** 4 groups of 2. So 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8!

**Mother:** You have got it, beta! "Times" is just a short way to say repeated addition.

## Counting Things in Groups Around Us

**Child:** Maa, are there groups of things in our house too?

**Mother:** Oh, everywhere! Look at our dining table. How many legs does it have?

**Child:** 4 legs.

**Mother:** And the 3 chairs around it?

**Child:** Each chair also has 4 legs. So that is 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 chair legs!

**Mother:** Add the table legs too. 12 + 4 = 16 legs in total! Now look at Papa's shoe rack. Each pair of shoes has 2 shoes. If there are 5 pairs, how many shoes?

**Child:** 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10 shoes!

**Mother:** Now count the wheels on the bicycles parked outside our building.

**Child:** Each cycle has 2 wheels. I can see 4 cycles. So 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8 wheels!

**Mother:** See? Once you start looking for groups, they are everywhere — in the kitchen, on the road, at school. Maths is all around us, beta!

**Child:** I will look for groups everywhere now, Maa!
