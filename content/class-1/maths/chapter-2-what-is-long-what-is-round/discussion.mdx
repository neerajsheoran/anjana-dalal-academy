## Discovering Shapes Around Us

**Child:** Maa, today teacher asked us to find long things and round things. But some things look both long AND round! I got confused.

**Mother:** That is a great observation, beta! Let us start with the easy ones first. Look at the rope hanging on the wall — what shape is it?

**Child:** It is long!

**Mother:** Yes! And look at the roti on the tawa — what shape is it?

**Child:** It is round, like a circle!

**Mother:** Perfect! **Long** things are stretched out in one direction, like a rope or a ruler. **Round** things are shaped like a circle, like a roti or a bangle.

## Learning the Four Shapes

**Child:** Maa, teacher also taught us some shape names. Like "sphere" and "cuboid." But I keep forgetting which is which!

**Mother:** Let me make it easy. Pick up that cricket ball — feel it. It is round from every single side. That shape is called a **sphere**. Now pick up the soap bar — it has flat sides, like a box. That is a **cuboid**.

**Child:** Oh! So my tiffin box is also a cuboid?

**Mother:** Exactly! Now look at the steel glass — it is round like a tube. That shape is called a **cylinder**. And that mehendi cone Dadi uses — it is round at the bottom and pointed at the top. That shape is called a **cone**.

**Child:** So the kulfi man's kulfi cone is also a cone shape! I love kulfi!

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! See, maths is everywhere — even in your favourite treats!

## Rolling and Sliding

**Child:** Maa, teacher showed us that some things roll and some things slide. Why is that?

**Mother:** Good question! Think about it — when you push a cricket ball on the floor, what happens?

**Child:** It rolls away fast!

**Mother:** Yes! Round things roll because they have curved surfaces. Now push your notebook on the table. Does it roll?

**Child:** No, it just slides!

**Mother:** Exactly! Flat things slide because their surface is smooth and flat. They cannot turn over and over like a ball.

**Child:** What about a coin, Maa? It is flat but also round!

**Mother:** Very clever, beta! A coin can do BOTH. Stand it on its edge and it rolls. Lay it flat and it slides. Some objects can do both because they have both round and flat parts.

## Building with Shapes

**Child:** Maa, can we build something with shapes? Teacher said box shapes are best for towers.

**Mother:** Let us try! Stack your lunch box, then put the pencil box on top, then the eraser on top of that. See — they sit nicely!

**Child:** It is like a tower! But when I put a ball on top, it rolls off!

**Mother:** That is because the ball is a sphere — it has no flat surface to sit on. Cuboids have flat tops, so they can be stacked. That is why buildings and houses use box-like bricks, not round balls!

**Child:** Maa, real builders are also using maths!

**Mother:** Absolutely, beta! Shapes are one of the most useful things in maths.
