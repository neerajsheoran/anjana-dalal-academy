## Meet Lina's Family

Lina lives in a small town nestled in the hills of Meghalaya. She stays with her **grandparents**, **parents**, and her little brother **Rit**.

Her uncles, aunts, and cousins live close by. They often gather for big meals and celebrations!

Lina stands on a wooden stool to look taller. But her Baba (grandfather) is still the **tallest** in the family, and tiny Rit is the **shortest**.

> **Think about it:** Who is the tallest person in your family? Who is the shortest?

---

## Tall and Short

We can compare how high people and things are.

- **Tall** means high up. A coconut tree is tall. A giraffe is tall.
- **Short** means not so high. A puppy is short. A mushroom is short.
- **Taller** means more tall than something else.
- **Shorter** means less tall than something else.

**Example:** Lina's Papa is **taller** than her Maa. But Baba is the **tallest** of everyone.

> **Did you know?** The **Qutub Minar** in Delhi is one of the tallest old towers in India! It was built hundreds of years ago.

---

## Long and Short

Things also have **length** — how stretched out they are. Some things are **long** and some are **short**.

- A saree is **long**. A handkerchief is **short**.
- A python is **longer** than a garden lizard.
- A highway is **longer** than a gali (lane).

| Longer        | Shorter       |
| ------------- | ------------- |
| A train       | A bike        |
| A river       | A puddle      |
| A broomstick  | A matchstick  |
| A garden hose | A drinking straw |

---

## Thick and Thin

Some things are **thick** (wide) and some are **thin** (narrow).

- A banyan tree trunk is **thick**. A bamboo stick is **thin**.
- A Ramayana book is **thick**. A pamphlet is **thin**.
- A ship's anchor rope is **thick**. A kite string is **thin**.

---

## Near and Far

Lina's house is **near** the chai stall but **far** from the school.

- **Near** means close by. Things you can touch without walking much are near.
- **Far** means a long way away. Things you need to travel to reach are far.

> **Think about it:** Is your home near or far from the nearest park?

---

## Measuring Length with Handspans

How can we find out how long something is? One simple way is to use our **handspan**.

A **handspan** is the distance from the tip of your thumb to the tip of your little finger when you stretch your hand wide open.

**Example:** Nani measured a shawl using her handspan. The shawl is about **6 handspans** long.

You can measure many things with your handspan:

| Object          | About How Many Handspans? |
| --------------- | ------------------------- |
| Your desk       | Try it!                   |
| A newspaper     | Try it!                   |
| Your pillow     | Try it!                   |
| Your leg        | Try it!                   |

> **Note:** For tiny things (like a key or an eraser), a handspan is too big. Use **finger widths** instead!

---

## Measuring with Footspans

For bigger distances, we use our **footspan**. A footspan is the length of one foot, from heel to toe.

You can measure:
- How far your bed is from the door.
- The length of the verandah.
- The width of the classroom.

> **Fun fact:** Your footspan and your Dadi's footspan are different sizes. So you may count different numbers for the same distance!

---

## Heavy and Light

Some things are **heavy** (hard to pick up) and some are **light** (easy to pick up).

- A **pumpkin** is heavy. A **tomato** is light.
- A **schoolbag full of books** is heavy. An **empty jhola** (cloth bag) is light.
- A **steel bucket full of water** is heavy. A **paper cup** is light.

| Heavy Things       | Light Things   |
| ------------------- | -------------- |
| Sack of wheat       | Cotton ball    |
| Iron kadhai         | Plastic plate  |
| Wooden chair        | Rubber ball    |
| Bag of potatoes     | Handkerchief   |

> **Think about it:** Who is heavier — you or your friend? You can find out using a **jhula** (see-saw) or a **tarazu** (weighing balance).

---

## How Much Water?

Different containers can hold different amounts of water. This is called **capacity**.

- A **balti** (bucket) holds more water than a **lota** (small pot).
- A **lota** holds more water than a **glass**.
- A **glass** holds more water than a **spoon**.

You can fill a balti using glasses of water:

| Container    | About How Many Glasses? |
| ------------ | ----------------------- |
| A lota       | About 2-3 glasses       |
| A bottle     | About 3-4 glasses       |
| A balti      | Many, many glasses!     |

> **Important:** Water is precious! We must not waste it.
> - Use a **balti** for bathing, not a running shower.
> - Always close the **tap** tightly after using.
> - Give leftover drinking water to **plants**.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word            | Meaning                                                               |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Tall / Short**  | How high something stands                                           |
| **Long / Short**  | How stretched out something is                                      |
| **Thick / Thin**  | How wide something is from side to side                             |
| **Near / Far**    | How close or distant something is                                   |
| **Heavy / Light** | How much something weighs — heavy weighs more, light weighs less    |
| **Handspan**      | The distance across your open hand, from thumb tip to pinky tip     |
| **Footspan**      | The length of your foot, used to measure bigger distances           |
| **Capacity**      | How much liquid a container can hold                                |
