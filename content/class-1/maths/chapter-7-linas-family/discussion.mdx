## Tall, Short, Long — A Chat with Maa

**Child:** Maa, today teacher taught us about tall, short, long, and all kinds of comparing words. But how is "tall" different from "long"?

**Mother:** Great question, beta! **Tall** is for things that stand up high — like a person, a tree, or a building. **Long** is for things that stretch out — like a rope, a train, or a snake.

**Child:** Oh! So Papa is TALL, but his belt is LONG?

**Mother:** Exactly! A coconut tree is tall because it stands up. But a river is long because it stretches far across the land.

**Child:** That makes sense, Maa!

## Thick, Thin, Heavy, Light

**Child:** Maa, what about thick and thin? Teacher showed us a big book and a thin pamphlet.

**Mother:** **Thick** means wide or fat from side to side. A dictionary is thick — it has so many pages! And **thin** means narrow. A kite string is very thin.

**Child:** Like the banyan tree near our park! Its trunk is SO thick. But the bamboo sticks are thin.

**Mother:** Perfect example! Now, do you know the difference between heavy and light?

**Child:** Heavy means it is hard to pick up, right? Like the bag of potatoes Nana carries!

**Mother:** Yes! A bag of potatoes is heavy. But a cotton ball is light — you can blow it off your hand! To find out which is heavier, we use a **tarazu** (balance scale) or a **jhula** (see-saw). The heavier side goes down and the lighter side goes up.

**Child:** Like when I sit on the see-saw with my little cousin — my side goes DOWN!

**Mother:** Ha ha, because you are heavier than your cousin!

## Measuring with Handspans and Footspans

**Child:** Maa, teacher asked us to measure our desk using our hands. But my answer was different from my friend's!

**Mother:** That is because your hands are different sizes! A **handspan** is the distance from your thumb tip to your pinky tip when you stretch your hand wide. A bigger hand means a bigger handspan.

**Child:** So if my hand is smaller, I need more handspans to cover the same desk?

**Mother:** Exactly! And for bigger distances, like the length of a room, we use our **footspan** — the length of our foot. But again, everyone's feet are different sizes, so answers may differ.

**Child:** That is why we need rulers and measuring tapes, right? So everyone gets the same answer?

**Mother:** Very smart thinking, beta! You will learn about those soon. For now, handspans and footspans are great for practice.

## Near and Far

**Child:** Maa, teacher said Lina's house is near the chai stall but far from school. Is our house near or far from my school?

**Mother:** What do you think? When Papa drops you by scooter, how long does it take?

**Child:** About 10 minutes!

**Mother:** And the grocery shop next to our building?

**Child:** Just one minute walking!

**Mother:** So the shop is **near** and your school is **far**. **Near** means close by, and **far** means you need to travel a longer distance to reach there.

**Child:** Our house is near the park too! I can see the slide from our balcony!

**Mother:** Yes, if you can see something from where you stand, it is usually near!

## How Much Water? — Capacity

**Child:** Maa, teacher taught us about "capacity" today. She said a bucket holds more water than a glass. But how much more?

**Mother:** Let us try! Take this glass and fill the balti using it. Count how many glasses it takes.

**Child:** One... two... three... oh Maa, this will take forever!

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! That is because the balti has a very big capacity — it can hold MANY glasses of water. A lota holds about 2-3 glasses, a bottle holds about 3-4 glasses, and a balti holds many many more.

**Child:** So a spoon has the smallest capacity?

**Mother:** Among the things we use at home, yes! A spoon, then a glass, then a lota, then a bottle, then a balti — from smallest to biggest.

**Child:** Maa, teacher also said we should not waste water.

**Mother:** She is absolutely right! Water is precious. We should use a balti for bathing instead of a shower, close the tap after washing hands, and give leftover water to our plants.

**Child:** I will remember that, Maa!
