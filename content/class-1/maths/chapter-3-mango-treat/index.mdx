## The Parrot Party

A big guava tree stands in Dadi's garden. One green parrot lands on a branch to eat guavas.

Soon another parrot flies in. Now there are **2** parrots munching together.

One more joins — now **3**! Then **4**, then **5**, then **6**, then **7**, then **8**!

Finally, one last parrot arrives. Now **9** parrots are having a guava party on the tree!

> **Notice:** Each time one more parrot came, the number went up by one. This is called **counting**!

## Counting from 1 to 9

When we count, we say numbers in a special order. Each number is **one more** than the number that comes before it.

| Number | Word  | One More Makes |
| ------ | ----- | -------------- |
| 1      | One   | 2              |
| 2      | Two   | 3              |
| 3      | Three | 4              |
| 4      | Four  | 5              |
| 5      | Five  | 6              |
| 6      | Six   | 7              |
| 7      | Seven | 8              |
| 8      | Eight | 9              |
| 9      | Nine  | —              |

## Counting with Fingers

Our fingers are a wonderful tool for counting!

- Hold up **1** finger — that shows one.
- Hold up **3** fingers — that shows three.
- Hold up **5** fingers — that is your whole hand!

You can show the same number in different ways. Show **4** by folding your thumb, or by folding your little finger instead. Both ways show 4!

> **Activity:** Show 3 fingers to a friend. Ask them to show 3 fingers in a different way!

## More, Less, or Equal

When we look at two groups of things, we can compare them. Which group has **more**? Which has **less**? Or are they **equal** (the same)?

**Example:** Kavya has 6 crayons. Rohan has 4 crayons.
- Kavya has **more** crayons.
- Rohan has **less** crayons.

**Example:** Isha has 5 marbles. Arjun also has 5 marbles.
- They have an **equal** number of marbles.

> **How to compare:** Put things in two groups. Now match them **one by one** (make pairs).
> - If one group has **extra things left**, it has **more**.
> - If one group **finishes first**, it has **less**.
>
> **Example:** You have **4 biscuits** and **2 children**. Give 1 biscuit to each child:
> - Child 1 gets 1 biscuit, Child 2 gets 1 biscuit.
> - Children are finished but **2 biscuits are still left**.
> - So biscuits are **more** and children are **less**.

## Let Us Count Things

Open your eyes wide and count things around you!

- **One** nose on your face
- **Two** wheels on a bicycle
- **Three** wheels on an auto-rickshaw
- **Four** walls in your room
- **Five** toes on one foot
- **Six** legs on a beetle
- **Seven** colours in a rainbow
- **Eight** tentacles on an octopus
- **Nine** players in a kho-kho team

> **Did you know?** The seven colours of the rainbow are called **VIBGYOR** — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red!

## Number Order

Numbers follow a fixed order. We always count: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9.

If some numbers are missing, we can fill them in:

- 1, 2, **3**, 4, 5
- 4, 5, **6**, **7**, 8
- 6, **7**, **8**, **9**

The number order goes from **smallest** to **largest**.

- **1** is the smallest number we have learned.
- **9** is the largest number we have learned so far.

> **Activity:** Can you count backwards? Try: 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 — blast off!

## Putting Groups Together

When we join two small groups, we get one bigger group.

- A bunch of **4** bananas and another bunch of **3** bananas together make **7** bananas.
- A plate of **2** laddus and another plate of **5** laddus together make **7** laddus.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word         | Meaning                                                          |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Counting** | Saying numbers in order: 1, 2, 3, 4...                          |
| **Number**   | A word or symbol that tells how many (like 1, 2, 3)              |
| **More**     | A bigger amount — 6 is more than 4                               |
| **Less**     | A smaller amount — 3 is less than 8                              |
| **Equal**    | The same amount — 5 and 5 are equal                              |
| **Order**    | Numbers arranged from smallest to largest or largest to smallest |
