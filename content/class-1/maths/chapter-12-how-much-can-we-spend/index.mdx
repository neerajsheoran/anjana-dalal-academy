## Indian Coins and Notes

In India, we buy things using **Rupees**. We write it as **Rs** or use the special sign **₹**.

Money comes in two forms — **coins** (round metal pieces) and **notes** (flat paper money).

### Coins We Use

| Coin | What It Looks Like |
|------|-------------------|
| **₹1** | A small, light silver coin |
| **₹2** | A bit bigger than the ₹1 coin |
| **₹5** | A coin with a smooth round edge |
| **₹10** | A special two-colour coin — silver in the middle, golden ring outside |

### Notes We Use

| Note | Its Colour |
|------|-----------|
| **₹10** | Chocolate brown |
| **₹20** | Greenish-yellow |
| **₹50** | Bright blue |
| **₹100** | Lavender (soft purple) |

> Coins are hard and made of metal. You can feel their weight. Notes are thin and made of special paper. Each note has a different colour so you can tell them apart easily!

## Adding Up Money

When we have several coins, we add their values to find the **total**.

**Example:** You have two ₹5 coins. How much is that?
5 + 5 = **₹10**

**Example:** You have one ₹10 note and two ₹2 coins. How much?
10 + 2 + 2 = **₹14**

**Example:** You have three ₹10 notes. How much?
10 + 10 + 10 = **₹30**

## Buying Things with Coins

We can pay the exact price using different coin combinations.

**Example:** A packet of chips costs **₹10**. Arjun can pay in different ways:

- **10** coins of ₹1
- **5** coins of ₹2
- **2** coins of ₹5
- **1** coin of ₹10

All of these give exactly ₹10!

**Example:** A samosa costs **₹8**. Arjun has a ₹5 coin.
He needs **₹3 more** (because 8 - 5 = 3).
He can give the ₹5 coin and 3 coins of ₹1.

## Paying in Different Ways

The same price can be paid with different mixes of coins and notes.

### Different ways to make ₹15:

| Way | What You Give |
|-----|--------------|
| Way 1 | One ₹10 coin + one ₹5 coin |
| Way 2 | Three ₹5 coins |
| Way 3 | One ₹10 coin + five ₹1 coins |
| Way 4 | One ₹10 coin + two ₹2 coins + one ₹1 coin |

### Different ways to make ₹20:

| Way | What You Give |
|-----|--------------|
| Way 1 | One ₹20 note |
| Way 2 | Two ₹10 coins |
| Way 3 | Four ₹5 coins |
| Way 4 | One ₹10 coin + two ₹5 coins |

> There are many roads to the same answer!

## Matching Same Amounts

Sometimes different sets of coins have the **same total value**.

- Five ₹2 coins = One ₹10 coin (both make ₹10)
- Two ₹5 coins = One ₹10 coin (both make ₹10)
- Two ₹10 notes = One ₹20 note (both make ₹20)

## Saving Money — The Gullak

A **gullak** is a clay or plastic money box with a tiny slit on top. You drop coins through the slit and save them!

Saving even a little bit each day adds up over time:

- Save **₹1 every day** — in 10 days you have ₹10.
- Save **₹2 every day** — in 10 days you have ₹20.
- Save **₹5 every day** — in 10 days you have ₹50!

> **Activity:** Find an old dabba (box) at home. Ask Maa to help you cut a small slit on the lid. Decorate it with colours and stickers. Now you have your very own gullak! Start dropping coins into it every day.
