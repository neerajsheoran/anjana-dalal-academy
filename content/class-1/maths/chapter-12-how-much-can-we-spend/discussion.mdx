## Indian Coins and Notes

**Child:** Maa, why are some money pieces round and hard, and some are flat like paper?

**Mother:** The round hard ones are called **coins** and the flat paper ones are called **notes**. Both are money, but they look and feel different.

**Child:** Why do we need both?

**Mother:** Coins are great for small amounts — like ₹1, ₹2, and ₹5. They are strong and last a long time. Notes are used for bigger amounts — like ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, and ₹100. Imagine carrying 100 coins of ₹1 — your pocket would be so heavy!

**Child:** Ha ha! My pocket would tear!

**Mother:** Exactly! That is why we have notes. One ₹100 note is light and fits easily in a wallet. But it has the same value as 100 coins of ₹1.

**Child:** Maa, the ₹10 coin has two colours — silver and gold. That is my favourite!

**Mother:** Yes, it is quite special. Every coin and note has its own look so we can tell them apart quickly.

## Adding Up Money

**Child:** Maa, I found some coins in my gullak. Two ₹5 coins and three ₹2 coins. How much money do I have?

**Mother:** Let us add them up! Start with the ₹5 coins. 5 + 5 = 10. Now add the ₹2 coins. 2 + 2 + 2 = 6. So the total is 10 + 6 = ₹16!

**Child:** ₹16! Can I buy something with that?

**Mother:** At Nisha Aunty's cart, a juice box costs ₹15. You have ₹16, so you can buy a juice box and still have ₹1 left!

**Child:** Wow! But what if I want ice cream? It costs ₹20.

**Mother:** Then you need ₹20 but you only have ₹16. You are ₹4 short. So you would need to save ₹4 more.

**Child:** Okay, Maa! I will save more in my gullak!

## Paying in Different Ways

**Child:** Maa, yesterday at the shop, the uncle gave Papa change — different coins for the same amount. How does that work?

**Mother:** Let me show you with an example. Say you want to pay ₹10. You can give one ₹10 coin — done! Or you can give two ₹5 coins — because 5 + 5 = 10. Or even five ₹2 coins — because 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10.

**Child:** So many ways to make the same amount!

**Mother:** Yes! Let us try ₹20. How can you make it?

**Child:** One ₹20 note!

**Mother:** That is one way. Any other?

**Child:** Hmm... two ₹10 coins! Because 10 + 10 = 20.

**Mother:** Shabash! One more?

**Child:** Four ₹5 coins? Because 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20!

**Mother:** Ekdum sahi! See, money is like a puzzle — there are always many ways to solve it. And that is why saving small coins in your gullak is so smart — they add up to big amounts!

**Child:** Maa, I am going to save ₹2 every day. In 10 days I will have ₹20!

**Mother:** That is the spirit, beta! Saving is a superpower.
