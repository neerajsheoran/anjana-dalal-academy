## What is a Pattern?

**Child:** Maa, teacher said patterns are everywhere. But what exactly is a pattern?

**Mother:** A pattern is something that repeats in the same order, again and again. Look at the border of your dupatta — see how the same small flower comes back over and over?

**Child:** Oh yes! Flower, leaf, flower, leaf, flower, leaf!

**Mother:** That is a pattern! The same group keeps repeating. Now look at our floor tiles. What do you see?

**Child:** White square, brown square, white square, brown square... It never stops!

**Mother:** Exactly! That is a pattern too. Once you know the rule, you can always tell what comes next.

**Child:** So patterns are like a song that keeps playing the same tune?

**Mother:** What a lovely way to say it, beta! Yes, patterns are like a tune that plays again and again.

## Colour and Shape Patterns

**Child:** Maa, I want to make a garland for Diwali. Can I use patterns?

**Mother:** Of course! Pick two colours of paper. Let us say orange and green. Now paste them one after the other — orange, green, orange, green...

**Child:** Orange, green, orange, green, orange, green! What comes next?

**Mother:** You tell me!

**Child:** Orange! Because the pattern starts again!

**Mother:** Shabash! Now let us try a harder one. What if we do: pink, pink, blue, pink, pink, blue...

**Child:** Hmm... the group is pink, pink, blue. So after blue it starts again — pink!

**Mother:** Very good! You can make patterns with shapes too. Like circle, triangle, circle, triangle...

**Child:** Or star, star, heart, star, star, heart!

**Mother:** Look at you — making patterns already! Diwali garland will look beautiful!

## Number Patterns

**Child:** Maa, can numbers also make patterns?

**Mother:** Oh yes! Let us try. I will say some numbers and you tell me what comes next. Ready? 2, 4, 6, 8...

**Child:** Hmm... each number is 2 more. So next is 10!

**Mother:** Correct! Now try this: 5, 10, 15, 20...

**Child:** Going up by 5 each time. Next is 25!

**Mother:** You are getting good at this. Here is a tricky one: 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3...

**Child:** Each number says itself twice! So next is 4!

**Mother:** Ekdum sahi! Number patterns are everywhere — in calendars, on clocks, even in your maths tables later. Finding the rule is the key.

**Child:** I love number patterns, Maa! They are like puzzles!

## Patterns in Indian Art

**Child:** Maa, teacher showed us pictures of rangoli. She said rangoli uses patterns. How?

**Mother:** Have you seen Nani draw kolam at her doorstep in Chennai? She puts dots first, then draws curvy lines around them. The same curve repeats on all four sides.

**Child:** Yes! It looks the same on every side!

**Mother:** That is a pattern — the same design repeating. Rangoli works the same way. During Diwali, we make a design in one corner and then repeat it in all four corners.

**Child:** Like folding a paper and cutting a shape — when you open it, the shape is everywhere!

**Mother:** Exactly! And it is not just rangoli. Look at the jaali work on old buildings like Hawa Mahal in Jaipur — the same tiny window shape repeats hundreds of times.

**Child:** Patterns make things so pretty, Maa!

**Mother:** They do! And the best part is, you can create your own patterns with anything — flowers, leaves, buttons, or even your coloured pencils.
