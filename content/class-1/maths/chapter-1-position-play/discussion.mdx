## Where Are Things? — Learning Position Words

**Child:** Maa, today our teacher played a game! She hid a toy and we had to say where it was. But I got confused sometimes.

**Mother:** That sounds like fun, beta! Those are called **position words**. We use them every single day. Tell me — where is the TV remote right now?

**Child:** It is **on** the sofa cushion!

**Mother:** Perfect! And where is Buddy the dog sleeping?

**Child:** He is **under** the table! He always goes there when it is hot.

**Mother:** See? You already know position words! **On** means something is touching the top of another thing. **Under** means it is below. Very simple!

---

## Inside and Outside

**Child:** Maa, what about inside and outside? Like when you say "come **inside** the house"?

**Mother:** Exactly! **Inside** means within something — like the sweets are **inside** the steel dabba. **Outside** means not within — like the neem tree is **outside** our window.

**Child:** Oh! So my crayons are **inside** my pencil box, and the garden is **outside** our door?

**Mother:** Very good! When you open your lunch box at school, what is **inside**?

**Child:** Poha and a banana! And sometimes you put a small chocolate **inside** too, Maa!

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! And what is **outside** the lunch box?

**Child:** My water bottle and my napkin!

**Mother:** See, you understand it perfectly.

---

## Above and Below

**Child:** Maa, is "above" the same thing as "on"? I always mix them up.

**Mother:** Good question! **On** means touching — like the plate is **on** the table, it touches the table. But **above** means higher up without touching. The ceiling fan is **above** our heads — it is up there but not touching us!

**Child:** Ohhh! So the aeroplane in the sky is **above** us, but the topi on my head is **on** my head?

**Mother:** Exactly right, beta! And **below** means lower than something. The floor is **below** our feet. When we stand on the terrace, the garden is **below** us.

**Child:** Like when we went to Shimla and looked down — the valley was **below** us!

**Mother:** What a wonderful example!

---

## Our Tiranga — Position in the Flag

**Child:** Maa, teacher also showed us the Indian flag. She said even the colours have positions!

**Mother:** Yes! Our flag is called the **Tiranga**. It has three stripes. The saffron colour is at the **top**, white is in the **middle**, and green is at the **bottom**.

**Child:** And there is a blue circle in the white part!

**Mother:** Right — that is the **Ashoka Chakra**. It sits in the **middle** of the white stripe. See, even our national flag uses position words!

**Child:** If someone asks on the phone, I can say "the chakra is in the middle of the white stripe" without pointing!

**Mother:** That is exactly why position words are so useful. You cannot always point at things!

---

## Before and After

**Child:** Maa, what about "before" and "after"? Like I wash my hands **before** eating?

**Mother:** That is "before" in time, but we also use it for position! Think of a train — you love watching trains at the railway crossing, right?

**Child:** Yes! The Shatabdi Express!

**Mother:** So the engine comes **before** all the coaches — it is at the front. And the guard's van is **after** all the coaches — it is at the back. In your school line, Ananya stands **before** you and Vikram stands **after** you.

**Child:** Oh, so "before" means in front and "after" means behind!

**Mother:** Exactly, beta!

---

## Why Position Words Help Us

**Child:** Maa, but why do I have to learn all these words? I can just point!

**Mother:** Think about this, beta. If Papa calls from office and asks, "Where did I keep my car keys?" you cannot point through the phone! You need to say, "They are **on** the shelf, **inside** the drawer, **near** the clock."

**Child:** Oh yes! And when we play hide-and-seek, I can say "Look **under** the charpai!" or "She is **behind** the almirah!"

**Mother:** Exactly! And in an emergency, if you tell someone "The kitten is stuck **on** the branch **above** the wall," they know exactly where to look. Position words help everyone understand where things are.

**Child:** Maa, I know so many position words now! My pencil box is **on** my desk, **inside** my room, **near** the window!

**Mother:** Three position words in one sentence! Wonderful, beta!
