## Adding Bigger Numbers — A Chat with Maa

**Child:** Maa, today teacher asked us to add 8 + 6. But the answer is more than 10! I ran out of fingers!

**Mother:** Ha ha, that happens, beta! But there is a trick. You do not need to count from the beginning. Just start from the bigger number and **count on**. So start at 8, and count 6 more: 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14. The answer is 14!

**Child:** Oh! That is so much faster than counting all of them from 1!

**Mother:** Yes! This is called **counting on**. Always start from the bigger number and count forward. It saves time.

## The Make 10 First Trick

**Child:** Maa, teacher showed us another trick. She called it "Make 10 First." But I did not fully understand it.

**Mother:** Let me explain with brinjals! Suppose you have 9 brinjals and you get 6 more. How many is that?

**Child:** 9 + 6... hmm, that is hard.

**Mother:** Here is the trick. 9 is very close to 10. It just needs 1 more to become 10. So take 1 from the 6. Now you have 10 and 5 left over. What is 10 + 5?

**Child:** 15! That is easy!

**Mother:** See? Making 10 first turns a hard sum into an easy one. 9 + 6 = 10 + 5 = 15!

**Child:** Can I use this trick for 8 + 7 too?

**Mother:** Of course! 8 needs 2 to make 10. Take 2 from the 7, leaving 5. So 8 + 7 = 10 + 5 = 15!

**Child:** Wow, Maa! This is like a magic trick for maths!

## Subtracting Bigger Numbers

**Child:** Maa, we also had to do 16 - 8 today. That is a big number to take away!

**Mother:** You can **count back** from 16. Go: 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8. How many steps did you take?

**Child:** Eight steps! And I landed on 8. So 16 - 8 = 8!

**Mother:** Perfect! For subtraction, we hop backward on the number strip. And here is a handy rule: if you subtract 0 from any number, the number does not change. 16 - 0 = 16.

**Child:** That makes sense! If I take away nothing, I still have everything!

## Stories with Addition and Subtraction

**Child:** Maa, can you tell me a maths story?

**Mother:** Sure! Auntie bought 15 gulab jamuns for a party. The children ate some, and only 6 were left. How many did the children eat?

**Child:** I start with 15 and the answer is 6 left... so I subtract! 15 - 6 = 9. They ate 9 gulab jamuns!

**Mother:** Excellent! Here is another one. A sweet shop had 8 boxes of barfi. They sold 3 boxes, then made 5 more boxes. How many do they have now?

**Child:** First, 8 - 3 = 5 boxes left. Then 5 + 5 = 10 boxes! They have 10 boxes now!

**Mother:** See, you can solve two-step problems already! You are becoming quite the maths expert, beta.

## Forward and Backward on the Number Strip

**Child:** Maa, teacher said addition goes forward and subtraction goes backward. Is that like walking?

**Mother:** Yes, exactly! Imagine numbers written on the floor from 0 to 20. When you add, you walk forward — towards bigger numbers. When you subtract, you walk backward — towards smaller numbers.

**Child:** And sometimes two different paths reach the same number! Like 5 + 3 = 8 and 14 - 6 = 8. Both reach 8!

**Mother:** That is a beautiful observation, beta. In maths, there are often many roads to the same answer. The important thing is to understand what you are doing — putting together or taking away.

**Child:** I love how maths is like a puzzle, Maa!

**Mother:** And you are getting better at solving it every single day, beta!
