## Adding 3-Digit Numbers

**Child:** Maa, today teacher taught us to add big numbers like 248 + 173. But sometimes the ones add up to more than 9! What do I do then?

**Mother:** Good question, beta! When the ones add up to 10 or more, you **regroup**. Think of it like this -- you have 8 toffees and someone gives you 3 more. That is 11 toffees. But you can only fit 9 in one box. So you put 10 into a bigger box (that becomes 1 ten), and keep 1 toffee in the ones place.

**Child:** Oh! So 11 ones = 1 ten and 1 one?

**Mother:** Exactly! And the same thing happens with tens. If the tens add up to 12, that is 1 hundred and 2 tens. You always push the extra to the next bigger place. This is called **carrying over**.

**Child:** Like when you have too many ₹10 coins and you exchange them for a ₹100 note!

**Mother:** What a perfect example, beta! You really understand this.

## Subtracting 3-Digit Numbers

**Child:** Maa, subtraction is harder. Like 315 - 148. I cannot take 8 from 5!

**Mother:** You are right, 8 is bigger than 5. So you need to **borrow**. Go to the tens place and take 1 ten. Break that 1 ten into 10 ones. Now you have 15 ones instead of 5.

**Child:** So 15 - 8 = 7 ones! But then the tens place becomes 0 instead of 1.

**Mother:** Yes, and then 0 tens minus 4 tens is not possible either. So you borrow from the hundreds place. Take 1 hundred, which becomes 10 tens. Now you have 10 tens minus 4 tens = 6 tens.

**Child:** And the hundreds become 2 instead of 3. So the answer is 167!

**Mother:** Perfect! Borrowing is just the opposite of carrying. When you carry, you send extra upward. When you borrow, you bring from above.

## Money: Notes and Coins

**Child:** Maa, teacher told us that long ago people did not use money. They just swapped things. Is that true?

**Mother:** Yes! It is called **barter**. If a farmer had extra wheat, he would give some to the potter and get clay pots in return. But it was tricky -- what if the potter did not want wheat?

**Child:** Then nobody could trade! That sounds very difficult.

**Mother:** Exactly. That is why money was invented. With money, the farmer can sell his wheat to anyone, get money, and then buy whatever he needs.

**Child:** Smart! And now we have so many types of notes -- ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500!

**Mother:** Yes! And remember, ₹500 is the same as 5 notes of ₹100, or 10 notes of ₹50. Knowing these relationships helps you calculate change quickly.

## Estimating Answers

**Child:** Maa, what is estimating? Teacher says we should estimate before solving.

**Mother:** Estimating means making a **smart guess** before doing the actual calculation. Round each number to the nearest hundred and add or subtract.

**Child:** Like 182 + 47? That is about 200 + 0 = 200?

**Mother:** Close! 182 is close to 200, and 47 is close to 50. So a better estimate would be about 200 + 50 = 250. But your way works too for a rough guess. The point is that when you solve and get the real answer, it should be close to your estimate. If you get something like 900, you know something went wrong!

**Child:** So estimating is like a safety check?

**Mother:** Exactly, beta! Even shopkeepers do mental estimates. When Maa buys sabzi for ₹185 and pays ₹500, I quickly estimate the change should be about 300. If the shopkeeper gives me only ₹200, I know something is wrong!

**Child:** Maa is a maths detective!

**Mother:** Ha ha, every good shopper needs to be!
