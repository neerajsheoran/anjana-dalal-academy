## Shapes You Can Hold

**Child:** Maa, what is the difference between a flat shape and a 3D shape?

**Mother:** Pick up your tiffin box. Can you feel it from all sides? It has height, width, and depth. That makes it a 3D shape -- a cuboid, to be exact!

**Child:** And a drawing of a rectangle on paper is flat?

**Mother:** Yes! A flat shape is called 2D -- it has only length and width. But your tiffin box is 3D because you can hold it in your hand and turn it around.

**Child:** So my cricket ball is also 3D?

**Mother:** Absolutely. Your cricket ball is a sphere -- a 3D shape that is round from every direction.

## Counting Faces, Edges, and Corners

**Child:** Maa, my teacher asked how many faces a cube has. What is a face?

**Mother:** A face is any surface of a 3D shape. Pick up a dice. Touch each flat side -- those are the faces. How many can you count?

**Child:** One, two, three... six! A dice has 6 faces!

**Mother:** Perfect! Now run your finger along the line where two faces meet. That line is called an edge. And the sharp point where edges come together is a corner.

**Child:** How many edges and corners does a dice have?

**Mother:** A dice has 12 edges and 8 corners. You can count them yourself!

**Child:** What about my cricket ball? Does it have edges?

**Mother:** No! A sphere has zero edges and zero corners. It has just one curved surface. That is why it rolls so easily in every direction.

## Building with Shapes

**Child:** Maa, can I build a toy temple using shapes?

**Mother:** What a lovely idea! What shape would you use for the main building?

**Child:** A cuboid! Like a box shape for the walls.

**Mother:** And for the shikhar on top?

**Child:** A cone! Because it is pointed at the top.

**Mother:** Wonderful! And what about the pillars at the entrance?

**Child:** Cylinders! Because pillars are round and tall like tubes.

**Mother:** You see? Every building around us is made of 3D shapes put together. Next time we visit a temple, try to spot all the shapes!

## The Magic of Dice

**Child:** Maa, I noticed something while playing Ludo. When I see 1 on top of the dice, the bottom always shows 6!

**Mother:** Well spotted! That is a rule for all standard dice. Opposite faces always add up to 7.

**Child:** So if 3 is on top, the bottom is 4?

**Mother:** Exactly! 3 + 4 = 7. And if 2 is on top?

**Child:** Then 5 is on the bottom! Because 2 + 5 = 7!

**Mother:** You have cracked the dice code! Next time you play, you can always guess what is on the hidden side.
