## Adding the Same Number Again and Again

**Child:** Maa, for Raksha Bandhan I want to make 4 gift packets. Each one needs 5 decorative stars. How many stars do I need in total?

**Mother:** Well, you need 5 stars for the first packet, 5 for the second, 5 for the third, and 5 for the fourth. So how would you write that?

**Child:** 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20 stars!

**Mother:** Correct! But there is a shorter way to write this. Since you are adding 5 four times, you can say "4 times 5" and write it as 4 x 5 = 20.

**Child:** Oh! So the x sign means "times"?

**Mother:** Yes! It is called multiplication. It is just a quick way to do repeated addition. Instead of adding the same number over and over, you multiply.

**Child:** That saves so much time, Maa!

## Sharing is Division

**Child:** Maa, we have 24 gulab jamuns and 6 guests are coming. How do we share them equally?

**Mother:** Think about it. Give 1 gulab jamun to each guest first. That uses up 6. Then give 1 more to each. That uses another 6. Keep going until all gulab jamuns are given out.

**Child:** 6, 12, 18, 24... I gave them out 4 times! So each guest gets 4!

**Mother:** Exactly. We write this as 24 divided by 6 equals 4, or 24 / 6 = 4.

**Child:** So division is like sharing equally?

**Mother:** Yes! And here is a secret -- division and multiplication are opposites. If 6 x 4 = 24, then 24 / 6 = 4 and also 24 / 4 = 6.

**Child:** They are like mirror images of each other!

**Mother:** What a beautiful way to put it!

## Times Tables and Patterns

**Child:** Maa, I have to learn the times-5 table. Is there a trick?

**Mother:** Look at the answers: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50. Do you notice anything about the last digit?

**Child:** It is always 0 or 5!

**Mother:** Right! When you multiply 5 by an even number like 2, 4, 6, the answer ends in 0. When you multiply by an odd number like 1, 3, 5, the answer ends in 5.

**Child:** So I can always check my answer. If it does not end in 0 or 5, I made a mistake!

**Mother:** That is a great way to verify your work. And here is another fun fact: if you add the times-2 answer and the times-3 answer for the same number, you get the times-5 answer!

**Child:** Let me check. 3 x 2 = 6 and 3 x 3 = 9. Adding them: 6 + 9 = 15. And 3 x 5 = 15! It works!

**Mother:** Because 2 + 3 = 5. So the times-2 and times-3 tables always combine to make the times-5 table.

## Big Multiplications Made Easy

**Child:** Maa, how do I multiply big numbers like 30 x 4? I do not know the table of 30!

**Mother:** You do not need the table of 30! Break it into smaller parts. What is 10 x 4?

**Child:** 40!

**Mother:** Good. And 30 is just 3 groups of 10. So 30 x 4 is the same as 3 groups of "10 x 4", which is 3 x 40.

**Child:** 40 + 40 + 40 = 120!

**Mother:** Perfect! You can always break big multiplications into smaller, easier ones. That is the beauty of maths -- there is always a simpler way!

**Child:** I want to try more, Maa. What about 50 x 4?

**Mother:** Think of it as 5 groups of 40.

**Child:** 5 x 40 = 200! Easy!

**Mother:** See? You are already multiplying like a champion!
