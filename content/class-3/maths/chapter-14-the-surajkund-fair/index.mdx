## Welcome to the Surajkund Fair!

The **Surajkund Mela** is a colourful craft fair held every year near **Faridabad, Haryana**. Artists, potters, weavers, and cooks come from every corner of India to show their work. Today, Tara and Kabir are visiting the fair with their Dadi. As they explore, let us learn about **symmetry**, **patterns**, and **maps**!

## What is Symmetry?

Look at a butterfly. Its left wing looks just like its right wing. Now look at a rangoli -- the left side often matches the right side perfectly.

When an object has **two halves that are mirror images** of each other, we say it has **symmetry**. The imaginary line that divides it into two matching halves is called the **line of symmetry**.

```
Symmetrical:          Not Symmetrical:
    *                     *
   * *                   *   *
  *   *                 *      *
 *     *               **      *
*       *              *         *
 *     *                 *     *
  *   *                    *  *
   * *                      *
    *
(Both sides match)    (Sides do not match)
```

> **Simple test:** Fold a paper shape along a line. If both halves sit exactly on top of each other, that line is the **line of symmetry** and the shape is symmetrical!

## Symmetry in Bead Chains (Malas)

At the fair, Tara and Kabir make bead chains using **8 beads** of 2 colours. Some chains are symmetrical and some are not.

**Symmetrical chain:**
```
Green Yellow Yellow Green | Green Yellow Yellow Green
       Left half          =        Right half
```

**Not symmetrical chain:**
```
Green Green Yellow Yellow | Green Yellow Green Yellow
       Left half          =/=      Right half
```

> **Check:** Look at the chain from the centre. Does the left pattern mirror the right? If yes, it is symmetrical!

## Symmetry in Rangolis

Across India, people make beautiful **rangoli** designs during festivals. Many are symmetrical!

- **Kolam** patterns from Tamil Nadu
- **Pookalam** flower designs from Kerala
- **Aipan** art from Uttarakhand

To check a rangoli for symmetry, draw a line through the centre. If the design on one side mirrors the other side, it is symmetrical.

> **Try this:** Draw half a flower on paper. Then complete the other half so it looks like a mirror reflection of the first half!

## Mirror Images

When you stand before a **mirror**, your reflection looks flipped. Your left hand appears on the right side, and your right hand appears on the left side.

### The Mirror Game

Tara places 4 stones on her side of a line. Kabir must place his stones as the **mirror image** on the other side.

```
Tara's side:     |    Kabir's side (mirror):
  X . . .        |       . . . X
  . . X .        |       . X . .
  . X . .        |       . . X .
  . . . X        |       X . . .
```

> **Key idea:** In a mirror image, **left becomes right** and **right becomes left**. Up and down stay the same!

## The Painter's Trick

A portrait artist at the fair drew only **half** of Tara's face and said: "Hold a mirror at the centre to see the complete picture!"

This trick works because a face is roughly **symmetrical** -- the left half resembles the right half.

But this does NOT work for everything. It fails for:
- A hand waving (left hand and right hand look different)
- A person facing sideways
- The digit **6** (its mirror reflection does not look like **6**)

## Tiling: Covering a Surface

The pathways at the fair are covered with **tiles**. Good tiling means:
- **No gaps** between tiles
- **No overlaps** (tiles must not stack on each other)

Which shapes make good tiles?

| Shape | Can it tile? |
|-------|:---:|
| **Squares** | Yes -- they fit snugly with no gaps |
| **Rectangles** | Yes -- they also fit perfectly |
| **Triangles** | Yes -- two triangles form a rectangle |
| **Circles** | No -- circles always leave curved gaps |

```
Good tiling (squares):     Bad tiling (circles):
+--+--+--+--+              O  O  O
+--+--+--+--+              O  O  O
+--+--+--+--+              (gaps everywhere!)
```

> **Activity:** Cut out small paper squares and triangles. Try covering a sheet of paper with them, leaving no empty spaces!

## Reading a Map

Tara and Kabir get separated from Dadi at the fair. Luckily, there is a **map** near the entrance! A map shows the layout of a place from **above**, like a bird flying overhead.

The Surajkund Fair map shows:
- **Parking** area
- **Ticket counter**
- **Food court**
- **Craft stalls** and shops
- **Giant wheel**
- **Lake**
- **Children's play zone**
- **Stage** (for performances)
- **Entry** and **Exit** gates

### Using directions on a map:

To reach a place, follow **directions**:
- Walk **straight** (forward)
- Turn **right**
- Turn **left**
- Go **back**

> **Example:** "Walk along the yellow path. Turn right at the food court. Walk straight. The play zone is on your left."

## Maze Puzzles

A **maze** is a puzzle with many paths, turns, and dead ends. Your goal is to find the one correct route from the **start** to the **finish** without running into a wall.

> **Strategy:** If you reach a dead end, go back and try a different turn. Keep exploring until you find the way through!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Symmetry** | When two halves of something are exact mirror images |
| **Symmetrical** | A shape or object that has symmetry |
| **Mirror image** | The reflection you see when something is flipped left-to-right |
| **Line of symmetry** | The line that divides a shape into two matching mirror halves |
| **Pattern** | Something that repeats in a regular way |
| **Tiling** | Covering a surface with shapes so there are no gaps and no overlaps |
| **Map** | A drawing showing where things are, as seen from above |
| **Directions** | Words like left, right, straight, and back that tell you which way to go |
| **Maze** | A puzzle where you find the path from start to finish through twists and turns |
