## Counting Beyond 200

You already know numbers up to 200. Let us keep going!

After 200, counting works the same way:

```
200, 201, 202, 203, ... 250, ... 298, 299, 300!
```

- **299 + 1 = 300** (Three Hundred!)
- **399 + 1 = 400** (Four Hundred!)
- **499 + 1 = 500** (Five Hundred!)

> **Pattern:** After every 99, the next number jumps to a new hundred. 199 becomes 200, 299 becomes 300, 399 becomes 400, and so on!

## Hundreds, Tens, and Ones

Every 3-digit number is built from three parts: **Hundreds (H)**, **Tens (T)**, and **Ones (O)**.

| Number | Hundreds (H) | Tens (T) | Ones (O) | Number Name |
|:------:|:---:|:---:|:---:|-------------|
| 246 | 2 | 4 | 6 | Two Hundred Forty Six |
| 318 | 3 | 1 | 8 | Three Hundred Eighteen |
| 405 | 4 | 0 | 5 | Four Hundred Five |
| 370 | 3 | 7 | 0 | Three Hundred Seventy |
| 500 | 5 | 0 | 0 | Five Hundred |

We can also write a number in **expanded form**:
- 246 = **200 + 40 + 6** (2 Hundreds + 4 Tens + 6 Ones)
- 405 = **400 + 0 + 5** (4 Hundreds + 0 Tens + 5 Ones)

> **Think of it like a mithai shop!** Big boxes hold 100 pieces each, medium boxes hold 10, and single pieces are loose. The number 246 means 2 big boxes, 4 medium boxes, and 6 loose pieces.

## The Number Line

Numbers sit on a **number line** in order from smallest to largest.

```
0----100----200----300----400----500
```

We can zoom in to see the numbers between two hundreds:

```
200---210---220---230---240---250---260
        |           |
       213         247
```

Every number has its own unique spot on the number line.

## Hop Hundreds Home (Number Grid)

Picture a tall apartment building where flats are numbered in rows of 10:

```
Floor 3:  201  202  203  204  205  206  207  208  209  210
Floor 2:  111  112  113  114  115  116  117  118  119  120
Floor 1:  101  102  103  104  105  106  107  108  109  110
Floor 0:  001  002  003  004  005  006  007  008  009  010
```

**Patterns in the building:**
- Moving **one flat to the right**: the number increases by **1**
- Moving **one floor up**: the number increases by **10**
- Moving **one flat right AND one floor up**: the number increases by **11**

> **Example:** The flat directly above flat 107 is flat 117. And if you climb 10 floors from flat 15, you reach flat 115!

## Comparing Numbers: Greater Than and Less Than

To compare two numbers, we use two special symbols:

- **>** means **greater than** (the first number is bigger)
- **<** means **less than** (the first number is smaller)

> **Memory trick:** The open side of the symbol always faces the **bigger** number. Imagine a hungry fish -- it always opens its mouth towards the larger meal!

### Steps to compare 3-digit numbers:

**Step 1:** Look at the **hundreds** digit first.

| Compare | Hundreds | Result |
|---------|:--------:|--------|
| 416 vs 312 | 4 vs 3 | 416 > 312 (4 hundreds beats 3 hundreds) |

**Step 2:** If the hundreds are equal, check the **tens**.

| Compare | Hundreds | Tens | Result |
|---------|:--------:|:----:|--------|
| 347 vs 384 | 3 = 3 | 4 vs 8 | 347 < 384 |

**Step 3:** If hundreds and tens are both equal, compare the **ones**.

| Compare | Hundreds | Tens | Ones | Result |
|---------|:--------:|:----:|:----:|--------|
| 263 vs 268 | 2 = 2 | 6 = 6 | 3 vs 8 | 263 < 268 |

## Making Numbers with Digits

Using the digits **1**, **5**, and **8** (each used only once), you can form these 3-digit numbers:

| Number | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|:------:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 158 | 1 | 5 | 8 |
| 185 | 1 | 8 | 5 |
| 518 | 5 | 1 | 8 |
| 581 | 5 | 8 | 1 |
| 815 | 8 | 1 | 5 |
| 851 | 8 | 5 | 1 |

- The **smallest** number is **158** (put the smallest digit in the hundreds place).
- The **largest** number is **851** (put the biggest digit in the hundreds place).

> **Rule:** For the smallest number, place digits from smallest to largest. For the biggest number, place them from largest to smallest!

## The Magical Count

Pick any number. Write its name. Count the letters. Write that new number's name. Count again. Keep going. You will always arrive at **FOUR**!

```
246 → "Two Hundred Forty Six" → 19 letters
 19 → "Nineteen" → 8 letters
  8 → "Eight" → 5 letters
  5 → "Five" → 4 letters
  4 → "Four" → 4 letters ← Stays at FOUR forever!
```

> **Magic:** It does not matter which number you begin with. You always end up at FOUR!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Hundreds** | The leftmost place in a 3-digit number, showing how many 100s |
| **Tens** | The middle place, showing how many 10s |
| **Ones** | The rightmost place, showing how many 1s |
| **Greater than (>)** | A symbol meaning the first number is bigger |
| **Less than (<)** | A symbol meaning the first number is smaller |
| **3-digit number** | A number with a hundreds, tens, and ones place (like 246 or 405) |
| **Expanded form** | Writing a number as a sum of its place values (like 246 = 200 + 40 + 6) |
| **Ascending** | Arranged from smallest to largest |
| **Descending** | Arranged from largest to smallest |
