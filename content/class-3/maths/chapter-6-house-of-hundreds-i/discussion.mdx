## Going Past 200

**Child:** Maa, I know numbers up to 200. What comes after that?

**Mother:** The counting keeps going the same way! After 200, you say 201, 202, 203... all the way up to 299. And then?

**Child:** 300! Three hundred!

**Mother:** Exactly! And after 300 comes 301, 302, and so on until 399. Then?

**Child:** 400! Four hundred! I see the pattern, Maa. Every time you finish a set of 99, a new hundred starts.

**Mother:** Brilliant! This pattern goes on and on -- 500, 600, 700, 800, 900, and even beyond!

## Breaking Numbers into Parts

**Child:** Maa, what does it mean when my teacher says "246 has 2 hundreds, 4 tens, and 6 ones"?

**Mother:** Let me explain with sweets. Imagine a mithai shop. The big dabba holds 100 pieces, the medium dabba holds 10, and single pieces are loose.

**Child:** Okay...

**Mother:** For 246 pieces of barfi, the shopkeeper would pack: 2 big dabbas (200 pieces), 4 medium dabbas (40 pieces), and 6 loose pieces.

**Child:** So 200 + 40 + 6 = 246!

**Mother:** That is called the expanded form. And the 2 sits in the "hundreds place", the 4 in the "tens place", and the 6 in the "ones place."

**Child:** What about a number like 405? There are 0 tens!

**Mother:** Right! 405 means 4 big dabbas, 0 medium dabbas, and 5 loose pieces. The zero is important -- it holds the tens place empty so we know it is four hundred five, not forty-five!

## Comparing Numbers

**Child:** Maa, how do I know which number is bigger -- 347 or 384?

**Mother:** Always start by comparing the hundreds digit. Both have 3 in the hundreds place, so they are equal there.

**Child:** Then I look at the tens?

**Mother:** Yes! 347 has 4 tens and 384 has 8 tens. Since 8 is more than 4, which number is bigger?

**Child:** 384 is bigger! So 347 is less than 384.

**Mother:** Perfect! We write it as 347 < 384. The open side of the symbol always faces the bigger number.

**Child:** Like a hungry fish opening its mouth towards the larger number!

**Mother:** What a wonderful way to remember it!

## Making the Biggest and Smallest Numbers

**Child:** Maa, my teacher gave us the digits 1, 5, and 8. She asked us to make the biggest and smallest 3-digit numbers using each digit only once.

**Mother:** For the biggest number, which digit should go in the hundreds place?

**Child:** The biggest digit! That is 8. Then 5 in tens and 1 in ones. So 851!

**Mother:** And for the smallest?

**Child:** The smallest digit first! 1 in hundreds, then 5, then 8. So 158!

**Mother:** Excellent! And if you write all possible numbers -- 158, 185, 518, 581, 815, 851 -- and arrange them from smallest to largest, 158 comes first and 851 comes last.

**Child:** This is like a puzzle, Maa! I love it!
