## The Calendar

A **calendar** tells us the **date**, the **day of the week**, and the **month**. Every family in India has one hanging on the wall or saved on a phone!

### Months of the Year

A year has **12 months**:

| Month | Days |
|-------|:----:|
| January | 31 |
| February | 28 or 29 |
| March | 31 |
| April | 30 |
| May | 31 |
| June | 30 |
| July | 31 |
| August | 31 |
| September | 30 |
| October | 31 |
| November | 30 |
| December | 31 |

> **Memory trick:** Make a fist. Count across the knuckles and dips. Every bump (knuckle) is a 31-day month, every dip is a 30-day month. February is the exception -- it has only 28 or 29 days!

![A fist showing the knuckle trick for remembering days in each month](knuckle-trick-months.png)

### Days of the Week

One week has **7 days**:

```
Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday
```

### Important relationships:

- **1 year** = **12 months** = **365 days** (or 366 in a leap year)
- **1 year** = about **52 weeks**
- **1 month** = about **4 weeks**
- **1 week** = **7 days**

## Reading Dates

Dates follow a pattern: **DD/MM/YYYY**

- **DD** = Day of the month (01 to 31)
- **MM** = Month number (01 to 12)
- **YYYY** = Year

**Example:** 14/11/2025 means **14th November, 2025** -- that is Children's Day!

| Date written | What it means |
|---|---|
| 15/08/2025 | 15th August, 2025 (Independence Day!) |
| 26/01/2026 | 26th January, 2026 (Republic Day!) |
| 02/10/2025 | 2nd October, 2025 (Gandhi Jayanti!) |

## How Old Are You?

Your **age** tells how many full years have passed since you were born.

If you were born in **2017** and this year is **2026**:
- 2026 - 2017 = **9 years old**

If your Maa was **28 years old** when you were born, and you are **9 now**:
- Maa is 28 + 9 = **37 years old**

> **Puzzle:** Nisha is **three times as old** as her sister Tanu. She is also **8 years older** than Tanu. Can you find their ages? Answer: Tanu is **4** and Nisha is **12**!

## The Clock

A **clock** shows us the **time**. The round clock with moving hands (analog clock) has:

- A **short hand** (hour hand) -- it points to the **hour**
- A **long hand** (minute hand) -- it points to the **minutes**
- Numbers **1 to 12** arranged in a circle

```
        12
    11      1
  10          2
  9            3
  8          4
    7      5
        6
```

---

## Reading Time

### O'Clock (Exact Hours)

When the **minute hand** points straight up to **12**, the time is an exact hour. Read the number the **hour hand** points to.

| Hour hand points to | We say |
|:---:|---|
| 6 | 6 o'clock |
| 10 | 10 o'clock |
| 12 | 12 o'clock |

### Quarter Past (15 minutes after)

When the **minute hand** points to **3**, it has moved **15 minutes** past the hour.

- Minute hand on 3, hour hand on 9 = **9:15** or **quarter past 9**

### Half Past (30 minutes after)

When the **minute hand** points to **6**, it has moved **30 minutes** past the hour.

- Minute hand on 6, hour hand between 4 and 5 = **4:30** or **half past 4**

| Time | What we say | Minute hand at |
|------|-------------|:---:|
| 6:00 | 6 o'clock | 12 |
| 6:15 | Quarter past 6 | 3 |
| 6:30 | Half past 6 | 6 |
| 7:00 | 7 o'clock | 12 |

## A Day in Sana's Life

A full day has **24 hours**. Here is how Sana spends her school day:

| Time | What Sana does |
|:----:|----------|
| 6:30 AM | Wakes up |
| 7:00 AM | Finishes getting ready |
| 7:30 AM | Eats breakfast |
| 8:00 AM | Leaves for school |
| 1:30 PM | Eats lunch at school |
| 2:30 PM | Comes back home |
| 4:00 PM | Plays with friends |
| 5:30 PM | Does homework |
| 9:00 PM | Goes to sleep |

**How long did Sana take to get ready?**
She woke up at 6:30 and finished by 7:00. That is **30 minutes**.

## How Long Does It Take?

Different activities take different amounts of time:

| Takes minutes | Takes hours | Takes days | Takes weeks | Takes months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brushing teeth | A cricket match | A wedding celebration | School exams | Growing a tomato plant |
| Drinking a glass of milk | A train journey | A holiday trip | Learning to swim | Building a room |
| Tying your shoelaces | Cooking a feast | Reading a thick book | Growing onions | A school year |

## Analog and Digital Clocks

We see two kinds of clocks in our daily life:

| Analog Clock | Digital Clock |
|---|---|
| Has hands that move around a circle | Shows numbers on a screen |
| Numbers 1-12 on the face | Shows time like 07:30 |
| Found on walls, wristwatches | Found on phones, ovens, TVs |

> **Fun project:** Make a **water clock** using two small plastic bottles! Poke a tiny hole in one cap, fill that bottle with water, and place it upside down over the empty bottle. Watch the water drip down. Mark the water level every 5 minutes -- now you have a timer!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Calendar** | A chart showing days, weeks, and months of a year |
| **Date** | The specific day of a month (like 14th or 26th) |
| **Month** | One of the 12 parts of a year (like March or October) |
| **Year** | 12 months or about 365 days |
| **Week** | A group of 7 days |
| **Hour hand** | The short hand on a clock that shows the hour |
| **Minute hand** | The long hand on a clock that shows the minutes |
| **O'clock** | An exact hour (when the minute hand is at 12) |
| **Quarter past** | 15 minutes after the hour (minute hand at 3) |
| **Half past** | 30 minutes after the hour (minute hand at 6) |
| **Analog clock** | A clock with hands moving in a circle |
| **Digital clock** | A clock that shows time as numbers on a screen |
