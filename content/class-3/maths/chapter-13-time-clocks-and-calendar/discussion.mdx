## The Calendar

**Child:** Maa, how many days are there in a year? Someone said 365 and someone else said 366!

**Mother:** Both are right! Most years have **365 days**, but every 4 years we get a special year called a **leap year** with **366 days**. In a leap year, February has 29 days instead of 28.

**Child:** Why does February always have fewer days than the other months?

**Mother:** That goes back to the ancient Romans who created our calendar! February was the last month they added, and it got the fewest days. Use the knuckle trick to remember -- make a fist and count. Every bump is 31 days, every dip is 30 days. February is the only exception with 28 or 29.

**Child:** Maa, what about weeks? How many weeks in a year?

**Mother:** About **52 weeks**! And each week has 7 days. Here is a fun thing -- can you tell me what day comes 7 days after a Monday?

**Child:** Monday again!

**Mother:** Exactly! The days go in a circle. They keep repeating every 7 days.

## Reading Dates

**Child:** Maa, my birthday card says 14/03/2017. What does that mean?

**Mother:** That is your birthday written in the DD/MM/YYYY format! DD means the day -- 14. MM means the month number -- 03, which is March. YYYY means the year -- 2017.

**Child:** So 15/08 means 15th August? That is Independence Day!

**Mother:** Very good, beta! And 26/01 is Republic Day, and 02/10 is Gandhi Jayanti. Dates are written this way so everyone understands them the same way.

**Child:** If I was born in 2017 and now it is 2026, I am 2026 minus 2017 equals 9 years old!

**Mother:** Perfect calculation! And how old will you be in 2030?

**Child:** 2030 minus 2017 is 13! I will be a teenager!

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes you will!

## Reading the Clock

**Child:** Maa, I always get confused about which hand shows the hour and which shows the minutes. They both look similar!

**Mother:** Remember this: the **short** hand is the **hour** hand, and the **long** hand is the **minute** hand. Short for hours, long for minutes. Think of it this way -- hours are bigger than minutes, but the hour hand is shorter. It is a funny opposite!

**Child:** Okay! So if the short hand is on 6 and the long hand is on 12, it is 6 o'clock?

**Mother:** Exactly! And what if the long hand moves to 3?

**Child:** Then 15 minutes have passed. So it is quarter past 6, which is 6:15!

**Mother:** And if the long hand goes all the way to 6?

**Child:** That is 30 minutes, so half past 6 -- 6:30!

**Mother:** Wonderful! You know what, let us play a game. Every time you see a clock today, tell me what time it shows. Deal?

**Child:** Deal, Maa!

## How Long Does It Take?

**Child:** Maa, why do we say some things take minutes and some take hours? What is the difference?

**Mother:** Think about brushing your teeth. It takes about 2 or 3 minutes. But watching a full movie takes about 2 hours. Minutes are small pieces of time, and hours are bigger pieces. 60 minutes make 1 hour!

**Child:** And some things take even longer, like days or weeks?

**Mother:** Yes! A wedding celebration takes a few days. Growing vegetables in our balcony garden takes weeks. And building our neighbour's new floor took months!

**Child:** What about making my birthday cake? How long does that take?

**Mother:** Mixing takes about 15 minutes, baking takes about 30 minutes, and decorating takes another 20 minutes. So about 1 hour in total. Now can you tell me -- was that minutes, hours, or days?

**Child:** About 1 hour! So it is measured in minutes and hours.

**Mother:** See, you already understand time perfectly, beta!
