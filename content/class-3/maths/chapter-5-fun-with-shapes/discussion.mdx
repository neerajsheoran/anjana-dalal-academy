## Shapes on Paper

**Child:** Maa, what is the difference between the shapes we learned in Chapter 2 and the ones we are learning now?

**Mother:** In Chapter 2, you learned about 3D shapes -- solid shapes you can hold, like a ball or a box. Now we are learning about 2D shapes -- flat shapes you can draw on paper, like a circle or a triangle.

**Child:** So a roti is a circle?

**Mother:** The flat surface of a roti is a circle, yes! And the door of our house? What shape is that?

**Child:** A rectangle! It has 4 sides and 4 corners.

**Mother:** Perfect. Now look at the floor tiles in our kitchen. What shape are they?

**Child:** They are squares! All 4 sides are the same!

**Mother:** You are getting very good at spotting shapes!

## Squares and Rectangles

**Child:** Maa, you said every square is a rectangle. But how? They look different!

**Mother:** A rectangle is any shape with 4 sides and 4 right-angle corners where opposite sides are equal. Right?

**Child:** Right.

**Mother:** Now think about a square. It has 4 sides, 4 right-angle corners, and its opposite sides are equal too -- because ALL sides are equal! So it follows every rule of a rectangle.

**Child:** Oh, I see! A square is just a special rectangle where all sides happen to be the same length!

**Mother:** Exactly! But a rectangle where the long sides are different from the short sides is NOT a square.

**Child:** So all squares are rectangles, but not all rectangles are squares. Got it, Maa!

## Corners and Right Angles

**Child:** Maa, my teacher talked about "right angles" today. What are they?

**Mother:** Pick up your maths textbook. Look at any corner of the book. That corner is a right angle -- it is exactly 90 degrees. We also call it a "square corner."

**Child:** Are all corners right angles?

**Mother:** No! Open a pair of scissors a little bit. The angle between the blades is less than a right angle -- it is sharper. Now open the scissors wide -- that angle is more than a right angle.

**Child:** How can I check if a corner is a right angle?

**Mother:** Place the corner of your book on it. If they match exactly, it is a right angle. If the corner is sharper than the book corner, it is less. If it is wider, it is more.

**Child:** Let me check the corners of our dining table... they match the book perfectly! So the table has right angles!

**Mother:** Correct!

## Making New Shapes

**Child:** Maa, can I make a square using triangles?

**Mother:** Yes! Take two right-angled triangles that are exactly the same size. Place their longest sides together. What do you see?

**Child:** It makes a square! Or wait... depending on how I place them, it could make a rectangle too!

**Mother:** Very good! You can also put two squares next to each other to make a rectangle. Shapes can be combined in many creative ways.

**Child:** That is like building with Lego blocks but with flat shapes!

**Mother:** Exactly! And there is a famous puzzle called a tangram that has 7 flat pieces. You can arrange them to make animals, birds, boats, and all sorts of figures.

**Child:** Can we get a tangram, Maa? I want to try making a bird!

**Mother:** We can make one at home! I will help you cut the 7 pieces from a square piece of cardboard. Then you can play with it as much as you like!
