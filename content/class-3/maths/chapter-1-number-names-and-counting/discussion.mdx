## Pebbles and Chickens

**Child:** Maa, how did people count things before numbers were invented?

**Mother:** Great question! Imagine a farmer in a village who cannot read or write. Every morning, his goats go out to graze. He puts one pebble into a pot for each goat that leaves.

**Child:** Like one pebble for one goat?

**Mother:** Exactly! And when the goats come back in the evening, he removes one pebble for each goat. If the pot is empty, all goats are home safe!

**Child:** What if there are still pebbles in the pot?

**Mother:** Then those many goats are still missing! If 2 pebbles remain, 2 goats have not come back. This is called tally marking.

**Child:** That is so clever, Maa! No numbers needed at all!

## The Name Game

**Child:** Maa, my friend Raj says his name is the shortest in our class. Is that true?

**Mother:** Well, let us count! R-A-J has 3 letters. What about your other friends?

**Child:** There is Priya -- P-R-I-Y-A -- that is 5 letters. And Subramaniam!

**Mother:** S-U-B-R-A-M-A-N-I-A-M -- that is 11 letters! So Subramaniam has the longest name and Raj has the shortest.

**Child:** Can we do this with number names too?

**Mother:** Of course! The number name "One" has 3 letters, "Three" has 5 letters, and "Seventeen" has 9 letters.

**Child:** Seventeen is so long! What about "Forty Three"?

**Mother:** Count all the letters in both words -- F-O-R-T-Y has 5 and T-H-R-E-E has 5. So "Forty Three" has 10 letters total! We do not count the space.

## Sorting and Grouping

**Child:** Maa, our teacher asked us to group things in our house. But how do I decide the groups?

**Mother:** Think about what some things have in common. Look at the kitchen. What needs electricity to work?

**Child:** The mixer! And the fridge! And the microwave!

**Mother:** Good. And what does not need electricity?

**Child:** The rolling pin, the tawa, plates, spoons...

**Mother:** See? You just made two groups! You can also group things differently -- by colour, by size, or by which room they belong to.

**Child:** So the same things can go into different groups depending on what rule I pick?

**Mother:** Exactly right! That is the fun of grouping.

## Collecting Data

**Child:** Maa, what does "collecting data" mean?

**Mother:** It means gathering information by looking around and counting. For example, stand at our balcony and count how many red cars, blue cars, and white cars pass by in 10 minutes.

**Child:** Oh, like a counting game!

**Mother:** Yes! You write it down in a table. Then you can answer questions like "Which colour car passed the most?" That table of numbers is your data.

**Child:** I want to try that right now, Maa!

**Mother:** Go ahead! Take a notebook and a pencil. You are becoming a little data scientist!
