## Counting Without Numbers

Imagine two sisters, **Kavya** and **Meera**, helping their grandmother count chickens at their farm in a village near Jaipur. But wait -- they had never been to school yet! How could they keep track of 12 chickens going out to the field every morning?

Their grandmother taught them a clever trick:

> **Trick:** Put one pebble in a bowl for each chicken that goes out. When a chicken comes back in the evening, take one pebble out. If the bowl is empty at night, all chickens are safe at home!

This is called **tally marking** -- keeping count by matching one thing to another, without using any numbers at all!

```
Morning:  🪨🪨🪨🪨🪨  (5 pebbles = 5 chickens went out)
Evening:  🪨🪨🪨      (3 pebbles left = 3 chickens still outside!)
```

If all pebbles are removed from the bowl, **every chicken has returned**. If 3 pebbles remain, then **3 chickens are still missing**.

## Comparing Names by Length

Did you know that every word is made up of **letters**? Some words are **long** and some are **short**. Let us look at some Indian fruits:

| Fruit | Number of Letters |
|------|:-:|
| Fig | 3 |
| Pear | 4 |
| Mango | 5 |
| Banana | 6 |
| Coconut | 7 |
| Pineapple | 9 |

- **Pineapple** has the most letters (9). It is the **longest** name.
- **Fig** has the fewest letters (3). It is the **shortest** name.

> **Try this:** Write down the names of 5 friends in your class. Count the letters in each name. Who has the longest name? Who has the shortest?

## Number Names and Their Letters

Just like fruits and animals, numbers have names too! We can count how many letters each number name has.

| Number | Number Name | Letters |
|:------:|-------------|:-------:|
| 1 | One | 3 |
| 2 | Two | 3 |
| 3 | Three | 5 |
| 4 | Four | 4 |
| 5 | Five | 4 |
| 7 | Seven | 5 |
| 12 | Twelve | 6 |
| 25 | Twenty Five | 9 |
| 38 | Thirty Eight | 11 |

When a number name has two words (like "Thirty Eight"), we count the letters in **both words** but not the space between them.

> **Fun fact:** India has a town called **Venkatanarasimharajuvaripeta** in Andhra Pradesh -- it has 28 letters! And there is a tiny river called **Ib** in Odisha with only 2 letters!

## Grouping Things

We can put things into **groups** based on what they have in common. This is called **sorting** or **grouping**.

### Example 1: Kitchen items

| Things that need fire/heat | Things that do not need fire/heat |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Stove | Plate |
| Pressure cooker | Glass |
| Tawa | Spoon |
| Oven | Bowl |

### Example 2: Living and Non-living

| Living things | Non-living things |
|---|---|
| Parrot, Fish, Tulsi plant | Pencil, Stone, Notebook |

The same set of things can be grouped in **different ways**. You could group animals by colour, by number of legs, by what they eat, or by where they live!

> **Remember:** Every item must fit into one group. Nothing should be left out!

## Collecting Information

You can gather information by **observing** and **counting**. This is called **collecting data**.

For example, look at the lunch boxes in your class:

| Type of Lunch | How many children? |
|---|---|
| Roti and sabzi | |
| Rice and dal | |
| Paratha | |
| Sandwich | |

Go around and ask your classmates. Count how many brought each type of lunch. Write the numbers in the table. Now you have collected data!

> **Try this:** Which type of lunch is the most popular in your class? Which is the least popular?

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Tally marks** | A method of counting by making one mark for each item |
| **Longest name** | The name that has the most letters |
| **Shortest name** | The name that has the fewest letters |
| **Number name** | The word form of a number (like "Seven" for 7) |
| **Grouping** | Sorting things together based on something they share |
| **Data** | Information collected by looking, asking, and counting |
