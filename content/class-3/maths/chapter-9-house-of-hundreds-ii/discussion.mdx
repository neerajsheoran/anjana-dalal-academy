## Numbers Beyond 500

**Child:** Maa, we learned numbers up to 500 before. But now teacher says we have to go all the way to 1000! That sounds so big!

**Mother:** It is big, but you already know the trick! After 500 comes 600, then 700, 800, 900, and finally 1000. They are just more hundreds stacked up.

**Child:** So 1000 is like 10 packets of 100?

**Mother:** Exactly! Think of it like this -- if you had 10 bundles of ₹100 notes, you would have ₹1000. That is one thousand.

**Child:** Oh, that is easy! So 847 means 8 hundreds, 4 tens, and 7 ones?

**Mother:** Perfect, beta! You are already a pro at this.

## Numbers on the Number Line

**Child:** Maa, teacher showed us a number line. She said every number has a place on it. But how do I find where a number lives?

**Mother:** Think of it like houses on a street. The number 653 -- first look at the hundreds digit, which is 6. So it lives in the 600s neighbourhood, between 600 and 700. Now look at the tens digit, which is 5. So it lives between 650 and 660.

**Child:** Like our house is on Road Number 12 and between House 45 and House 47?

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! Just like that. Every number has neighbours on both sides. The number just before 653 is 652, and just after is 654.

**Child:** What about 700? Where does it live?

**Mother:** 700 sits right at the border -- it is exactly at the start of the 700s neighbourhood. Its neighbours are 699 and 701.

## Number Patterns and Number Sentences

**Child:** Maa, I love patterns! Teacher wrote 125, 225, 325, 425 and asked what comes next. I said 525!

**Mother:** Very good! How did you figure it out?

**Child:** Each number is 100 more than the one before. So 425 plus 100 is 525!

**Mother:** That is called the **rule** of the pattern. Once you know the rule, you can keep going forever! What if the pattern was 1000, 998, 996, 994?

**Child:** Hmm... each number is 2 less. So the next one is 992, then 990!

**Mother:** Excellent! Now, did you know that we can write the same number in many different ways?

**Child:** How, Maa?

**Mother:** Take 542. We can write it as 500 + 40 + 2. Or as 500 + 42. Or even as 600 - 58! These are all called **number sentences**.

**Child:** Wow, the same number has so many different outfits!

**Mother:** Ha ha, what a lovely way to say it! Yes, just like you can wear different clothes but you are still the same person.

## Bhutasankhya: Word Numerals

**Child:** Maa, teacher told us something really cool. In ancient India, people used words like "Moon" for 1 and "Eyes" for 2. Is that true?

**Mother:** Yes! This system is called **Bhutasankhya**. Indian scholars used everyday words to represent numbers. Moon means 1 because there is only 1 moon. Eyes means 2 because we have 2 eyes. Directions means 4 because there are 4 main directions.

**Child:** That is so clever! So if someone said "Moon-Eyes", they meant 12?

**Mother:** Exactly! And "Eyes-Moon" would mean 21. The order matters, just like in normal numbers.

**Child:** I want to make my own word numbers! Can "cricket stumps" mean 3?

**Mother:** Why not? There are 3 stumps at each end, so "stumps" could stand for 3. You are already thinking like an ancient Indian mathematician!
