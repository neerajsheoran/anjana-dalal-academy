## How Much Can a Vessel Hold? (Capacity)

Pour water into a glass and it holds a little. Pour water into a bucket and it holds a lot! The amount of liquid a vessel can hold is called its **capacity**.

Different vessels have different capacities:

| Vessel | Capacity |
|--------|----------|
| Spoon (chamach) | Very tiny |
| Cup (pyaali) | A little |
| Bowl (katori) | A bit more |
| Jug (jag) | Quite a lot |
| Bucket (balti) | A whole lot! |

> **Simple test:** Want to know which vessel holds more? Fill one vessel with water and pour it into the other. If the water overflows, the first vessel holds more!

## Comparing Capacities

To compare how much two different containers hold, pour their contents into **identical containers**. Then just look at the water levels!

**Example:** Deepa, Farhan, and Gita each drink juice from different-shaped glasses. To find who has the most juice, pour all three into same-sized glasses and compare.

## The Litre

Just as we use a **metre** to measure length, we use a **litre** to measure how much liquid a vessel holds.

A **litre** is a standard unit for measuring liquids.

**How much is 1 litre?**
- A standard water bottle from a shop holds about 1 litre.
- The steel container your milkman uses for measuring is often 1 litre.
- A large jug holds about 1 litre.

| Vessel | About how much? |
|--------|----------|
| A teacup | Much less than 1 litre |
| A bowl | Less than 1 litre |
| A mug | About half a litre |
| A jug | About 1 litre |
| A bucket | Several litres |

---

## Half Litre and Quarter Litre

Just like we split a metre into smaller parts, we can split a litre too:

| Measurement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **1 litre** | A full litre |
| **Half litre** | Half of 1 litre (about the size of a tall mug) |
| **Quarter litre** | One-fourth of 1 litre (about the size of a teacup) |

**How they connect:**

```
1 litre = 2 half litres = 4 quarter litres
```

| Half litres | Litres |
|:---:|:---:|
| 2 half litres | 1 litre |
| 4 half litres | 2 litres |

| Quarter litres | Litres |
|:---:|:---:|
| 2 quarter litres | Half a litre |
| 4 quarter litres | 1 litre |

> **Think green:** Making one single-use plastic bottle (1 litre) uses about **5 litres** of water in the factory! Carrying your own reusable bottle helps save water.

## Heavy or Light? (Weight)

Hold a thick textbook in one hand and a pencil in the other. Which hand drops lower? The one with the textbook! That is because the book is **heavier**.

> **Activity:** Hold your tiffin box in one hand and your eraser in the other. Keep holding for 30 seconds. The hand that feels tired first is holding the **heavier** object!

---

## Comparing Weights

We can compare weights using a **pan balance** (tarazu).

```
    ____
   /    \
  /      \
 | Tiffin |     | Eraser  |
 |________|     |_________|
     down            up
  (heavier)       (lighter)
```

The **heavier** side drops **down**. The **lighter** side goes **up**.

If both sides stay at the **same level**, the two objects weigh the **same**.

## Measuring Weight with Everyday Objects

Before using proper weights, we can use small objects as measuring units:

| Measuring unit | Good for weighing |
|---|---|
| **Coins** | Very light things (pencils, erasers) |
| **Matchbox filled with sand** | Medium things (pencil box, small toy) |

**Example:**
- A pencil might balance with **4 one-rupee coins**.
- A pencil box might balance with **2 matchboxes of sand**.

## The Kilogram

A **kilogram** (kg) is a standard unit for measuring weight. It is the same everywhere.

**How heavy is 1 kilogram?**
- A packet of atta (wheat flour) from the shop is usually **1 kg**.
- A packet of sugar is often **1 kg**.

| Object | Weight |
|--------|--------|
| A banana | Less than 1 kg |
| A pencil box | Less than 1 kg |
| A bag of dal | More than 1 kg |
| A pumpkin | More than 1 kg |
| A packet of sugar | About 1 kg |

---

## Half Kilogram and Quarter Kilogram

| Measurement | Everyday example |
|---|---|
| **1 kilogram (1 kg)** | A packet of atta |
| **Half kilogram (1/2 kg)** | A small packet of paneer |
| **Quarter kilogram (1/4 kg)** | A packet of jeera (cumin) |

**How they connect:**

```
1 kg = 2 half kg = 4 quarter kg
```

> **Home activity:** Pick up a 1 kg sugar packet and feel its weight. Now try to find other things at home that feel about the same. A full water bottle (1 litre) also weighs about 1 kg!

## The Tricky Balls Puzzle

Here is a fun challenge! You have **3 cricket balls** that look identical. One ball is heavier than the other two. You have a **pan balance** but no weights.

**Can you find the heavy ball in just 1 weighing?**

1. Place ball A on the left pan and ball B on the right pan.
2. If one side goes down, that ball is the **heavy** one.
3. If both sides stay level, then ball C (the one you did NOT put on the balance) is the **heavy** one!
