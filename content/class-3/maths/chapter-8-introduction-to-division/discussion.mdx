## Sharing Equally — Why Does It Matter?

**Child:** Maa, today Priya and I had one samosa left in the tiffin. She broke it and took the bigger piece! That is not fair, right?

**Mother:** Not fair at all, beta! When two people share something, both pieces should be the **same size**. That is called **equal sharing**.

**Child:** But how do I know if two pieces are the same size?

**Mother:** Simple trick -- put one piece on top of the other. If they match exactly, they are equal. Try it next time with a piece of paper!

**Child:** Okay Maa, I will do that!

## What is a Half?

**Child:** Maa, teacher said when I divide something into 2 equal parts, each part is called a "half". Is that true even for a chapati?

**Mother:** Absolutely! When I fold a chapati right down the middle and both sides match, each side is **one half**. And what happens if I put both halves back together?

**Child:** We get the full chapati again!

**Mother:** Exactly! **2 halves make 1 whole**. You see halves everywhere -- when I cut a banana in the middle, when Papa folds the newspaper, when you share your Parle-G biscuit with your brother.

**Child:** Oh! So every time I break a biscuit in the middle and both pieces look the same, those are halves?

**Mother:** Yes, beta. But remember -- if one piece is bigger and one is smaller, they are NOT halves. Both must be equal!

## Half and Double

**Child:** Maa, what is a double? Teacher said half and double are opposites.

**Mother:** Let me give you an example. You have **4 marbles**. If I give you the same number again, how many do you have?

**Child:** 4 plus 4 is 8! So 8 is the double of 4?

**Mother:** Perfect! Double means **two times**. And going the other way, 4 is the **half** of 8, because 8 divided by 2 is 4.

**Child:** So to find the double, I multiply by 2. And to find the half, I divide by 2?

**Mother:** You got it! Try this -- Dadi gave you ₹20. What is the double?

**Child:** ₹40! And half of ₹20 is ₹10!

**Mother:** Wah, my mathematician!

## What is a Quarter?

**Child:** Maa, what if we have to share equally between 4 people instead of 2?

**Mother:** Then we split the thing into **4 equal parts**. Each part is called a **quarter**. Think of it this way -- during Diwali, I cut a barfi into 4 equal pieces for you, your brother, Papa, and me. Each of us gets one quarter.

**Child:** Oh, so 4 quarters make 1 whole barfi! But Maa, is a quarter the same as half of a half?

**Mother:** That is a very smart observation! Yes, beta. Take a paper and fold it in half. Now fold that half in half again. Open it -- you will see 4 equal parts. Each part is a quarter. So a **quarter is half of a half**.

**Child:** And 2 quarters are the same as 1 half! Because 2 out of 4 parts is the same as half!

**Mother:** Brilliant! You are thinking like a real mathematician.

## More Than Half or Less Than Half?

**Child:** Maa, I ate most of a gulab jamun and only a little bit was left. Did I eat more than half?

**Mother:** Think about it. If you ate the bigger portion and only a tiny bit was left, then yes -- you ate **more than half**. If you had eaten a small bite and most of the gulab jamun was still there, you would have eaten **less than half**.

**Child:** And if I ate exactly the same amount as what is left?

**Mother:** Then you ate **exactly half**! The trick is to compare the part you ate with the part that is left. Are they equal, or is one bigger?

**Child:** This is easy, Maa! I think I always eat more than half!

**Mother:** Ha ha, I know that very well, beta!
