## The Seed Hiding Game

**Child:** Maa, Nani Maa plays this game where she hides seeds under a cloth and I have to guess how many she hid. How does she always know if I guess wrong?

**Mother:** Because she knows the total! Say she has 18 tamarind seeds and you can see 11 on the table. She just subtracts in her head: 18 minus 11 is 7. So she knows exactly 7 seeds are hidden!

**Child:** Oh! So if I know the total and one part, I can always find the other part?

**Mother:** Exactly! That is the idea behind subtraction. If you have the whole and one piece, just subtract to find the missing piece.

**Child:** Let me try. If there are 22 seeds and I can see 13... then 22 minus 13 is... 9 seeds are hidden!

**Mother:** Wonderful! You cracked Nani Maa's trick!

## Jumping on the Number Line

**Child:** Maa, my teacher says we can add by jumping on a number line. But what does that mean?

**Mother:** Imagine a row of stepping stones numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on. If you stand on stone 18 and want to add 9, you jump forward 9 stones.

**Child:** So I land on 27? Because 18 + 9 = 27?

**Mother:** Yes! And here is a trick to make it easier. Instead of 9 tiny jumps, first jump 2 steps to reach 20 (a nice round number), and then jump 7 more to reach 27.

**Child:** Oh, so I break the 9 into 2 and 7?

**Mother:** Exactly. Jumping to a round number first makes the maths simpler. You can use the same trick for subtraction -- just jump backward!

## Adding Big Numbers

**Child:** Maa, how do I add 28 and 34? Those are big numbers!

**Mother:** Let us break them into tens and ones. 28 is 2 tens and 8 ones. 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones.

**Child:** So I add the tens together: 2 tens plus 3 tens is 5 tens, which is 50.

**Mother:** Good! Now add the ones: 8 ones plus 4 ones is 12 ones.

**Child:** But 12 ones is more than 10! What do I do?

**Mother:** 12 ones is the same as 1 ten and 2 ones. So add that extra ten to the 5 tens. You get 6 tens and 2 ones, which is 62!

**Child:** 28 + 34 = 62! That was not so hard after all!

**Mother:** See? Breaking numbers into tens and ones makes everything simpler.

## Estimating Before Solving

**Child:** Maa, what does "estimate" mean?

**Mother:** It means making a smart guess before you solve the problem. For example, if I ask you "Is 88 + 15 more than 100 or less than 100?" what would you guess?

**Child:** 88 is close to 90, and 90 + 15 is 105. So it is more than 100!

**Mother:** Perfect! You rounded 88 to 90 and then added 15. That is estimating! Now solve it properly.

**Child:** 88 + 15... 88 plus 2 is 90, plus 13 more is 103. Wait, let me recount. 88 + 12 = 100, plus 3 more = 103. Hmm, but Maa, I said 105 when estimating!

**Mother:** That is fine! An estimate does not have to be exact. It just has to be close. You got "more than 100" which was correct. The actual answer 103 is indeed more than 100.

**Child:** I see! Estimating helps me check if my answer makes sense!

**Mother:** Exactly right. Always estimate first, then solve. If your answer is very different from your estimate, you should check your work again.
