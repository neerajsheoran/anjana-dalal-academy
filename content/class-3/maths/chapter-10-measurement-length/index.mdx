## Measuring Length

Have you ever wondered how tall the classroom door is, or how long your teacher's desk is? To find out, you need to **measure** it.

There are many things you can use to measure:
- Your **hand span** (stretch your hand wide open -- the distance from thumb tip to little finger tip)
- Your **footsteps** (walk heel-to-toe and count)
- A **pencil** or a **strip of paper**
- A proper **metre rod** or **measuring tape**

> **Something interesting:** When Meera and Aarav both measure the same desk using their hand spans, Meera counts 6 spans and Aarav counts 7 spans. Why? Because Meera's hands are bigger! Different people get different answers with non-standard tools.

---

## Comparing Lengths

To figure out which of two things is **longer** or **shorter**, we compare them.

### Simple ways to compare:

1. **Line them up:** Place both things next to each other with one end matching. The one that sticks out further is **longer**.

2. **Use a string:** If you cannot move the objects, cut a string to match the length of one. Then hold the string against the other object and compare.

```
Meera's ribbon:   |----------|
Aarav's ribbon:   |--------------|

Aarav's ribbon is LONGER.
```

> **Real-life use:** Want to check if a new bookshelf can fit against a wall? Measure the wall with a string, then hold the string along the shelf. If the string is longer, the shelf will fit!

## Measuring Without a Ruler

Before using proper tools, we can measure with things around us:

| Measuring tool | Works well for |
|---|---|
| **Hand span** | Small things like books, boxes |
| **Footsteps** | Distances across a room or corridor |
| **Pencil lengths** | Medium things like desks, charts |
| **Paper strips** | Walls, blackboards, tables |

But here is the problem -- everyone's hand, foot, and pencil are **different sizes**. So we need a measuring tool that is **the same for everyone**.

## The Metre

A **metre** is a standard unit of length. It is exactly the same everywhere in India and the whole world!

**How long is one metre?**
- About the height of a small child (around 3 years old).
- About the width of a single bed.
- A teacher's metre rod shows exactly one metre.

### Making your own metre rope:

1. Get a **metre rod** from your teacher and a piece of **string**.
2. Tie a knot at one end of the string.
3. Lay the string along the metre rod.
4. Tie a knot at the other end where the rod finishes.

Now you have your very own **metre rope** for measuring!

## Things Around Us: More or Less Than a Metre?

| Less than one metre | About one metre | More than one metre |
|---|---|---|
| A ruler (30 cm) | Width of a classroom door | A classroom wall |
| A lunch box | Height of a young child | A hallway |
| A textbook | A large umbrella when open | A mango tree |
| A cricket bat handle | A walking cane | A school bus |

## Half a Metre and Quarter of a Metre

We can break one metre into smaller parts:

```
One metre rope:
|---------------------------------------------------|

Fold in half = Half a metre:
|-------------------------|

Fold in half again = Quarter of a metre:
|------------|
```

- **Half a metre** = the metre rope folded into 2 equal parts
- **Quarter of a metre** = the metre rope folded into 4 equal parts

![A metre rope folded into half and quarter showing the relative sizes](metre-half-quarter.png)

| Measurement | About how long? |
|---|---|
| Quarter of a metre | About the length of a big pencil |
| Half a metre | About the length from your elbow to your fingertips |
| One metre | About the width of a door |

## Interesting Body Measurements

Our bodies have some surprising measurement connections:

| Statement | Try it yourself! |
|---|---|
| Your head is about **3 hand spans** around | Wrap a string around your head and check! |
| Your **forearm** (elbow to wrist) is about as long as your **foot** | Place your foot along your forearm! |
| Your **height** is about the same as your **arms stretched wide** | Stand against a wall and check! |

> **Note:** These are not perfectly exact for every person, but they are surprisingly close for most people. Measure yourself and your classmates to see!

## Estimating Lengths

**Estimating** means making a smart guess before you actually measure. The more you practise, the better your guesses become!

Try these:
1. Guess how many hand spans long your desk is. Then measure and check.
2. Guess whether the blackboard is more or less than 2 metres wide. Then measure.
3. Cut a piece of ribbon that you think is exactly one metre. Then compare with a metre rod!

> **Why estimate?** Estimating helps you think about sizes before measuring. Builders, tailors, and carpenters all estimate before they measure!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Length** | How long, tall, or wide something is |
| **Measure** | To find the exact size of something |
| **Hand span** | The width of your open hand from thumb to little finger |
| **Metre** | A standard unit for measuring length (same everywhere) |
| **Half metre** | Half of one metre |
| **Quarter metre** | One-fourth of one metre |
| **Estimate** | A smart guess about a measurement before actually measuring |
| **Compare** | Checking two things to see which is longer or shorter |
