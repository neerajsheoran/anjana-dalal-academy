## How Much Can a Vessel Hold?

**Child:** Maa, why do you use a big pot for cooking dal but a small pot for making chai?

**Mother:** Because dal needs more water than chai! The big pot can **hold** more liquid. The amount a vessel can hold is called its **capacity**.

**Child:** So the bucket in the bathroom has more capacity than my water glass?

**Mother:** Much more! If you tried to pour a full bucket of water into a glass, the water would overflow everywhere. The bucket holds many, many glasses of water.

**Child:** How do I find out which of two pots holds more?

**Mother:** Easy trick -- fill one pot with water and pour it into the other. If the water overflows, the first pot holds more. If the second pot still has space left, then the second pot holds more!

## The Litre

**Child:** Maa, when you buy milk, the packet says "1 litre". What is a litre?

**Mother:** A **litre** is a standard way to measure liquids. Just like we use metres to measure how long something is, we use litres to measure how much liquid we have.

**Child:** So 1 litre is always the same amount?

**Mother:** Yes! Whether you buy milk in Delhi or in Chennai, 1 litre is the same amount everywhere. That is what makes it a **standard unit**.

**Child:** And what about half a litre?

**Mother:** Half a litre is exactly what it sounds like -- half of 1 litre. Your tall steel mug holds about half a litre. And a small teacup holds about a quarter litre.

**Child:** So 2 of my tall mugs would fill a full litre bottle?

**Mother:** Exactly right, beta! 2 half litres make 1 whole litre.

## Heavy or Light?

**Child:** Maa, today teacher asked us to hold a thick book in one hand and a pencil in the other. The book hand went down! Why?

**Mother:** Because the book is **heavier** than the pencil. When something is heavy, it pulls your hand down more.

**Child:** Is there a way to compare weights properly?

**Mother:** Yes! People use a **pan balance** -- a tarazu. You put one thing on each side. The heavier thing makes its side go down. The lighter thing goes up.

**Child:** Like the one the sabzi wala uses at the market?

**Mother:** Exactly! He puts vegetables on one side and metal weights on the other. When both sides are level, he knows the vegetables weigh the same as the weights.

**Child:** And the standard unit for weight is kilogram, right?

**Mother:** Yes! A **kilogram** is the standard unit. Pick up the sugar packet in our kitchen -- it is 1 kg. Now pick up a banana. The banana is much lighter, less than 1 kg. And the rice bag is heavier -- it is more than 1 kg.

## Half Kilogram and Quarter Kilogram

**Child:** Maa, at the shop I saw a packet that said "500 grams". Is that the same as half a kilogram?

**Mother:** Yes, beta! 500 grams is half a kilogram. And 250 grams is a quarter kilogram. Just like with litres:

**Child:** So 2 packets of 500 grams make 1 kilogram?

**Mother:** Correct! And 4 packets of 250 grams also make 1 kilogram. You are getting very good at this!

**Child:** Maa, when you go shopping tomorrow, can I come and read all the weight labels?

**Mother:** Of course! That will be the best way to practise. You can check whether things are measured in kilograms, half kilograms, or quarter kilograms.
