## Noticing Things Around Us

One rainy morning, **Tara** sat by the window watching the rain. She looked around her room and began wondering -- "My desk is made of **wood**, my water bottle is **plastic**, the window is **glass**, and this coin in my hand is **metal**. Everything is made of something!"

Tara started a game. She pointed at each object and tried to guess its material:

- The **door** -- wood
- The **mirror** -- glass
- Her **eraser** -- rubber
- The **curtain** -- cloth
- Her mother's **copper puja lota** -- metal

"Everything in the world is made of some material!" she said to herself. And she was right!

---

## What Are Objects Made Of?

Every single object around us is made from one or more **materials**. Here are the most common ones:

| Material | Objects Made From It |
|---|---|
| **Wood** | Desks, doors, almirahs, cricket bats, rolling pins (belan) |
| **Metal** | Keys, locks, tawa, pressure cooker, coins, scissors |
| **Glass** | Windows, spectacles, mirrors, light bulbs, drinking glasses |
| **Plastic** | Water bottles, toothbrush handles, lunch boxes, combs, buckets |
| **Cloth (Fabric)** | Shirts, sarees, curtains, towels, school bags |
| **Clay** | Matkas, kulhads, diyas, flower pots, bricks |
| **Rubber** | Erasers, tyres, slippers (chappals), rubber bands |

> Look around your classroom right now. How many different materials can you spot?

## Where Do Materials Come From?

Every material has a **source** -- a place in nature where it begins:

| Material | Where It Comes From |
|---|---|
| **Wood** | Trunks and branches of **trees** |
| **Metal** | Special rocks deep inside the earth called **ore** (dug out through mining) |
| **Glass** | Made by heating **sand** to an extremely high temperature until it melts |
| **Cloth** | Cotton from **cotton plants**, wool from **sheep**, silk from **silkworms** |
| **Clay** | Wet **soil and mud** from river banks and fields |
| **Rubber** | Sticky liquid called **latex** that flows from **rubber trees** |
| **Plastic** | Made from **petroleum** (crude oil found deep underground) |

> **Surprising fact:** The glass in your windows was once ordinary sand! When sand is heated to over 1700 degrees, it melts and can be shaped into glass objects.

## Can You See Through It?

Have you noticed that you can see clearly through a window but not through a wall? Materials behave differently when light hits them:

| Type | What Happens to Light | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** | Light passes through completely -- you can see clearly | Window glass, clean water, cling wrap |
| **Translucent** | Only some light passes through -- things look blurry | Butter paper, frosted bathroom glass, thin oiled paper |
| **Opaque** | No light passes through -- you cannot see at all | Wooden door, metal plate, cardboard, thick curtain |

Tara could see the mango tree outside through the **glass window** because glass is transparent. But she could not see through the **brick wall** because brick is opaque.

> **Warning:** Never look directly at the sun, even through glass or sunglasses. The sun's rays are so strong they can damage your eyes permanently.

## Fun with Coloured See-Through Materials

If you wrap a piece of **red cellophane paper** around a torch and shine it on a white wall, the wall looks red! The coloured material lets only its own colour of light pass through.

Try these fun experiments:

- Look through a **blue sweet wrapper** -- everything looks blue!
- Look through a **yellow cellophane** -- the world turns yellow!
- Put a **blue wrapper over a yellow one** -- things may look greenish!

This is why coloured glasses on buildings and cars make the inside look a certain colour.

## How Materials Feel and Behave

Materials have special **qualities** that we can test with our senses. These qualities are called **properties**:

| Property | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Smooth** | Feels flat and even when you touch it | Mirror, polished marble floor, silk cloth |
| **Rough** | Feels bumpy and uneven | Sandpaper, tree bark, jute bag |
| **Shiny** | Sparkles and reflects light | Gold jewellery, steel plate, polished copper |
| **Dull** | Does not sparkle or reflect light | Cardboard, rubber, unpolished wood |
| **Hard** | Difficult to scratch or break | Diamond, iron nail, stone |
| **Soft** | Easy to press and squeeze | Cotton, sponge, foam pillow |
| **Flexible** | Can be bent without breaking | Rubber band, thin plastic sheet, rope |
| **Rigid** | Stays stiff and cannot be bent | Iron rod, glass pane, wooden ruler |

## Listening to Materials

Tara picked up a **steel spoon** and tapped it on different objects. Each one made a different sound!

| What She Tapped | Sound She Heard |
|---|---|
| **Steel thali** | Tang-tang! (loud, ringing) |
| **Wooden table** | Thuk-thuk! (dull, solid) |
| **Glass tumbler** | Ting! (clear, high-pitched) |
| **Plastic dabba** | Thup-thup! (soft, flat) |
| **Cotton pillow** | Almost no sound at all! |

> Tara had an idea: she tapped different objects in a rhythm and made her own music! You can create a "kitchen band" using vessels of different materials.

## Bending and Stretching

Some materials bend easily, and some refuse to bend at all:

- A **rubber band** stretches and springs back -- it is **elastic**.
- A **steel spoon** stays stiff no matter how hard you try -- it is **rigid**.
- A **cotton dupatta** folds and drapes any way you want -- it is **flexible**.

Imagine wearing a shirt made of **glass** -- it would crack the moment you moved! Or sitting on a chair made of **cotton** -- you would sink right through! We use **the right material for the right job**.

## Solids, Liquids, and Gases

Everything in the world exists in one of three forms:

| Form | What It Is Like | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid** | Has its own shape and stays put | Book, coin, ice cube, chapati, stone |
| **Liquid** | Flows and fills the shape of whatever container it is poured into | Water, milk, oil, soup, honey |
| **Gas** | Spreads in all directions and fills up any space | Air, steam from a kettle, smell of agarbatti |

- Put a **stone** in a glass, a bowl, or on a plate -- it keeps the same shape. That is a solid.
- Pour **water** into a glass -- it takes the glass's shape. Pour it into a bowl -- it takes the bowl's shape. That is a liquid.
- Open a bottle of **perfume** in one corner of the room -- soon the smell reaches every corner. That is how a gas behaves.

> **Tricky ones:** Sand flows like water, but each tiny grain is actually a solid! Butter is soft and squishy, but it is also a solid because it holds its shape.

## Things from Nature and Things We Make

Everything around us falls into two groups:

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural** | Found in nature without human effort | Rivers, mountains, clouds, birds, flowers, soil |
| **Man-made** (Artificial) | Created by people using natural materials | Buildings, clothes, vehicles, books, furniture |

Natural things can be **living** (like trees, fish, and butterflies) or **non-living** (like rocks, water, and sunlight).

> Every man-made thing started as something natural! A **wooden chair** was once a tree. A **glass window** was once sand. A **cotton shirt** was once a fluffy cotton plant.

---

## Key Points to Remember

- Every object is made of some **material** -- wood, metal, glass, plastic, cloth, clay, or rubber.
- Each material has a **source** in nature -- trees give wood, sand gives glass, ore gives metal, petroleum gives plastic.
- **Transparent** materials let light through, **translucent** let some light through, and **opaque** block all light.
- Materials have **properties** like smooth/rough, hard/soft, shiny/dull, and flexible/rigid.
- Different materials make **different sounds** when tapped.
- Matter exists as **solids** (fixed shape), **liquids** (flow and fill containers), or **gases** (spread everywhere).
- Things are either **natural** (from nature) or **man-made** (created by people).
- We choose the **right material** for every object based on its properties.
