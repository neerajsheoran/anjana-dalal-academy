## Travelling to the Mela

**Child:** Maa, can we go to the mela this weekend? My friend Ananya said there is a giant ferris wheel!

**Mother:** Yes! We will all go together -- you, Baba, Dadi, and me. But first, let us plan. We need to catch the bus from the main road.

**Child:** Maa, Dadi walks slowly. Will she be okay on the bus?

**Mother:** That is very thoughtful of you, beta. Buses have special **reserved seats** for senior citizens and people who need extra help. Baba and you can help Dadi climb up the steps. The conductor will also help.

**Child:** Maa, why do children get half tickets and adults get full tickets?

**Mother:** Because children take up less space and are lighter, the fare is less for them. When you grow up, you will need a full ticket too!

**Child:** And Maa, why should I not put my hand out of the window on the bus?

**Mother:** Because another vehicle, a tree branch, or a pole could hit your hand. Keeping your hands and head inside keeps you safe. That is an important rule for any vehicle -- bus, train, or car.

## Fun and Food at the Mela

**Child:** Maa, the mela was so much fun! I loved the puppet show about the clever fox.

**Mother:** I liked the toy stalls. Did you see those beautiful wooden puppets? They were made by hand by craftspeople who spend many hours creating each one.

**Child:** And the gol gappe were SO tasty! Why did you make us wash hands before eating, Maa?

**Mother:** Think about it -- at the mela, you touched toys, held the railing on the ride, and played with mud near the tent. Your hands had germs on them that you cannot see with your eyes. If you eat without washing, those germs go into your stomach and can make you sick.

**Child:** Oh! That is why Rohan got a tummy ache last time -- he did not wash his hands!

**Mother:** Exactly. And one more important thing -- did you notice what Baba did with the empty plates after eating?

**Child:** He put them in the dustbin!

**Mother:** Good. Keeping the mela clean is everyone's duty. Imagine if every visitor threw rubbish on the ground -- the mela would become a rubbish dump! Always find a dustbin and use it.

## Safety and Helpers at the Mela

**Child:** Maa, why were there so many police and ambulances at the mela? Was something wrong?

**Mother:** No, beta, nothing was wrong. They are there **just in case** something happens. When thousands of people gather in one place, there could be small accidents, someone could faint from the heat, or a child could get lost.

**Child:** There was also a tent that said "Lost and Found." What is that?

**Mother:** If a child gets separated from their family in the crowd, they can go to that tent. The volunteers there announce the child's name on the loudspeaker, and the family comes to find them. That is why you should always **remember your parents' phone number** and your home address.

**Child:** I know your number, Maa! And Baba's too!

**Mother:** Very good! Also, did you notice the fire truck parked near the gate? With so many food stalls using gas and fire, there is always a small chance of fire. Firefighters are trained to put out fires quickly. Every helper at the mela has an important job -- police, doctors, firefighters, and volunteers -- all working together to keep everyone safe.
