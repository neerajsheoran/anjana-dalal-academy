## Keeping Our Body Clean

**Child:** Maa, today our teacher said that germs are everywhere -- on the desk, on the door handle, even on my pencil! That is so scary!

**Mother:** It is not scary, beta. Germs are indeed everywhere, but they cannot harm you easily if you stay clean. That is why we wash our hands with soap -- the soap chases the germs away!

**Child:** Oh! So when I wash my hands before eating, I am protecting myself?

**Mother:** Exactly! And when you brush your teeth, take a bath, trim your nails, and wear clean clothes -- all of these stop germs from making you sick. Think of cleanliness as your invisible shield!

**Child:** Like a superhero shield? I like that!

**Mother:** Yes! And you know what? When you are clean, you also feel fresh and happy. Nobody likes sitting next to someone with dirty nails and messy hair, right?

**Child:** Ha ha, true! Okay Maa, I will be the cleanest superhero in my class!

## Good Habits for Every Day

**Child:** Maa, I brushed my teeth only once today -- in the morning. Is that enough?

**Mother:** Not quite, beta. We should brush **twice** -- once after waking up and once before going to sleep. At night, food bits are stuck between your teeth. If you do not brush, germs eat those bits and make holes in your teeth!

**Child:** Holes in my teeth?! That sounds terrible!

**Mother:** Those holes are called cavities, and they can really hurt. That is why brushing at night is just as important as brushing in the morning.

**Child:** What about washing hands? I wash them before eating. When else?

**Mother:** After using the toilet, after playing outside, and after touching a pet. Basically, anytime your hands might have picked up germs.

**Child:** That is a lot of washing! But if it keeps me healthy, I will do it.

**Mother:** Good boy! And remember -- at least 8 to 10 hours of sleep, plenty of water, and one hour of outdoor play every day. These are the building blocks of a strong, healthy body.

## Cleaning Teeth -- Old and New Ways

**Child:** Maa, Ritu told me her Nana cleans his teeth with a tree branch! Is that even real?

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes it is very real! It is called **datun** -- an ancient Indian practice. Ritu's Nana probably uses a neem twig. He chews one end until it becomes soft like a brush and uses it to clean his teeth and massage his gums.

**Child:** But why a neem twig? Why not mango or guava?

**Mother:** Neem has special properties that kill germs. For thousands of years, people in India used twigs of neem, babool, and karanj trees for cleaning teeth. In fact, the idea of our modern toothbrush came from this ancient practice!

**Child:** Really?! So our toothbrush is inspired by a tree branch?

**Mother:** Yes, beta! And Dadi still uses salt and charcoal powder sometimes. Natural cleaning methods have been part of Indian life for ages.

**Child:** Neem is bitter though, right?

**Mother:** Very bitter! But babool twigs taste milder. Both keep teeth strong and gums healthy. Our ancestors were very wise about health.

## Nature's Cleaning Kit

**Child:** Maa, if people did not have soap and shampoo long ago, how did they stay clean?

**Mother:** Great question! They used gifts from nature. For washing hair, women used **reetha** -- also called soapnut. When you rub reetha in water, it makes foam just like shampoo! They also used shikakai and amla for shiny hair.

**Child:** What about bathing soap?

**Mother:** Instead of soap, people used a paste of **besan** mixed with **haldi** and cream. It cleaned the skin and made it glow. Even today, brides in Indian weddings have a haldi ceremony!

**Child:** Oh yes, I saw that at Masi's wedding! Everyone rubbed haldi paste on her face and arms!

**Mother:** That is right! And for washing clothes, reetha water worked just like detergent. All natural, no chemicals.

**Child:** Nature really has everything we need, Maa!

## Running, Jumping, and Playing

**Child:** Maa, after running three rounds of the playground today, my heart was going dhak-dhak-dhak so fast! I thought something was wrong.

**Mother:** Nothing is wrong, beta! That is your heart exercising. When you run, your heart pumps blood faster to send energy to your legs and arms. Over time, this makes your heart stronger.

**Child:** So the dhak-dhak is actually a good thing?

**Mother:** Yes! And the heavy breathing means your lungs are working hard to take in more air. The sweating cools your body down. All of these are signs that exercise is doing its job.

**Child:** Nani cannot run because of her knees. Does she get exercise?

**Mother:** Nani does gentle chair exercises every morning. She also plays carrom and sudoku to keep her mind sharp. She always says, "A lazy body and a lazy mind both get rusty!"

**Child:** I love how Nani says that! So our mind also needs exercise?

**Mother:** Absolutely! Games like chess, puzzles, and even reading are exercise for the brain. A truly healthy person keeps both body and mind active.

## Playing in Safe Places

**Child:** Maa, Rohan and his friends were playing cricket on the main road yesterday. A car almost hit the ball and nearly scared them!

**Mother:** That is very dangerous, beta! The main road is NOT a safe place to play. Cars, buses, and autos move fast, and a driver may not see children in time.

**Child:** Then where should we play?

**Mother:** Parks, playgrounds, school grounds, and your building compound are all safe places. They have open space with no vehicles.

**Child:** What about the terrace? Some children play there.

**Mother:** Only if it has a proper **railing** all around. A terrace without a railing is very unsafe -- you could fall from a height. And never play near a construction site or near a pond without an adult around.

**Child:** Got it, Maa -- parks and playgrounds are safe, roads and open terraces are not.

## Being Smart Around Strangers

**Child:** Maa, today a man at the park offered me a chocolate. I said no. Did I do the right thing?

**Mother:** You did exactly the right thing, beta! I am very proud of you. We should never accept food, sweets, or gifts from people we do not know.

**Child:** But he looked like a nice uncle. He was smiling.

**Mother:** Not all strangers are bad, but we cannot tell who is safe and who is not just by looking at them. That is why we follow safety rules -- never accept things from strangers, never go anywhere with them, and never get into their car.

**Child:** What if someone says "Your Maa sent me to pick you up"?

**Mother:** Do you remember our secret code word?

**Child:** Yes! It is "Tulsi"!

**Mother:** If the person does not know the code word "Tulsi," then do NOT go with them. Find a teacher or a trusted adult and tell them what happened.

**Child:** The code word is like a key! Only the right person has it.

**Mother:** Exactly, beta. You are already very smart about safety!
