## Keeping Our Body Clean

Think about your favourite white shirt. If you do not wash it for days, it becomes dirty and starts smelling. Our body is the same! Every day, dust, sweat, and tiny germs collect on our skin, hair, and teeth. Cleaning our body every day keeps germs away and helps us stay fresh and healthy.

**A morning checklist for a clean, healthy you:**

- Did I **brush my teeth** properly?
- Did I **take a bath** with soap and water?
- Did I **comb my hair** neatly?
- Did I **wash my hands** with soap?
- Are my **fingernails and toenails** trimmed short and clean?
- Did I put on **clean clothes**?

When our body is clean, we feel **confident and cheerful**. Nobody likes sitting next to someone who smells bad or has dirty nails, right? Cleanliness makes us pleasant to be around.

## Good Habits for Every Day

Staying healthy is not about doing one big thing once in a while. It is about doing small good things **every single day**.

| Good Habit | When and How Often |
|---|---|
| **Brush your teeth** | Two times -- once after waking up, once before sleeping |
| **Take a bath** | Every single day |
| **Wash hands with soap** | Before every meal, after using the toilet, and after playing outside |
| **Rinse your mouth** | After every meal or snack |
| **Drink water** | 6 to 8 glasses throughout the day |
| **Eat nutritious food** | Three meals and two healthy snacks daily |
| **Sleep well** | At least 8-10 hours every night |
| **Play outdoors** | At least one hour every day |

> When you follow these habits every day without fail, your body becomes like a strong fort -- germs cannot easily attack you!

## Cleaning Teeth -- Old and New Ways

**Ritu's Nana** (grandfather) lives in a village near Varanasi. Every morning, he breaks a fresh twig from the **neem tree** in his courtyard and uses it to clean his teeth. He chews one end until it becomes soft like a little brush, and then he rubs it on his teeth and gums.

This age-old Indian practice is called **datun**. For thousands of years, people in India used twigs of **neem**, **babool**, and **karanj** trees to clean their teeth. Neem is especially good because it kills germs naturally.

Today, most of us use a **toothbrush and toothpaste**. But the idea of a bristled stick for cleaning teeth actually came from the ancient Indian practice of datun!

> Neem twigs taste quite bitter, but babool twigs have a milder flavour. Both keep teeth and gums healthy.

## Nature's Cleaning Kit

Long before shampoos, soaps, and detergents were invented, Indian families used natural ingredients for cleaning. Many of these are still used in villages today!

| What to Clean | Natural Ingredient Used |
|---|---|
| **Teeth** | Neem or babool twigs (datun), charcoal powder, salt |
| **Hair** | Reetha (soapnut), shikakai, amla powder |
| **Skin** | Besan (gram flour) with haldi (turmeric) and malai (cream) |
| **Clothes** | Reetha (soapnut) water |
| **Floors** | A paste of cow dung mixed with mud (this also keeps insects away!) |

> You can make a natural cleaner for the kitchen by soaking orange or lemon peels in vinegar for two weeks. It smells fresh and cleans well -- no chemicals needed!

## Animals Have Cleaning Habits Too!

We are not the only ones who like staying clean. Animals have their own clever ways:

- **Crows** take a bath by splashing in puddles and birdbaths.
- **Cats** spend a lot of time licking their paws and fur to keep clean.
- **Elephants** suck up water in their trunk and spray it all over their body -- their own personal shower!
- **Monkeys** sit together and pick tiny insects out of each other's hair. This is called **grooming**.
- **Dogs** roll on the ground and shake hard to get rid of dirt and water.
- **Parrots** spread their wings in the rain to get a free bath!

## Running, Jumping, and Playing

Have you noticed what happens to your body when you run really fast? Your heart beats loudly -- dhak-dhak-dhak! Your face turns pink. You start breathing heavily. Drops of sweat appear on your forehead.

All of this means your body is **exercising**! Exercise makes your heart pump faster, your lungs breathe deeper, and your muscles work harder. Over time, this makes everything stronger.

**Fun ways to exercise every day:**

- Playing **catch-catch** or **tag** with friends
- **Skipping** with a rope
- **Cycling** around the colony
- Doing **yoga** stretches in the morning
- **Dancing** to your favourite song
- **Climbing** the monkey bars at the park

Neha's **Nani** has pain in her knees and cannot run. But she does gentle **chair exercises** every morning and plays **carrom** and **sudoku** to keep her mind sharp. She always says, "A lazy body and a lazy mind both get rusty!"

> Exercise is not just for the body. Games like chess, puzzles, and reading also exercise our **mind**!

## Playing in Safe Places

Playing is wonderful, but we must choose **safe spots**. Here is how to decide:

| Place | Safe or Not? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **A park or garden** | Safe | Open area with no vehicles |
| **School playground** | Safe | Designed for children with teachers nearby |
| **Your building compound** | Safe | Familiar area with neighbours around |
| **A busy main road** | Not safe | Cars, buses, and trucks can hit you |
| **An open terrace without railing** | Not safe | You could fall from a height |
| **Near a construction site** | Not safe | Falling bricks, sharp objects, and machines |
| **Near a pond or river without an adult** | Not safe | Risk of drowning |

## Being Smart Around Strangers

When we are outside -- at a market, a park, or a fair -- we may see people we do not know. These are **strangers**. Most strangers are ordinary people, but we must still be careful:

- **Never accept** food, sweets, or toys from a stranger
- **Never go anywhere** with a stranger, even if they say "Your Maa sent me"
- **Never climb into** a stranger's car or auto
- Stay close to your **parents or a trusted grown-up** in crowded places
- If someone makes you feel **uncomfortable**, tell your parents or teacher right away

> **Safety tip:** Create a **secret code word** with your parents. If someone comes to pick you up, they must know the code word. If they do not know it, do not go with them!

## Tracking Your Health Habits

A simple way to build good habits is to **track** them. Make a chart for the week and put a tick mark every day you complete a habit:

| Habit to Track | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| **Brushed teeth twice** | Prevents cavities and bad breath |
| **Took a bath** | Keeps skin clean and germ-free |
| **Ate fruits and vegetables** | Gives vitamins for a strong body |
| **Slept 8-10 hours** | Helps body and brain recharge |
| **Played outside for 1 hour** | Keeps heart and muscles strong |
| **Limited screen time** | Too much TV or phone hurts eyes and posture |

> When you sleep well, eat well, play well, and stay clean, you feel like a **superhero** -- full of energy and ready for anything!

## Key Points to Remember

- **Cleanliness** is the foundation of good health -- brush twice, bathe daily, wash hands often, and trim your nails.
- Indians have used **natural cleaning methods** for thousands of years -- neem datun for teeth, reetha for hair, and besan for skin.
- **Exercise** makes our heart, lungs, and muscles stronger -- play outdoors every day.
- Choose **safe places** to play -- parks and playgrounds, not roads or rooftops.
- Be **careful with strangers** -- never accept gifts from them or go with them. Use a secret code word with your parents.
- Both **body and mind** need exercise -- play active games and also think-games like chess and puzzles.
- Track your daily habits to **build a healthy routine** that lasts a lifetime.
