## Why Our Body Needs Food

**Child:** Maa, today I was playing cricket and suddenly I felt so tired that I could not even lift the bat. Why does that happen?

**Mother:** Because your body ran out of energy, beta! Food is like fuel for our body -- just like a car cannot run without petrol, our body cannot work without food.

**Child:** So when I eat breakfast, I am actually filling up my fuel tank?

**Mother:** Exactly! And food does not just give energy. It also helps you grow taller and stronger, and it protects you from falling sick.

**Child:** That is three jobs! No wonder Maa always says "finish your food"!

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! And different tastes mean different things. Sweet foods like gud give quick energy. Sour foods like nimbu have vitamins. Even bitter karela is very good for health!

**Child:** Ugh, I still do not like karela, Maa!

**Mother:** That is okay, beta. There are so many other foods to choose from!

## Aarav Learns About Good Food

**Child:** Maa, my friend Kabir eats only Maggi noodles and chips every day. He says he does not like dal-roti.

**Mother:** Hmm, that is like Aarav's story. You know Aarav?

**Child:** Who is Aarav?

**Mother:** Aarav was a boy who loved cricket but ate only plain rice and chips. He felt tired all the time and could not play well. The doctor told him his body was like a house built only with bricks -- no cement, no doors, no windows.

**Child:** That sounds like a broken house!

**Mother:** Right! Our body also needs many different types of food -- grains for energy, vegetables for strength, fruits for vitamins, dal for growing muscles, and milk for strong bones. When Aarav started eating all these, he became the best bowler in his team in just two months!

**Child:** Maybe I should tell Kabir this story!

**Mother:** Good idea. Tell him eating right is the real superpower.

## What Should We Eat?

**Child:** Maa, how do I know if my food is healthy? My thali has roti, dal, rice, and sabzi. Is that enough?

**Mother:** That is already very good! But here is a simple trick -- look at the **colours** on your plate. The more colourful your thali, the healthier it is!

**Child:** Colours? Like what?

**Mother:** Green from palak or beans, orange from gajar, white from rice and dahi, yellow from dal, red from tamatar. Each colour gives your body something different.

**Child:** Oh! My plate has green, yellow, and white today. I need more colours!

**Mother:** Yes! Add a piece of fruit like an orange or a guava, and you will have a rainbow thali!

**Child:** Rainbow thali! I like that idea, Maa!

## Eating with the Seasons

**Child:** Maa, why does Dadi make gajar ka halwa only in winter? I want it in summer too!

**Mother:** Gajar ka halwa is made with ghee and sugar, beta. It gives a lot of warmth to the body. In summer, your body is already hot, so eating heavy warm food can make you uncomfortable.

**Child:** Oh! That is why we eat watermelon and drink nimbu shikanji in summer -- to cool down?

**Mother:** Exactly right! In summer, our body sweats a lot and loses water. Cool fruits like tarbooz and kharbooja put the water back. In winter, warm foods like sarson ka saag and bajra roti help keep us warm from inside.

**Child:** And in monsoon, Maa makes pakode and ginger chai!

**Mother:** Yes! Warm snacks feel so cozy when it is raining outside, do they not?

**Child:** Now I understand why we eat different things in different months. Our body is very smart!

## Where Does Our Food Come From?

**Child:** Maa, my teacher asked us what part of the plant we eat when we eat palak. I said leaves and got it right!

**Mother:** Very good! We eat so many different parts of plants. Leaves like palak and methi, roots like gajar and mooli, stems like aloo and adrak...

**Child:** Wait, Maa -- aloo is a stem?! I thought it was a root!

**Mother:** Many people think that! But aloo grows from the stem of the potato plant underground. It is called a **tuber**, which is a type of stem.

**Child:** What about rice and wheat?

**Mother:** Those are **seeds** of their plants. Moong and chana are also seeds. Even badam and kaju are seeds!

**Child:** So we eat leaves, roots, stems, seeds, and fruits. Plants give us so much!

**Mother:** Yes, beta. And we even eat some flowers -- like kele ka phool in South India!

## Food from Animals

**Child:** Maa, is everything we eat from plants?

**Mother:** Not everything. Milk comes from cows, buffaloes, and goats. From milk, we make dahi, paneer, ghee, and makhan. Honey comes from bees, and eggs from hens.

**Child:** And salt? Where does salt come from?

**Mother:** Interesting question! Salt comes from the sea -- sea water is dried in the sun and salt crystals are left behind. Some salt also comes from mines deep underground. Salt is neither from a plant nor from an animal!

**Child:** And water is also neither! So salt and water are the special ones.

**Mother:** Exactly, beta. Two very important ingredients that are in a category of their own!

## Never Waste Food

**Child:** Maa, at the school canteen today, I saw children throwing half-eaten rotis into the dustbin. It made me feel bad.

**Mother:** You are right to feel that way, beta. In India, we say **Anna Daan** -- giving food -- is one of the greatest acts of kindness. But sadly, many people waste food while so many children go to bed hungry.

**Child:** What can I do about it, Maa?

**Mother:** Start with yourself. Take only as much as you can finish. If you have leftover roti at home, we can give it to the street dogs. And if your friend forgets their tiffin, share yours!

**Child:** I did that last week! Priya forgot her tiffin and I gave her half my paratha. She was so happy!

**Mother:** That was very kind, beta. Remember, a shared meal always tastes better than eating alone.
