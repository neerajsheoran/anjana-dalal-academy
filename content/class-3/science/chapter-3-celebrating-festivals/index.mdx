## Travelling and Road Safety

**Meera** lives in Jaipur. During her school holidays, she is going with her family to visit her **Bua** (father's sister) who lives in a small village near Dehradun in the hills. They will take a long bus ride through the mountains.

The bus climbs higher and higher on winding roads. Meera sees pine trees, waterfalls, and valleys below. The hills are covered with wildflowers in pink, yellow, and white.

![A bus travelling on a winding mountain road with the Himalayas in the background](himalayan-road-trip.png)

> The **Himalayas** stretch across the northern edge of India like a giant wall of snow and rock. The name "Himalaya" comes from Sanskrit and means **"abode of snow"**. The tallest peak on Earth, **Mount Everest** (about 8,848 metres high), stands in our neighbouring country Nepal.

While travelling, Meera notices many **road signs** along the way. Her Baba explains what each one means:

| Sign | What It Tells Us |
|---|---|
| **No Parking** | Do not stop your vehicle here |
| **Honking Prohibited** | No horn-blowing in this quiet zone |
| **Speed Breaker Ahead** | Slow down -- a bump is coming |
| **No U-Turn** | Do not turn around here |
| **Construction Zone** | Workers on the road -- drive carefully |
| **School Zone** | Children crossing -- go very slow |

**Tips for staying safe on roads:**

- Always fasten the seatbelt in a car
- Put on a helmet when cycling
- Walk on the footpath, not in the middle of the road
- Look left, then right, then left again before crossing

## Spring Festivals of India

When Meera reaches Bua's village, the whole place is celebrating **spring**! After months of cold winter, the sun feels warm again. Flowers are blooming everywhere, and green leaves are sprouting on the trees.

Meera sees beautiful **buransh** (rhododendron) flowers -- bright red blossoms that cover the hillsides. Bua tells her that in the hills, people welcome spring by placing fresh flowers at their doorstep.

Spring is celebrated across India in many wonderful ways:

| Festival | Where It Is Celebrated | What Makes It Special |
|---|---|---|
| **Holi** | All across India | Splashing colours, singing, and dancing |
| **Kashmir Tulip Festival** | Kashmir | Stunning gardens full of tulips in every colour |
| **Buransh Festival** | Uttarakhand / Himachal | Bright red rhododendron flowers on hillsides |
| **Vishu** | Kerala | Vishu Kani arrangement of golden flowers, fruits, and rice at dawn |
| **Sarhul** | Jharkhand | Offerings of sakhua blossoms and fresh grains to nature |

> **Vishu Kani** is a beautiful custom in Kerala. On the morning of Vishu, people set up a tray with golden **konna** flowers, rice, fruits, vegetables, and a mirror. The first thing you see that morning should be this lovely display -- it is believed to bring happiness for the whole year!

**Sarhul** is the spring festival of Jharkhand. Villagers dress in their finest traditional outfits and walk together in a procession to the sacred **sakhua tree**. They offer sakhua blossoms and fresh young grains to the Earth and the Sun with deep thankfulness. The celebrations are full of singing and lively dancing to drums like the **dhol** and **nagada**.

## Festival Food and Traditions

Every Indian festival comes with its own **special dishes**! Here are some favourites:

| Festival | Its Special Dish |
|---|---|
| **Holi** | Gujiya (sweet stuffed pastry) |
| **Chhath Puja** | Thekua (crunchy wheat sweet) |
| **Bihu** | Pitha (rice cakes with filling) |
| **Christmas** | Plum Cake |
| **Onam** | Sadya (grand feast served on a banana leaf) |
| **Ugadi** | Holige (sweet flatbread with filling) |
| **Eid-ul-Fitr** | Seviyan (sweet vermicelli in milk) |

People also wear **new clothes** during festivals. Families, friends, and neighbours all come together to celebrate, share food, and enjoy.

India has festivals in **every season** of the year:

| Season | Some Festivals |
|---|---|
| **Spring** | Holi, Vishu, Sarhul, Ugadi |
| **Summer** | Rath Yatra, Buddha Purnima |
| **Monsoon** | Teej, Onam, Janmashtami |
| **Winter** | Diwali, Christmas, Lohri, Makar Sankranti |

## Spending Time with Relatives

Meera had a wonderful time at Bua's village. She played with her cousins, tasted fresh **mandua ki roti** (millet bread) with spicy **rai ka saag** (mustard leaf vegetable), and learned how to milk a cow!

> Festivals and family visits help us discover new foods, customs, and traditions from different parts of India. They remind us that even though we live in different places, we share the same joy of celebrating together.

---

## Key Points to Remember

- India celebrates **festivals in every season** -- spring, summer, monsoon, and winter.
- **Spring** is a season of new beginnings when flowers bloom and nature wakes up.
- Each festival has its own **special food, clothing, and customs**.
- **Road signs** warn us about dangers and help keep us safe while travelling.
- The **Himalayas** are the mightiest mountains in the world, lying in northern India.
- Visiting **relatives** teaches us about different cultures, foods, and ways of life.
- Festivals bring people of all backgrounds **together** in celebration.
