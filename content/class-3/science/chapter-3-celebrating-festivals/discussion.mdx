## Travelling and Road Safety

**Child:** Maa, why are there so many signs on the road when we go to the hills?

**Mother:** Those are **road signs**, beta. They tell the driver important things, like where to slow down, where not to honk, and where workers are fixing the road. Without those signs, travelling in the mountains would be very dangerous.

**Child:** I saw one sign with a triangle and a bump drawn on it. What does that mean?

**Mother:** That is the **Speed Breaker Ahead** sign. It means there is a bump on the road, so the driver should slow down. If the bus goes too fast over a bump, everyone inside will bounce around and could get hurt!

**Child:** Maa, why do we wear seatbelts in the car?

**Mother:** Imagine you are sitting on a swing and it suddenly stops. You would fly forward, right? The same thing happens in a car if it brakes suddenly. A seatbelt holds you in your seat and keeps you safe. It is like the car giving you a protective hug!

**Child:** And the Himalayas -- they are so tall, Maa! Are they the tallest mountains?

**Mother:** Yes! The Himalayas are the highest mountains on Earth. "Himalaya" means "home of snow" in Sanskrit. The tallest peak, Mount Everest, is about 8,848 metres high -- that is taller than 2,000 houses stacked on top of each other!

## Spring Festivals of India

**Child:** Maa, why does everyone get so happy during spring?

**Mother:** After months of cold winter, spring brings warmth back. The sun shines brightly, flowers bloom in every colour, and new green leaves appear on the trees. It feels like nature is waking up from a long sleep! That is why so many festivals happen during spring.

**Child:** I know Holi! We play with colours!

**Mother:** Yes, Holi is celebrated all over India. But did you know that in Jharkhand, people celebrate **Sarhul** during spring? They walk in a procession to the sakhua tree and offer fresh flowers and grains to the Earth and Sun, thanking nature for giving them food and water.

**Child:** And what about Kerala? Do they celebrate spring too?

**Mother:** Absolutely! In Kerala, they celebrate **Vishu**. On Vishu morning, the first thing you see should be a beautiful display called **Vishu Kani** -- with golden konna flowers, rice, fruits, and a mirror. People believe that starting the day with this lovely sight brings happiness for the whole year.

**Child:** Every place has its own way of celebrating! That is so nice.

## Festival Food and Traditions

**Child:** Maa, what is the best part of festivals?

**Mother:** For me, it is the food! Every festival has its own special dish. During Holi, we make **gujiya** -- those sweet stuffed pastries you love. During Chhath Puja, your Dadi makes **thekua**. And during Eid, our neighbour Salma Aunty sends us her delicious **seviyan**.

**Child:** I love seviyan! And during Onam, what do they eat?

**Mother:** In Kerala, families sit on the floor and eat a grand feast called **Sadya**, served on a banana leaf. It has rice, sambar, vegetables, payasam, and many more dishes -- all vegetarian! It is like a rainbow of tastes on one leaf.

**Child:** Maa, why do we wear new clothes during festivals?

**Mother:** Wearing new clothes is a way of celebrating new beginnings. Festivals mark special occasions, and we want to look our best. But the most important part of any festival is spending time with family and sharing happiness with neighbours and friends. That is what brings everyone together.
