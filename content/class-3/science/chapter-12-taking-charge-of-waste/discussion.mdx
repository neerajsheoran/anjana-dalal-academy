## Where Does All the Rubbish Come From?

**Child:** Maa, today our teacher asked us to count how many things we throw away in one day. I counted twelve! Banana peel, biscuit wrapper, juice box, pencil shavings...

**Mother:** Twelve items in one day! Now imagine -- if every person in our city throws away twelve things, that is lakhs of items going into the dustbin every single day!

**Child:** That is a LOT of rubbish! Where does it all go?

**Mother:** It is collected by safai karmacharis and taken to big dumping grounds outside the city. But these grounds are overflowing. That is why we see garbage piles on roadsides and near rivers.

**Child:** Ugh, those smell so bad! And I have seen flies buzzing around them.

**Mother:** Exactly. Those flies carry germs from the garbage pile straight to our food. That is how diseases spread. If we do not learn to manage our waste, our cities will become very unhealthy places.

**Child:** So what can we do about it, Maa?

**Mother:** That is the right question to ask! We have three superpowers -- **Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle**.

## Why Ignoring Waste is Dangerous

**Child:** Maa, I saw a cow eating from a garbage pile yesterday. She was chewing on a plastic bag! Will she be okay?

**Mother:** Sadly, beta, many cows in Indian cities fall sick because they eat plastic bags mixed with food scraps. The plastic cannot be digested and gets stuck inside them. Some cows even die from this.

**Child:** That is terrible! It is not the cow's fault -- she was just looking for food.

**Mother:** You are right. And that is not the only problem. When people burn plastic, it releases poisonous smoke that makes the air dirty. Waste near rivers poisons the water. Dirty water puddles become breeding grounds for mosquitoes.

**Child:** Mosquitoes! That is how dengue and malaria spread, right?

**Mother:** Yes, beta. One careless plastic bag thrown on the road can hurt an animal, pollute water, or help mosquitoes breed. That is why managing waste is not a choice -- it is a responsibility.

## Reduce -- Create Less Waste

**Child:** Maa, you said Reduce is the strongest superpower. But how do I reduce? I already need things!

**Mother:** Think about it this way, beta. When we go to the market, do we carry our own cloth bag or ask for plastic bags?

**Child:** We use our cloth bag!

**Mother:** That is reducing! Every time you use a cloth bag, one plastic bag is NOT created. Now think about your water bottle. You carry your steel bottle to school. Your friend Rohan buys a new plastic bottle every day from the canteen.

**Child:** So Rohan creates five plastic bottles of waste every week, but I create zero!

**Mother:** Exactly! You can also reduce by sharing old books with your younger cousin instead of throwing them away, using both sides of notebook pages, and taking your own tiffin box for street food.

**Child:** The best waste is the waste that was never created!

**Mother:** Now you are thinking like a waste warrior, beta!

## Reuse -- Give Old Things a Second Life

**Child:** Maa, Nani never throws anything! She keeps old sarees, newspapers, glass jars, and even ice cream sticks. Papa says her room is a museum!

**Mother:** Ha ha! But Nani is actually very wise. She stitches old sarees into beautiful **gudris** -- patchwork quilts. She folds old newspapers into paper bags for the sabzi wala. She stores our spices in old jam jars.

**Child:** That is reuse! She gives old things a new life!

**Mother:** Exactly. Instead of buying new things, she finds a second use for old ones. You can do it too -- use ice cream sticks to make a photo frame, turn a shoe box into a treasure chest, or use old T-shirts as floor cloths.

**Child:** I once used a shoe box to make a tiny house for my toy cars!

**Mother:** That is brilliant reuse! Nani always says, "Nothing is waste until you stop thinking about it." And she is absolutely right.

## Recycle -- Turn Old Into New

**Child:** Maa, what is recycling? Is it the same as reusing?

**Mother:** Not quite. Reusing means using the same thing again in a new way -- like using an old jar to store dal. Recycling means sending old materials to a **factory** where they are completely broken down and made into brand new products.

**Child:** Like what?

**Mother:** Old newspapers go to a paper factory and come back as fresh new paper. Old aluminium cans are melted and made into new cans. Broken glass is melted and made into new bottles.

**Child:** That is like magic! Old paper becomes new paper!

**Mother:** Yes! But recycling only works if we **sort our waste** first. That is why we have two different coloured dustbins.

**Child:** Green and blue!

**Mother:** Right. Wet waste like banana peels and leftover food goes in the **green bin**. It rots and becomes compost for plants. Dry waste like plastic, metal, and paper goes in the **blue bin**. It is sent for recycling.

**Child:** So if I put a banana peel in the blue bin, it messes everything up?

**Mother:** Exactly! Sorting properly makes the whole system work.

## Sorting Waste -- Green Bin and Blue Bin

**Child:** Maa, how do I remember which waste goes where?

**Mother:** Here is a simple trick. Ask yourself -- did this come from a living thing, and will it rot? If yes, it goes in the **green bin**. Banana peels, tea leaves, egg shells, flower petals, leftover rice -- all rot naturally and become compost.

**Child:** And if it will NOT rot?

**Mother:** Then it goes in the **blue bin**. Plastic wrappers, glass bottles, metal tins, old newspapers -- these do not rot on their own but can be recycled into new things.

**Child:** What about an old battery? That does not rot.

**Mother:** Very good question! Old batteries are **special waste** -- they contain harmful chemicals. They should not go in either dustbin. Many shops and schools have special collection points for old batteries.

**Child:** I will remember -- green for rot, blue for recycle, and batteries go to special collection points!

## Salute to Our Safai Karmacharis

**Child:** Maa, today Ananya in our class made a thank-you card from old newspaper and gave it to the safai didi at school. The didi was SO happy! She had the biggest smile.

**Mother:** What a lovely thing to do! Safai karmacharis work so hard for us, beta. They wake up before dawn and sweep streets, collect garbage, and clean drains in the sun, rain, and cold. Without them, our cities would be buried in rubbish.

**Child:** But some children in my class make fun of them. That makes me angry.

**Mother:** You should be angry about that, beta. Every job that keeps our world clean deserves respect. The best way to show them we care is to make their work easier.

**Child:** How can I do that?

**Mother:** Never throw rubbish on the road -- always use a dustbin. Sort your waste properly at home. Offer the garbage collector a glass of water when he comes. And if you see someone littering, gently remind them to use a dustbin.

**Child:** I will also make a thank-you card like Ananya did. And Maa, I want to wrap broken glass in old cloth before throwing it, so the didi does not cut her hands.

**Mother:** Beta, that is so thoughtful. You really are a kind person. Remember, sometimes a small act of kindness means the world to someone.
