## Where Does All the Rubbish Come From?

Every single day, our homes, schools, and markets create a mountain of **waste**. Think about what happens from breakfast to bedtime:

- Your Maa peels onions and potatoes -- **vegetable peels** go in the bin
- You finish a packet of biscuits -- the **plastic wrapper** gets thrown away
- Papa reads the newspaper -- the **old paper** piles up
- You sharpen your pencil -- tiny **wood shavings** fall into the dustbin
- An old toy breaks -- it joins the rubbish pile

Other types of waste include **empty bottles, old batteries, torn clothes, broken bangles**, and **leftover food**.

If nobody takes care of this waste, it piles up on streets, near houses, and along rivers. The neighbourhood becomes dirty, smelly, and full of **flies and germs**.

> Look at the dustbin in your kitchen right now. What kind of waste do you see inside it? Can you count how many different types there are?

---

## Why Ignoring Waste is Dangerous

When waste sits around in the open, it causes real harm to people, animals, and the planet:

| Danger | How It Happens |
|---|---|
| **Animals choke on plastic** | Cows, dogs, and birds swallow plastic bags mixed with food scraps and can choke or die |
| **Poisonous smoke** | Burning plastic and rubber releases toxic fumes that pollute the air we breathe |
| **Dirty water** | Waste dumped near rivers and drains poisons the water, making it undrinkable |
| **Mosquito breeding** | Stagnant dirty water in piles of waste is the perfect home for mosquitoes that spread dengue and malaria |
| **Diseases spread** | Rotting food attracts rats, flies, and cockroaches that carry germs into our homes |

> Every year, thousands of cows in Indian cities fall sick after eating plastic bags. Just one careless act of throwing a plastic bag on the road can hurt an innocent animal.

## The Three Magic Words -- Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

The smartest way to handle waste is to follow three simple rules. Think of them as **three superpowers**:

---

## Reduce -- Create Less Waste

**REDUCE** is the strongest superpower. It means stopping waste **before it is even created**!

**How to reduce waste in your life:**

- Carry a **cloth bag** when going to the market -- say no to plastic bags
- Carry your own **steel water bottle** instead of buying plastic water bottles
- Take your **own tiffin box** for street food instead of using disposable plates
- Buy only what you **actually need** -- not just what looks tempting
- Share **old books and toys** with younger children instead of throwing them away
- Use both sides of a **notebook page** before getting a new notebook

> Think of it this way: the best waste is the waste that was **never created**.

![Reduce — create less waste; cloth bags, steel bottles, tiffin, share old things](reduce-waste.png)

## Reuse -- Give Old Things a Second Life

**REUSE** means finding a new use for something instead of tossing it in the bin.

| Instead of Throwing Away... | Give It a New Life! |
|---|---|
| **Old newspapers** | Use them to wrap gifts, line shelves, or fold into paper bags |
| **Glass jars** (jam, pickle) | Store spices, dal, or use as pen stands |
| **One-side-used paper** | Use the blank side for drawing, rough work, or shopping lists |
| **Old T-shirts** | Cut into strips to use as **pocha** (floor cloths) |
| **Old sarees and dupattas** | Stitch into **gudris** (patchwork quilts) or shopping bags |
| **Ice cream sticks** | Build tiny houses, photo frames, or bookmarks |
| **Shoe boxes** | Decorate them and use as storage for small items |

> Nani still keeps a basket of old cloth strips in her room. Whenever something needs tying, wrapping, or wiping, she reaches for her basket. "Nothing is waste," she says, "until you stop thinking about it."

![Reuse — old newspapers, jars, T-shirts, sarees given a second life](reuse-things.png)

## Recycle -- Turn Old Into New

**RECYCLE** means sending used materials to a factory where they are broken down and made into **brand-new products**.

| Used Material | What It Becomes After Recycling |
|---|---|
| **Old newspapers and notebooks** | Fresh paper, cardboard boxes |
| **Aluminium cans** | New cans, foil, and bike parts |
| **Glass bottles and jars** | New glass bottles and windows |
| **Plastic bottles** (limited) | Bags, pipes, or playground equipment |
| **Iron and steel scrap** | New tools, utensils, and building materials |

But recycling only works if we first **sort our waste properly**.

## Sorting Waste -- Green Bin and Blue Bin

You may have seen **two coloured dustbins** at your school gate, in your park, or at the bus stop. Each colour has a purpose:

| Dustbin | Colour | What Goes In | What Happens to It |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wet waste bin** | Green | Banana peels, leftover dal-rice, tea leaves, egg shells, dried flowers, garden leaves | Rots naturally and becomes **compost** -- a rich food for plants and soil |
| **Dry waste bin** | Blue | Plastic wrappers, metal tins, glass bottles, old newspapers, cardboard | Sent to **recycling centres** where it is turned into new products |

**How to remember:** If it came from a living thing and will rot -- it goes in **GREEN**. If it will NOT rot on its own -- it goes in **BLUE**.

> Compost from the green bin is like magic food for plants! Mix it into the soil in your garden, and watch your plants grow tall and green.

## Shining Examples of Clean India

Many places in India have shown the whole country how to manage waste beautifully:

| Place | What Makes It Special |
|---|---|
| **Silluk, Arunachal Pradesh** | Called a "zero waste village" -- spotlessly clean streets lined with trees, almost no trash anywhere |
| **Chhota Narena village** | One of the first villages in India to become completely waste-free |
| **Indore, Madhya Pradesh** | Has won the title of India's cleanest city multiple times |
| **Mysuru, Karnataka** | Known for its clean roads, beautiful gardens, and excellent waste collection |

> The **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** (Clean India Mission) was launched to inspire every Indian to keep their surroundings clean and manage waste responsibly.

## National Cleanliness Day

On **30 January** every year, India observes **National Cleanliness Day**. This is a day to remember our responsibility towards keeping India clean. On this day, you can take a pledge:

- I will always throw waste in the **right dustbin** -- green for wet, blue for dry
- I will carry a **cloth bag** to the market
- I will **reduce, reuse, and recycle** as much as possible
- I will keep my **room, school, and neighbourhood** clean
- I will **never litter** on roads, parks, or public places

![National Cleanliness Day on 30 January — take the pledge](national-cleanliness-day.png)

## Small Steps You Can Take Every Day

Keeping your world clean does not need big actions -- just small, regular ones:

- **Keep your desk tidy** -- put pencils, erasers, and books back in their place
- **Use the correct dustbin** -- wet waste in green, dry waste in blue
- **Never throw wrappers on the ground** -- always find a dustbin (carry the wrapper in your pocket if needed!)
- **Wrap broken glass** in old cloth or thick paper before throwing it away so that the waste collector is not hurt
- **Say thank you** to the safai karmachari (sanitation worker) who cleans your school or street

> **"Want not, waste not."** The less we want, the less waste we create. Simple living is the cleanest way of living!

## Salute to Our Safai Karmacharis

The **safai karmacharis** (sanitation workers) who sweep our streets, collect our garbage, and clean our drains are true heroes of every city and village. They wake up before dawn and work in sun, rain, and cold to keep our surroundings healthy.

How can we show them our respect?

- **Never litter** -- every piece of trash on the road means more work for them
- **Sort your waste** at home so their job of separating becomes easier
- **Offer them water** on a hot day
- Make a small **thank-you card** from waste paper and give it to them
- Teach younger children to **respect their work**

> When Ananya made a thank-you card from old newspaper and gave it to the safai didi at school, the didi's face lit up with the biggest smile. Sometimes a small act of kindness means the world to someone.

## Key Points to Remember

- Every day, we create waste from cooking, eating, studying, and daily activities -- **peels, wrappers, old papers, broken items**, and more.
- Unmanaged waste causes **air pollution, water pollution, diseases**, and is **dangerous for animals**.
- The three golden rules are **Reduce** (create less waste), **Reuse** (give old things a new purpose), and **Recycle** (turn old materials into new products).
- **Green dustbins** hold wet waste (food, leaves) that becomes compost. **Blue dustbins** hold dry waste (plastic, metal, paper) that goes for recycling.
- Places like **Silluk, Indore, and Mysuru** show that clean living is possible when everyone works together.
- **30 January** is observed as National Cleanliness Day in India.
- **Safai karmacharis** deserve our deepest respect and gratitude for keeping our world clean.
