## Signs of Rain

**Child:** Maa, today it suddenly started raining during games period! We all had to run inside. How does rain come so fast?

**Mother:** Rain does not come without warning, beta. Nature always gives hints. Did you notice anything different in the sky before the rain started?

**Child:** Hmm, the sky became very dark! And the wind was blowing hard.

**Mother:** Exactly! When thick dark clouds cover the sky and a strong breeze starts blowing, it means rain is on its way. Did you hear any frogs croaking?

**Child:** Yes! There were frogs near the school pond making so much noise!

**Mother:** Frogs croak loudly when rain is coming. Even ants start running in long lines to take their food to higher ground. Nature is very smart, beta.

**Child:** So if I see dark clouds and hear frogs, I should carry my umbrella?

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! That would be very wise.

## The Journey of a Raindrop

**Child:** Maa, when it rains, where does all that water go? There are so many puddles after rain, but later they disappear!

**Mother:** Great observation! Think of it like this -- when rain falls on soft soil in our garden, what happens?

**Child:** The soil becomes wet and muddy.

**Mother:** That is because the water **soaks into the soil**. It goes deeper and deeper underground. This underground water is called **groundwater**. That is what comes out when people dig wells!

**Child:** Oh! But what about rain that falls on the road? The road does not soak it up.

**Mother:** Right! On roads and cemented areas, water cannot go underground. So it collects in puddles or flows along the road into drains. Some of this water reaches streams, then rivers, and finally the sea.

**Child:** And the puddles? They dry up when the sun comes out!

**Mother:** Yes, the sun heats the water and it turns into invisible water vapour. This vapour goes up into the sky, forms clouds, and comes down as rain again. It is like a never-ending circle!

**Child:** Wah Maa, water is so clever -- it keeps going round and round!

## How Water Reaches Our Homes

**Child:** Maa, I opened the tap this morning and no water came. Papa said the motor is off. What motor?

**Mother:** You see, beta, water does not come to our tap on its own. First, water is collected from a river or an underground well. Then it is cleaned at a treatment centre. After that, it is stored in big tanks. From those tanks, pipes carry the water to our building's tank. The motor pumps it up!

**Child:** So many steps just for water! In Nani's village, I saw people using a hand pump. They pushed the handle up and down and water came out.

**Mother:** Yes, in villages many people use hand pumps or wells. In some places, people even carry water in pots from a river far away.

**Child:** That must be so hard, Maa. We just turn a tap!

**Mother:** That is why we should never waste even a single glass of water, beta. Many people work very hard just to get the water we use so easily.

## Water in Our Daily Lives

**Child:** Maa, I used water today for drinking, brushing teeth, washing hands, and bathing. That is already four times before school!

**Mother:** And that is just the morning! Think about all the water used for cooking your lunch, washing your tiffin box, mopping the floor, watering the tulsi plant...

**Child:** So much water! What happens if there is no water for one whole day?

**Mother:** We would not be able to cook, bathe, or even flush the toilet. Our body is made up of nearly 70 percent water, beta. Without drinking water, a person can only survive for about three days.

**Child:** Only three days?! Water really is like liquid gold!

**Mother:** Exactly. That is why we call it a **precious gift**.

## Storing Water

**Child:** Maa, why does Dadi always keep water in that brown clay pot? We have a fridge!

**Mother:** That clay pot is called a **matka**, beta. It has tiny holes that we cannot see. When water sits inside, some of it seeps through these tiny holes and evaporates. This process makes the remaining water **cool** naturally -- no electricity needed!

**Child:** That is so cool! I mean, literally cool!

**Mother:** Ha ha! And remember the copper lota Dadi uses? Copper slowly kills germs in the water. Our grandparents were very wise -- they chose the right container for the right purpose.

**Child:** Is that why Dadi says copper water is good for the tummy?

**Mother:** Yes, beta. Scientists have also found this to be true. A copper vessel left with water overnight helps make the water cleaner.

## Water is Precious -- Save Every Drop!

**Child:** Maa, my friend Arjun leaves the tap running while brushing his teeth. Is that okay?

**Mother:** Not at all! A running tap wastes about 5-6 litres of water every minute. That is nearly one whole bucket wasted just while brushing!

**Child:** Oh no! I will tell Arjun to turn it off.

**Mother:** Good idea. And do you know what else saves water? Taking a bucket bath instead of standing under a running shower. A bucket uses about 15 litres, but a 10-minute shower can use over 100 litres!

**Child:** I did not know that! What about the water we use to wash vegetables?

**Mother:** We can collect it in a pan and use it to water our plants. The plants do not mind a little vegetable flavour!

**Child:** Ha ha! Maa, from today I will be a water saver.

**Mother:** That is wonderful, beta. Remember -- every drop you save today is a drop available for someone tomorrow.

## Sharing Water with Others

**Child:** Maa, I saw Sharma uncle has kept a matka with a glass outside his gate. Why?

**Mother:** That is a beautiful Indian tradition, beta. During summer, when the sun is blazing hot, anyone who passes by -- a rickshaw puller, a delivery person, a worker -- can drink cool water from that pot.

**Child:** Can we do that too?

**Mother:** Of course! We can also keep a bowl of water on the balcony for birds. In summer, birds find it very hard to get water.

**Child:** Like the sparrows that come to our window every morning?

**Mother:** Yes! If you keep a shallow dish with some pebbles and water, you will see sparrows, mynas, and maybe even a bulbul come to drink. You will love watching them!

**Child:** I will set it up tomorrow morning, Maa! And I will also keep a bowl near the gate for the street dogs.

**Mother:** What a kind thought, beta. Sharing water is sharing life.
