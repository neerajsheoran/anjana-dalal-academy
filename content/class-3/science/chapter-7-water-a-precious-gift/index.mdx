## Signs of Rain

Have you ever stepped outside and felt the air become thick and warm? That heavy feeling means rain might be on its way! Nature gives us many hints before the rain arrives.

**Clues that rain is coming:**

- The sky fills with **thick, dark clouds**
- A strong **breeze** begins to blow
- **Frogs** start croaking loudly
- **Ants** rush in lines carrying food to higher ground
- The air feels **sticky and warm**

![Clues that rain is coming — clouds, breeze, frogs, ants, sticky air](rain-clues.png)

When rain finally falls, it can look very different each time. Sometimes it is a gentle **drizzle** -- tiny drops that tickle your face. Other times, it pours so hard that the drops bounce off the ground! On some days, the rain comes down at a **slant** because the wind pushes it sideways.

> During the monsoon season, many parts of India get heavy rainfall for weeks. Farmers wait eagerly for the monsoon because their crops need this water to grow.

## The Journey of a Raindrop

![The journey of a raindrop from cloud to soil, stream, river, or sea](raindrop-journey.png)

Imagine you are a tiny raindrop falling from a cloud. Where would you end up? It depends on where you land!

| Where You Land | What Happens to You |
|---|---|
| **Soft soil or a garden** | You sink slowly into the earth |
| **A cemented road or footpath** | You sit on top in a **puddle** or slide away |
| **A hillside** | You roll downhill and join a small **stream** |
| **A rooftop** | You slide off into the drain or a water tank |

Water that seeps deep into the soil collects between layers of rock and sand far below the surface. This hidden water is called **groundwater**. People reach this water by digging **wells** or drilling **borewells**.

![Groundwater — water hidden below the surface reached by wells and borewells](groundwater.png)

Water that flows along the surface joins **streams**. Streams meet and become **rivers**. Rivers carry water to **lakes** and finally to the **sea**.

![Streams becoming rivers becoming lakes and the sea](streams-rivers.png)

> When the sun comes out after rain, puddles slowly dry up. The water turns into invisible **water vapour** and rises back into the sky. Later, it forms clouds again -- and the cycle continues!

## How Water Reaches Our Homes

Think about your morning routine. You turn on the tap and water flows out. But where did that water come from?

**The path water takes to reach your tap:**

1. Water is collected from a **river, lake, or underground well**.
2. It goes to a **treatment centre** where dirt and germs are removed.
3. Clean water is pumped into large **overhead tanks** or **water towers**.
4. From there, it travels through **underground pipes** to your building.
5. The building's own tank stores the water until you open the tap.

![Water reaching homes — from river to treatment to overhead tank to tap](water-to-homes.png)

Not everyone gets water from taps. In India, people use many different methods:

| Method | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **Tap water** | Flows through pipes connected to the city supply |
| **Hand pump** | A handle is pushed up and down to pull water from below the ground |
| **Open well** | A bucket tied to a rope is lowered into the well and pulled up |
| **Water tanker** | Large trucks deliver water to areas where pipe supply is not available |
| **Carrying from a source** | People walk to a nearby river or pond and carry water home in pots |

> In many villages across Rajasthan and Maharashtra, women and children walk several kilometres every day just to fetch drinking water. This shows how precious water truly is.

## Water in Our Daily Lives

From the moment we wake up to the time we go to bed, we use water in dozens of ways:

- **Drinking** -- our body needs at least 6-8 glasses every day
- **Cooking** -- dal, rice, sabzi, chai -- everything needs water
- **Bathing** and **washing hands and face**
- **Brushing teeth** and **rinsing the mouth**
- **Washing clothes** and **cleaning utensils**
- **Mopping floors** and **cleaning the house**
- **Watering plants** in the garden or balcony
- **Flushing the toilet**

Our body is made up of nearly **70% water**! Without drinking water, a person can survive only about **three days**. That is why water is sometimes called **liquid gold**.

![Water in our daily lives — drinking, cooking, bathing, cleaning](water-daily-use.png)

## Storing Water

In many Indian homes, water supply comes only for a few hours each day. Families must store enough water to last the whole day. Over the centuries, Indians have used many clever containers for storing water:

| Container | Material | Special Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Matka** (clay pot) | Clay | Keeps water naturally **cool** in summer |
| **Surahi** | Clay | Has a narrow neck that keeps water extra cool |
| **Lota** | Brass or copper | Copper kills germs in water slowly |
| **Steel drum** | Stainless steel | Strong and long-lasting |
| **Plastic tank** | Plastic | Lightweight and holds a large amount |
| **Glass bottle** | Glass | Easy to see how much water is left |

![Storing water — matka, surahi, lota, drum, tank, bottle](storing-water.png)

> Dadi always says, "Drink water from a **copper lota** -- it keeps your tummy healthy!" Scientists have found that copper really does help kill harmful germs in water.

## Water is Precious -- Save Every Drop!

India has over 140 crore people, but the amount of fresh water is limited. Many cities face **water shortage** during summer. Rivers and lakes are drying up. We must learn to save water every single day.

**Simple ways to save water:**

- Close the tap tightly while **brushing your teeth** or **soaping your hands**
- Take a **bucket bath** instead of a long shower -- it uses much less water
- Ask your parents to **fix dripping taps** right away -- one dripping tap wastes hundreds of litres each month!
- Water from **washing rice or vegetables** can be used to water plants
- Do not play with the **garden hose** just for fun

![Simple ways to save water every day](save-water.png)

**Can dirty water be used again?** Yes! Water that has been used for washing hands or rinsing clothes is called **greywater**. It can be reused for:

- Watering the garden
- Flushing the toilet
- Washing the car or the courtyard

![Reusing greywater — garden, flushing, washing](reuse-greywater.png)

> Imagine filling a bucket while brushing your teeth -- that water could be enough for a small child to drink for a whole day! Every drop saved is a drop earned.

## Sharing Water with Others

In Indian tradition, offering water to a thirsty person is considered one of the kindest acts. During the blazing summer heat, many families place a **ghada** (earthen pot) with a glass outside their gate so that anyone passing by can drink cool water.

We should also think about animals and birds:

- **Birds** -- Place a shallow **bowl of water** on your balcony or terrace. In summer, birds struggle to find water.
- **Stray dogs and cats** -- Keep a bowl of water near your gate for them.
- **Workers** -- The postman, the vegetable seller, the garbage collector -- offer them a glass of water when they visit.

**Making a simple bird water station:**

1. Find a wide, flat dish (an old plate or lid works well)
2. Place two or three small **pebbles** inside so birds can stand on them
3. Fill the dish with **clean water**
4. Keep it in a shaded spot on your balcony or window ledge
5. Refill it with fresh water every morning

> When Meera put a water dish on her balcony, she saw sparrows, mynas, and even a bulbul come to drink! She felt so happy watching them.

![Sharing water with others — thirsty people, birds, stray animals, workers](sharing-water.png)

## Key Points to Remember

- Nature gives us clues before rain -- dark clouds, strong breeze, croaking frogs, and marching ants.
- Rainwater follows different paths -- it soaks into soil as **groundwater**, flows into **streams and rivers**, or evaporates back into the sky.
- Water reaches our taps through a system of **collection, cleaning, storage, and pipes**.
- We use water for **drinking, cooking, bathing, cleaning**, and many more daily needs.
- Indians have stored water in **matkas, surahis, copper lotas**, and other vessels for centuries.
- Water is **limited and precious** -- we must **save it** by fixing leaks, closing taps, and reusing water.
- Sharing water with **thirsty people, birds, and animals** is a beautiful Indian tradition.
