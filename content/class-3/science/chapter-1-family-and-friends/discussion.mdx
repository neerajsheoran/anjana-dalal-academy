## Family Members and Types of Families

**Child:** Maa, why do some of my friends have so many people at home and I have only a few?

**Mother:** That is because families come in different sizes, beta. Your friend Priya lives in a joint family -- her grandparents, uncle, aunty, and cousins all live together in one big house. Our family is smaller -- just us, Baba, and your Naani. Both types are perfectly wonderful.

**Child:** Maa, what do we call Baba's sister?

**Mother:** We call her **Bua**. And your Bua's husband is called **Fufa**. In India, we have special names for every relative. Your Amma's brother is your **Mama**, and his wife is your **Mami**.

**Child:** That is so many names to remember! But Maa, is Golu our cat also part of our family?

**Mother:** Of course! Many families in India keep cows, dogs, parrots, and goats. They feed them, take care of them when they are sick, and love them. Golu is very much our family member. That is why we must always be gentle with animals and never hurt them.

## Helping and Learning from Family

**Child:** Maa, why does Naani wake up so early every morning?

**Mother:** Your Naani has been waking up at 5 o'clock since she was a young girl in her village. She says the early morning air is the freshest. She makes chai and poha for all of us. That is her way of showing love.

**Child:** And what does Naana teach me?

**Mother:** Your Naana reads the newspaper to you so you learn new words. He also tells you stories about freedom fighters. Everyone in a family shares what they know best. Your Baba fixes things, I cook and teach music, and you water the plants every evening. Even little Chintu helps by putting his toys back in the basket!

**Child:** Maa, Rohan next door taught Didi how to make a kite yesterday!

**Mother:** See? We learn from neighbours and friends too. Savita Aunty taught Didi stitching. When everyone shares their skills, the whole neighbourhood becomes like one big family.

## Fun and Games with Family and Friends

**Child:** Maa, tell me about the games you played when you were small.

**Mother:** Oh, we used to play **kho-kho** in the school ground every evening! Your Naana taught me **gilli-danda** too. On rainy days, my mother made hot bhajiye and we all played ludo and antakshari inside the house.

**Child:** That sounds just like what we do on rainy Sundays!

**Mother:** Yes! Families have been enjoying these things together for generations. Playing together makes everyone happy. And you know what else is important? Taking care of those who need extra help.

**Child:** Like when Naani's knee hurts and I bring her walking stick?

**Mother:** Exactly! Grandparents sometimes need help. Babies like Chintu need help with everything -- eating, bathing, getting dressed. And when someone is sick, we give them rest and warm soup. Caring for each other is what makes a family strong.
