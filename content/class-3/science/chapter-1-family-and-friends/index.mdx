## Family Members and Types of Families

**Aarav** lives in a cosy house in Lucknow with his family. His **Amma** (mother) and **Baba** (father) take care of everyone. His elder sister **Didi** helps him with homework, and his baby brother **Chintu** is learning to walk. Their pet cat **Golu** loves to curl up on the sunny balcony.

Aarav's **Naani** (mother's mother) and **Naana** (mother's father) also live with them. They came from their village to stay with the family after Naana retired.

> A family is a group of people who love each other, look after each other, and stay together through good times and tough times.

Families come in different sizes:

- A **small family** (also called a nuclear family) has parents and their children.
- A **big family** (also called a joint family) includes grandparents, uncles, aunties, cousins, and more!

| Family Member | What We Call Them |
|---|---|
| Mother's mother | Naani |
| Mother's father | Naana |
| Father's sister | Bua |
| Mother's brother | Mama |
| Father's younger brother | Chacha |

Some families also have animals as part of their family. **Cows, dogs, cats, parrots, and goats** are loved and cared for just like family members in many Indian homes.

## Helping and Learning from Family

In Aarav's house, everyone has something special they do:

- **Naani** wakes up early and makes fresh **chai** and **poha** for everyone.
- **Naana** reads the newspaper aloud so Aarav can learn new words.
- **Baba** fixes things around the house on weekends.
- **Amma** manages the kitchen and also teaches music.
- **Didi** helps fold the laundry and set the dinner table.
- **Aarav** waters the tulsi plant and the rose bushes every evening.

Their neighbour **Savita Aunty** is wonderful at stitching. She taught Didi how to sew buttons on a shirt. Savita Aunty's son **Rohan** knows how to make beautiful kites from old newspapers and bamboo sticks.

> We learn many valuable things from our family members, neighbours, and friends. Everyone around us has something special to teach.

Our grandparents share old folk tales, teach us forgotten recipes, and tell us about the games they played as children. Our parents teach us cooking, cleaning, and how to be responsible. And sometimes, children teach elders too -- like showing them how to make a video call on the phone!

## Fun and Games with Family and Friends

Aarav's family loves spending time together. On Sunday evenings, everyone gathers in the courtyard.

Some popular games they play:

| Game | How to Play |
|---|---|
| **Kho-kho** | Two teams -- one sits in a row, the other chases and tags them |
| **Stapoo** (Hopscotch) | Draw boxes on the ground and hop on one foot |
| **Carrom** | Flick the striker to pocket the carrom coins on a board |

During the monsoon, when it pours outside, Naani makes crispy **bhajiye** (fritters) and everyone sits together playing **ludo** or singing old Hindi songs.

Didi sometimes reads a storybook aloud to Chintu and Aarav. Naana tells them about his childhood village, the river nearby, and the big mango tree where he used to climb.

**Who needs special care in a family?**

- **Grandparents** may need help climbing stairs or reading small letters.
- **Babies and toddlers** cannot eat, dress, or bathe on their own.
- **Anyone who is unwell** needs rest, warm soup, and medicine.

We should always be **gentle and caring with animals** too. Aarav never pulls Golu's tail. He gives Golu fresh milk every morning and strokes her softly.

---

## Key Points to Remember

- A **family** includes parents, grandparents, siblings, and sometimes pets.
- Families can be **small or big** -- every family is unique and wonderful.
- Family members **love, help, and care** for each other every day.
- We learn useful skills from **elders, neighbours, and friends**.
- We should be **kind to all animals** and never hurt them.
- Spending time **playing, cooking, and talking** together keeps a family happy.
- **Relatives** like uncles, aunties, and cousins may live far away but are always part of our family.
