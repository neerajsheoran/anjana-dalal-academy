## Animals and Plants Living Together

**Child:** Maa, today in school we learned that animals live near plants. But why do animals need plants so much?

**Mother:** Think about it, beta. What do you need every day? Food and a home, right? Plants provide both to animals. A mango tree gives monkeys their favourite fruit. A banyan tree gives crows a safe place to build their nest. A field of grass feeds the cow that gives us milk. Without plants, animals would have no food and no shelter.

**Child:** So every animal is connected to some plant?

**Mother:** Yes! Even animals that eat other animals are connected. A frog sits on a lily pad and catches insects. A bird sits on a tree branch and catches the frog. It is all linked through plants. That is why when we cut down forests, many animals lose their homes and food.

**Child:** Maa, what about elephants? They are so big! What do they eat?

**Mother:** Elephants eat a LOT -- grass, leaves, bamboo shoots, and even bark! A single elephant can eat over 100 kilograms of food in one day. That is like eating the weight of two of you! They need large forests with plenty of plants to survive.

**Child:** And butterflies just drink nectar from flowers?

**Mother:** Yes, butterflies sip sweet nectar using a long, thin tongue called a proboscis -- it works like a tiny straw! And while they drink, they also help the flower by carrying pollen from one flower to another. So the butterfly gets food, and the flower gets help making seeds. They help each other!

## Life in the Soil and Monsoon

**Child:** Maa, is there really life inside the soil? It just looks like mud to me!

**Mother:** Oh, the soil is like a busy underground city! Pick up a handful of earth from the garden and look carefully. You might see tiny ants running, earthworms wriggling, and small beetles crawling. All these creatures live in the soil.

**Child:** What do earthworms do?

**Mother:** Earthworms are nature's gardeners! As they tunnel through the soil, they loosen it and mix it up. Their droppings are rich in nutrients that help plants grow better. Farmers love earthworms because they make the soil healthy without any chemicals.

**Child:** Maa, why does everything become so green during the monsoon?

**Mother:** That is a beautiful question. Many seeds lie quietly in the soil for months, waiting for water. As soon as the first big rain falls, these seeds soak up the water and begin to sprout. Within days, tiny green shoots poke out of the ground. Insects that were hiding underground also come out. The whole Earth comes alive after the rain -- that is why the monsoon is like a magical switch that turns everything green!

**Child:** So the seeds were sleeping?

**Mother:** In a way, yes! Scientists call it **dormancy**. The seeds rest until conditions are just right -- enough water, warmth, and light. Then they wake up and start growing. Nature is very patient and very wise.

## Bird Sounds and the Life Cycle of a Butterfly

**Child:** Maa, I hear birds every morning but I do not see them. How can I spot them?

**Mother:** Try this -- wake up at sunrise and stand near a tree. Be very still and quiet. First, listen. You will hear the koel singing "ku-hoo, ku-hoo." The sparrows will be chirping "chee-chee." The pigeons will coo "gutargu." Once you hear a sound, slowly look in that direction. You will spot the bird!

**Child:** Why do birds sing so much in the morning?

**Mother:** Morning is the busiest time for birds. They wake up hungry and call out to each other, marking their territory and searching for food. By afternoon, it gets hot and they rest quietly. In the evening, they sing again as they fly back to their nests. If you cup your hands behind your ears, you can hear them even better!

**Child:** Maa, tell me the butterfly story again -- how a caterpillar turns into a butterfly.

**Mother:** It is truly amazing! First, a butterfly lays a tiny egg on a leaf. A small caterpillar hatches from the egg and starts eating leaves day and night, growing bigger and fatter. Then one day, it stops eating and wraps itself in a hard shell called a **cocoon**. Inside the cocoon, an incredible change happens -- the caterpillar's body completely transforms! After some days, the cocoon cracks open and out comes a beautiful butterfly with colourful wings.

**Child:** It is like magic!

**Mother:** It feels like magic, but it is science! This process is called **metamorphosis** -- a big word that means "a change in form." The same creature that crawled on the ground now flies freely in the sky. Nature is full of such wonders!
