## The Clay Artist's Workshop

In a small lane near the river in Varanasi, a girl named **Kavya** watches her **Dadi** shape beautiful things from clay. Dadi is a **kumhar** (potter) -- her family has been making clay objects for many, many generations.

Here is how Dadi turns a lump of clay into a beautiful pot:

1. She digs up **clay** from the muddy riverbank and carries it home.
2. She soaks the clay in water and **mashes** it with her feet until it becomes smooth -- no lumps!
3. She **kneads** the clay with her hands, just like kneading atta for chapati.
4. She places the soft clay on the **chak** (potter's wheel) and spins it with her foot.
5. As the wheel spins, she shapes the clay with her fingers -- pressing, pulling, and smoothing.
6. The shaped pot is left in the **sun** for a day or two to dry completely.
7. The dried pot goes into a very hot oven called a **bhatti** (kiln). The fierce heat makes the soft clay become rock-hard.
8. After cooling, Kavya and Dadi **paint** the pot with bright colours and beautiful designs.

> The potter's oven is called a **kiln** or **bhatti**. It gets so hot inside that the clay changes forever -- it becomes hard, waterproof, and strong enough to hold food and water.

## Treasures Made by Kumhars

Potters create many things that are part of everyday Indian life:

| Clay Object | How We Use It |
|---|---|
| **Matka** (water pot) | Stores drinking water and keeps it naturally cool |
| **Kulhad** | Tiny clay cup -- chai from a kulhad tastes special! |
| **Gullak** (piggy bank) | Children save coins inside it |
| **Diya** | Small oil lamp lit during Diwali and puja |
| **Surahi** | Long-necked pot that keeps water extra cool |
| **Tandoor** | A clay oven used to bake naan and roti |
| **Clay plates and bowls** | Used at weddings and fairs -- eco-friendly, no washing needed! |

Kavya loved helping Dadi the most during the weeks before **Diwali**. They would make hundreds of tiny diyas, paint them in gold and red, and sell them at the local market. Kavya felt proud seeing families light up their homes with diyas she had painted herself.

## Try It Yourself -- Make Clay at Home

You do not need to buy clay from a shop! Here is how to prepare it yourself:

1. Collect **soil** from a garden or a place where the ground is soft and muddy.
2. Use a **sieve** (chalni) to remove small stones, leaves, and twigs.
3. Put the sieved soil in a wide bowl and add **water** until it is well soaked.
4. Let it sit for two or three days. The heavy sand will settle at the bottom.
5. Carefully pour away the extra water from the top.
6. What remains is smooth, soft **clay** -- ready to shape!
7. Mould it into any shape and let it dry in the **sun**. Your creation will harden naturally.

## Designs Inspired by Nature

Look closely at a **peacock's tail feather** -- it has circles within circles in shimmering blue and green. Look at a **spider's web** -- it has perfect spirals. Nature is the greatest artist!

Potters and craftspeople often copy these natural patterns onto their work:

| Patterns Found In | What They Look Like |
|---|---|
| **Tiger** | Bold black stripes on orange fur |
| **Leopard** | Round spots scattered across golden skin |
| **Butterfly wings** | Symmetrical designs in many colours |
| **Fish** | Overlapping scales arranged in neat rows |
| **Neem leaves on a branch** | Leaves arranged alternately, one after another |
| **Sunflower** | Seeds spiralling outward from the centre |
| **Snail shell** | A perfect spiral curving inward |

> Next time you are outdoors, look for patterns -- on bark, on leaves, on insects, on feathers. You will be amazed at how many you find!

## Famous Pottery of India

Different regions of India have their own special pottery styles, each with a unique colour and character:

- **Blue Pottery** from **Jaipur, Rajasthan** -- bright blue and white designs, inspired by Persian art
- **Terracotta** from **Bankura, West Bengal** -- reddish-brown figures of horses and elephants
- **Black Pottery** from **Longpi, Manipur** -- dark, elegant pots made without a potter's wheel
- **Khurja Ceramics** from **Uttar Pradesh** -- colourful cups, plates, and vases

These art forms have been passed down from **parent to child** for hundreds of years. Each piece carries the story of its region.

## From Clay to Bricks

Did you know that the **bricks** used to build your school were made from the same type of mud that potters use? Here is how bricks are made:

1. Mud is mixed with water and sometimes straw or sand for strength.
2. The wet mixture is pressed into rectangular **moulds** (think of it like filling an ice tray with mud instead of water).
3. The moulded bricks are laid out in the **sun** to dry.
4. Once dry, they are stacked inside a huge **kiln** and baked at very high heat for many hours.
5. Out come hard, strong bricks -- ready to build walls, houses, and buildings!

## Building Walls -- One Brick at a Time

**Sameer** lives in Lucknow. His family is building a new room at the back of their house. Every evening after school, Sameer watches the masons at work.

He noticed something interesting: the bricks in each row are **shifted** compared to the row below. The mason explained, "If I stack bricks directly on top of each other, the wall will have long cracks running from top to bottom and fall easily. By **overlapping** the bricks, every crack is blocked by a solid brick above it. This makes the wall much stronger!"

Sameer thought, "So even building a wall needs clever thinking!"

## Houses Around India

India is a land of different climates -- scorching deserts, freezing mountains, heavy-rain forests, and humid coasts. People build homes using whatever materials nature provides nearby:

| Type of House | Built Using | Found In | Why This Material |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pakka house** | Bricks, cement, steel | Cities and towns across India | Strong and long-lasting |
| **Kachcha house** | Mud, cow dung, straw | Villages in Rajasthan, UP, Bihar | Keeps cool in hot summers |
| **Wooden house** | Timber logs and planks | Himachal Pradesh, Kashmir, Assam | Wood insulates against bitter cold |
| **Bamboo house** | Bamboo poles and thatched grass | Meghalaya, Assam, Tripura | Bamboo grows fast and is flexible in earthquakes |
| **Stilt house** | Wood on raised poles | Flood-prone areas in Assam and Kerala | Stays above water during floods |
| **Houseboat** | Wood, floating on water | Dal Lake, Srinagar, Kashmir | A floating home on a beautiful lake |
| **Tent** (dera) | Thick cloth or animal skins | Desert communities in Rajasthan | Easy to pack up and move |

> **Why mud houses stay cool:** Mud walls are thick and absorb heat slowly. The inside stays comfortable even when it is blazing hot outside. Cow dung coating on walls also keeps insects away!

## Staying Safe While Building

Building things is exciting, but it can also be dangerous if we are not careful. Workers at construction sites follow strict **safety rules**:

- Wear a **helmet** to protect the head from falling objects
- Wear a **bright-coloured safety jacket** so others can see you clearly
- Wear **strong, closed shoes** -- never slippers on a building site
- Never stand under a **crane** or heavy load being lifted
- Follow instructions from the **site supervisor**

**Safety at home and school too:**

- Do not run on **wet or slippery floors** -- you can slip and fall
- Stay away from **electrical sockets and wires** -- electricity can give a dangerous shock
- Walk, do not run, on **staircases**
- Know the **fire exit** route at school
- Keep **sharp objects** like scissors and knives in safe places

## Key Points to Remember

- A **kumhar** (potter) makes pots, diyas, matkas, and many objects from **clay**.
- Clay objects are dried in the sun and then baked in a **kiln (bhatti)** to become hard and strong.
- **Bricks** are also made from mud and baked in kilns -- they are the building blocks of our homes.
- **Natural patterns** from animals, plants, and shells inspire potters and artists to create beautiful designs.
- India has famous **pottery traditions** in Jaipur, Bankura, Manipur, and Khurja.
- Houses across India are built from **different materials** -- bricks, mud, wood, bamboo, or cloth -- depending on the local climate and resources.
- **Safety rules** at building sites, at home, and at school protect us from accidents and injuries.
