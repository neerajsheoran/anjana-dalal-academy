## Animals and Plants in Our Homes

**Child:** Maa, there is a chhipkali on the wall again! I do not like it. Can we chase it away?

**Mother:** I know lizards look a little scary, beta, but do you know what they do? They eat mosquitoes and other tiny insects in our home. Without lizards, we would have many more mosquitoes biting us at night!

**Child:** Oh! So the chhipkali is actually helping us?

**Mother:** Yes. And the makdi (spider) in the corner -- she catches flies and mosquitoes in her web. Even the ants on the kitchen floor are just looking for food to take back to their colony. Most of these creatures are harmless. They do not want to hurt us.

**Child:** Then why do they come into our house?

**Mother:** Because our homes are warm, safe, and have food. These creatures are just looking for a comfortable place to live, just like us! That is why we should never squash them or hurt them. If a creature bothers you, we can gently guide it outside. But never harm it.

**Child:** What about Golu our cat? She was invited, right?

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! We chose to bring Golu home. Dogs, cats, parrots, and fish are pets we invite into our families. Cows and goats give us fresh milk. We take care of them, and they bring us joy and nourishment. That is how living together works -- everyone benefits.

## How Plants and Animals Help Each Other

**Child:** Maa, how do animals help plants? I thought only plants help animals by giving food.

**Mother:** It works both ways, beta! Think about our mango tree. A monkey eats a mango and throws the seed far away. That seed lands in the soil, and one day a brand new mango tree grows there. The monkey planted a tree without even knowing!

**Child:** That is so funny! The monkey is a gardener!

**Mother:** And squirrels are even funnier. They collect nuts and bury them in the soil to eat later. But they forget where they hid many of those nuts! Those forgotten nuts grow into new trees. Scientists say squirrels accidentally plant thousands of trees every year.

**Child:** What about bees? They just buzz around flowers.

**Mother:** Bees do something very important. When a bee sits on a flower to drink nectar, tiny grains of pollen stick to its body. When the bee flies to the next flower, the pollen rubs off. This is called **pollination**, and it is how many plants make fruits and seeds. Without bees, we would not have mangoes, apples, or guavas!

**Child:** And keenchue (earthworms)?

**Mother:** Earthworms burrow through the soil and their droppings make the soil **fertile** -- rich and healthy. Healthy soil means healthier plants. That is why your Naana always says "earthworms are the best friends of a farmer."

**Child:** So everyone is helping everyone else!

**Mother:** Exactly! Plants feed animals. Animals spread seeds and make soil healthy. People water plants, plant new trees, and take care of animals. And in return, we get food, fresh air, wood, medicine, milk, and honey. It is a beautiful circle of giving and receiving. That is what **living in harmony** means.

## The Grey Wagtails and Caring for Nature

**Child:** Maa, can I adopt a plant? My teacher said we can make a plant friend!

**Mother:** What a wonderful idea! Let us choose the champa tree near our gate. You can give it a name, water it every morning, and watch it through the seasons. Right now it has green leaves. In a few weeks, it will grow white flowers with the most beautiful smell.

**Child:** I will call it Champi! And Maa, can I do the ant experiment too? The one where you put sugar, papad, and roti on the ground?

**Mother:** Of course! Put a pinch of each near an ant trail and wait. You will see the ants rush to the sugar first. They love sweet things! Watch how they carry crumbs much bigger than their own body back to their colony. Ants are some of the hardest-working creatures on Earth.

**Child:** Maa, tell me about the wagtail birds -- the ones that children celebrate!

**Mother:** In Valparai, a hill town in Tamil Nadu, there is a lovely tradition. Every winter, small birds called **Grey Wagtails** fly down from the cold mountains to warmer towns. When the first Grey Wagtail arrives in Valparai, the school children put up welcome posters and share sweets with everyone! They call it **Vaalatti Kuruvi** in Tamil.

**Child:** Why do they celebrate a bird?

**Mother:** Because it shows that the people of Valparai care about nature. They notice the birds, they welcome them, and they teach their children to love wildlife. That is what living in harmony is all about -- paying attention to the plants and animals around us, respecting them, and letting them be part of our world. When we take care of nature, nature takes care of us.
