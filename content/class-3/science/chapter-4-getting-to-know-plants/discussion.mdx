## Types of Plants

**Child:** Maa, our teacher said there are many types of plants. How do we tell them apart?

**Mother:** Look at the plants around our house, beta. See that tall **neem tree** in the park? It has one thick trunk made of hard wood and many branches. That is a **tree**. Now look at our **tulsi** plant -- it is smaller, has many thin brown stems coming out near the ground, and looks bushy. That is a **shrub**.

**Child:** And what about the pudina in our kitchen garden? It is so tiny!

**Mother:** That is an **herb**. Herbs have soft green stems that you can bend easily. They are smaller than shrubs. And the **money plant** climbing up our balcony wall? That is a **climber** -- it cannot stand on its own, so it holds on to the wall for support.

**Child:** What about the kaddu growing in Naani's field? It spreads all over the ground!

**Mother:** Excellent observation! That is a **creeper**. Creepers have thin stems too, but instead of climbing up, they spread sideways along the ground. Kaddu, tarbooz, and lauki are all creepers. Some plants can even be both -- if they find a wall, they climb; if not, they creep!

**Child:** And Maa, you said rice and wheat come from grasses. But grasses are so small!

**Mother:** Not all grasses are small! **Bamboo** is a grass, and it grows taller than many trees! Grasses have special hollow stems and thin, flat leaves. The chawal we eat, the gehu in our roti -- they all come from seeds of large grasses. Amazing, right?

## Parts of a Plant and Leaves

**Child:** Maa, what do the different parts of a plant do?

**Mother:** Think of a plant like a little factory. The **roots** are like anchors -- they hold the plant firmly in the soil and soak up water from deep underground. The **stem** is like a water pipe that carries water up to the leaves.

**Child:** And the leaves?

**Mother:** The leaves are the **kitchen** of the plant! They use sunlight to cook food for the entire plant. Without leaves, a plant would starve.

**Child:** What about the flowers? Are they just for looking pretty?

**Mother:** Flowers look beautiful, but they have an important job -- they help the plant make **new baby plants**. After flowers do their work, they turn into **fruits**. And inside the fruit, there are **seeds**. If you plant those seeds in soil and water them, a new plant grows!

**Child:** Maa, why do different plants have different shaped leaves?

**Mother:** Every plant has adapted to where it lives. The **peepal** leaf is heart-shaped with a pointed tip so rainwater drips off easily. The **lotus** leaf is waxy, so water rolls off like tiny silver balls. The **khejri** in Rajasthan has very tiny leaves so it does not lose too much water in the hot desert.

**Child:** So leaves are like clues that tell us about the plant!

**Mother:** Exactly! And not just the shape -- you can tell plants apart by their **smell** too. Crush a tulsi leaf and a pudina leaf. They smell completely different, right? Our nose is a wonderful plant detective!

## Fun Facts about Indian Plants

**Child:** Maa, what is the biggest flower in the world?

**Mother:** It is called **Rafflesia**, and it grows in Mizoram in Northeast India. It can be as wide as an open umbrella! But here is a funny thing -- it smells terrible, like rotting meat. That bad smell actually attracts insects that help the flower make seeds.

**Child:** Eww! A stinky flower! What about bamboo? You said it is a grass, but it is SO tall!

**Mother:** Yes, bamboo is the tallest grass on Earth. People make furniture, baskets, scaffolding, and even houses from bamboo. In Northeast India, people eat bamboo shoots as a delicious vegetable!

**Child:** Maa, where does the sugar in our chai come from?

**Mother:** From the stem of **sugarcane**! Sugarcane is also a type of grass. When you chew a piece of sugarcane, sweet juice comes out. That juice is processed in factories to make the white sugar we use at home. So every time you have chai, you are tasting the sweetness of a grass!
