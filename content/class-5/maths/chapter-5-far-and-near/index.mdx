## Units of Measuring Length

We pick different units depending on what we are measuring:

| Unit | Short form | Used for |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Millimetre | mm | Tiny things — thickness of a SIM card, width of a pencil lead |
| Centimetre | cm | Small things — length of a crayon, width of a notebook |
| Metre | m | Medium things — height of a classroom door, length of a swimming pool |
| Kilometre | km | Long distances — distance between towns and cities |

---

## Relationships Between Units

```
10 mm  = 1 cm
100 cm = 1 m
1,000 m = 1 km
```

> **"Kilo" means thousand.** So 1 kilometre = 1,000 metres. The same prefix appears in kilogram (1,000 grams) and kilolitre (1,000 litres).

---

## Millimetres — Measuring Tiny Things

Look closely at your ruler. Between every centimetre mark, there are **10 tiny lines**. Each gap is **1 millimetre (mm)**.

**1 cm = 10 mm**

Millimetres help us measure small things with precision:
- A new plant shoot pushing out of a seed
- Tiny screws in a pair of spectacles
- The thickness of a ₹5 coin

### Converting between mm and cm:
- 38 mm = 3 cm 8 mm (because 38 = 30 + 8, and 30 mm = 3 cm)
- 5 cm 2 mm = 52 mm (because 5 cm = 50 mm, plus 2 mm)

---

## Centimetres and Metres

**100 cm = 1 m**

### Converting between cm and m:
- 346 cm = 3 m 46 cm (because 346 = 300 + 46, and 300 cm = 3 m)
- 4,528 cm = 45 m 28 cm
- 7 m 15 cm = 715 cm

---

## Metres and Kilometres

**1,000 m = 1 km**

When we walk **1,000 metres**, we say we have walked **1 kilometre**.

### Converting between m and km:
- 27 km = 27,000 m
- 5,340 m = 5 km 340 m
- 18 km 75 m = 18,075 m

## Choosing the Right Unit

| What you are measuring | Best unit |
|----------------------|-----------|
| Height of the Qutub Minar | 73 **metres** |
| Length of a lunch box | 20 **centimetres** |
| Depth of a swimming pool | 3 **metres** |
| Length of a pencil | 17 **centimetres** |
| Distance between Delhi and Mumbai | **kilometres** |
| Thickness of a notebook cover | **millimetres** |

## Comparing Lengths

To compare lengths, first convert them to the **same unit**.

**Example:** Which is longer — 380 cm or 4 m?
- 4 m = 400 cm
- 380 cm &lt; 400 cm
- So 380 cm **&lt;** 4 m

**Example:** Which is longer — 7 m 8 cm or 7 m 80 cm?
- 7 m 8 cm = 708 cm
- 7 m 80 cm = 780 cm
- So 7 m 8 cm **&lt;** 7 m 80 cm

## Adding and Subtracting Lengths

### Method 1: Add like units separately

**Example:** 5 km 350 m + 3 km 750 m
- km: 5 + 3 = 8 km
- m: 350 + 750 = 1,100 m = 1 km 100 m
- Total: 8 km + 1 km 100 m = **9 km 100 m**

### Method 2: Convert everything to one unit, then calculate

**Example:** 50 m - 12 m 45 cm
- 50 m = 5,000 cm
- 12 m 45 cm = 1,245 cm
- 5,000 - 1,245 = 3,755 cm = **37 m 55 cm**

---

## Multiplying and Dividing Lengths

**Example:** A kurta needs 2 m 50 cm of cloth. How much for 12 kurtas?
- 12 x 2 m 50 cm = 12 x 2 m + 12 x 50 cm
- = 24 m + 600 cm = 24 m + 6 m = **30 m**

**Example:** A 15 km 300 m highway stretch is built equally over 5 days. How much per day?
- 15 km 300 m = 15,300 m
- 15,300 / 5 = **3,060 m = 3 km 60 m** per day

## Feet and Inches

In daily life, height is often measured in **feet and inches**:
- **1 inch is about 2 cm 5 mm** (2.54 cm)
- **1 foot = 12 inches, which is about 30 cm**

> Stand against a wall, have someone mark your height, and measure it in feet and inches!
