## Key Concepts

- **60 seconds = 1 minute**; **60 minutes = 1 hour**; **24 hours = 1 day**
- **12-hour format** uses AM (midnight to noon) and PM (noon to midnight)
- **24-hour format** counts from 00:00 to 23:59 — no AM/PM needed
- To convert PM to 24-hour: **add 12** to the hour (3:30 PM = 15:30)
- To convert AM to 24-hour: keep as is (7:30 AM = 07:30)
- **12:00 PM** (noon) = 12:00 hours; **12:00 AM** (midnight) = 00:00 hours
- Hours to minutes: **multiply by 60** (3 hours = 180 minutes)
- Minutes to hours: **divide by 60** (90 min = 1 hr 30 min)
- Minutes to seconds: **multiply by 60** (4 min = 240 sec)
- Seconds to minutes: **divide by 60** (200 sec = 3 min 20 sec)
- To find **duration**: count forward from start time to end time
- To find **end time**: add duration to start time
- To find **start time**: subtract duration from end time
- The seconds hand sweeps one full circle every **60 seconds** (1 minute)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Second** | A very short time unit; 60 seconds = 1 minute |
| **Minute** | A short time unit; 60 minutes = 1 hour |
| **Hour** | A time unit; 24 hours = 1 day |
| **AM** | Ante Meridiem — midnight to noon |
| **PM** | Post Meridiem — noon to midnight |
| **24-hour format** | Time shown as 00:00 to 23:59, used by railways and airports |
| **Duration** | How long something lasts from start to finish |
| **Elapsed time** | The amount of time that has passed between two events |

## Remember These

- **12:00 AM is midnight, 12:00 PM is noon** — students often mix these up
- When calculating duration across an hour boundary, **split the problem** (e.g., 7:40 to 8:20 = 20 min + 20 min = 40 min)
- In 24-hour format, **13:00 = 1:00 PM**, not 1:00 AM — subtract 12 to get PM time
- Seconds matter in sports — race winners can be decided by **just 1 second**
