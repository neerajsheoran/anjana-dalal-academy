## Why Do We Need Seconds?

**Child:** Maa, why do we need seconds? Minutes and hours seem enough.

**Mother:** Do you remember watching the Kho-Kho final at your school's sports day? The chaser tagged the runner in the last moment — just a split second decided the match!

**Child:** Yes! It was so close! Everyone was shouting!

**Mother:** That is exactly why we need seconds. In a school race, the gap between first and second place might be just 1 or 2 seconds. In the Olympics, swimmers win gold by just one-hundredth of a second! Without seconds, we would not be able to tell who won.

**Child:** So seconds are for when every tiny moment counts?

**Mother:** Exactly. You would not time a train journey in seconds — that is hours. And you would not time a clap in hours — that is a fraction of a second. The right unit for the right situation.

## 12-Hour and 24-Hour Time

**Child:** Maa, the railway ticket says departure at 15:15 hours. But our clock only goes up to 12! What does that mean?

**Mother:** That is the 24-hour format. Indian Railways and airports use it to avoid confusion. In the regular 12-hour format, 3:15 could be afternoon or early morning — you need AM or PM to be clear. In the 24-hour system, 15:15 can only mean 3:15 PM. And 03:15 can only mean 3:15 AM. No mix-ups!

**Child:** So I just add 12 to convert afternoon times?

**Mother:** Yes! 1 PM is 13:00, 4 PM is 16:00, 8 PM is 20:00. For AM times, the numbers stay the same — 9 AM is 09:00 hours. Two special cases to remember: 12 noon is 12:00 hours, and 12 midnight is 00:00 hours.

**Child:** What about the Shatabdi Express? If it departs at 06:15 and arrives at 12:30, how long is the journey?

**Mother:** From 06:15 to 12:15 is exactly 6 hours. Then 12:15 to 12:30 is 15 more minutes. Total: 6 hours 15 minutes. The 24-hour clock makes this calculation so clean!

**Child:** Now I understand why train timetables use it. No confusion about morning or evening!

## Converting Between Time Units

**Child:** Maa, my dance class is 50 minutes long. My friend said that is 3,000 seconds. Is she right?

**Mother:** Let us check! 50 minutes times 60 seconds per minute equals... can you calculate?

**Child:** 50 times 60. I know 5 times 60 is 300, so 50 times 60 is 3,000. She is right!

**Mother:** And if I told you that you practised tabla for 175 minutes this week? How many hours is that?

**Child:** Divide by 60. 60 times 2 is 120. 175 minus 120 is 55. So that is 2 hours and 55 minutes!

**Mother:** Perfect. The golden rule: going from bigger units to smaller — multiply. Going from smaller to bigger — divide. The same pattern applies to length, weight, and capacity conversions.

**Child:** Oh! So all unit conversions follow the same idea!

**Mother:** Now you are seeing the big picture, beta.

## Finding Time Duration

**Child:** Maa, yesterday school started at 8:00 AM and ended at 2:15 PM. How long was I in school?

**Mother:** Count forward from the start. From 8:00 to 12:00 is 4 hours. From 12:00 to 2:00 is 2 more hours. From 2:00 to 2:15 is 15 minutes. Total: 6 hours 15 minutes.

**Child:** Is there a quicker way?

**Mother:** Convert to 24-hour format and subtract. End time is 14:15, start time is 08:00. Hours: 14 minus 8 equals 6. Minutes: 15 minus 0 equals 15. Answer: 6 hours 15 minutes!

**Child:** What if the minutes part needs borrowing? Like 10:05 minus 8:40?

**Mother:** Sharp thinking! You cannot subtract 40 from 5, so borrow 1 hour from the 10. That turns 10:05 into 9 hours 65 minutes. Now subtract: 9 minus 8 equals 1 hour, 65 minus 40 equals 25 minutes. Answer: 1 hour 25 minutes.

**Child:** Just like borrowing in regular subtraction — but here we borrow 60 instead of 10!

**Mother:** Exactly the same idea, just with a different base.

## Seconds in Real Life

**Child:** Maa, how many seconds are in a whole day?

**Mother:** Let us work it out. 1 day equals 24 hours. 24 times 60 gives 1,440 minutes. 1,440 times 60 gives... 86,400 seconds!

**Child:** 86,400 seconds! It sounds like a lot, but the day goes by so fast.

**Mother:** Here is something wonderful. Your heart beats roughly once every second. So in one day, your heart beats about 86,400 times! In a year, that is roughly 3 crore 15 lakh heartbeats.

**Child:** And in the Olympics, sprinters run 100 metres in under 10 seconds. That means they cover about 10 metres every second!

**Mother:** Faster than most cars in city traffic! And ISRO's rockets, during launch, cover several kilometres every second. The same tiny unit — a second — measures both a heartbeat and a rocket launch. Only the scale changes.

**Child:** Seconds really are racing, just like the chapter name says!

## Planning Our Day with Time

**Child:** Maa, I want to plan my Sunday properly. How do I figure out how much free time I have?

**Mother:** Write down everything you do and how long each thing takes. Sleeping — 9 hours. Getting ready — 45 minutes. Meals — 1 hour 30 minutes. Homework — 1 hour 30 minutes. Cricket practice — 1 hour. Painting class — 1 hour. Let us add them up.

**Child:** 9 hours plus 45 minutes is 9 hours 45 minutes. Plus 1 hour 30 minutes is 11 hours 15 minutes. Plus 1 hour 30 minutes is 12 hours 45 minutes. Plus 1 hour is 13 hours 45 minutes. Plus 1 hour is 14 hours 45 minutes.

**Mother:** A day has 24 hours. So your free time is 24 hours minus 14 hours 45 minutes. That is 9 hours 15 minutes!

**Child:** Over 9 hours of free time? That feels like a lot! But it never seems that way.

**Mother:** Because small things add up. Watching "just one more" video ten times is almost an hour gone. Managing your time well is not just maths — it is a life skill. Try keeping a time diary for one week, and you will be amazed where the hours disappear.

**Child:** I will make a time chart, like a mini data project!

**Mother:** Now you are connecting this chapter to the Data chapter. That is exactly how real learning happens.
