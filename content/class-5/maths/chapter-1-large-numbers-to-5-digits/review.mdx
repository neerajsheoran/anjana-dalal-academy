## Key Concepts

- Numbers up to **5 digits** (up to 99,999) use the columns: Ones, Tens, Hundreds, Thousands, **Ten Thousands (TTh)**
- Every **10 of one unit** makes **1 of the next bigger unit** (10 Ones = 1 Ten, 10 Tens = 1 Hundred, etc.)
- A **comma** is placed after the thousands column to make large numbers easy to read (e.g., 52,413)
- **Expanded form** splits a number into the value each digit contributes: 52,413 = 50,000 + 2,000 + 400 + 10 + 3
- To **compare** two numbers, first check the **number of digits** — more digits means a larger number
- If both numbers have the **same number of digits**, compare from the **leftmost digit** one by one
- **Ascending order** = smallest to biggest; **Descending order** = biggest to smallest
- **Rounding off** means finding the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand
- Rounding shortcut: look at the digit **just to the right** of the place you are rounding
- If that digit is **0-4**, round **down**; if it is **5-9**, round **up**
- We use the **same ten digits** (0-9) to write any number, just placed in different columns
- Large numbers appear in daily life: distances, prices, populations, odometer readings
- Swapping digits in a number can make it bigger or smaller — useful for puzzles

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Place value** | The worth of a digit depending on its position in the number |
| **Expanded form** | Splitting a number into the sum of each digit's value |
| **Ascending order** | Listing numbers from smallest to biggest |
| **Descending order** | Listing numbers from biggest to smallest |
| **Rounding off** | Finding the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand |
| **Digit** | The ten symbols (0-9) used to write any number |

## Remember These

- A number with **more digits is always larger**, even if its first digit looks smaller (31,002 > 8,999)
- When rounding, always look at the digit **to the right** of the rounding place — not the digit itself
- In expanded form, a **zero digit** contributes nothing (e.g., 10,005 = 10,000 + 5, no hundreds/tens)
- Place the **comma after the thousands place** to avoid misreading large numbers
