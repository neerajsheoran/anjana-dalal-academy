## Multiplication — Many Ways to Multiply

You already know your multiplication tables. In this chapter, we will explore how to multiply **larger numbers** using different clever strategies — and discover that there is always more than one path to the answer!

---

## Order Doesn't Matter in Multiplication

When you multiply two numbers, swapping the order gives the same result:

- **3 x 7 = 7 x 3 = 21**
- **6 x 9 = 9 x 6 = 54**
- **8 x 15 = 15 x 8 = 120**
- **4 x 0 = 0 x 4 = 0**

> This is called the **commutative property** of multiplication. Whether you think of it as 3 groups of 7 or 7 groups of 3, the total is the same.

## Multiplying by 10s, 100s, and 1,000s

### Multiplying by 10:
Every digit shifts **one place to the left** — just put a zero at the end.
- 6 x 10 = **60**
- 35 x 10 = **350**
- 70 x 10 = **700**

### Multiplying by 100:
Every digit shifts **two places left** — put two zeros at the end.
- 4 x 100 = **400**
- 9 x 100 = **900**
- 15 x 100 = **1,500**

### Multiplying by 1,000:
Every digit shifts **three places left** — put three zeros at the end.
- 3 x 1,000 = **3,000**
- 7 x 1,000 = **7,000**
- 25 x 1,000 = **25,000**

### Multiplying two multiples of 10:
- 40 x 30 = 4 x 3 x 10 x 10 = **12 x 100 = 1,200**
- 50 x 70 = 5 x 7 x 100 = **3,500**
- 300 x 20 = **6,000**

> **Shortcut:** Count the trailing zeros in both numbers, multiply the non-zero parts, then stick all those zeros back on.

## Strategy 1: Break and Multiply (Distributive Property)

Split one number into parts, multiply each part, then add the results together.

### Example: 16 x 7
- Break 16 into 10 + 6
- 10 x 7 = 70
- 6 x 7 = 42
- 70 + 42 = **112**

### Example: 14 x 23
- Break 14 into 10 + 4, and 23 into 20 + 3
- 10 x 20 = 200
- 10 x 3 = 30
- 4 x 20 = 80
- 4 x 3 = 12
- 200 + 30 + 80 + 12 = **322**

This can be arranged as a **grid** (area model):

```
        20      3
   +--------+------+
10 |  200   |  30  |  230
   +--------+------+
 4 |   80   |  12  |   92
   +--------+------+
      280      42    = 322
```

## Strategy 2: Doubling and Halving

If you **double** one number and **halve** the other, the product stays the same!

### Example: 4 x 15
- Double 4 -> 8, Halve 15 -> 7.5... not clean.
- Better: Halve 4 -> 2, Double 15 -> 30
- 2 x 30 = **60** (much easier!)

### Example: 24 x 5
- Halve 24 -> 12, Double 5 -> 10
- 12 x 10 = **120**

> **When does this shine?** When one number is 5, 25, or 50 — doubling it gives 10, 50, or 100, which are very easy to multiply with.

More examples:
- 5 x 14 = 10 x 7 = **70**
- 25 x 16 = 50 x 8 = **400**
- 50 x 32 = 100 x 16 = **1,600**

---

## Strategy 3: Nearest Multiple

Round one number to a nearby multiple of 10, multiply, then fix the small error.

### Example: 6 x 19
- 19 is close to 20
- 6 x 20 = 120
- We multiplied by 1 extra, so subtract: 120 - 6 = **114**

### Example: 13 x 21
- 21 is close to 20
- 13 x 20 = 260
- We multiplied by 1 less, so add: 260 + 13 = **273**

More examples:
- 98 x 12 = 100 x 12 - 2 x 12 = 1,200 - 24 = **1,176**
- 8 x 47 = 8 x 50 - 8 x 3 = 400 - 24 = **376**

## Multiplying 3-Digit by 1-Digit Numbers

### Example: 315 x 6

Break 315 into 300 + 10 + 5:
- 6 x 300 = 1,800
- 6 x 10 = 60
- 6 x 5 = 30
- Total: 1,800 + 60 + 30 = **1,890**

---

## Multiplying 3-Digit by 2-Digit Numbers

### Example: 372 x 14

Break into parts:
- 4 x 372: 4 x 300 = 1,200; 4 x 70 = 280; 4 x 2 = 8 -> **1,488**
- 10 x 372: 10 x 300 = 3,000; 10 x 70 = 700; 10 x 2 = 20 -> **3,720**
- Total: 1,488 + 3,720 = **5,208**

### Example: 486 x 125

- 5 x 486 = 2,430
- 20 x 486 = 9,720
- 100 x 486 = 48,600
- Total: 2,430 + 9,720 + 48,600 = **60,750**

## Waste and Composting — A Real-Life Problem

A household of 5 produces about **40 kg** of kitchen waste per month.

**In a year:** 12 x 40 = ?
- 10 x 40 = 400
- 2 x 40 = 80
- Total: **480 kg** of waste!

By composting, they can produce about 160 kg of compost, saving **₹4,000** per year (at ₹25 per kg).

> **Remember:** Reducing waste and composting kitchen scraps saves money and protects our environment!

## Dairy Cooperative

A dairy cooperative gathers milk from **315 farmers**, each owning at least **6 milk-giving buffaloes**.

Minimum buffaloes: 315 x 6 = **1,890 buffaloes**

Among these, **372 Murrah buffaloes** (a famous breed from Haryana) each produce **14 litres of milk** daily:
- 372 x 14 = **5,208 litres** of milk every day!

> **Amritaben Patel** was a pioneer in India's milk cooperative movement, helping thousands of women dairy farmers earn fair prices for their milk.

## Multiplication Patterns

### The 1-1-1 pattern:
```
    1 x 1     = 1
   11 x 11    = 121
  111 x 111   = 12,321
 1111 x 1111  = 1,234,321
```

### Multiplying by 11:
```
11 x 15 = 165    (1, 1+5, 5)
11 x 27 = 297    (2, 2+7, 7)
11 x 43 = 473    (4, 4+3, 3)
```

### The 9-pattern:
```
1 x 9 + 1 = 10
12 x 9 + 2 = 110
123 x 9 + 3 = 1,110
1234 x 9 + 4 = 11,110
```

## The Merchant's Reward — A Puzzle

A merchant offers three helpers a choice:
1. Start with **4** coins, **double** every day for 7 days
2. Start with **2** coins, **triple** every day for 7 days
3. Start with **1** coin, **multiply by 4** every day for 7 days

| Day | Helper 1 (x2) | Helper 2 (x3) | Helper 3 (x4) |
|-----|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| Start | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Day 1 | 8 | 6 | 4 |
| Day 2 | 16 | 18 | 16 |
| Day 3 | 32 | 54 | 64 |
| Day 4 | 64 | 162 | 256 |
| Day 5 | 128 | 486 | 1,024 |
| Day 6 | 256 | 1,458 | 4,096 |
| Day 7 | 512 | 4,374 | 16,384 |

**Helper 3 wins by a huge margin!** Even though she began with just 1 coin, multiplying by 4 grows far faster than doubling or tripling.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Product** | The answer you get when you multiply two numbers |
| **Commutative property** | Changing the order of multiplication does not change the product (4 x 9 = 9 x 4) |
| **Distributive property** | Breaking a number into parts and multiplying each part separately |
| **Doubling** | Multiplying by 2 |
| **Halving** | Dividing by 2 |
| **Estimate** | Finding a rough answer by rounding |
