## Perimeter — The Boundary Length

The **perimeter** of a shape is the **total distance around its boundary** — the length you would travel if you walked all the way around the edge.

![Rectangle with all four sides labeled showing how perimeter is the total boundary length](perimeter-rectangle.png)

### How to Find Perimeter:
Simply add up all the side lengths!

**Example:** A rectangle that is 18 cm long and 12 cm wide:
- Perimeter = 18 + 12 + 18 + 12 = **60 cm**

### Useful Formulas:

**Rectangle:**
> Perimeter = 2 × Length + 2 × Breadth

**Square** (all four sides are equal):
> Perimeter = 4 × Side length

**Example:** A square with side 7 cm:
- Perimeter = 4 × 7 = **28 cm**

---

## Area — The Space Inside

The **area** of a shape is how much **flat surface it covers** — like how much fabric you need to cover a cushion.

### Finding Area by Counting Squares

![A rectangle on a square grid with unit squares counted inside to show area](area-counting-squares.png)

Place the shape on a **grid of unit squares** and count how many squares fit inside.

- A square with side 1 cm covers an area of **1 square cm** (written as 1 cm²)
- Count complete squares, and estimate for partial squares

### Comparing Areas

To tell which shape is larger, place both on the same grid and count. You can also **cut one shape and overlap** it on the other to compare directly.

---

## Which Shapes Tile (Cover Without Gaps)?

When covering a surface like a floor or tabletop, some shapes fit together perfectly and some do not:

| Shape | Tiles without gaps? |
|-------|:------------------:|
| Squares | Yes |
| Rectangles | Yes |
| Triangles | Yes |
| Circles | No (they always leave gaps!) |

> The area of a tabletop = the number of square tiles needed to cover it completely (no gaps, no overlaps).

---

## Area and Perimeter of Rectangles

### Area of a Rectangle

Picture a rectangle built from square patches — 5 rows with 8 patches in each row:
- Area = 5 × 8 = **40 square units**

The number of rows = the **length**, the number of columns = the **breadth**.

> **Area of Rectangle = Length × Breadth**

**Example:** Length = 35 m, Breadth = 22 m
- Area = 35 × 22 = **770 square metres**

### Perimeter of a Rectangle

> **Perimeter of Rectangle = 2 × Length + 2 × Breadth**

**Example:** Length = 9 cm, Breadth = 5 cm
- Perimeter = 2 × 9 + 2 × 5 = 18 + 10 = **28 cm**

---

## Area and Perimeter of Squares

Since all sides of a square are equal:

> **Area of Square = Side × Side**

> **Perimeter of Square = 4 × Side**

**Example:** Side = 6 cm
- Area = 6 × 6 = **36 square cm**
- Perimeter = 4 × 6 = **24 cm**

---

## Important Discovery: Same Area ≠ Same Perimeter!

![Three different rectangles all with area 12 square units but different perimeters shown on a grid](same-area-different-perimeter.png)

**Shapes with equal area can have unequal perimeters**, and shapes with equal perimeters can have unequal areas!

### Example — Same area (18 square units), different perimeters:

| Shape | Dimensions | Area | Perimeter |
|-------|-----------|------|-----------|
| Rectangle A | 9 × 2 | 18 | 22 |
| Rectangle B | 6 × 3 | 18 | 18 |
| Rectangle C | 18 × 1 | 18 | 38 |

All three cover 18 square units, but their boundary lengths are very different!

### Example — Same perimeter (20 units), different areas:

| Shape | Dimensions | Perimeter | Area |
|-------|-----------|-----------|------|
| Rectangle A | 9 × 1 | 20 | 9 |
| Rectangle B | 7 × 3 | 20 | 21 |
| Rectangle C | 5 × 5 | 20 | 25 |

All three have a boundary of 20 units, but different amounts of space inside. The **square** (5 × 5) gives the **biggest area**.

> **Key insight:** Increasing the area does not automatically increase the perimeter — and the other way round!

---

## Finding Missing Dimensions

If you know the area and one side, you can work out the other:

**Example:** Area = 72 square m, Length = 18 m. What is the breadth?
- Breadth = Area ÷ Length = 72 ÷ 18 = **4 m**

---

## Estimating Area of Irregular Shapes

For shapes that are not neat rectangles (like your palm print or a leaf):
1. Place the shape on a **square grid**
2. Count every full square that lies inside
3. For partly-covered squares: if more than half is inside, count it as 1; if less, count it as 0
4. Add them up for an **approximate area**

---

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Perimeter** | The total distance around the boundary of a shape |
| **Area** | The amount of flat surface a shape covers |
| **Square unit** | The area of a square with side 1 unit — the basic unit for measuring area |
| **Length** | The longer side of a rectangle |
| **Breadth** | The shorter side of a rectangle (also called width) |
| **Tiling** | Covering a surface with shapes so there are no gaps or overlaps |
