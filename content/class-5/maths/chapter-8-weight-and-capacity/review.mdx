## Key Concepts

- Weight units from lightest to heaviest: **mg → g → kg → quintal → tonne**
- **1,000 mg = 1 g**; **1,000 g = 1 kg**; **100 kg = 1 quintal**; **1,000 kg = 1 tonne**
- **10 quintals = 1 tonne**
- Capacity units: **1,000 ml = 1 litre**
- To convert kg to g: multiply by 1,000 (4 kg 200 g = 4,200 g)
- To convert g to kg: divide by 1,000 (12,350 g = 12 kg 350 g)
- To convert litres to ml: multiply by 1,000 (2 l 500 ml = 2,500 ml)
- When adding weights/capacities: add like units separately, then regroup if needed
- When subtracting: convert everything to the smaller unit first
- When multiplying: multiply kg and g parts separately, then combine
- When dividing: convert to the smaller unit, divide, then convert back
- Always convert to the **same unit** before comparing weights or capacities
- Check a weighing scale's **maximum capacity** and **smallest division** before using it

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Milligram (mg)** | A tiny unit of weight; 1,000 mg = 1 g |
| **Gram (g)** | A small unit of weight; 1,000 g = 1 kg |
| **Kilogram (kg)** | A standard unit of weight; 100 kg = 1 quintal |
| **Quintal** | A large unit of weight; 10 quintals = 1 tonne |
| **Tonne** | The largest common weight unit; 1 tonne = 1,000 kg |
| **Millilitre (ml)** | A small unit of capacity; 1,000 ml = 1 litre |
| **Litre (l)** | A standard unit of capacity for liquids |
| **Capacity** | The maximum amount of liquid a container can hold |

## Remember These

- **6 kg 75 g = 6,075 g**, NOT 6,750 g — the 75 g stays as 75, not 750
- **8 kg 8 g = 8,008 g** — do not forget the zero in between
- **2 l 500 ml = 2,500 ml**, NOT 2,050 ml — convert litres fully before adding ml
- Always make sure the scale needle points to **zero** before weighing anything
