## Measuring Weight

We measure how heavy things are using **weight** (mass). The unit we choose depends on how heavy the object is:

| Unit | Short form | Used for |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Milligram | mg | Very light things — medicines, spices |
| Gram | g | Small things — a coin, a biscuit, a banana |
| Kilogram | kg | Medium things — a bag of atta, a watermelon |
| Quintal | q | Heavy things — a large sack of rice |
| Tonne | t | Very heavy things — a loaded truck, an elephant |

---

## Relationships Between Units

```
1,000 mg = 1 g
1,000 g  = 1 kg
100 kg   = 1 quintal
10 quintals = 1 tonne
1,000 kg = 1 tonne
```

> **From lightest to heaviest:** mg -> g -> kg -> quintal -> tonne

---

## Converting Between Grams and Kilograms

**1 kg = 1,000 g**

### Examples:
- 4 kg 200 g = 4,000 g + 200 g = **4,200 g**
- 6 kg 75 g = 6,000 g + 75 g = **6,075 g** (not 6,750 g!)
- 8 kg 8 g = **8,008 g**
- 12,350 g = 12 kg 350 g
- 5,060 g = 5 kg 60 g

> **Common mistake:** 6 kg 75 g is NOT 6,750 g. Be careful! 75 g stays as 75 g, not 750. Always convert the kg part fully to grams first, then add.

---

## Milligrams — For Very Light Things

**1 g = 1,000 mg**

Milligrams are used for:
- Weight of a grain of rice (about 25 mg)
- **Medicine tablets** (like a 500 mg crocin tablet)
- **Gold jewellery** (5 g 200 mg = 5,200 mg)

### Converting:
- 5 g 200 mg = 5,000 mg + 200 mg = **5,200 mg**
- A 3 g sugar packet = **3,000 mg**

---

## Quintals and Tonnes — For Very Heavy Things

| Conversion | Example |
|----------------|---------|
| 100 kg = 1 quintal | A big sack of wheat |
| 1,000 kg = 1 tonne | A Maruti car |
| 5,000 kg = 5 tonnes | A heavy truck with cargo |

## Comparing Weights

To compare weights, convert them to the **same unit** first.

**Example:** Which is heavier — 2 kg 300 g or 2,400 g?
- 2 kg 300 g = 2,300 g
- 2,300 g < 2,400 g
- So 2 kg 300 g **&lt;** 2,400 g

**Example:** Compare 7 kg 50 g and 7,050 g.
- 7 kg 50 g = 7,050 g
- They are **equal**!

---

## Reading a Weighing Scale

Weighing scales come in different types:
- **Spring scales** with a dial and a needle
- **Digital scales** that display the number directly
- **Balance scales** where you match with known metal weights

Always check before measuring:
1. What is the **maximum** the scale can weigh?
2. What does each **small mark** on the dial represent?
3. Make sure the needle points to **zero** before placing anything!

## Adding and Subtracting Weights

### Method 1: Add like units separately

**Example:** 3 kg 400 g + 2 kg 850 g
- kg: 3 + 2 = 5 kg
- g: 400 + 850 = 1,250 g = 1 kg 250 g
- Total: 5 + 1 kg and 250 g = **6 kg 250 g**

### Method 2: Convert everything to grams

**Example:** 3 kg 400 g - 2 kg 850 g
- 3,400 g - 2,850 g = **550 g**

---

## Multiplying and Dividing Weights

### Multiplying:
**Example:** A bag of oranges weighs 8 kg 250 g. A shopkeeper has 4 such bags.
- 4 x 8 kg = 32 kg
- 4 x 250 g = 1,000 g = 1 kg
- Total: **33 kg**

### Dividing:
**Example:** 6 kg 400 g of almonds shared equally into 4 boxes.
- 6 kg / 4 = 1 kg per box, with 2 kg remaining
- 2 kg = 2,000 g, plus 400 g = 2,400 g
- 2,400 g / 4 = 600 g per box
- Each box: **1 kg 600 g**

## Measuring Capacity

**Capacity** tells us how much liquid a container can hold.

| Unit | Short form | Used for |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Millilitre | ml | Small amounts — a spoonful of syrup, a cup of tea |
| Litre | l | Larger amounts — a water bottle, a bucket |

**1 litre = 1,000 ml**

### Converting:
- 3 l = 3,000 ml
- 2 l 500 ml = 2,500 ml (not 2,050 ml!)
- 4 l 6 ml = 4,006 ml
- 11,250 ml = 11 l 250 ml

> **Common mistake:** 2 l 500 ml = 2,500 ml, not 2,050 ml. Remember: 2 l = 2,000 ml, so add 2,000 + 500 = 2,500 ml.

---

## Adding and Subtracting Capacities

Works the same way as weight!

**Example:** 3 l 600 ml + 2 l 700 ml
- l: 3 + 2 = 5 l
- ml: 600 + 700 = 1,300 ml = 1 l 300 ml
- Total: **6 l 300 ml**

**Example:** 8 l - 4 l 200 ml
- Convert: 8,000 ml - 4,200 ml = **3,800 ml = 3 l 800 ml**

## Real-Life Problems

### Grocery Shopping:
Lakshmi bought 3 kg 200 g dal for daily use and 2 kg 650 g extra for a festival.
Total: 3 kg 200 g + 2 kg 650 g = **5 kg 850 g**

### Water Bottles:
Riya carries a 750 ml bottle to school and drinks 2 full bottles each day.
Total: 750 ml + 750 ml = 1,500 ml = **1 litre 500 ml**

### Petrol Pump:
- A truck fills 400 litres x 4 trucks = 1,600 litres
- A bus fills 250 litres x 5 buses = 1,250 litres
- A car fills 45 litres x 8 cars = 360 litres

### The Maharaja's Tradition:
A maharaja donates rice equal to **12 times his weight** on his birthday. If he donated 960 kg, his weight = 960 / 12 = **80 kg**.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Milligram (mg)** | A tiny unit of weight; 1,000 mg = 1 g |
| **Gram (g)** | A small unit of weight; 1,000 g = 1 kg |
| **Kilogram (kg)** | A standard unit of weight; 100 kg = 1 quintal |
| **Quintal** | A large unit of weight; 10 quintals = 1 tonne |
| **Tonne** | The largest common unit of weight; 1 tonne = 1,000 kg |
| **Millilitre (ml)** | A small unit of capacity; 1,000 ml = 1 litre |
| **Litre (l)** | A standard unit of capacity for liquids |
| **Capacity** | The maximum amount of liquid a container can hold |
