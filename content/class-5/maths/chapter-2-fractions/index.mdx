## What Are Fractions?

Imagine your mother makes a round chapati and cuts it into 4 equal pieces. Each piece is **one-fourth** or **1/4** of the whole chapati. If she cuts it into just 2 equal pieces, each piece is **one-half** or **1/2**.

A **fraction** describes a **part of a whole**. The whole could be a chapati, a chocolate bar, a glass of milk, or anything that can be divided into equal parts.

> **Remember:** For fractions to be fair, the parts must be **equal in size**. If you cut a paratha unevenly, the bigger piece is not really "one-half"!

---

## Equivalent Fractions

**Equivalent fractions** are different-looking fractions that actually show the **same amount**.

Imagine a rectangle. Shade **1 out of 3** equal strips — that is **1/3**. Now draw a line across the middle, splitting the rectangle into 6 equal parts. The shaded region now covers **2 out of 6** parts — that is **2/6**. But the shaded area did not change!

So: **1/3 = 2/6**

### Finding Equivalent Fractions

Multiply (or divide) both the **top number (numerator)** and the **bottom number (denominator)** by the **same number**.

```
1/3 = 2/6 = 3/9 = 4/12 = 5/15 = ...
(x2)  (x3)  (x4)   (x5)
```

**Another example:**
```
2/5 = 4/10 = 6/15 = 8/20 = ... = 20/50 = 40/100
```

> **Think of it like this:** Slicing each piece into smaller equal slices changes how many pieces there are, but the total amount stays exactly the same.

### Discovering Equivalents with Paper Strips

Take strips of paper of the same length. Fold one into 2 equal parts (halves), another into 4 parts (fourths), another into 6 parts (sixths), and so on. Compare them side by side:

- 1 half = 2 fourths → **1/2 = 2/4**
- 1 half = 3 sixths → **1/2 = 3/6**
- 1 half = 4 eighths → **1/2 = 4/8**
- 1 third = 2 sixths → **1/3 = 2/6**
- 1 fourth = 3 twelfths → **1/4 = 3/12**

## Comparing Fractions — Same Denominator

When fractions have the **same denominator** (same-sized pieces), simply compare the **numerators** (how many pieces).

**Example:** Anita ate 1/5 of a ladoo tray. Kavya ate 3/5 of the same tray. Who ate more?

3 pieces out of 5 > 1 piece out of 5 → **3/5 > 1/5**

More examples:
- **5/8 > 2/8** (5 eighth-pieces is more than 2 eighth-pieces)
- **4/7 > 3/7**
- **6/9 > 1/9**

> **Rule:** Same denominator? The fraction with the **bigger numerator** wins.

---

## Comparing Fractions — Same Numerator

When fractions have the **same numerator** (same number of pieces), the one with the **smaller denominator** is bigger!

**Why?** A smaller denominator means the whole was cut into **fewer pieces**, so each piece is **larger**.

**Example:** Rohan drank 3/5 of a glass of lassi. Meena drank 3/8 of the same sized glass. Who drank more?

- A **1/5** piece is bigger than a **1/8** piece (fewer cuts = bigger portions).
- So **3 pieces of 1/5** > **3 pieces of 1/8**.
- Therefore: **3/5 > 3/8**

More examples:
- **2/3 > 2/7** (thirds are bigger than sevenths)
- **4/6 > 4/9** (sixths are bigger than ninths)
- **5/10 > 5/12** (tenths are bigger than twelfths)

> **Rule:** Same numerator? The fraction with the **smaller denominator** is greater.

## Fractions Greater Than 1

What if you eat **more pieces** than what makes one whole?

### Example with Thirds

Amma cuts each dosa into 3 equal parts.

- **3 thirds** = 3/3 = **1 whole dosa**
- **4 thirds** = 4/3 = **1 and 1/3 dosas** (written as 1 and 1/3)
- **7 thirds** = 7/3 = **2 and 1/3 dosas** (written as 2 and 1/3)
- **9 thirds** = 9/3 = **3 whole dosas**

On a number line:

```
0    1/3   2/3   3/3=1   4/3   5/3   6/3=2   7/3   8/3   9/3=3
|-----|-----|------|------|------|------|------|------|------|
```

### Example with Sixths

Dadi ate 8 pieces of barfi, where each piece is 1/6 of a whole barfi:
- 8/6 = 1 whole barfi + 2/6 barfi = **1 and 1/3 barfis**

> **Quick check:** A fraction is **greater than 1** when the numerator is **bigger** than the denominator. For instance, 7/4 > 1 because 7 > 4.

### Mixed Numbers

A **mixed number** combines a whole number with a fraction:
- 7/3 = **2 and 1/3** (two and one-third)
- 11/4 = **2 and 3/4** (two and three-fourths)
- 5/2 = **2 and 1/2** (two and a half)

## Comparing Fractions Using 1 as a Reference

A quick way to compare: check whether each fraction is **less than, equal to, or greater than 1**.

- **Numerator &lt; Denominator** → fraction is **less than 1**
- **Numerator = Denominator** → fraction **equals 1**
- **Numerator > Denominator** → fraction is **greater than 1**

**Example:** Compare 5/6 and 9/7.
- 5/6 &lt; 1 (because 5 &lt; 6)
- 9/7 > 1 (because 9 > 7)
- So **5/6 &lt; 9/7**

---

## Comparing Fractions Using 1/2 as a Reference

Another helpful trick: check if each fraction is **less than, equal to, or greater than 1/2**.

**How to tell if a fraction equals 1/2:**
The numerator is exactly half the denominator.
- 4/8 = 1/2, 5/10 = 1/2, 6/12 = 1/2

**More than 1/2:**
The numerator is **more than** half the denominator.
- 5/6 > 1/2 (because 5 > 3, and half of 6 is 3)

**Less than 1/2:**
The numerator is **less than** half the denominator.
- 2/9 &lt; 1/2 (because 2 &lt; 4.5, and half of 9 is 4.5)

**Example:** Compare 5/9 and 3/8.
- 5/9 > 1/2 (since 5 > 4.5)
- 3/8 &lt; 1/2 (since 3 &lt; 4)
- So **5/9 > 3/8**

## Fun Fraction Challenge

> If a ladybug is **1/2 cm** long, what is the total length of **10 ladybugs** lined up end to end?
>
> 10 x 1/2 = 10/2 = **5 cm**

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Fraction** | A number showing a part of a whole (like 1/3, 2/5) |
| **Numerator** | The top number — tells how many parts you have |
| **Denominator** | The bottom number — tells how many equal parts the whole is divided into |
| **Equivalent fractions** | Different fractions that show the same amount (1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6) |
| **Mixed number** | A number with a whole part and a fraction part (like 3 and 1/4) |
| **Unit fraction** | A fraction with 1 as the numerator (like 1/4, 1/6, 1/10) |
