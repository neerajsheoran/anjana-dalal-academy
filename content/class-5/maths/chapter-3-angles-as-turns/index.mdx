## What Is a Turn?

Stand up and spin on the spot. That movement is a **turn**! Turns happen everywhere — the hands of a clock turn, a ceiling fan turns, a gate swings open with a turn, even a steering wheel needs a turn to change direction.

- A **full turn** brings you right back to where you started — like a spinning top completing one full circle.
- A **half turn** makes you face the opposite direction — if you were looking at the door, now you are looking at the window behind you.
- A **quarter turn** takes you one-fourth of the way around — like turning from facing the TV to facing the balcony.

---

## Turns and Angles

Hold a pencil still on a table and rotate a ruler around the same point. The **gap** between the pencil and the ruler is called an **angle**. So:

> **An angle measures how much something has turned** between two lines (or arms) that meet at a point.

---

## Types of Angles

### Right Angle (1/4 turn)

A **right angle** is exactly a **quarter turn**. It looks like the corner of a notebook or a tile on the floor.

You spot right angles in:
- Corners of doors, tables, and screens
- The shape of the letter "L"
- Where a wall meets the ceiling

### Acute Angle (less than 1/4 turn)

An **acute angle** is **smaller than a right angle** — a narrow, sharp opening.

*Picture:* A partly opened pair of scissors, or the tip of a samosa.

### Obtuse Angle (between 1/4 and 1/2 turn)

An **obtuse angle** is **wider than a right angle but not yet a straight line** — a broad, spread-out opening.

*Picture:* A hand fan opened wide, or the slope at the top of a tiled roof.

### Straight Angle (1/2 turn)

A **straight angle** is exactly a **half turn** — it looks like a flat, straight line (180 degrees).

*Picture:* Facing forward, then spinning around to face completely backwards.

## How Turns Add Up

| Turn | What it looks like |
|------|-------------------|
| 1/8 turn | A small angle (half of a right angle) |
| 1/4 turn | A right angle |
| 1/2 turn | A straight angle (flat line) |
| 3/4 turn | Three right angles together |
| Full turn | Complete circle — back to the start |

### Combining turns:
- **2 quarter turns** = 1 half turn
- **2 half turns** = 1 full turn
- **4 quarter turns** = 1 full turn
- **2 eighth turns** = 1 quarter turn

> **Experiment:** Make two small acute turns one after another. Can two acute turns combined still be acute? Sometimes yes (if both are very small). Other times they add up to a right angle or even an obtuse angle!

## Making an Angle Measuring Tool

You can build a simple angle checker:

1. **Cut a circle** from chart paper.
2. **Fold it in half** — this crease marks the half-turn line.
3. **Fold in half again** — now you have quarter-turn marks.
4. **Fold once more** — now you have eighth-turn marks (8 equal wedges).
5. Fix a straw at the centre. The folds show you where 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, and 1/2 turns fall.

You can also fold a circle into **6 equal parts** (fold in half, then into thirds) to measure 1/6 and 1/12 turns.

## Angles and the Clock

The minute hand of a clock is a wonderful way to see turns in action:

| Minute hand moves by | Turn completed |
|---------------------|---------------|
| 15 minutes | 1/4 turn (right angle) |
| 30 minutes | 1/2 turn (straight angle) |
| 45 minutes | 3/4 turn |
| 60 minutes | Full turn (complete circle) |
| 5 minutes | 1/12 turn |
| 10 minutes | 1/6 turn |

> **Think about it:** The minute hand sweeps through a **full circle every 60 minutes** (1 hour). So every minute, it covers 1/60 of a full turn.

---

## Clockwise and Anti-clockwise

- **Clockwise** = the direction clock hands travel (right, then down, then left, then up).
- **Anti-clockwise** (or counter-clockwise) = the **reverse** direction.

*Everyday examples:*
- Turning a water tap on → usually **anti-clockwise**
- Turning a water tap off → usually **clockwise**
- Opening a pickle jar → **anti-clockwise**
- Tightening a jar lid → **clockwise**

## Turns and Directions

If you face **North** and make a quarter turn **clockwise**, you end up facing **East**.

| Starting direction | Turn | You now face |
|----------|------|-------------|
| North | 1/4 clockwise | East |
| North | 1/2 clockwise | South |
| North | 3/4 clockwise | West |
| North | Full turn | North (back to start) |
| East | 1/4 anti-clockwise | North |
| South | 1/4 clockwise | West |

> **Fun fact:** 1 right angle clockwise from North = East. 2 right angles = South. 3 right angles = West. 4 right angles = right back to North!

## Everyday Objects and Turns

Think about how far these items can turn:

| Object | Maximum turn |
|--------|-------------|
| A hair clip | Less than 1/4 turn |
| A wooden door on hinges | About 1/4 turn (some open to 1/2 turn) |
| Kitchen tongs | Less than 1/4 turn |
| A pair of scissors | About 1/4 turn |
| A laptop screen | About 1/2 turn |
| A water tap | About 1/4 to 1/2 turn |
| A ceiling fan | Full turn (and goes on spinning!) |

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Angle** | The amount of turn between two lines meeting at a point |
| **Right angle** | A quarter turn — looks like the corner of a square |
| **Acute angle** | Smaller than a right angle — a narrow, sharp angle |
| **Obtuse angle** | Bigger than a right angle but smaller than a straight angle — a wide angle |
| **Straight angle** | A half turn — appears as a flat line |
| **Clockwise** | The direction clock hands travel |
| **Anti-clockwise** | The opposite direction to clock hands |
| **Full turn** | A complete spin — 360 degrees — ending where you began |
