## Key Concepts

- An **angle** measures how much something has **turned** between two lines meeting at a point
- A **full turn** = complete circle = back to start
- A **half turn** = face the opposite direction
- A **quarter turn** = one-fourth of a circle = a **right angle**
- A **right angle** looks like the corner of a square or notebook
- An **acute angle** is smaller than a right angle (narrow, sharp opening)
- An **obtuse angle** is bigger than a right angle but smaller than a straight angle (wide opening)
- A **straight angle** = half turn = flat line (180 degrees)
- 2 quarter turns = 1 half turn; 4 quarter turns = 1 full turn
- **Clockwise** = direction clock hands travel; **Anti-clockwise** = opposite direction
- The minute hand completes a **full turn every 60 minutes**
- 15 minutes = 1/4 turn; 30 minutes = 1/2 turn; 5 minutes = 1/12 turn
- Turns and **compass directions** are linked: 1 quarter turn clockwise from North = East
- Combining two small turns can produce a larger angle type

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Angle** | The amount of turn between two lines meeting at a point |
| **Right angle** | A quarter turn — looks like the corner of a square |
| **Acute angle** | Smaller than a right angle — a narrow, sharp angle |
| **Obtuse angle** | Bigger than a right angle but smaller than a straight angle |
| **Straight angle** | A half turn — appears as a flat line (180 degrees) |
| **Clockwise** | The direction clock hands travel |
| **Anti-clockwise** | The opposite direction to clock hands |
| **Full turn** | A complete spin — 360 degrees — ending where you began |

## Remember These

- From North: 1 right angle clockwise = East, 2 = South, 3 = West, 4 = back to North
- A **right angle is exactly 1/4 turn**, not just "any square-looking corner" — it must be precise
- Opening a tap is usually **anti-clockwise**; closing it is usually **clockwise**
- Two acute angles can add up to a right angle, an obtuse angle, or even stay acute — it depends on their sizes
