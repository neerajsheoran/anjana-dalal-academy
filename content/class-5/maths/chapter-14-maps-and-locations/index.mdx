## The Four Directions

There are four main (cardinal) directions:

![Compass rose showing North, South, East, and West directions](compass-rose-directions.png)

```
        North (N)
          ↑
West (W) ← → East (E)
          ↓
        South (S)
```

### How to figure out directions:
- **Face the rising Sun** — you are looking **East**.
- Your **left hand** points **North**.
- Your **right hand** points **South**.
- **Behind you** is **West**.

> Migratory birds like the **Arctic Tern** travel thousands of kilometres across continents every year — they have an incredible built-in sense of direction!

## Using Directions in Daily Life

### In Your Room:
Stand in the middle of your room and work out which wall faces which direction. Then you can describe things like:
- "My study table is on the **East** wall"
- "The door is to the **South**"
- "My bookshelf is to the **West**"

### On a Map:
Maps normally show **North at the top**. Using this rule:
- Right side = **East**
- Left side = **West**
- Bottom = **South**

## Reading Maps and Routes

![Simple street map showing roads, buildings, and a bus route with directional labels](street-map-example.png)

A street map shows roads, buildings, parks, and landmarks as seen from above.

### Following a Route:
**Example:** A school bus starts from the depot.
1. Drives **North** along the main road
2. Takes a **right turn** → now heading **East** on Temple Road to reach Stop 1
3. Takes a **left turn** → now heading **North** on Garden Lane to reach Stop 2

### Answering Map Questions:
- "Which shops are to the West of the library?" → Look to the left of the library on the map.
- "Which bus stop is nearest to Anita's house?" → Measure the distances on the map.
- "In which direction should Ravi walk to reach the park?" → Check the map layout.

---

### Reading a Metro Map

A metro map uses different **coloured lines** to show different routes and marks the **stations** where trains halt.

### Key features:
- Different colours = different metro lines (Red, Green, Blue, etc.)
- Where two lines meet = **interchange stations** (you can switch trains)
- A key or legend explains what each symbol and colour means

### Finding Your Route:
To travel from one station to another:
1. Locate both stations on the map
2. See if they are on the **same line** → ride directly without changing
3. If on **different lines** → find an interchange station to switch
4. There may be **several possible routes** — the best one usually needs the fewest line changes

## Grid Maps and Coordinates

![Grid map of a zoo showing animals at different coordinate positions](grid-coordinates-zoo-map.png)

A **grid** divides a map into squares using numbered horizontal and vertical lines. This makes it simple to pinpoint **exact positions**.

### How to read grid coordinates:

Coordinates are written as **(column, row)** — always **horizontal first, then vertical**.

**Example:** To locate position **(3, 2)**:
1. Start at (0, 0) — the bottom-left corner
2. Move **3 steps East** (horizontal) to reach column 3
3. Move **2 steps North** (vertical) to reach row 2
4. The square where they meet is (3, 2)

### Zoo Map Example:
- **Elephant** is at **(2, 1)** — column 2, row 1
- **Parrot** is at **(5, 3)** — column 5, row 3

> **Remember:** Coordinates go **(across, up)** — think of it as "walk along the corridor, then climb the stairs."

## Directions and the Ant Grid

On a grid, an ant can only crawl along the lines. We describe its path using **directions and distances**.

**Example:** To reach the sugar:
- "Crawl 4 cm towards the **East**"

**Example:** To reach the jaggery:
- "Crawl 3 cm **East**, then 2 cm **North**"

There can be **many routes** to the same spot — but the **shortest path** avoids any backtracking.

## Making Your Own Map

You can draw a map of any place:
1. **Pick a location** — your school, your room, your colony
2. **Mark North** — draw a compass arrow
3. **Sketch the outline** — roads, buildings, trees, water bodies
4. **Add a key/legend** — explain what each symbol stands for
5. **Label important spots**

> Maps use **symbols** to represent real things — a small square for a building, a green patch for a park, a blue line for a river, etc.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cardinal directions** | The four main directions — North, South, East, West |
| **Map** | A flat drawing that shows places as seen from above |
| **Key / Legend** | A box on the map explaining the meaning of each symbol |
| **Grid** | A network of horizontal and vertical lines dividing a map into squares |
| **Coordinates** | A pair of numbers (column, row) describing an exact position on a grid |
| **Route** | The path followed to travel from one place to another |
| **Compass** | An instrument whose needle always points North, helping you find directions |
