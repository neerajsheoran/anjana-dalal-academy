## Key Concepts

- The four **cardinal directions** are **North, South, East, West**
- Face the **rising Sun** and you face East; left = North, right = South, behind = West
- On most maps, **North is at the top**, East is right, West is left, South is at the bottom
- A **map** is a flat drawing showing places as seen from above
- A **key/legend** explains what each symbol on the map means
- A **grid** divides a map into squares using numbered lines for easy location
- **Coordinates** are written as **(column, row)** — horizontal first, then vertical
- Think of coordinates as: "walk along the corridor (horizontal), then climb the stairs (vertical)"
- Start reading coordinates from **(0, 0)** at the bottom-left corner
- A **metro map** uses coloured lines for routes; where lines meet = **interchange stations**
- On a grid, there can be **many routes** to the same point, but the **shortest path** avoids backtracking
- Turning **right** when facing North means you now face **East**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cardinal directions** | The four main directions — North, South, East, West |
| **Map** | A flat drawing showing places as seen from above |
| **Key / Legend** | A box on the map explaining each symbol |
| **Grid** | A network of horizontal and vertical lines dividing a map into squares |
| **Coordinates** | A pair of numbers (column, row) describing an exact position on a grid |
| **Route** | The path followed to travel from one place to another |
| **Compass** | An instrument whose needle always points North |

## Remember These

- Coordinates are always **(across, up)** — never the other way around
- North is at the **top** of most maps — do not assume your "front" is North in real life without checking
- At an **interchange station**, you can switch from one metro line to another
- The **shortest path** on a grid has no unnecessary backtracking — every step moves you closer to the destination
