## Division — The Reverse of Multiplication

**Child:** Maa, the chapter says division is the reverse of multiplication. Can you explain what that means?

**Mother:** Sure, beta. Imagine you have 42 toffees and you want to share them equally among 6 friends. You need to find the number that, when multiplied by 6, gives 42. Since 6 times 7 is 42, each friend gets 7 toffees. So 42 divided by 6 is 7.

**Child:** Oh! So when I divide, I am really asking "what do I multiply by to get this number"?

**Mother:** Exactly right! And here is the best part — if you know one multiplication fact, you automatically get two division facts. Since 6 times 7 equals 42, you know that 42 divided by 6 is 7, AND 42 divided by 7 is 6.

**Child:** That is like getting two gifts for the price of one!

**Mother:** Ha ha, the best deal in maths!

## Mental Strategies for Division

**Child:** Maa, dividing big numbers in my head feels really hard. Like 1,536 divided by 4 — where do I even start?

**Mother:** Break the number into parts that are easy to work with. What is the biggest chunk of 1,536 that you can easily divide by 4?

**Child:** Maybe 1,200? Because 1,200 divided by 4 is 300.

**Mother:** Perfect. Now what remains? 1,536 minus 1,200 is 336. And 336 divided by 4?

**Child:** Hmm... 320 divided by 4 is 80, and 16 divided by 4 is 4. So 80 plus 4 is 84! The full answer is 300 plus 84 equals 384!

**Mother:** Wonderful! And here is another shortcut — for dividing by 4, just halve the number twice. Try 196 divided by 4.

**Child:** Half of 196 is 98. Half of 98 is 49. So the answer is 49! That was so quick!

**Mother:** And for dividing by 8, halve three times. These tricks save so much time.

## Division with Remainders

**Child:** Maa, at my birthday party, we had 29 chocolates to share among 4 friends. Everyone got 7, but there was 1 left over. That leftover is the remainder, right?

**Mother:** Spot on! 29 equals 4 times 7 plus 1. The formula is: Dividend equals Divisor times Quotient plus Remainder. We write it as N equals D times Q plus R.

**Child:** What do we do with that 1 extra chocolate?

**Mother:** Ha ha, in real life the birthday boy eats it! But in maths, the remainder simply tells us that 29 does not split evenly into 4. One important rule — the remainder must always be smaller than the divisor.

**Child:** Why is that?

**Mother:** Think about it. If the remainder were 4 or more, you could give one more chocolate to each friend. That means you have not finished dividing. So if you ever get a remainder equal to or bigger than the divisor, go back and check your work.

## Long Division — Two Different Approaches

**Child:** Maa, in the chapter, Meera and Anita both divided 738 by 6. Meera took many small steps but Anita did it in just three big steps. Which way is correct?

**Mother:** Both are correct! They both got 123. Meera subtracted 6 times 20 repeatedly — it is slow but safe. Anita jumped straight to 6 times 100 — faster but you need to be confident with your tables.

**Child:** It is like travelling from Delhi to Agra. You can stop at every town along the way, or you can take the expressway directly!

**Mother:** What a lovely comparison! When you are just learning, take the smaller steps like Meera. As you gain confidence, you will naturally start taking bigger jumps like Anita. The destination is the same.

**Child:** So the method does not matter as long as the answer is right?

**Mother:** Exactly. Your teacher wants to see your thinking process, not a race to the finish.

## Real-Life Problems — Lakshmi's Coconut Grove

**Child:** Maa, the chapter says Lakshmi got 684 litres of coconut oil from 5,472 coconuts. That is amazing!

**Mother:** Kerala and Karnataka grow the most coconuts in India. Let us check that answer — 5,472 divided by 8 coconuts per litre. Break it up: 4,000 divided by 8 is 500, and 1,472 divided by 8... 1,200 divided by 8 is 150, and 272 divided by 8 is 34. So 500 plus 150 plus 34 equals 684 litres!

**Child:** And Raju sold tender coconuts at ₹40 each and earned ₹9,960. So he sold 9,960 divided by 40 coconuts. How do I calculate that?

**Mother:** Use the nearest round number. 40 times 200 is 8,000. Subtract: 9,960 minus 8,000 equals 1,960. Now 40 times 49 is 1,960. So 200 plus 49 equals 249 coconuts!

**Child:** And his profit is selling price minus buying cost. He spent 249 times 15 which is 3,735 rupees. So 9,960 minus 3,735 is 6,225 rupees profit!

**Mother:** Beta, you just did a complete business calculation. Raju would be impressed!

## Mathematical Statements — Checking Your Work

**Child:** Maa, how do I make sure my division answer is correct?

**Mother:** Always use the formula: Dividend equals Divisor times Quotient plus Remainder. Plug your answer back in and verify. For example, if you divided 853 by 5 and got quotient 170 with remainder 3, check: 5 times 170 is 850, plus 3 is 853. It matches!

**Child:** What if it does not match?

**Mother:** Then somewhere you made a mistake — go back and redo your steps. This checking method is like a shopkeeper counting money twice before giving change. The extra effort saves you from errors.

**Child:** I will always verify from now on. It is like having my own answer key!

**Mother:** That is the smartest habit you can build in maths, beta. Checking your work is never a waste of time.
