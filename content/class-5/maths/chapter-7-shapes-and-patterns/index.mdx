## Weaving Mats — Patterns in Art

Have you seen a woven basket or a bamboo mat? If you look closely, you will notice a repeating design. Weaving always follows a **rule** — just like patterns in maths!

A basic weave follows this rule:
- **Row 1:** Thread goes under 1, over 1, under 1, over 1... (repeat)
- **Row 2:** Thread goes over 1, under 1, over 1, under 1... (repeat)

Each row does the **opposite** of the row above it. You can make different designs by changing the pattern — like "2 over, 1 under" or "3 over, 2 under".

## Tiling and Tessellation

**Tessellation** means covering a flat surface with shapes that fit together **without any gaps or overlaps**.

### Which shapes tessellate?

| Shape | Can it tessellate alone? | How many meet at one point? |
|-------|-------------|------------------------|
| Equilateral Triangle | Yes | 6 triangles at each point |
| Square | Yes | 4 squares at each point |
| Regular Hexagon | Yes | 3 hexagons at each point |
| Regular Pentagon | No | Gaps appear between them |
| Regular Octagon | No | But octagons + squares together can tile! |

> **Key fact:** Only three regular shapes can tessellate by themselves — **triangles, squares, and hexagons**. However, you can mix different shapes (like octagons with squares, or hexagons with triangles) to create stunning tessellations!

## Types of Triangles

### Classified by sides:

| Type | What makes it special |
|------|-------------|
| **Equilateral** | All 3 sides are the same length. All 3 angles are also equal. |
| **Isosceles** | Exactly 2 sides are equal. The 2 angles facing the equal sides are also equal. |
| **Scalene** | No sides are equal. No angles are equal either. |

> **Quick test:** Fold an isosceles triangle along its line of symmetry. The two halves overlap perfectly — proving two sides and two angles match!

---

## Types of Quadrilaterals (4-sided shapes)

### Special quadrilaterals:

| Shape | Properties |
|-------|-----------|
| **Square** | All 4 sides equal. All 4 angles are right angles. |
| **Rectangle** | Opposite sides equal. All 4 angles are right angles. |
| **Rhombus** | All 4 sides equal. Opposite angles are equal. Angles are NOT necessarily right angles. |
| **Parallelogram** | Opposite sides are equal and parallel. Opposite angles are equal. |
| **Kite** | Two pairs of neighbouring (adjacent) sides are equal. |

### How they are related:
- A **square** is a special rectangle (with all sides equal).
- A **square** is also a special rhombus (with right angles).
- A **rectangle** is a special parallelogram (with right angles).

## Which Shape Am I?

- "All my angles are right angles, but my sides are not all equal" → **Rectangle**
- "All my sides are equal, but my angles are not all right angles" → **Rhombus**
- "All my sides are equal AND all my angles are right angles" → **Square**
- "My opposite sides and opposite angles are equal" → **Parallelogram**
- "I have two pairs of neighbouring sides that are equal" → **Kite**

## Making Shapes from Triangles

When you cut a rhombus along its diagonals, you get **4 triangles**. With these triangles you can build:

- **Using 2 triangles:** Different triangles (isosceles), parallelograms, kites
- **Using 3 triangles:** Bigger triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons (5-sided shapes)
- **Using all 4 triangles:** Squares, rectangles, larger triangles, pentagons, hexagons

## Tangram

A **tangram** is an ancient puzzle made of 7 pieces cut from a single square:
- 2 large triangles
- 1 medium triangle
- 2 small triangles
- 1 square
- 1 parallelogram

With these 7 pieces, you can form hundreds of different shapes — birds, boats, houses, animals, letters, and much more!

## Circles and Shapes

Here is a fun discovery:

1. Draw a **circle** using a compass.
2. Draw **two diameters** (lines passing through the centre).
3. Join the 4 points where the diameters touch the circle.
4. You always get a **rectangle**!

If the two diameters cross at right angles, you get a **square**.

## 3D Shapes — Cubes and Beyond

### Cube:
- 6 faces (all squares)
- 12 edges
- 8 vertices (corners)

**Painted cube puzzle:** If you glue 27 small cubes into a 3 x 3 x 3 big cube and paint the outside:
- **8 cubes** have 3 faces painted (the corner cubes)
- **12 cubes** have 2 faces painted (the edge cubes, not at corners)
- **6 cubes** have 1 face painted (the centre cube of each face)
- **1 cube** has 0 faces painted (the one hiding right in the middle!)

### Icosahedron:
A 3D shape with **20 triangular faces** — "icosa" means twenty.

### Dodecahedron:
A 3D shape with **12 pentagonal faces** — "dodeca" means twelve.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Tessellation** | Covering a flat surface with shapes, leaving no gaps or overlaps |
| **Equilateral triangle** | A triangle with all 3 sides (and angles) equal |
| **Isosceles triangle** | A triangle with 2 equal sides (and 2 equal angles) |
| **Scalene triangle** | A triangle with no equal sides |
| **Parallelogram** | A 4-sided shape with opposite sides equal and parallel |
| **Rhombus** | A parallelogram with all 4 sides equal |
| **Kite** | A 4-sided shape with 2 pairs of neighbouring equal sides |
| **Tangram** | A puzzle of 7 pieces that fit together to form a square |
