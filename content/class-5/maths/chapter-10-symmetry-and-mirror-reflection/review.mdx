## Key Concepts

- **Symmetry** means one part of a design mirrors or repeats another in a balanced way
- **Reflection symmetry (line symmetry)**: one half is the exact mirror image of the other half across a **line of symmetry**
- **Rotational symmetry**: a shape looks the same after being turned partway around (before completing a full turn)
- Some shapes have **only reflection** symmetry (like letter A), some have **only rotational** symmetry (like letter S), and some have **both** (like letter H)
- Letters with vertical line of symmetry: A, H, T, V, X, O
- Letters with horizontal line of symmetry: E, H, K, X, O
- Letters with rotational symmetry at 1/2 turn: H, N, S, O
- Digits 0 and 8 have **both** reflection and rotational symmetry
- To make a shape with 1 line of symmetry: fold paper once, draw half, cut and unfold
- To make a shape with 2 lines of symmetry: fold paper twice, draw one-quarter, cut and unfold
- A 4-blade pinwheel has rotational symmetry — it looks the same after every **quarter turn**
- **Block printing** in traditional Indian art uses symmetry to create repeating patterns

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Reflection symmetry** | One half mirrors the other half exactly across a line |
| **Line of symmetry** | The line that splits a shape into two matching mirror halves |
| **Rotational symmetry** | A shape looks the same after being turned partway around |
| **Vertical line** | A line running top to bottom |
| **Horizontal line** | A line running left to right |

## Remember These

- The letter **N** has rotational symmetry but **no** reflection symmetry
- The letter **A** has reflection symmetry but **no** rotational symmetry
- The number **1001** has both horizontal reflection and rotational symmetry
- To **test** for reflection symmetry, fold along the line — halves must overlap perfectly; to test for rotational symmetry, spin the shape and check if it looks identical before a full turn
