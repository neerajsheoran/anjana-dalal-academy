## Adding Larger Numbers

In Chapter 1, we learnt to read and write 5-digit numbers. Now it is time to **add and subtract** them — using the same column-by-column method, just with more digits!

---

## Addition Using Place Value

To add large numbers, line up the digits by their **place value** — Ones beneath Ones, Tens beneath Tens, and so on. Then add each column starting from the right.

### Example: 2,365 + 1,478

```
  Th  H  T  O
   2  3  6  5
+  1  4  7  8
-----------
   3  8  4  3
```

- Ones: 5 + 8 = 13 → write 3, carry 1
- Tens: 6 + 7 + 1 = 14 → write 4, carry 1
- Hundreds: 3 + 4 + 1 = 8
- Thousands: 2 + 1 = 3

**Answer: 3,843**

### Adding 5-digit numbers: 24,750 + 37,680

```
  TTh  Th  H  T  O
    2   4  7  5  0
+   3   7  6  8  0
------------------
    6   2  4  3  0
```

**Answer: 62,430**

> **Tip:** You do not need to label the columns every time. Just make sure the digits are lined up properly — Ones below Ones, Tens below Tens, and so on.

## Subtraction Using Place Value

Subtraction also works column by column from right to left. When the top digit is smaller, **regroup** (borrow) from the column on its left.

### Example: 3,500 - 1,276

```
  Th  H  T  O
   3  5  0  0
-  1  2  7  6
-----------
   2  2  2  4
```

- Ones: Cannot take 6 from 0, so borrow. 10 - 6 = 4
- Tens: After lending to Ones, now 9 - 7 = 2 (borrowed from Hundreds)
- Hundreds: 4 - 2 = 2
- Thousands: 3 - 1 = 2

**Answer: 2,224**

> **Always verify** your subtraction by adding back: 2,224 + 1,276 = 3,500. Correct!

### Subtracting 5-digit numbers: 18,200 - 11,365

```
  TTh  Th  H  T  O
    1   8  2  0  0
-   1   1  3  6  5
------------------
    0   6  8  3  5
```

**Answer: 6,835**

## Relationship Between Addition and Subtraction

Addition and subtraction are **opposite operations**. If you know one, you can figure out the other!

- If **53 + 28 = 81**, then **81 - 28 = 53** and **81 - 53 = 28**
- If **245 + 76 = 321**, then **321 - 76 = 245** and **321 - 245 = 76**

> This is a fantastic way to **double-check your work** — after subtracting, add your answer back and see if you land on the starting number.

## Making Sums Equal

Here is a fun challenge: given two groups of numbers, can you **swap one number from each group** to make both totals the same?

**Example:**
- Group A: 2, 5, 6, 8 → total = 21
- Group B: 1, 3, 7, 10 → total = 21

The totals are already equal! But what if we had:
- Group A: 2, 5, 6, 8 → total = 21
- Group B: 1, 4, 7, 11 → total = 23

Combined total is 21 + 23 = 44. For equal shares, each group needs 44 / 2 = **22**. Group A is 1 short, Group B has 1 extra. Swap **5** (from A) with **4** (from B):
- New Group A: 2, 4, 6, 8 = 20... Hmm, that does not work.
- Try swapping **6** (from A) with **7** (from B):
- New Group A: 2, 5, 7, 8 = 22 and New Group B: 1, 4, 6, 11 = 22. Done!

## Sums of Consecutive Numbers

**Consecutive numbers** follow one after another without gaps: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5...

### Interesting patterns:

**Sum of 2 consecutive numbers:** 1+2=3, 2+3=5, 3+4=7, 4+5=9
- The totals are always **odd**!

**Sum of 3 consecutive numbers:** 1+2+3=6, 2+3+4=9, 3+4+5=12
- The totals are always **multiples of 3**
- Each total = **3 times the middle number**!

**Sum of 4 consecutive numbers:** 1+2+3+4=10, 2+3+4+5=14, 3+4+5+6=18
- The totals are always **even**
- The gap between successive totals is always **4**

### Quick trick for adding consecutive numbers:

- **3 consecutive numbers:** Total = **3 x middle number**
  - 45 + 46 + 47 = 3 x 46 = **138**

- **5 consecutive numbers:** Total = **5 x middle number**
  - 36 + 37 + 38 + 39 + 40 = 5 x 38 = **190**

## Quick Sums and Differences

### Finding what to add to reach 100, 1,000, or 10,000:

**Shortcut:** To find 47 + ? = 100:
1. First figure out what makes 99: 47 + 52 = 99
2. Add 1 more: 47 + **53** = 100

**More examples:**
- 64 + **36** = 100
- 738 + **262** = 1,000
- 5,204 + **4,796** = 10,000

### Subtracting 9 or 99 quickly:

- Subtracting 9 = subtract 10, then add 1 back
  - 85 - 9 = 85 - 10 + 1 = **76**
  - 54 - 9 = **46**

- Subtracting 99 = subtract 100, then add 1 back
  - 243 - 99 = 243 - 100 + 1 = **144**
  - 851 - 99 = **752**

## Even and Odd Numbers

- **Even numbers** end in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8. They can be perfectly paired with nothing left over.
- **Odd numbers** end in 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9. When paired, there is always 1 left over.

### What happens when you add them?

| You add | Result is |
|---------|----------|
| Even + Even | Always **Even** |
| Odd + Odd | Always **Even** |
| Even + Odd | Always **Odd** |

> **Why?** Two odd numbers each have a "leftover 1." Put them together and those two leftovers pair up, making the total even!

## Palindrome Numbers

A **palindrome number** reads the same forwards and backwards.

**Examples:** 44, 272, 505, 7667, 31,013

**Palindromes between 200 and 300:** 202, 212, 222, 232, 242, 252, 262, 272, 282, 292

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Regroup** | Exchange 10 of one unit for 1 of the next bigger unit (or the reverse) during addition or subtraction |
| **Consecutive numbers** | Numbers that follow one after another without gaps |
| **Palindrome** | A number (or word) that reads the same forwards and backwards |
| **Even number** | A number divisible by 2 (ends in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8) |
| **Odd number** | A number not divisible by 2 (ends in 1, 3, 5, 7, 9) |
| **Estimate** | A rough calculation or guess close to the real answer |
