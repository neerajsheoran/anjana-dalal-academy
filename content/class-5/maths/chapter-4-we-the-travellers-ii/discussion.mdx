## Adding Big Numbers — It Is Just More Columns

**Child:** Maa, adding small numbers is easy. But when the numbers have 4 or 5 digits, I panic and make mistakes!

**Mother:** Do not panic! It is the exact same process — just more columns. Think of it like eating a meal. Whether the thali has 3 bowls or 6 bowls, do you eat differently?

**Child:** No, I eat one bowl at a time!

**Mother:** Same with addition! You handle one column at a time — Ones first, then Tens, then Hundreds, and so on. Whether it is a 2-digit number or a 5-digit number, the method is identical.

**Child:** But I keep forgetting to carry!

**Mother:** Think of carrying this way. You have 13 toffees. You put 10 of them in a packet and keep 3 loose. You write 3 in the current column and send the packet of 10 to the next column — that is carrying! Whenever any column adds up to 10 or more, you bundle 10 into the next column.

**Child:** Oh! So it is like making bundles of 10 and passing them along?

**Mother:** Exactly! Try this: 24,750 + 37,680. Start from the right. Ones: 0 + 0 = 0. Tens: 5 + 8 = 13, write 3, carry 1. Hundreds: 7 + 6 + 1 = 14, write 4, carry 1. Thousands: 4 + 7 + 1 = 12, write 2, carry 1. Ten Thousands: 2 + 3 + 1 = 6. Answer: 62,430!

**Child:** That was not so hard after all!

## Subtraction with Borrowing — Do Not Worry!

**Child:** Maa, subtraction is fine until I need to borrow. Like 3,500 minus 1,276 — how do I take 6 from 0?

**Mother:** Good question! You cannot give away 6 when you have 0 — just like you cannot lend ₹6 when your wallet is empty. So you **borrow** from your neighbour column on the left!

**Child:** But the Tens column also has 0!

**Mother:** Then the Tens column borrows from the Hundreds column. The Hundreds column has 5, so it lends 1 to the Tens column. Now Tens has 10. Then Tens lends 1 to the Ones column. Now Ones has 10, and 10 - 6 = 4.

**Child:** It is like a chain! The Ones asks the Tens, the Tens asks the Hundreds?

**Mother:** Exactly — a borrowing chain! And here is a tip to check yourself — always **verify subtraction by adding**. If 3,500 - 1,276 = 2,224, then 2,224 + 1,276 should give 3,500. It is like double-checking your own homework!

**Child:** That is clever! I can catch my own errors!

## Addition and Subtraction Are Partners

**Child:** Maa, the teacher says addition and subtraction are "reverse operations." What does that mean?

**Mother:** Think about a train ride. You board at Jaipur and travel 320 km to Delhi. Then you return from Delhi to Jaipur — another 320 km. You end up exactly where you started! Addition moves you forward, subtraction brings you back.

**Child:** So if 53 + 28 = 81, then 81 - 28 = 53?

**Mother:** Exactly! They **undo** each other. Like tying your shoelaces and untying them. Wrapping a gift and opening it. Putting money into a piggy bank and taking it out. One does, the other reverses.

**Child:** So if I know 321 - 76 = 245, then 245 + 76 = 321!

**Mother:** Shabash! You can always use one to check the other. Never submit your maths test without verifying at least the big problems!

## Even and Odd — A Pattern That Never Breaks

**Child:** Maa, what is so special about even and odd numbers? They seem ordinary.

**Mother:** Ordinary? They follow one of the most unbreakable patterns in all of maths! Tell me — at a dance class, if 10 students come, can everyone form a pair?

**Child:** Yes! 10 is even, so 5 pairs.

**Mother:** What if 9 students come?

**Child:** One student has no partner... because 9 is odd.

**Mother:** Now here is the amazing part. Add two odd numbers — say 7 + 5. What do you get?

**Child:** 12. That is even!

**Mother:** It always works that way! Odd + Odd = Even. Why? Because each odd number has one "extra" that cannot pair up. Put two groups with one extra each together, and the two extras pair up. Everyone has a partner now!

**Child:** What about even + odd?

**Mother:** Try it. 6 + 3 = 9. Even + Odd always gives Odd. The even group is fully paired, but the odd group still has that one lonely extra.

**Child:** And even + even?

**Mother:** 8 + 4 = 12. Always even! Both groups are fully paired, so the combined group is also fully paired. This pattern holds for ALL numbers — even 50,000 + 1 = 50,001 — even + odd = odd. It NEVER breaks!

**Child:** Wow, maths patterns really are like laws of nature!

## Consecutive Numbers — The Hidden Patterns

**Child:** Maa, what are consecutive numbers?

**Mother:** Numbers that follow one after another — like people standing in a queue at the chai stall. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5... or 58, 59, 60, 61... No gaps, no jumps.

**Child:** Okay, but what is special about them?

**Mother:** Here is a magic trick. Pick any 3 consecutive numbers.

**Child:** Alright — 15, 16, 17.

**Mother:** Add them up.

**Child:** 15 + 16 + 17 = 48.

**Mother:** Now multiply the **middle number** by 3.

**Child:** 16 x 3 = 48! Same answer!

**Mother:** It works EVERY time! The sum of 3 consecutive numbers always equals 3 times the middle one. Try 30, 31, 32.

**Child:** 30 + 31 + 32 = 93. And 31 x 3 = 93. It works again!

**Mother:** The reason is lovely — one number is 1 less than the middle, one is 1 more. The "minus 1" and "plus 1" cancel out perfectly, leaving exactly 3 times the middle number. Maths always has a **reason** behind the magic!

## Quick Mental Maths — Impress Your Friends!

**Child:** Maa, my classmate Isha can do subtraction in her head so fast. How?

**Mother:** Let me teach you a trick. To subtract 99 from any number, what do you normally do?

**Child:** Borrow, carry... it takes forever.

**Mother:** Forget all that! 99 is just 100 minus 1. So subtracting 99 means subtracting 100 and then adding 1 back! Try 851 - 99.

**Child:** 851 - 100 = 751... plus 1 = **752**! That was instant!

**Mother:** Same trick works for adding 99. Instead of adding 99, add 100 and subtract 1. Try 347 + 99.

**Child:** 347 + 100 = 447, minus 1 = **446**!

**Mother:** And for 9? Subtract 10, add 1 back. For 999? Subtract 1,000, add 1 back. This trick works whenever a number is close to a round number. Even the mithai-wala uses this — if your bill is ₹198, he thinks "₹200 minus ₹2 change"!

**Child:** I am going to use this in the oral test tomorrow!

**Mother:** Now you will be the fastest one, beta!
