## Why Do We Need Different Units?

**Child:** Maa, why are there so many units — mm, cm, m, km? Can we not just use one?

**Mother:** Imagine I ask you — how tall are you? And you answer, "I am 1,370 millimetres tall."

**Child:** That sounds odd! I would just say "137 cm" or "about 4 feet 6 inches."

**Mother:** Now imagine I ask, "How far is Nani's house in Lucknow?" And you say, "It is 5,00,000 centimetres."

**Child:** Way too many digits! I would say 500 kilometres!

**Mother:** See? Different units for different sizes. Millimetres for tiny things like the screws in your spectacles. Centimetres for objects you hold — pens, spoons, books. Metres for rooms, buildings, and trees. Kilometres for distances between places. It is like using different vessels in the kitchen — a teaspoon for salt, a glass for water, a bucket for mopping!

**Child:** Each unit fits a different "size" of thing!

**Mother:** Perfectly said!

## The Magic Number: 10

**Child:** Maa, how are all these units connected?

**Mother:** Everything is built on **10** (or powers of 10):
- 10 millimetres = 1 centimetre
- 100 centimetres = 1 metre (that is 10 times 10)
- 1,000 metres = 1 kilometre (that is 10 times 10 times 10)

**Child:** So it is always multiplying by 10?

**Mother:** Yes! That is the beauty of the metric system. "Milli" means one-thousandth, "centi" means one-hundredth, "kilo" means thousand. These prefixes appear everywhere — not just for length! You will find them in weight (milligrams, kilograms) and liquids (millilitres, litres) too.

**Child:** So "kilo" always means 1,000? Like 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams?

**Mother:** Exactly! Learn the prefixes once and you understand all units of measurement. It is like a master key that opens many locks!

## Millimetres — When Tiny Details Matter

**Child:** Maa, when would anyone need millimetres? They are so small!

**Mother:** Look at your ruler. Those tiny lines between each centimetre are millimetres. Now, remember when we went to the jeweller for Dadi's gold bangle?

**Child:** Yes, the jeweller measured her wrist very carefully.

**Mother:** Right! Gold bangles are measured in millimetres because even 1 mm more or less changes the fit. The SIM card in your phone is about 15 mm long. Your pencil lead is 0.7 mm thick. Medicine tablets are a few millimetres across. Small but important things need small, exact units!

**Child:** Even the tiny screw in my spectacles must be measured in millimetres!

**Mother:** Absolutely! The optician uses millimetres to fit those little screws perfectly.

## Converting Between Units — The Golden Rule

**Child:** Maa, I get confused when converting. Is 7 m 15 cm equal to 715 cm or 7,015 cm?

**Mother:** Let me give you a foolproof method. Think of it like money. ₹7 and 15 paise — how many paise in total?

**Child:** ₹7 = 700 paise, plus 15 paise = 715 paise!

**Mother:** Same thing! 7 m = 700 cm, plus 15 cm = **715 cm**. The rule is always: convert the big unit into the small unit by multiplying, then add the remaining small units.

**Child:** What about the other way? Like 5,340 metres to kilometres?

**Mother:** Divide! 5,340 / 1,000. Since 1 km = 1,000 m, you get 5 km and 340 m left over. So 5,340 m = **5 km 340 m**. Imagine exchanging 5,340 one-rupee coins at the bank — you get 5 bundles of ₹1,000 and 340 coins remaining!

**Child:** So big to small = multiply, small to big = divide?

**Mother:** That is the golden rule! Write it down and circle it twice in your notebook!

## The Statue of Unity — India's Pride

**Child:** Maa, how tall is the Statue of Unity?

**Mother:** **182 metres!** It is in Kevadia, Gujarat, and it is the tallest statue in the **entire world**. Even taller than the India Gate in Delhi, which is 42 metres.

**Child:** How much taller?

**Mother:** You tell me! 182 - 42 = ?

**Child:** 140 metres! That is more than 3 times the height of India Gate!

**Mother:** And in centimetres, the Statue of Unity is 18,200 cm. In millimetres, it is 1,82,000 mm. The number changes with the unit, but the actual height stays the same.

**Child:** Like how ₹100 = 10,000 paise — same value, different number!

**Mother:** Perfect connection, beta!

## The Rajdhani Express — Maths on the Railway

**Child:** Maa, have you taken the Rajdhani Express?

**Mother:** Yes! The Delhi to Mumbai Rajdhani covers about **1,384 km**. It is one of the fastest trains in India.

**Child:** How many metres is that?

**Mother:** Let us work it out — 1,384 x 1,000 = **13,84,000 metres**. That is nearly 14 lakh metres!

**Child:** And in centimetres?

**Mother:** 13,84,000 x 100 = **13,84,00,000 cm** — over 13 crore centimetres! See how quickly the numbers grow when you switch units?

**Child:** No wonder we use kilometres for long trips. Imagine a ticket saying "13 crore 84 lakh centimetres"!

**Mother:** Ha ha! The ticket would be bigger than the train itself! That is exactly why picking the right unit matters.

## Why This Chapter Matters

**Child:** Maa, will I really need to convert units in real life?

**Mother:** Beta, you already do it without noticing! When we buy cloth, the shopkeeper measures in metres — "3 metre 50 cm" for your salwar. When the doctor measures your height, it is in centimetres — "137 cm." When you check Google Maps for Nani's house, it shows kilometres. And when Papa buys nails at the hardware shop, they are measured in millimetres!

**Child:** And I need to convert when comparing — like whether 4 km is more or less than 3,800 m.

**Mother:** Exactly! 4 km = 4,000 m, which is more than 3,800 m. Without converting, you would be guessing. With converting, you **know**. And knowing is always better than guessing — in maths and in life!

**Child:** Maths is really just... measuring the world with the right tool, is it not?

**Mother:** That is a very wise way to say it, beta. I am proud of you!
