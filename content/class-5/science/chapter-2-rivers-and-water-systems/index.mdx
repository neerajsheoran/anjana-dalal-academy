## Meet the Godavari

What if a river could talk and tell us about its travels? The **Godavari** would have one of the most exciting stories in all of India! It is a mighty river that starts its journey at a place called **Trimbakeshwar** in the **Western Ghats of Maharashtra**, in the hills known as the **Brahmagiri Hills**.

At its beginning, the Godavari is nothing more than a narrow trickle of water -- so small you could step across it! But as it moves forward, other small streams join it from the sides. These streams that merge into a bigger river are called **tributaries**. With every tributary that joins, the Godavari grows wider, deeper, and more powerful.

After travelling an incredible **1,465 kilometres** across the country, the Godavari finally reaches the **Bay of Bengal** on the eastern coast. This makes it the **second longest river in India**, right after the Ganga.

> People lovingly call the Godavari **"Dakshina Ganga"** -- meaning the Ganga of the South -- because of its great importance and the reverence people have for it.

## Perennial and Seasonal Rivers

Rivers can be grouped based on when they carry water:

- A **perennial river** flows throughout the year, in all seasons. The Godavari is perennial because it receives water from tributaries and rainfall continuously.
- A **seasonal river** carries water only during and shortly after the monsoon. Once the rains stop, these rivers may shrink to a trickle or dry up completely.

## Life Along the Godavari

The Godavari's banks and the forests around it are home to remarkable wildlife -- **tigers prowl through the dense trees**, **barking deer dart through the undergrowth**, and the rare **Indian golden gecko** basks on rocks. Magnificent **red sandalwood trees** grow in these forests, prized for their beautiful wood.

When the Godavari finally reaches the ocean, it does not simply end. Instead, it fans out into many smaller channels, forming a wide, flat area called a **delta**. Here, the river's freshwater mixes with salty seawater. This special zone is the **Coringa Mangrove Forest** -- a unique habitat where the river completes its long journey.

## How Rivers Support People

Since the earliest days of human history, people have chosen to build homes and villages beside rivers. The reason is simple -- rivers provide almost everything needed for daily life:

- **Drinking water** for families and communities
- **Irrigation** -- channelling river water to farms for growing rice, wheat, and vegetables
- **Cooking and cleaning** in everyday household work
- **Industry** -- factories use river water in manufacturing many products
- **Fishing** -- providing food and income for countless families
- **Transport** -- boats have carried people and goods along rivers for thousands of years
- **Tourism** -- beautiful river areas attract visitors from far and wide

> River water reaches our taps and fields through a network of **pipes, canals, and channels** that spread it across towns and farmland.

## Dams -- Storing Water, Changing Flow

![Cross-section diagram of a dam showing reservoir, water flow, and hydroelectric power generation](dam-cross-section.png)

To manage river water better, people build **dams** -- massive barriers across a river that hold back the water and create a large stored body of water called a **reservoir**. There are over **900 dams** built on the Godavari river alone!

**What makes dams valuable?**
- They store water during the rainy season and release it slowly during **dry months**, ensuring water supply all year.
- They supply water for **farming** across large areas through canals.
- Rushing water from dams spins giant wheels to produce **electricity** (called hydropower).

**But dams also come with problems:**
- Building a dam **floods huge stretches of land** upstream, including forests, fields, and villages.
- Families living in those areas are **forced to leave their ancestral homes** and resettle elsewhere.
- Entire **wildlife habitats get submerged**, displacing animals and destroying ecosystems.

> **Something to ponder:** When you turn on a tap at home, that water may have travelled hundreds of kilometres from a river through pipes and canals. That is why we should never take it for granted!

> **Record-breaking dam:** The **Hirakud Dam** across the Mahanadi river in Odisha stretches over **25 kilometres**, making it one of the longest earthen dams on the planet!

## River Pollution

![Illustration showing sources of river pollution: factory waste, plastic, farm runoff, and household sewage](river-pollution-sources.png)

As cities and factories grow along riverbanks, rivers often become dumping grounds for waste. Here are the main sources of pollution:

- **Plastic bags, bottles, and wrappers** thrown carelessly into the water
- **Chemical waste from factories** that flows directly into the river
- **Soap and detergent water** from homes draining into the river
- **Chemical fertilisers from farms** that wash off the fields during rain

When fertilisers enter river water, they cause tiny water plants called **algae** to grow extremely fast. These algae spread across the surface like a thick **green carpet**, blocking sunlight from reaching the water below. Without sunlight and oxygen, fish and other water creatures suffocate and die.

> Here is a worrying thought: river water that **appears perfectly clear** can still contain invisible harmful chemicals and bacteria. Looking clean and being safe are two very different things!

> **Hopeful efforts:** The **Namami Gange programme** is a major national effort to clean up the Ganga river. Along the Godavari too, communities and authorities are working together to stop waste from entering the water and revive the river's health.

## When a River Floods

Rivers usually flow calmly within their banks. But when monsoon rains become extremely heavy, rivers can swell beyond their limits and **flood the surrounding land**.

**The damage caused by floods:**

| **Problem** | **What Gets Affected** |
|---|---|
| Water contamination | Safe drinking water |
| Damaged roads and bridges | Transport and travel |
| Destroyed crops | Food supply and farmers' income |
| Power outages | Electricity for homes and hospitals |
| Spread of waterborne diseases | People's health |
| Collapsed or flooded houses | Shelter and safety |
| Closed schools | Children's education |

**How do trees fight floods?** Tree roots grip the soil tightly and act like a natural sponge, soaking up rainwater slowly. When forests are chopped down, bare ground cannot absorb water. Rain rushes off the surface straight into rivers, causing sudden, severe flooding.

**Staying safe during floods:**
- Pay attention to weather warnings on radio, TV, or phone alerts
- Keep an emergency bag ready with water, dry food, medicines, and a torch
- Move to higher ground when flood warnings are issued
- Never try to walk or drive through floodwater
- Follow instructions from rescue teams and local authorities

## When a River Runs Dry

Too little water is just as dangerous as too much. When monsoon rains fail or are very weak, rivers shrink, reservoirs empty, and people face a **severe water shortage**.

> **Real-life examples:** In 2019, **Chennai** faced such a terrible water crisis that the government arranged **special trains carrying water** from distant areas to supply the city. In 2022, unusually heavy rains caused massive **flooding in Bengaluru**, submerging homes and roads. These events show that both extremes -- too much and too little water -- can cause enormous hardship.

## Saving Our Rivers

Protecting our rivers is a responsibility that belongs to every one of us:

- **Use water thoughtfully** -- do not let it go to waste
- **Shut off taps completely** when you are done using them
- **Get leaky taps repaired** with the help of elders
- **Reuse water** wherever possible -- water from rinsing fruits can nourish your garden plants
- **Never dump waste** into rivers, streams, drains, or any water body
- **Plant trees** near riverbanks and water bodies
- **Encourage everyone around you** to care about water conservation

> **Every drop you save helps a river flow!** The **National Water Mission** works across India to help communities manage and conserve water wisely.

> **Remarkable creature:** The **Ganges river dolphin**, India's **national aquatic animal**, cannot see at all! It navigates through dark, murky river water by producing clicking sounds and listening to the echoes -- a natural version of sonar!
