## Why Water Matters

![Why water matters — water in everyday life](why-water-matters.png)

Picture your morning routine -- brushing teeth, having a bath, drinking a glass of water, eating breakfast cooked with water. Before you even leave for school, you have already used water in at least four different ways! Now imagine a day without any water at all. Sounds impossible, right?

Every creature on our planet -- from the tiniest ant to the tallest elephant -- depends on water to stay alive. Farmers need it for their fields, factories need it to make products, and our bodies need it to work properly. In fact, about **70 percent of your body is made up of water**!

> Here is a surprising comparison: imagine filling up a big bucket with 100 glasses of water. Out of those 100 glasses, only about **3 glasses would be freshwater**. And out of those 3, only **half a glass** is easily available for us to drink and use. The rest is frozen in glaciers or deep underground!

![A surprising comparison — how little water on Earth is actually drinkable freshwater](why-water-matters-here-is-a-surprising-comparison.png)

## Three Forms of Water

![Three states of water: ice (solid), liquid water, and steam (gas) with arrows showing transitions](three-forms-of-water.png)

Water has a magical ability -- it can change its shape and form! It exists in **three different states**:

| **Form** | **State** | **Where You See It** |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid | Flows and takes the shape of its container | Water from a tap, raindrops, rivers |
| Solid | Firm and holds its shape | Ice cubes in your lassi, snow on mountains |
| Gas (Water Vapour) | Floats in the air, usually invisible | Steam rising from hot dal, fog on winter mornings |

How does water switch between these forms?

![How water switches between solid, liquid, and gas forms](three-forms-of-water-how-does-water-switch-between-these-forms.png)

- When you put water in the **freezer**, it gets so cold that it turns into **ice** (liquid becomes solid).
- When Maa boils water for chai on the stove, the hot water turns into **steam** that rises up (liquid becomes gas).
- When steam touches a cold steel plate or lid, tiny **water droplets** form on it (gas becomes liquid again).

## The Water Cycle

![Diagram of the water cycle showing evaporation, condensation, cloud formation, and rainfall](the-water-cycle.png)

Have you ever wondered how rivers keep flowing year after year? Where does all that water come from? The answer is the **water cycle** -- nature's own way of recycling water!

Here is how this amazing cycle works:

1. The **sun warms up** the water in rivers, lakes, ponds, and oceans.
2. Some of this water turns into **water vapour** (an invisible gas) and floats upward into the sky. This is called **evaporation**.
3. High above the ground, the air is much colder. The water vapour **cools down** and changes back into millions of tiny water droplets. These droplets group together to form **clouds**. This cooling process is called **condensation**.
4. As more and more droplets gather, the clouds become heavy. Eventually, the water falls back to earth as **rain, snow, or hail**. This is called **precipitation**.
5. The rainwater flows into rivers and lakes, soaks into the ground, or reaches the oceans -- and the whole process begins once more!

> The water cycle never stops. It has been running for millions of years. The water you drank this morning might have once been part of a cloud over the Himalayas or a wave in the Arabian Sea!

## Groundwater

![Cross-section diagram showing rainwater seeping through soil layers to form groundwater](groundwater-1.png)

![Groundwater — second view of how water collects underground](groundwater-2.png)

When rain falls on open ground, not all of it flows into rivers. Some of it **slowly sinks through the soil**, passing through layers of sand, gravel, and rock. This water collects deep below the surface and is called **groundwater**.

People bring this hidden water up using **wells, handpumps, borewells, and tube wells**.

**How does groundwater get refilled?**

![How does groundwater get refilled — open soil, soak pits, and trees](groundwater-how-does-groundwater-get-refilled.png)

- When rain falls on **open soil, fields, and forests**, the water naturally seeps down and refills the underground supply.
- Building **soak pits** and **planting trees** helps rainwater go back underground.

> **A big challenge in cities:** Roads covered with tar and concrete, along with tall buildings, block rainwater from reaching the soil below. This means groundwater is being pumped out but not being refilled -- a serious problem!

![A big challenge in cities — concrete blocks rainwater from reaching the soil](groundwater-a-big-challenge-in-cities.png)

> **Smart solutions from India:** In Rajasthan, villages have been building small mud-and-stone structures called **Johads** for hundreds of years to trap rainwater and send it underground. The village of **Hiware Bazar in Maharashtra** transformed itself from a drought-hit area to a water-rich village by building small check dams and harvesting every drop of rain!

![Smart solutions from India — Johads in Rajasthan and Hiware Bazar's check dams](groundwater-smart-solutions-from-india.png)

## Surface Water and How Rivers Flow

![Surface water and how rivers flow — from mountain streams to seas](surface-water-and-how-rivers-flow.png)

Rivers, ponds, and lakes hold water on the earth's surface. A river typically starts as a small stream **high up in the mountains** -- often from melting snow or a spring -- and flows downhill across the land.

Water always follows **gravity**, moving from higher places to lower places. It winds around hills, cuts through valleys, and gathers in flat low-lying areas to form ponds and lakes.

> **Fascinating fact:** The **Luni river** in Rajasthan is unusual because it never reaches any sea or ocean. Instead, it disappears into the salty marshlands of the **Rann of Kutch** in Gujarat!

Some Indian rivers flow eastward and empty into the **Bay of Bengal** (like the Ganga and Godavari), while others flow westward into the **Arabian Sea** (like the Narmada and Tapi). The direction depends on the slope of the land and the position of mountain ranges.

## Life in Water

Freshwater bodies like rivers, lakes, and ponds are home to a rich variety of **plants and animals** that depend on water to survive.

**Animals that live in or near water:**

![Animals that live in or near water — fish, frog, turtle, dragonfly, kingfisher, water snake](life-in-water-animals-that-live-in-or-near-water.png)

| **Creature** | **What Makes It Special** |
|---|---|
| Fish | Use gills to breathe underwater and fins to swim |
| Frog | Spends part of its life in water and part on land (amphibian) |
| Freshwater turtle | Has a protective shell and lives in rivers and ponds |
| Dragonfly | Lays eggs near water; its babies (nymphs) grow up underwater |
| Kingfisher | Dives from branches to catch fish with its sharp beak |
| Water snake | Swims gracefully and feeds on fish and frogs |

**Plants that grow in water:**

![Plants that grow in water — lotus, water lily, hyacinth, reeds](life-in-water-plants-that-grow-in-water.png)

| **Type** | **Examples** |
|---|---|
| Float freely on the surface | Water hyacinth, duckweed |
| Rooted at the bottom, flowers float on top | Lotus, water lily |
| Grow along the edges of ponds | Reeds, bulrushes |

> **Amazing animal:** The **Fishing Cat**, found in India's marshes and wetlands, has **partially webbed feet** -- like built-in flippers! It actually dives headfirst into the water to grab fish with its paws.

![Amazing animal — the Fishing Cat of India's wetlands](life-in-water-amazing-animal.png)

> **A park like no other:** The **Keibul Lamjao National Park** in Manipur is the **world's only floating national park**. It sits on masses of tangled vegetation called **phumdis** that float on Loktak Lake. The rare **Sangai deer** lives only here!

## Water Plants Have Special Features

![Water plants have special features — flat leaves and waxy coating](water-plants-have-special-features.png)

Plants living in water face a unique challenge -- too much water around them! They have developed clever ways to handle this:
- **Lotus and water lily** have wide, flat leaves that spread across the water surface like little rafts, helping them catch sunlight.
- Many water plants coat their leaves with a **waxy layer** that acts like a raincoat. Water simply rolls off instead of soaking in, keeping the plant from getting waterlogged.

## Food Chains in Water

![Simple aquatic food chain showing tiny plants to small fish to big fish to heron](food-chains-in-water.png)

In every pond or river, living things are connected through what they eat. Tiny water plants and algae are eaten by small insects and fish. These small fish become food for bigger fish. And larger birds like herons swoop down to catch the bigger fish. This linked chain of eating is called a **food chain**.

If even one link in this chain breaks -- say, all the small fish disappear -- the entire chain gets disturbed. The bigger fish would go hungry, and the tiny plants might grow out of control. That is why protecting every creature in the water matters!

## Why We Must Save Water

![Why we must save water — simple habits to save every drop](why-we-must-save-water.png)

Freshwater is **rare and valuable**. With more people and growing cities, the demand for water keeps increasing. Here are some everyday habits that can help:

- **Close the tap** while soaping your hands or brushing your teeth -- do not let water run uselessly.
- **Take shorter baths** and avoid filling buckets to the brim.
- **Fix dripping taps** quickly -- even a slow drip wastes thousands of litres in a year.
- **Reuse water wisely** -- the water used to wash rice or vegetables is great for watering plants.
- **Spread the word** -- remind family members and friends that saving water is everyone's job.

> **Every single drop matters!**

> India opened its first dedicated water heritage centre -- the **Jal Shakti Museum** in New Delhi -- to help people understand the importance of water and inspire them to protect it.
