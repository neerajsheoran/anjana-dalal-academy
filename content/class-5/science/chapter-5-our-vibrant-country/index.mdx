## A Special Day -- Republic Day

Every year on **26 January**, something wonderful happens in schools all across India. Students stand in neat rows near the flagpole, the **tricolour** is hoisted with pride, drums roll, and hundreds of voices join together to sing the **National Anthem**. It is Republic Day!

But why is 26 January such an important date?

- On **15 August 1947**, India won its **independence** after a long and brave struggle by countless freedom fighters.
- But a free country also needs a set of rules that tells everyone how the nation should be run fairly and justly.
- Leaders like **Dr B.R. Ambedkar** led the team that carefully wrote India's **Constitution** -- a book containing the laws, rights, and duties for every citizen.
- On **26 January 1950**, India officially adopted this Constitution and became a **republic** -- a nation governed by its own people through elected leaders.
- That is why we celebrate **Republic Day** every year on this date!

> The Republic Day parade in New Delhi is a spectacular celebration featuring fighter aircraft flying in formation, beautifully decorated **tableaux (jhankis)** from each state, thrilling performances by the **Army, Navy, and Air Force**, and vibrant cultural programmes.

## Finding India in Currency Notes

Have you ever examined an Indian currency note really carefully? There are many fascinating details hidden on it!

**What you can discover on a currency note:**
- A portrait of **Mahatma Gandhi** on the front -- and if you hold the note up to a light, a **hidden second image** of Gandhi ji appears!
- The **denomination** (value) printed clearly, along with the note's distinctive **colour**
- Text written in **many different languages** -- a reminder that India speaks hundreds of tongues!
- Beautiful images of famous **Indian monuments** on the back
- The **Indian currency symbol** (the rupee sign)
- Tiny **raised bumps and patterns** that help people with visual impairment identify the note just by touching it
- A small image of **round spectacles** -- the symbol of the **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** (Clean India Mission), inspired by Gandhiji's iconic glasses

> **Helpful technology:** The **MANI app** allows visually impaired people to identify different currency notes through sound alerts and vibrations on their phone.

**Monuments featured on Indian currency notes:**

| **Note Value** | **Monument Shown** |
|---|---|
| 10 rupees | Sun Temple, Konark (Odisha) |
| 20 rupees | Ellora Caves (Maharashtra) |
| 50 rupees | Hampi with Stone Chariot (Karnataka) |
| 100 rupees | Rani ki Vav (Gujarat) |
| 200 rupees | Sanchi Stupa (Madhya Pradesh) |
| 500 rupees | Red Fort (Delhi) |

## The National Emblem

India's **National Emblem** features **three majestic lions** standing atop a circular base. It symbolises **power, bravery, and self-assurance**. Beneath the lions sits the **Ashoka Chakra**. This emblem appears on all official government papers and even on your **Aadhaar card**.

> **Conservation triumph:** Asiatic lions once roamed vast stretches of India. Over time, hunting and habitat loss reduced their numbers to just a few dozen. Today, thanks to dedicated conservation work, **several hundred lions** live in the **Gir forests of Gujarat** -- the only place on Earth where Asiatic lions survive in the wild!

## India's Many Languages

India is among the most **multilingual nations** on the planet. Over **a thousand different languages** are spoken across its length and breadth! Each language carries its own treasure of stories, songs, poems, and wisdom.

The same everyday word can sound completely different depending on the language. Take the word for "mango" as an example:

| **Language** | **Word for Mango** |
|---|---|
| Hindi | Aam |
| Tamil | Maampalam |
| Telugu | Mamidi |
| Marathi | Amba |
| Bengali | Aam |

> **Bridge across languages:** Today, there are mobile apps and websites that can translate between languages instantly. They make it easier to learn new languages and understand people from different regions of India!

## Diversity in Clothing and Headgear

Across India, people wear a wonderful variety of clothes and headgear, each reflecting the traditions and climate of their region:

- In **Rajasthan**, men often wear bright, colourful turbans called **saafa** or **pagri**
- In **Himachal Pradesh**, the traditional **topi** (cap) with a colourful band is a common sight
- Headgear often tells you about a person's home region, their community, or their profession

> **Headgear for safety:** Helmets are a different kind of headgear -- worn by soldiers, construction workers, firefighters, and motorcyclists to protect their heads. In India, wearing a helmet while riding a two-wheeler is **required by law**!

India's cultural richness shines brilliantly through its **musical instruments** and **dance traditions**:

**Traditional Indian percussion instruments:**

- Kathakali (Kerala)
- Manipuri (Manipur)
- Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh)

- Sattriya (Assam)

> India is like a dense, thriving forest -- just as a forest draws its strength from having many different kinds of trees, shrubs, and flowers supporting one another, India draws its strength from its extraordinary **diversity** of cultures, languages, and traditions!

## The Spirit of Togetherness

Despite the incredible variety in our languages, clothing, food, religions, and customs, Indians live together with a spirit of **unity and mutual support**. This togetherness is what makes India truly special.

India carries a glorious heritage that includes **Ayurveda, yoga, and meditation** -- ancient practices that the whole world now embraces. At the same time, India is racing ahead with modern achievements:
- **Space scientists** sending missions to the moon and Mars (like the Chandrayaan and Mangalyaan programmes)
- **Farmers** adopting new technologies for better crop yields
- **UPI** -- the world's most widely used digital payment platform
- **High-speed trains** linking cities across the country
- **Aadhaar** -- the planet's largest biometric identity system

> India holds the record for the **longest written Constitution** of any country. It is also the world's **largest democracy**, where over 140 crore people have a voice!

> India operates the **biggest postal network** on Earth and has the **second longest road network** in the world.
