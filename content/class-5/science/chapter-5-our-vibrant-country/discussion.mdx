## A Special Day -- Republic Day

**Child:** Maa, I know we celebrate Republic Day on 26 January, but why that particular date? We already celebrate Independence Day on 15 August, right?

**Mother:** Great question, beta! On 15 August 1947, India became free from British rule. But freedom alone was not enough -- the country needed its own set of laws and rules to make sure everyone is treated fairly. Dr B.R. Ambedkar led a team that spent nearly three years carefully writing India's Constitution. On 26 January 1950, this Constitution came into effect, and India officially became a republic -- a country governed by its own citizens through elected leaders.

**Child:** So 15 August celebrates our freedom, and 26 January celebrates the rules that help us use that freedom wisely?

**Mother:** Beautifully put! The Constitution gives every Indian fundamental rights -- the right to be treated equally, freedom to express your thoughts, the right to education, and many more. It is the longest written constitution of any country in the world!

**Child:** And the Republic Day parade in Delhi is amazing -- with jets and state floats!

**Mother:** Each state's tableau in the parade showcases something unique about that state. Tamil Nadu might display Bharatanatyam dancers, Rajasthan might show its grand forts, and Assam might feature the Bihu harvest dance. It is a grand celebration of India's incredible diversity all in one place!

## Our National Flag and Currency Notes

**Child:** Maa, I know our flag has three colours -- saffron, white, and green. But what does each one mean?

**Mother:** The saffron stripe at the top represents bravery and sacrifice -- the courage of our freedom fighters. The white band in the middle stands for peace and honesty -- the values we want to live by. The green at the bottom symbolises growth and the fertility of our land. And right in the centre sits the Ashoka Chakra -- a blue wheel with 24 spokes.

**Child:** Why exactly 24 spokes?

**Mother:** Each spoke represents one hour of the day, signifying that there should be continuous progress and movement forward -- the nation should never stand still. The Chakra comes from the Lion Capital built during Emperor Ashoka's time at Sarnath, which is also the basis for our National Emblem.

**Child:** The chapter says that currency notes have hidden pictures! I want to check my piggy bank.

**Mother:** Go ahead! Hold any note up against a bright light and you will see a second, hidden portrait of Mahatma Gandhi appear. The notes also carry text in 17 different languages, pictures of famous Indian monuments on the back, and special raised marks that help visually impaired people identify the note just by touching it.

**Child:** Which monument is on the 500 rupee note?

**Mother:** The Red Fort in Delhi. The 100 rupee note shows Rani ki Vav from Gujarat, the 50 has the stone chariot at Hampi, and the 10 has the Sun Temple of Konark. Each note is like a tiny history lesson in your pocket!

## India's Many Languages

**Child:** Maa, how many languages do people speak in India?

**Mother:** Over a thousand! India is one of the most multilingual places on Earth. The Constitution officially recognises 22 languages in its Eighth Schedule, but people across the country speak hundreds more -- each with its own unique script, literature, folk songs, and proverbs.

**Child:** In my class alone, we have students who speak Hindi, Tamil, Kannada, Bengali, and Gujarati at home!

**Mother:** And yet you all understand each other because you share common languages like Hindi and English at school. But here is the interesting part -- the same word can be completely different in each language. "Mango" is aam in Hindi, maampalam in Tamil, mamidi in Telugu, and amba in Marathi. Different sounds, same delicious fruit!

**Child:** I want to learn more languages!

**Mother:** There are wonderful apps that can help with that. And learning a new language is about much more than just new words -- it opens a window into a whole different way of thinking and living. Every language carries within it the wisdom, humour, and stories of the people who speak it.

## National Symbols -- Pride and Purpose

**Child:** Maa, why does India have a national animal, a national bird, a national flower, and so on? What is the purpose?

**Mother:** Each symbol represents something deeply meaningful about our country. The tiger is our national animal -- powerful, graceful, and majestic. India is home to roughly 75 percent of all the wild tigers left on Earth! The peacock, our national bird, represents beauty, colour, and the joy of the monsoon rains when it dances. The lotus, our national flower, grows in muddy water yet blooms spotlessly clean -- it symbolises purity and rising above difficult circumstances.

**Child:** And the banyan tree is our national tree because it is so massive and gives shade to everyone?

**Mother:** Yes! A single banyan tree can spread over an enormous area using its aerial roots that drop down from branches and become new trunks. The Great Banyan Tree near Kolkata is so huge that it looks like an entire forest, but it is actually just one tree! It represents India sheltering and supporting all its people.

**Child:** What about the Ganges river dolphin?

**Mother:** That is our national aquatic animal. It is nearly blind -- it cannot see in the muddy river water. Instead, it produces clicking sounds and listens to the echoes bouncing back from objects, just like a submarine's sonar. It can survive only in clean, flowing rivers. Protecting the dolphin means protecting our rivers, which benefits every single person who depends on those rivers for water.

**Child:** Every symbol has a deeper meaning behind it!

**Mother:** And each one reminds us of what we need to protect -- our forests, our wildlife, our rivers, and the incredible diversity that makes India unique.

## India's Diversity -- Our Greatest Strength

**Child:** Maa, the chapter compares India to a forest. How is a country like a forest?

**Mother:** Think about it this way. A forest with only one type of tree is fragile -- if a disease attacks that tree, the whole forest could be wiped out. But a forest with hundreds of different trees, bushes, flowers, and animals is strong because they support each other. If one species struggles, others keep the ecosystem going. India is the same -- our diversity of languages, religions, foods, clothing, dances, and music makes us resilient and strong.

**Child:** Like how we have Bharatanatyam from Tamil Nadu, Kathak from Uttar Pradesh, Odissi from Odisha -- each state has its own art!

**Mother:** And different musical instruments too -- tabla from the North, mridangam from the South, dhol from Punjab, khol from Bengal. Each region offers something unique, and together they weave the rich, colourful fabric of Indian culture.

**Child:** The chapter also mentions modern achievements -- UPI, Chandrayaan, Aadhaar. India is not just about tradition!

**Mother:** India beautifully blends the ancient with the modern. We have Ayurveda, which is over 3,000 years old, AND we launched Chandrayaan to explore the moon. Our ancestors invented the concept of zero centuries ago, AND today we built UPI -- the world's most widely used digital payment system. The Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad seats 1,32,000 people, making it the largest cricket stadium on the planet!

**Child:** Our diversity really is our superpower.

**Mother:** Never forget that, beta. India's beauty lies in its differences. The moment we stop respecting each other's languages, customs, and beliefs is the moment we become weaker. Unity in diversity -- that is the heart and soul of India.
