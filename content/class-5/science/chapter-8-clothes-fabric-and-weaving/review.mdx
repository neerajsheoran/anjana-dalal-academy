## Key Concepts

- **Weaving** is interlocking threads to create fabric: vertical threads = **warp**, horizontal threads = **weft**
- The weft goes over one warp thread, under the next, creating a criss-cross pattern
- A **loom** is a wooden frame used for weaving; fabric made on a hand loom is called **handloom fabric**
- India has been weaving for over **4,000 years**; handloom provides livelihood to over **45 lakh families**
- **Spinning** is twisting thin fibres together into thread/yarn; a **charkha** (spinning wheel) does this
- **Khadi** is self-spun, self-woven cloth promoted by **Mahatma Gandhi** during the freedom struggle as a symbol of **self-reliance (atmanirbhar)**
- **Fibres** are thin, hair-like materials twisted into thread; two types: **natural** and **synthetic**
- **Natural fibres**: cotton (plant), silk (silkworm cocoon), wool (sheep/goat fleece), jute (stem), linen (flax), bamboo
- **Synthetic fibres**: nylon, polyester, rayon, terylene -- made from chemicals in factories
- Silk journey: silk moth lays eggs, caterpillars (silkworms) eat mulberry leaves, spin **cocoon**, cocoon placed in hot water, silk thread unwound, woven into fabric
- **Stitching** = joining fabric with needle and thread; simplest stitch is the **running stitch**
- The **tailorbird** uses its beak as a needle to stitch leaves together for its nest
- The **Baya Weaver** bird interlaces grass blades to build hanging pouch-shaped nests
- Indian clothes are recycled: handed down, repurposed as cleaning cloths/bags, made into **quilts (rajai/kantha)**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Weaving** | Interlocking warp and weft threads to create fabric |
| **Warp** | Vertical threads stretched on a loom |
| **Weft** | Horizontal threads passed through the warp |
| **Loom** | A frame used for weaving cloth |
| **Spinning** | Twisting fibres together to make thread or yarn |
| **Charkha** | A spinning wheel used to twist fibres into thread |
| **Khadi** | Hand-spun, hand-woven cloth; symbol of India's freedom movement |
| **Fibre** | Thin, hair-like raw material twisted into thread |
| **Natural fibre** | Fibre from plants or animals (cotton, silk, wool, jute) |
| **Synthetic fibre** | Man-made fibre from chemicals (nylon, polyester, rayon) |
| **Running stitch** | Basic stitch with needle going up-down-up-down in a line |

## Remember These

- India was the **first country** to grow cotton and make clothing from it
- India is the **largest producer of jute** in the world
- **Muslin** cloth was so thin it was called **"woven air"** -- a full saree could pass through a finger ring
- **Pashmina** wool comes from the **Changthangi goat** in Ladakh's freezing mountains
- A single **Patola saree** can take **6 months to a full year** to weave
- **Kala cotton** from Gujarat grows without chemicals and needs very little water
- Handloom weaving uses **zero electricity** -- entirely hand and foot operated
