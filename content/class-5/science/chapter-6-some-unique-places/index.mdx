## India -- A Land of Unique Places

Imagine packing your bags and going on a trip across India. You would cross shimmering seas, walk through forests where tree roots grow above water, climb misty green hills, and discover animals that live nowhere else on the planet. India is so vast and varied that every corner holds a surprise. Let us set off on this exciting journey!

## The Andaman and Nicobar Islands

Way out in the **Bay of Bengal**, far from mainland India, lies a sparkling chain of over 500 islands called the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**. The capital city is **Sri Vijaya Puram** (earlier known as Port Blair). At the very bottom of this island chain sits **Indira Point** -- the **southernmost spot in all of India**.

> **What exactly is an island?** Picture a piece of land with water all around it, like a giant rock sitting in the middle of a lake. That is an island! India has more than a thousand islands scattered across its surrounding seas.

### Plants and Animals Found Nowhere Else

Because islands are cut off from the mainland by water, the plants and animals living on them develop in their own special ways over thousands of years. The Andaman Islands are home to creatures you will not find anywhere else on Earth:

| **Unique Creature** | **Why It Is Special** |
|---|---|
| Andaman wood pigeon | Lives only on these islands -- nowhere else |
| Andaman hornbill | A rare bird seen only in this part of the world |
| Buttress-root trees | Trees with huge root walls spreading outward at the base for extra support |

> Think of islands like classrooms that have been sealed shut for centuries. The students inside learn differently from everyone outside. Over time, they become completely unique!

### A Colourful World Beneath the Waves

The waters around the Andaman Islands hide stunning **coral reefs**. Corals are tiny living creatures that slowly build hard, colourful structures underwater. Over centuries, millions of corals create enormous underwater neighbourhoods where fish, sea turtles, and starfish live together.

> These coral reefs work like shields for the coastline, protecting the shore from powerful waves. They are also home to an incredible variety of sea life -- truly an underwater wonder!

### The Mysterious North Sentinel Island

Among the Andaman Islands is a place shrouded in mystery -- **North Sentinel Island**. A small group of people lives here in the same way their ancestors did thousands of years ago, hunting and gathering food from the forest and sea. They do not welcome outsiders, and the government has declared that **nobody may visit** the island.

> During the devastating 2004 tsunami, the Sentinelese people survived safely. Experts believe they noticed warning signs in nature -- shifts in the wind, unusual behaviour of animals, and changes in the sea. Their ancient bond with nature turned out to be their greatest protection!

### Staying Safe on Water

If you ever travel by boat or ferry to the islands, always wear a **life jacket**. Even strong swimmers need one because the open sea is unpredictable. A life jacket keeps you floating if you accidentally fall into the water.

## The Sundarbans -- Home of the Mangroves

Our next stop takes us to the coast of **West Bengal**, where the mighty rivers Ganga and Brahmaputra meet the sea. Here stretches the **Sundarbans** -- the **largest mangrove forest on the entire planet**.

### Trees That Breathe Through Their Roots

Mangrove trees are truly extraordinary. They thrive in places where most other trees would perish -- in salty, muddy, water-logged soil.

| **Amazing Feature** | **How It Helps** |
|---|---|
| Roots stick up **above the water** | Allow the trees to take in air from the muddy, salty ground |
| Roots grip the soil tightly | Stop ocean waves and storms from washing away the land |
| Can survive in **salt water** | Most trees wilt and die in salt water, but mangroves flourish |

### Swimming Tigers

The Sundarbans is the kingdom of the **Royal Bengal Tiger**. Unlike tigers in other forests, these big cats are brilliant swimmers! They paddle between islands and even hunt fish and crabs in the water. Local honey collectors and fishermen who enter the forest sometimes wear **masks on the back of their heads**. Why? Because tigers prefer to attack from behind. A face-like mask on the back of the head tricks the tiger into thinking the person is watching it!

> For generations, families in the Sundarbans have lived alongside tigers and the forest. They fish in the rivers, collect wild honey, and gather firewood -- always respecting the forest that sustains them. This is what it means to live **in harmony with nature**.

## Northeast India -- Green Hills and Great Traditions

Travel northeast and you enter a world of mist-covered mountains, rushing rivers, and thick forests. **Northeast India** has eight states -- the famous **"Seven Sisters"** (Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Tripura) plus **Sikkim**.

### A Region Bursting with Life

The Northeast is a treasure chest of nature and culture:
- **Dense forests** full of rare orchids and butterflies
- Unique **wildlife** including the one-horned rhinoceros and clouded leopard
- Stunning **handwoven textiles** made by local communities
- Vibrant **folk dances, songs, and festivals**
- Communities that have lived **close to nature** for centuries

### Saving the Hargila Bird

In Assam, a group of determined women noticed that the **Hargila** (Greater Adjutant Stork) was disappearing. These tall, unusual-looking birds were losing their nesting trees. The women organised themselves, taught schoolchildren to appreciate the birds, and guarded the nesting sites. Thanks to their dedication, the Hargila population has been steadily increasing!

### Bridges Grown from Living Roots

Deep in the forests of Meghalaya, you can walk across **living root bridges** -- actual bridges made from the roots of rubber fig trees! The Khasi people guide young roots across streams, and over 15 to 20 years, these roots grow thick and strong enough to carry the weight of many people walking at once. Some of these bridges are centuries old and actually grow **stronger** with each passing year.

> While concrete bridges crack and crumble over time, living root bridges grow tougher. What a brilliant example of humans and nature working as a team!

> **Spicy fact:** The **Bhut Jolokia**, also called the ghost pepper, grows in Northeast India. It is one of the hottest chillies on the planet! Farmers sometimes smear it on fences to keep wild elephants away from their fields.

> **Rainy record:** **Mawsynram in Meghalaya** holds the title of the wettest place on Earth where people live -- it receives more rainfall than anywhere else!

## The Western Ghats -- A Nature Hotspot

Running along the western edge of India like a green spine is a chain of ancient forested hills called the **Western Ghats**. They pass through **six states**: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu.

### A Treasure House of Life

Scientists call the Western Ghats a **biodiversity hotspot** -- a region packed with an astonishing variety of living things, many of which exist only here and nowhere else.

| **Special Species** | **What Makes It Remarkable** |
|---|---|
| Lion-tailed macaque | A shy monkey found only in these hills |
| Indian giant squirrel | Famous for its striking multi-coloured fur |
| Tigers and elephants | Roam freely in protected forests and reserves |
| Great hornbill | A magnificent bird with a large casque on its beak |

### Where Great Rivers Are Born

Many of India's lifeline rivers -- the **Godavari**, **Krishna**, and **Kaveri** -- begin their journey as tiny streams high up in the Western Ghats. Millions of people downstream depend on these rivers for drinking water, farming, and daily life.

### Wisdom of the Forest People

**Tribal communities** have lived in the Western Ghats for countless generations. They know which forest plants cure fevers, which roots can heal wounds, and which berries are safe to eat. This traditional knowledge, passed down from elders to children, is priceless.

> **Fruity surprise:** Scientists have discovered over **200 different varieties of mangoes** growing wild in the Western Ghats! They are also studying dozens of types of jackfruit and jamun found here.

### When People Saved a Forest

In Kerala, a magnificent patch of ancient rainforest called **Silent Valley** was once in danger. The government had planned to build a dam that would have drowned the entire forest under water. But ordinary people -- villagers, college students, school teachers, and scientists -- came together in the **"Save Silent Valley Movement"** and raised their voices. The dam was cancelled, and today Silent Valley stands tall as a **protected national park**.

> This movement proved that when people unite for a cause they believe in, they can truly **change the course of history**.

## Protected Areas

To safeguard precious habitats, the government sets up **protected areas** -- national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, and tiger reserves. Inside these zones, cutting trees and harming animals is strictly forbidden.

> The Western Ghats alone contain over **50 protected areas** to keep their incredible wildlife safe!

> **Tiger nation:** India shelters approximately **75 per cent** of all the wild tigers left on Earth. And Sikkim has earned the distinction of being India's **first fully organic state**, where farming is done entirely without chemical fertilisers or pesticides.

## Everything is Connected

Every region we visited -- from the coral-fringed Andaman Islands to the mist-wrapped root bridges of Meghalaya and the spice-scented forests of the Western Ghats -- holds unique treasures.

| **Place** | **Its Special Gift** |
|---|---|
| Andaman and Nicobar Islands | One-of-a-kind wildlife, coral reefs, ancient island communities |
| Sundarbans | World's largest mangrove forest, swimming Royal Bengal Tigers |
| Northeast India | Living root bridges, vibrant cultural traditions, extraordinary biodiversity |
| Western Ghats | Biodiversity hotspot, birthplace of great rivers, rich storehouse of medicinal plants |

> These wonders belong to all of us. If we want future generations to see coral reefs glowing underwater, tigers swimming through mangroves, and root bridges growing stronger each year, we must **learn about, respect, and protect** the land we call home.
