## What Is Energy?

**Child:** Maa, my teacher said energy is what makes things happen. But that sounds so broad! Can you give me a clearer picture?

**Mother:** Sure, beta. Picture this -- when you kick a football in the park, the ball flies because your leg gave it movement energy. When I light the gas stove, the flame heats the tawa -- that is heat energy. When you clap your hands, the sound you hear is sound energy. Energy is simply the ability to cause a change -- to move something, warm something up, light something, or create a noise.

**Child:** So if there was no energy at all, nothing would happen?

**Mother:** Nothing at all! No breeze, no light, no warmth, no sound -- the world would be completely frozen, dark, and silent. Energy is what keeps the universe alive. And here is a fascinating fact -- energy is never really lost. It just changes from one form to another.

**Child:** Like when I eat rajma chawal, the energy in the food becomes running energy when I race my friends?

**Mother:** Perfect example! Your body is like an energy converter. The food energy becomes movement when you run, heat when you feel warm after playing, and even sound when you shout "I won!" at the finish line.

## Sources of Energy — Food, Fuel, and Electricity

**Child:** Maa, on days when I skip my morning nashta, I feel so tired by the time the lunch bell rings. Why does that happen?

**Mother:** Because your body runs on food energy, just like a scooter runs on petrol. When you eat, your body converts the food into a usable form of energy that powers your muscles and brain. Skip a meal, and your body has less fuel to work with -- so you feel weak and sleepy.

**Child:** Is that why Neeraj Chopra and other athletes eat special food?

**Mother:** Exactly! An athlete training for hours every day burns much more energy than someone sitting at a desk. So athletes eat foods rich in energy -- lots of rotis, rice, dal, paneer, fruits, and milk. But everyone needs a balanced diet to stay active and healthy.

**Child:** The chapter talks about petrol and diesel for vehicles. Is fuel similar to food?

**Mother:** In a way, yes! Both store chemical energy inside them. When petrol burns in a car engine, it releases heat and motion that makes the wheels turn. When your body digests food, it releases energy for your activities. But there is a big difference -- burning petrol creates poisonous fumes and pollutes the air, while digesting food does not!

**Child:** So our body is actually cleaner than a car engine?

**Mother:** Much cleaner! That is why scientists are working hard to create electric vehicles that run on batteries charged by solar or wind energy -- so that transportation becomes as clean as possible.

## Electricity — Powerful but Dangerous

**Child:** Maa, think about our house -- the fan, the fridge, the TV, the mixer grinder, the charging cable for your phone. Everything needs electricity! Where does all of it come from?

**Mother:** Most of India's electricity comes from thermal power plants that burn coal. The coal heats water into steam, the steam pushes a turbine wheel, and the spinning turbine generates electricity. But burning all that coal produces huge clouds of smoke and gases that pollute the air and make people sick.

**Child:** Is there a better way to make electricity?

**Mother:** Many better ways! Solar panels turn sunlight directly into electricity without any smoke. Wind turbines use the force of moving air. Hydroelectric dams use the power of flowing rivers. India has been building amazing clean energy projects -- the Bhadla Solar Park in Rajasthan is one of the largest solar plants in the entire world!

**Child:** The chapter also warns that electricity can be dangerous. How do I stay safe?

**Mother:** Golden rules, beta -- never touch a switch or plug with wet hands because water helps electricity flow and you could get a painful shock. Never stick anything into an electrical socket. Stay far away from any wires lying on the ground after a storm -- they could be carrying live current. And if you see something sparking or smoking, do not touch it. Just tell an adult right away.

## Clean Energy — Sun, Wind, and Water

**Child:** Maa, why do people call solar energy "clean"?

**Mother:** Because sunlight is free and turning it into electricity produces no smoke, no ash, and no dirty waste. Compare that to a coal plant -- workers have to dig coal from deep underground, trucks carry it to the plant, it gets burned producing tonnes of smoke and ash, and then the leftover waste has to be dumped somewhere. Solar panels just sit quietly on a rooftop, soaking up sunshine and making electricity silently.

**Child:** What happens on cloudy or rainy days? Do solar panels stop working completely?

**Mother:** They produce less electricity on overcast days but do not stop entirely. And we can store the extra electricity made on sunny days in big batteries for use at night or during rain. This storage technology is getting better and more affordable every year.

**Child:** The chapter mentions water mills called gharaats in the hills. Those sound really old-fashioned!

**Mother:** They have been around for centuries! In villages across Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand, people have used gharaats to grind wheat and rice using nothing but the force of flowing mountain streams. No electricity, no diesel -- just water power. It is the exact same principle behind a modern hydroelectric dam, just on a smaller, simpler scale.

**Child:** So our ancestors were already using clean energy before anyone even called it that!

**Mother:** Absolutely. And traditional Indian houses were designed cleverly too -- big courtyards for natural light, thick walls that kept rooms cool in summer and warm in winter, windows placed to catch the breeze. Today's "green buildings" are rediscovering these very same ancient ideas.

## Energy Efficiency — Getting More from Less

**Child:** Maa, what exactly does "energy efficiency" mean?

**Mother:** It means achieving the same outcome while using less energy. The best everyday example is the LED bulb. An old-style bulb used 60 watts of electricity and wasted most of it as heat -- your hand would burn if you touched it! An LED bulb gives the same brightness using only about 9 watts. Same light, but nearly seven times less electricity!

**Child:** So if we swap all our old bulbs for LEDs, we save a lot of money on the electricity bill?

**Mother:** Let me show you the maths. Say you have 8 bulbs running 5 hours a day. With 60-watt old bulbs, that is 2,400 watt-hours daily. With 9-watt LEDs, it is only 360 watt-hours. You save over 2,000 watt-hours a day! Over a year, that adds up to roughly 740 units of electricity. At around 8 rupees per unit, you save almost 6,000 rupees just on lighting!

**Child:** Six thousand rupees! That is enough for a new school bag AND books!

**Mother:** And imagine if every household in India made that switch -- the electricity saved across the country would be enormous. Tiny individual choices can create massive collective impact.

## Saving Energy Every Day

**Child:** Maa, apart from LED bulbs, what else can I do to save energy?

**Mother:** Simple things that really add up. Switch off lights and fans every time you step out of a room -- it sounds obvious, but most people forget. Pull open the curtains during the daytime and let sunlight brighten the room instead of flipping a switch. Walk or cycle to your friend's house instead of asking Papa to drive you. On cold evenings, put on a warm sweater instead of turning on a room heater.

**Child:** At school, I always see fans running in empty classrooms during the games period!

**Mother:** If your classroom has 4 fans at 75 watts each, running them empty for even one hour wastes 300 watt-hours. Over the entire school year, that is a huge amount of electricity going to waste for no reason. You could volunteer to be the "switch-off monitor" who makes sure everything is turned off when the class leaves!

**Child:** The Sun sends more energy to Earth in one hour than all humans use in a whole year. If only we could capture more of it!

**Mother:** That is exactly where India is heading, beta. With massive investments in solar parks, wind farms, and battery storage, the future of energy in India is getting brighter -- literally!
