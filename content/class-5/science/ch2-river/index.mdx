## River - Introduction

A **river** is a large, natural flow of water that moves across the land.

**Importance of Rivers**

1. **Source of Water:** Rivers give us water for drinking, cooking, and washing.
2. **Help in Farming:** Farmers use river water to grow crops.
3. **Home for Animals:** Many fish and water animals live in rivers.
4. **Transport:** Boats and ships travel on rivers to carry people and goods.
5. **Electricity:** Big dams on rivers help to make electricity.
6. **Clean Environment:** Rivers help keep nature healthy.

### How a River Begins and Flows

1. Glaciers are large masses of frozen snow and ice found in high mountains. When these glaciers melt, the melted water flows down the mountains as small streams. These streams join together and gradually form a river.

   ![](Ch-2.%2001.png)
2. As the river flows down the mountains and move across the land **many smaller streams join it.** These small rivers are called **tributaries**. When tributaries join the main river, they make it **wider, stronger, and deeper.**

![](Ch-2.%2002.png)

3. The river continues its journey through plains, towns, forests, and villages until it finally reaches the sea or ocean.

   ![](Ch-2.%2003.png)
4. Before entering the sea, the river spreads out into many small branches. These branches together form a **delta**, which is a triangular area of land made from the soil carried by the river.

   ![](Ch-2.%2004.png)

## Cultural Importance of River

Rivers are important in India not only for water but also for our **culture, traditions, and history**. Many rivers like the Ganga, Yamuna, and Jamuna are considered **sacred** because people believe they are holy and protect life.

**Festivals on Riverbanks**

Many important festivals and rituals are celebrated near rivers.

For example:

* Godavari Pushkaram (celebrated once in 12 years)
* Ganga Dussehra
* Kumbh Mela

People gather near riverbanks to pray, take dips, and perform rituals.

![](Ch-2.%2005.png)

## Historical Importance of River

In ancient times, many cities and civilizations grew near rivers because:

* Water was easily available
* Land near rivers was fertile
* It was easy to travel and trade using boats

Great civilizations like the **Indus Valley Civilization** also developed near rivers.

## Wildlife Around River

Rivers are home to many living beings. The water and the land around the river create a safe place for plants and animals to survive.

![](Ch-2.%2007.png)

Many animals live in rivers and near rivers. Fish, turtles, and frogs live in the water. Crocodiles and snakes are also found in rivers. Birds, deer, and monkeys come near the river to drink water. Rivers give animals water, food, and a place to live.

## Dams

Dams are very large walls built across rivers to **store water**.&#x20;

When river water is blocked by a dam, it collects in a huge water body called a **reservoir**.

![](Ch-2.%2008.png)

Dams are helpful in many ways:

* **Irrigation**: Farmers use the stored water to grow crops, especially in dry months.
* **Electricity**: Dams help produce hydroelectric power, which gives electricity to homes and factories.
* **Drinking Water**: Many towns and cities get clean drinking water from reservoirs created by dams.

### How Dams Affect Nature and People

**D**ams are helpful, but they also cause some problems.:

* **Forests Get Flooded**: When a reservoir fills with water, large areas of forests can get submerged.
* **Animals Lose Their Homes**: Animals living in these forests have to move to new places.
* **People Are Displaced**: Villages near the river may get flooded by the reservoir, and families must leave their homes and shift elsewhere.

**Why We Must Think Carefully**

Dams give us many benefits, but they also change the natural flow of the river and affect the environment.

So, it is important to build dams wisely and take care of the people, plants, and animals living in those areas.

## River Pollution

Rivers become polluted when harmful things are mixed into their water.

![](Ch-2.%2009.png)

### How River Gets Polluted

* **Plastic bags and wrappers** thrown on the ground get washed into rivers.
* **Dirty water** from kitchens, bathrooms, and drains enters rivers.
* **Factory waste** containing chemicals is released into river water.
* **Fertilisers and pesticides** from farms get washed into rivers during rain.

### How Polluted River Harms Living Beings

Pollution affects everyone who depends on the river:

* **Animals in water** (fish, turtles, insects) get sick or die when they swallow plastic or live in dirty water.
* **Plants** cannot grow properly in polluted water.
* **Birds and animals** near the river lose their natural home.
* **People** who use polluted river water for drinking, bathing, or cooking may fall ill.

  ![](Ch-2.%2010.png)

### Why We Must Keep Rivers Clean

Rivers give us freshwater and support many types of life.

If rivers stay polluted, clean water becomes limited and many living beings will suffer. Keeping rivers clean is important for our health, for nature, and for the future.

## Floods

Floods happen when there is too much rain, and the river cannot hold all the extra water. The river water rises, overflows its banks, and spreads across the land. This sudden and large flow of water can cause a lot of damage.

![](Ch-2.%2011.png)

### Effects of Floods on People and Places

Floods affect many parts of daily life:

* **Houses** get filled with water or washed away.
* **Crops** growing in fields are destroyed.
* **Roads and bridges** get damaged, making travel difficult.
* **Electricity** may stop working for days.
* **Dirty water** can spread diseases and make people sick.
* **Schools and shops** may remain closed.
* **Animals** also lose their homes and food.

### Learning Safety During Floods

Children learn important safety steps such as:

* Staying away from floodwater
* Moving to higher ground
* Not touching electrical wires
* Listening to adults and following emergency instructions

These steps help keep everyone safe until the floodwater goes down.

## Water Scarcity

Water scarcity means there is **not enough water** for people to use. This happens when **rivers and lakes dry up** or when it does not rain for a long time.

When there is water scarcity:

* People do not get enough water for drinking, cooking, or bathing.
* Farmers cannot grow crops.
* Animals also struggle to find water.

A real example is **Chennai**, where the water in reservoirs became very low, and people had to bring water from faraway places. This teaches us that we must **save water** and not waste it.
