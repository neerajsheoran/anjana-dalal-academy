## River - Introduction

A **river** is a large, natural flow of water that moves across the land.

**Importance of Rivers**

1. **Source of Water:** Rivers give us water for drinking, cooking, and washing.
2. **Help in Farming:** Farmers use river water to grow crops.
3. **Home for Animals:** Many fish and water animals live in rivers.
4. **Transport:** Boats and ships travel on rivers to carry people and goods.
5. **Electricity:** Big dams on rivers help to make electricity.
6. **Clean Environment:** Rivers help keep nature healthy.

### How a River Begins and Flows

1. Glaciers are large masses of frozen snow and ice found in high mountains. When these glaciers melt, the melted water flows down the mountains as small streams. These streams join together and gradually form a river.

   ![](Ch-2.%2001.png)
2. As the river flows down the mountains and move across the land **many smaller streams join it.** These small rivers are called **tributaries**. When tributaries join the main river, they make it **wider, stronger, and deeper.**

![](Ch-2.%2002.png)

3. The river continues its journey through plains, towns, forests, and villages until it finally reaches the sea or ocean.

   ![](Ch-2.%2003.png)
4. Before entering the sea, the river spreads out into many small branches. These branches together form a **delta**, which is a triangular area of land made from the soil carried by the river.

   ![](Ch-2.%2004.png)

## Cultural Importance of River

Rivers are important in India not only for water but also for our **culture, traditions, and history**. Many rivers like the Ganga, Yamuna, and Jamuna are considered **sacred** because people believe they are holy and protect life.

**Festivals on Riverbanks**

Many important festivals and rituals are celebrated near rivers.

For example:

* Godavari Pushkaram (celebrated once in 12 years)
* Ganga Dussehra
* Kumbh Mela

People gather near riverbanks to pray, take dips, and perform rituals.
