## Water - Introduction

Water is the most important for life. It is provided by nature.

Water is used for:

* Drinking
* Cooking food
* Growing crops
* Cleaning and other daily activities













All living beings, including humans, animals, birds, and plants, depend on water for their survival.

Water is also a home for many plants and animals. Without water, life on Earth would not be possible. Therefore, water is one of the most important natural resources, and we must use it wisely and save it for the future.

Most of the Earth is covered with water, but we have only a little water for our use. This is because there are two types of water on Earth.

* **Salty water**, which is found in seas and oceans and cannot be used for drinking.
* **Freshwater**, which we get from rivers and other freshwater sources.

If the whole hand represents salty water on Earth, then only the fingertip represents fresh water. So, fresh water is very limited.













---

## Water Cycle

The water cycle is the way water keeps going up into the air as vapour and comes back down as rain again and again.



























1. **Evaporation** *(Water goes up)*

The Sun heats the water in rivers, lakes, and oceans, and the water goes up into the air as vapour.

2. **Condensation** *(Clouds form)*

The water vapour cools down in the sky and clouds form from tiny water drops.

3. **Precipitation** *(Water falls down)*

When the clouds become heavy, the water falls down as rain, snow, or hail.

4. **Collection** *(Water gathers)*

The water gathers again in rivers, lakes, ponds, oceans, or goes into the ground.

---

## Ground Water

Groundwater is the water that is stored deep under the ground in the soil and rocks.

**How Water Collects Underground?**

When it rains, some of the water flows into rivers, lakes, and ponds. Some water soaks into the soil and goes down through the layers of earth. This stored water is called groundwater.























---

## Surface Water

Surface water is the water we can see on the Earth’s surface.

This includes **rivers, ponds, lakes**, and other water bodies that stay on top of the land. It is one of the main natural sources of **freshwater** along with groundwater.















Pond and lake water is still water because it does not flow.













The water in a river keeps flowing from one area to another.

---

## Different Forms of Water

Water can appear in three different forms, depending on how hot or cold it is.

* As a liquid — like rain or the water we drink.
* As a solid — like ice or snow when water freezes.
* As a gas — called water vapour, which we cannot see, formed when water is heated.















Water keeps changing between these forms all the time in nature.

---

## Aquatic Animals

Water bodies like ponds, lakes, and rivers are home to many plants and animals.

Many animals live inside water or close to water because they need water for breathing, moving, or finding food.

Animals that live in water are called **aquatic animals**.

Examples

* **Fish** – They have fins to swim and gills to breathe in water.
* **Water snakes** – They can swim and catch fish or frogs.
* **Freshwater turtles** – They live in ponds and rivers.
* **Frogs** – They can live on land and in water.
