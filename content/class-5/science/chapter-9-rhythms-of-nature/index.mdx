## Everything Changes

Think back to when you were in Class 3. You were shorter, your handwriting was bigger, and you probably had different favourite games. Now look at yourself -- you have grown taller, you know so many new things, and you can do so much more!

Changes happen **all around us, all the time**. Some take years, some happen over days, and some occur in the blink of an eye.

**Changes you can spot every day:**

![Everything changes — changes you can spot every day](everything-changes-changes-you-can-spot-every-day.png)

- You **grow taller** bit by bit each month
- Tree leaves **turn yellow**, fall off, and fresh ones sprout again
- Flowers **open up** in the morning and close at dusk
- The sky shifts from **pink at dawn** to **deep blue at noon** to **orange at sunset**
- The Sun appears to **travel across the sky** from east to west

## Day and Night

The most familiar rhythm of nature is the daily shift from **daylight to darkness** and back again.

### What Causes Day and Night?

![What causes day and night — the Earth spinning on its axis](what-causes-day-and-night.png)

It looks like the Sun travels across the sky -- climbing up in the east and going down in the west. But here is the surprising truth: **the Sun does not move at all**! It is our **Earth that spins** -- rotating like a top.

Here is how it works:
1. The Earth is shaped like a giant ball (a sphere).
2. It keeps **spinning on an imaginary line** through its centre, called its **axis** -- completing one full spin in about **24 hours** (one day).
3. Whichever side of the Earth is **facing the Sun** receives sunlight -- that side has **day**.
4. The opposite side, turned **away from the Sun**, is in shadow -- that side has **night**.
5. As the Earth keeps rotating, every place gets its turn in the sunlight and then in the dark.

> **Try this experiment:** Hold a ball (representing the Earth) in front of a torch (representing the Sun). The side of the ball facing the torch is bright (day), and the other side is dark (night). Now slowly spin the ball -- see how different parts take turns being lit up? That is exactly how day and night work!

> **Globe facts:** A **globe** is a small model of the Earth. The blue parts show seas and oceans, which cover nearly **three-fourths** of the Earth's surface. The rest is land where countries, cities, and villages are located.

> When India is enjoying a sunny afternoon, people in countries on the other side of the world are fast asleep because it is nighttime there!

> **First rays of dawn:** The village of **Dong** in Arunachal Pradesh is called India's **"First Village of the Sunrise"** -- every morning, it is the very first place on Indian soil to be touched by the Sun's rays.

## Changes Across the Year -- Seasons

Just as day gives way to night and night to day, nature also follows a **larger repeating pattern** over the course of a year. The air turns warm, then scorching hot, then the rains arrive, the air cools down, winter sets in, and then it warms up again. This repeating cycle is what we call **seasons**.

### India's Six Seasons

![India's six seasons — Vasanta, Grishma, Varsha, Sharad, Hemant, Shishir](indias-six-seasons.png)

The ancient Indian calendar recognises not four but **six distinct seasons**, each lasting roughly two months:

| **Season** | **Sanskrit Name** | **Approximate Months** | **What Happens** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring | **Vasanta** | February - March | Trees burst into flower, weather feels delightful |
| Summer | **Grishma** | April - June | Blazing heat, mangoes ripen on trees |
| Monsoon | **Varsha** | July - August | Clouds pour heavy rain, the land turns lush green |
| Autumn | **Sharad** | September - October | Skies clear up, air feels soft and mild |
| Pre-winter | **Hemant** | November - December | Mornings and evenings turn chilly |
| Winter | **Shishir** | January - February | Cold winds blow, fog blankets the land |

> **Same country, different weather:** Seasons feel very different depending on where you are in India. Winter in **Kerala** is gentle and pleasant, while winter in **Kashmir** brings thick snowfall. Monsoon drenches **Assam** with floods of rain, while the **Thar Desert** in Rajasthan barely gets a sprinkle.

## How Seasons Affect Life Around Us

Seasons are not just about weather changes -- they shape nearly everything in our world.

### Plants Change with Seasons

![Plants change with seasons — flowering, ripening, falling leaves, resting](plants-change-with-seasons.png)

- In **spring**, bare branches suddenly explode with blossoms and new green leaves
- In **summer**, juicy fruits like mango, lychee, and jamun ripen under the hot sun
- During the **monsoon**, rain drenches the soil and everything grows wild and green
- In **autumn**, leaves on many trees turn golden and drift to the ground
- In **winter**, some trees stand completely bare, resting before the next spring

### Animals Respond to Seasons

![Animals respond to seasons — frogs, snakes, migratory birds, ants](animals-respond-to-seasons.png)

- When the **monsoon** arrives, frogs emerge from hiding and fill the night with their loud croaking
- **Snakes** slither out of flooded burrows during the rains -- the festival of **Nag Panchami** falls during this season as a way to show respect for these creatures of nature
- In **winter**, birds like the Siberian Crane fly thousands of kilometres from cold lands to warm Indian wetlands
- When **ants march in long lines carrying eggs uphill**, villagers know that rain is on its way!

> **Farmer's friend:** In parts of Odisha, when the **koel (cuckoo)** begins its melodious call, farmers recognise it as nature's signal that the rains are approaching. That is when they start sowing their seeds!

### Farming and Seasons

![Farming and seasons — Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid crops](farming-and-seasons.png)

Different crops need different weather, so farmers plant specific crops in specific seasons:

| **Season** | **Crop Type** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| Monsoon (Kharif) | Need plenty of rain and warmth | Rice, maize, cotton, sugarcane |
| Winter (Rabi) | Need cooler, drier weather | Wheat, mustard, peas, gram |
| Summer (Zaid) | Short-season crops in the heat | Watermelon, cucumber, moong dal |

### Festivals and Seasons

![Festivals and seasons — Pongal, Holi, Baisakhi, Diwali tied to seasons](festivals-and-seasons.png)

Many of India's festivals are deeply tied to the rhythm of seasons and harvests:

| **Festival** | **Season / What It Celebrates** |
|---|---|
| **Pongal / Makar Sankranti** | Winter -- gratitude for the harvest and the returning warmth of the Sun |
| **Holi** | Spring -- joy of colourful blossoms and the end of winter |
| **Baisakhi / Gudi Padwa / Vishu / Rongali Bihu** | Spring -- new year and spring harvest celebrations |
| **Diwali** | Autumn -- lights celebrating the completed harvest season |

> India celebrates over **1,000 festivals** throughout the year -- perhaps the most festive country on Earth!

### What We Eat and Wear Changes Too

- In **summer**, we reach for cooling treats like sliced watermelon, chilled lassi, and raw mango panna, and dress in light cotton clothes
- During the **monsoon**, the smell of hot pakoras frying and steaming cups of chai fill every home, and we carry umbrellas and wear raincoats
- In **winter**, we warm up with gajar ka halwa, roasted peanuts, and dry-fruit laddoos, and bundle up in thick sweaters and cosy shawls

## Keeping a Nature Journal

One of the most exciting ways to understand nature's rhythms is to maintain a **Seasons Journal** -- a personal notebook where you write down and draw what you observe in nature.

**What to watch for and record:**

| **Category** | **Things to Notice** |
|---|---|
| **Plant life** | Which trees are flowering? Are fruits ripening? Are leaves turning colour or falling? |
| **Birds and animals** | What birds do you hear singing? Are insects buzzing around? Are birds building nests? |
| **Sky and weather** | Is it sunny, cloudy, rainy, or foggy? What time does the Sun rise and set? |
| **Water** | Is it raining? Are puddles forming? Are ponds full or shrinking? |
| **People around you** | What clothes are people wearing? What seasonal foods are in the market? Which festivals are happening? |

## The Rhythm of Life

![The rhythm of life — seasons setting the heartbeat of everything we do](the-rhythm-of-life.png)

Seasons are much more than shifts in temperature -- they set the **heartbeat of life itself**. The crops we grow, the food we eat, the clothes we wear, and the festivals we celebrate all dance to the tune of seasons.

> Seasons remind us that **change is natural and beautiful**. Just as surely as night follows day, spring follows winter, and rain follows the heat. These patterns repeat faithfully, year after year.

By paying attention to these rhythms, we begin to see the grand **cycles of life** that connect every living thing on Earth. Each time you step outside, you are witnessing nature's timeless rhythm unfolding -- **one season at a time**.

> India is a land of astonishing variety -- it contains both the **wettest inhabited place on Earth** (Mawsynram in Meghalaya) and one of the **driest** (the Thar Desert in Rajasthan). That is the remarkable diversity of our country's climate!
