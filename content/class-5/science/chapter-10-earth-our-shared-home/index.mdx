## The Blue Planet

When astronauts float high above the Earth in their spaceship, they see something breathtaking -- a glowing blue ball wrapped in swirling white clouds, with patches of green and brown land peeking through. There are no fences, no painted lines between countries -- just one beautiful, shared home.

> **Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla**, the first Indian to reach the International Space Station, shared his experience: "The Earth looks completely one, no border is visible from outside. We all are part of humanity and the Earth is our one home."

> **Wing Commander Rakesh Sharma**, the first Indian to travel to space, was asked by the Prime Minister how India looked from up there. His famous reply: **"Saare Jahaan Se Achcha"** -- the finest in the entire world!

## We Are All Connected

From the heights of space, cities and villages vanish. Country borders disappear. What remains is a living planet where nature flows freely across every boundary -- wind carries clouds from one continent to another, rivers cross from one country to the next, and birds fly over oceans without needing a passport.

People around the world are linked in countless ways too:
- The **clothes** in your wardrobe may have fabric from one country and buttons from another
- The **food** on your plate may include spices, grains, and fruits that travelled from faraway lands
- **Ideas, music, stories, games, and inventions** jump across borders and become part of everyone's life

> The Earth is like a giant family home -- everything and everyone in it is connected through nature, trade, travel, and shared care.

## Story 1: The Travelling Birds

Have you ever spotted **pink and black birds** swooping across the sky in large, noisy flocks? Those are **Rosy Starlings**!

Every **winter**, enormous flocks of Rosy Starlings fly thousands of kilometres from the cold grasslands of **southern Russia and Mongolia** all the way to warm, sunny India. Here is what makes them remarkable:

- They arrive in India seeking **warmth and food** during the harsh northern winter
- They feast on **locusts and grasshoppers** that damage crops, acting as natural pest controllers for Indian farmers
- They demonstrate beautifully how **nature connects distant countries** -- a small bird bridging two continents!

> When animals travel freely and safely across the globe, it reminds us that nature knows no borders. A bird from Russia helping an Indian farmer grow food -- that is global teamwork at its finest!

## Story 2: Yoga -- India's Gift to the World

**Yoga** has been practised in India for more than **3,000 years**. Ancient sages developed it as a way to keep the body strong, the mind calm, and the spirit at peace. Over the centuries, travellers, scholars, and teachers carried yoga from India to distant lands.

Today, yoga is practised in **nearly every country** on the planet!

> In 2014, the **United Nations** officially declared **21 June** as the **International Day of Yoga**. On that day, millions of people across the globe roll out their mats and practise yoga together!

| **Yoga's Origin** | **India** |
|---|---|
| **Where it is practised today** | Nearly every country in the world |
| **International Day of Yoga** | 21 June |
| **How ancient** | More than 3,000 years |

> A practice that began in Indian forests and ashrams thousands of years ago is now cherished by people on every continent -- truly a gift from India to the world.

## Story 3: The Chilli -- A Spice That Changed Our Food

Try to picture Indian cooking without chillies. No spicy aloo sabzi, no fiery mirchi ka salan, no tangy achaar. Hard to imagine, right? But here is the astonishing truth -- **chillies did not originally grow in India**!

- Chilli plants first grew only in **South America**, near present-day Mexico
- Around **400 to 500 years ago**, Portuguese sailors brought chilli seeds to India
- Indians fell in love with the fiery flavour, and chillies quickly became essential in our cooking
- Before chillies reached India, our ancestors used **black pepper** (kali mirch) to add heat to their food

> Many other everyday foods also arrived in India from across the seas: **potatoes, tomatoes, peanuts, and cashew nuts** all originally came from the Americas!

## Story 4: Sugar -- India's Gift to the World

Just as the chilli travelled to India, **sugar journeyed from India to the rest of the world**!

- In ancient times, people everywhere used **honey** and fruit juices as their only sources of sweetness
- India was the first place where people learned to press sugarcane and make **jaggery** (gur) from its juice
- Indian craftspeople later discovered how to refine jaggery into white **sugar**
- Through ancient **trade routes**, this knowledge spread to Arab lands, Europe, and beyond

> India also shared **rice, mangoes, and bananas** with the world. These foods, born on Indian soil, are now enjoyed by people in every corner of the globe!

## Story 5: The Mexican Marigold in India

The cheerful orange and yellow **marigold** flowers that brighten every Indian wedding, temple doorway, and Diwali celebration actually have roots in a faraway land -- **Mexico**!

- In Mexico, marigolds hold a special place in festivals honouring ancestors
- The flower was carried across oceans by traders and explorers
- Indians loved its vivid, warm colours so much that the marigold became a beloved part of our own traditions

> A flower born in Mexico, adopted by India, and now inseparable from our celebrations -- a perfect example of how cultures enrich each other across distances!

## Story 6: Indian Cows in Brazil

Centuries ago, **Portuguese traders** carried a few Indian cows across the ocean to **Brazil** in South America. These cows were hardy, gave excellent milk, and thrived in Brazil's warm tropical climate -- which is similar to India's.

Today, more than **three-fourths of Brazil's milk supply** comes from three Indian cattle breeds:

| **Indian Breed** | **Originally From** |
|---|---|
| **Gir** | Gujarat |
| **Kankrej** | Gujarat |
| **Ongole** | Andhra Pradesh |

These Indian breeds became so valuable to Brazil that their images appear on Brazilian **postage stamps and coins**!

## Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam -- The World is One Family

Ancient Indian wisdom gave us a phrase that captures a powerful idea:

> **"Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam"** -- meaning **"The entire world is one family."**

Every person, every animal, every tree, every river, and even the wind and the sky -- all belong to this one great family. We live together on the same planet, breathe the same air, and depend on each other for survival.

**What this means in our daily lives:**
- When we treat everyone -- no matter where they come from -- with **kindness and respect**, we practise this ideal
- We must take care of the Earth because it supports **every form of life**, not just ours
- Protecting a forest in one part of the world helps clean the air for people thousands of kilometres away -- we are truly all connected

- **Your name**
- **House / Building number**
- **Street / Lane**
- **Village / Town / City**
- **District**
- **State / Union Territory**
- **Country: India**

> **Digital India:** **DIGIPIN** is a modern system that gives every tiny location in India a unique 10-character code -- like a digital address. It helps postmen, ambulance drivers, and delivery workers find even the smallest village quickly and accurately.

## Our Earth Needs Care

Earth is the **only planet** we know of that supports life. For thousands of years, it has given us air to breathe, water to drink, soil to grow food, and shelter for every living creature. But our planet needs looking after too.

**Simple ways each of us can care for the Earth:**
- **Conserve water** -- every drop saved matters
- **Plant trees** -- they purify the air, cool the land, and give shelter to birds and animals
- **Cut down on waste** -- reuse bags, bottles, and containers instead of throwing them away
- **Choose clean energy** -- solar, wind, and water power instead of burning coal and oil
- **Respect every living creature** -- animals, birds, insects, and plants are all part of our family

> The Earth is not merely our home -- it is a **precious gift** that we hold in trust for every generation that will come after us.

> When we take care of the Earth, we are taking care of each other -- because we are **all members of the same family**.
