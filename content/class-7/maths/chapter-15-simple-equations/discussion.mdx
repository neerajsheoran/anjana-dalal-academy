## Expressions and Equations: What is the Difference?

**Child:** Maa, what is the difference between an expression and an equation?

**Mother:** An expression like 4x + 3 is just a mathematical phrase -- it has no equals sign and can take many values depending on x. An equation like 4x + 3 = 19 makes a definite claim: these two things are equal. That claim is either true or false depending on the value of x.

**Child:** So an expression is open-ended, but an equation has a specific answer?

**Mother:** Exactly. The answer -- the value that makes both sides equal -- is called the solution. Think of it like a weighing scale at a kirana shop. The shopkeeper puts dal on one side and keeps adding weights on the other until it balances. When it does, the weight of the dal is known.

**Child:** So an equation is like a balance scale, and solving it means finding what makes it level?

**Mother:** Perfect way to see it, beta.

## The Balance Principle

**Child:** Maa, how do I solve equations without guessing?

**Mother:** Use the balance principle. Whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other side to keep things equal. If x - 5 = 14, add 5 to both sides: x = 19. If y + 9 = 25, subtract 9 from both sides: y = 16.

**Child:** And for multiplication and division?

**Mother:** If 4x = 28, divide both sides by 4: x = 7. If n/6 = 5, multiply both sides by 6: n = 30. The trick is to undo whatever operation has been applied to the unknown. Subtraction undoes addition, division undoes multiplication, and vice versa.

**Child:** It is like peeling the wrapping off a gift to see what is inside!

**Mother:** Lovely analogy! Each step removes one layer of operations until the unknown stands on its own. And always check your answer by putting it back into the original equation. If 4 times 7 equals 28, you know x = 7 is right.

## Solving Two-Step Equations

**Child:** What about equations like 4x - 7 = 21? That has two operations on x.

**Mother:** Two steps, beta. First, undo the subtraction: add 7 to both sides, giving 4x = 28. Second, undo the multiplication: divide by 4, giving x = 7. Check: 4 times 7 minus 7 = 21. Correct!

**Child:** Let me try one. 2m + 9 = 31. Subtract 9: 2m = 22. Divide by 2: m = 11. Check: 2 times 11 + 9 = 31. Yes!

**Mother:** Excellent. The pattern is always the same: first handle the addition or subtraction, then handle the multiplication or division. It is like a recipe -- follow the same steps every time and you will always reach the answer.

**Child:** Maa, is there a name for this kind of step-by-step method?

**Mother:** Mathematicians call it an algorithm -- a reliable procedure that produces the correct result every time it is followed properly. The word itself comes from the name of al-Khwarizmi, a great mathematician from the 9th century.

## Unknown on Both Sides

**Child:** Maa, what do I do when the unknown appears on both sides, like 4x + 3 = 2x + 15?

**Mother:** Gather all the x terms on one side. Subtract 2x from both sides: 2x + 3 = 15. Then subtract 3: 2x = 12. Divide by 2: x = 6. Check: left side is 4 times 6 + 3 = 27. Right side is 2 times 6 + 15 = 27. Equal!

**Child:** Let me try 6n - 5 = 4n + 9.

**Mother:** Go ahead.

**Child:** Subtract 4n: 2n - 5 = 9. Add 5: 2n = 14. Divide by 2: n = 7. Check: 6 times 7 minus 5 = 37, and 4 times 7 plus 9 = 37. Both sides match!

**Mother:** Beautifully done. The strategy never changes: unknowns to one side, constants to the other, then simplify. This same approach will carry you through much harder equations in higher classes.

## Cracking Word Problems

**Child:** Maa, word problems confuse me. How do I turn words into an equation?

**Mother:** Let us practise together. Deepa is 6 years older than three times Arjun's age. If Deepa is 21, how old is Arjun?

**Child:** Let Arjun's age be a. "Three times Arjun's age" is 3a. "6 years older" means add 6. So Deepa's age is 3a + 6 = 21.

**Mother:** Now solve it.

**Child:** 3a + 6 = 21. Subtract 6: 3a = 15. Divide by 3: a = 5. Arjun is 5 years old!

**Mother:** Perfect. Here is another. The sum of three consecutive numbers is 54. Find them.

**Child:** Let the smallest be x. The numbers are x, x + 1, x + 2. Adding them: x + x + 1 + x + 2 = 54. That gives 3x + 3 = 54. So 3x = 51, and x = 17. The numbers are 17, 18, and 19!

**Mother:** The word "algebra" comes from Arabic, from a book by al-Khwarizmi. But Indian mathematicians like Brahmagupta and Aryabhata were solving equations centuries before that. Every time you set up an equation and solve it, you are walking in the footsteps of mathematical giants.
