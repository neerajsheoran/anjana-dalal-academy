## Key Concepts

- **Decimals** express values between whole numbers using place value extended to the right of the ones place
- **Tenths** (1/10 = 0.1): one whole split into 10 equal parts; 10 tenths = 1 whole
- **Hundredths** (1/100 = 0.01): one tenth split into 10 parts; 100 hundredths = 1 whole; 10 hundredths = 1 tenth
- The **decimal point** separates the whole-number part from the fractional part
- Place values extend rightward: Units . Tenths Hundredths Thousandths -- each position is **1/10** of the one to its left
- Adding and subtracting decimals: **line up the decimal points** and work column by column
- **Carrying** in decimals works the same as whole numbers: 10 hundredths carry into 1 tenth, 10 tenths carry into 1 unit
- **Borrowing** also works the same: borrow 1 unit = 10 tenths, borrow 1 tenth = 10 hundredths
- **Equivalent decimals:** adding trailing zeros after the last decimal digit does not change the value (4.2 = 4.20 = 4.200)
- Zeros **between** the decimal point and a digit **do** change value: 4.2 is NOT the same as 4.02
- To compare decimals: compare whole parts first, then tenths, then hundredths, and so on
- Reading 5.3: "five point three" or "five and three-tenths"
- Reading 0.07: "zero point zero seven" or "seven hundredths"

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Decimal | A number using a decimal point to express values less than one |
| Decimal point | The dot (.) separating whole-number part from fractional part |
| Tenth | One of 10 equal parts of a unit (1/10 = 0.1) |
| Hundredth | One of 100 equal parts of a unit (1/100 = 0.01) |
| Thousandth | One of 1000 equal parts of a unit (1/1000 = 0.001) |
| Place value | The worth of a digit based on its position |
| Equivalent decimals | Decimals representing the same value (e.g., 0.6 and 0.60) |

## Remember These

- **4.2 and 4.02 are different numbers** -- zeros between the decimal point and a digit matter
- **4.2 and 4.20 are the same** -- trailing zeros after the last decimal digit do not change value
- When adding/subtracting, always **align decimal points vertically** before computing
- The word "decimal" comes from Latin "decem" (ten), connected to Sanskrit "dasha"
