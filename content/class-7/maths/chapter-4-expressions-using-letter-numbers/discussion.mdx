## Letters as Stand-ins for Numbers

**Child:** Maa, why do we suddenly have letters in maths? Letters are for Hindi and English, not for equations!

**Mother:** Think of it this way, beta. Meera is 5 years younger than her brother Arjun. When Arjun is 12, Meera is 7. When Arjun is 18, Meera is 13. Instead of listing every possibility, we simply write m = a - 5, where "a" stands for Arjun's age and "m" for Meera's.

**Child:** So the letter is like a blank space that can be filled with any number?

**Mother:** Exactly! We call them variables or letter-numbers. They capture a general rule in one compact line. For instance, Rajesh uncle sells samosas at Rs 15 each and chai at Rs 12 per cup. If he sells **s** samosas and **c** cups of chai, his earnings are 15s + 12c.

**Child:** And to find the actual amount, I just plug in the numbers?

**Mother:** Yes. For 20 samosas and 35 cups: 15 times 20 plus 12 times 35 = 300 + 420 = Rs 720. One formula, infinite possibilities — that is the magic of algebra.

## Simplification and Like Terms

**Child:** Maa, the perimeter of a rectangle is l + w + l + w. Can I write this more neatly?

**Mother:** Of course! Collect the like terms. l + l = 2l, and w + w = 2w. So the perimeter is 2l + 2w. That is the simplified version.

**Child:** What makes terms "like" or "unlike"?

**Mother:** Like terms involve the same letter-number. So 8m, 2m, and 15m are all like terms — they all have m. You combine them: 8m + 2m + 15m = 25m. But 8m and 4n are unlike terms and cannot be merged. It is like mixing rice and dal — 5 cups of rice plus 3 cups of dal does not become 8 cups of anything. It stays as 5 cups rice and 3 cups dal.

**Child:** So 5h + 3h = 8h, but 5h + 3k stays as 5h + 3k?

**Mother:** Perfectly said! And the reason 5h + 3h equals 8h is actually the distributive property: (5 + 3) times h = 8h. It connects back to the arithmetic we learned earlier.

## Brackets and Sign Changes

**Child:** Maa, a dhaba charges 50p + 30d for p plates and d drinks, and refunds 5p + 3d when you leave. What is the actual cost?

**Mother:** The net cost is (50p + 30d) - (5p + 3d). When you remove brackets after a minus sign, every sign inside flips. So it becomes 50p + 30d - 5p - 3d.

**Child:** Then I group like terms: 50p - 5p = 45p, and 30d - 3d = 27d. The answer is 45p + 27d?

**Mother:** Spot on! This is one of the most important algebra rules — minus outside the brackets means flip every sign inside. Plus outside means no changes. In exams, careless sign mistakes are the number one reason students lose marks.

**Child:** I will always double-check: minus outside, flip inside!

**Mother:** Good habit, beta. One wrong sign can ruin an entire answer.

## Expressions That Look Similar But Are Not

**Child:** Maa, are 4t and 4 + t the same? They both have 4 and t.

**Mother:** Let us check with a number. When t = 3, 4t = 12 but 4 + t = 7. When t = 6, 4t = 24 but 4 + t = 10. Completely different!

**Child:** So 4t means 4 multiplied by t, and 4 + t means 4 added to t. They have the same ingredients but the operation is different.

**Mother:** Right. Another common confusion: 8y - 5 versus 8(y - 5). The first means "5 less than 8 times y." The second means "8 times the quantity 5 less than y." When y = 10, the first gives 75 and the second gives 40.

**Child:** How do I avoid mixing them up?

**Mother:** Substitute a test value and check. If two expressions give different numbers for the same variable value, they are different expressions. This is also a great exam technique — if you are unsure about a simplification, plug in a simple number like 2 or 3 and verify.

## The Distributive Property with Variables

**Child:** Maa, Ritu scored 5a - 2b, 9a - 3b, and 4a - b in three quiz rounds. What is her total?

**Mother:** Add them all: (5a - 2b) + (9a - 3b) + (4a - b). Since the plus sign precedes each bracket, signs remain unchanged. Now group: 5a + 9a + 4a = 18a, and -2b - 3b - b = -6b. Total is 18a - 6b.

**Child:** If correct answers score 5 marks and wrong ones lose 2 marks, that is 18 times 5 minus 6 times 2 = 90 - 12 = 78!

**Mother:** Wonderful! Now Ritu's friend Kavya scored 20a - 4b. Who did better?

**Child:** Let me find the difference: 20a - 4b - (18a - 6b) = 20a - 4b - 18a + 6b = 2a + 2b. Since a and b are both positive, 2a + 2b is always positive. Kavya always scores more!

**Mother:** Brilliant reasoning! You just used algebra to prove something that is true no matter what the actual values of a and b are. That is the real power of algebra — it does not just compute answers, it proves general truths.
