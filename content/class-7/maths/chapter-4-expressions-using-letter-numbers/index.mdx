In everyday conversations, we often describe number relationships without knowing the exact numbers: "Meera is 5 years younger than her brother" or "the perimeter of a square is 4 times its side." In this chapter, we learn to write such relationships in a compact and powerful way using **letter-numbers** (variables) and **algebraic expressions**.

## The Idea of Letter-Numbers

### Letters Standing In for Numbers

Meera is 5 years younger than her brother Arjun. Instead of writing "Arjun's age" over and over, we pick a letter:

Let **a** = Arjun's age, and **m** = Meera's age.

Then the relationship is simply:

**m = a - 5**

This is an **algebraic expression**. The letters **a** and **m** are **letter-numbers** — they represent quantities that can change.

| Arjun's age (a) | Expression for Meera's age | Meera's age (m) |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 12 | 12 - 5 | 7 |
| 18 | 18 - 5 | 13 |
| 30 | 30 - 5 | 25 |

Going the other way, if we know Meera's age, we can find Arjun's: **a = m + 5**

If Meera is 15, then Arjun is 15 + 5 = **20 years old**.

### Patterns with Matchsticks

Deepa is creating triangles using matchsticks. Each triangle requires 3 matchsticks.

- 4 triangles need 4 x 3 = 12 matchsticks
- 9 triangles need 9 x 3 = 27 matchsticks
- 50 triangles need 50 x 3 = 150 matchsticks

The general rule: **Number of matchsticks = 3 x n**, where **n** is the number of triangles.

This expression **3 x n** (written as **3n**) works for any number of triangles.

### Expressions with More Than One Variable

Rajesh sells samosas at Rs 15 each and chai at Rs 12 per cup at his stall. If he sells **s** samosas and **c** cups of chai:

**Total earnings = s x 15 + c x 12 = 15s + 12c**

For 20 samosas and 35 cups of chai:
Total = 15 x 20 + 12 x 35 = 300 + 420 = **Rs 720**

> **The Big Idea:** Algebraic expressions let us capture general relationships in one neat formula. Such expressions are often called **formulas**.

### Perimeter Formulas

| Shape | Formula |
|-------|---------|
| Square with side **s** | Perimeter = **4s** |
| Equilateral triangle with side **t** | Perimeter = **3t** |
| Regular pentagon with side **p** | Perimeter = **5p** |
| Regular hexagon with side **h** | Perimeter = **6h** |

## Dropping the Multiplication Sign

In algebra, we simplify how we write multiplication:

- **3 x n** is written as **3n**
- **7 x k** is written as **7k**
- **s x 15** is written as **15s** (number always goes first)

We simply drop the multiplication symbol to make expressions shorter and cleaner.

**Example:** Find the value of **9w** when w = 3.
9w = 9 x 3 = **27**

**Example:** Find the value of **4d + 7** when d = 5.
4d + 7 = 4 x 5 + 7 = 20 + 7 = **27**

> **Watch Out for These Traps:**
> - If b = -3, then 8 - b = 8 - (-3) = **11**, not 5
> - If g = 5, then 2g = 2 x 5 = **10**, not 25
> - If r = 6, then 5r - 4 = 5 x 6 - 4 = 30 - 4 = **26**, not 22

## Simplifying Algebraic Expressions

### The Rectangle's Perimeter

The perimeter of a rectangle with length **l** and width **w**:

p = l + w + l + w

Since addition lets us rearrange:

p = l + l + w + w = **2l + 2w**

The expressions l + w + l + w and 2l + 2w are **equal** — they produce the same result for any l and w. We say 2l + 2w is the **simplified form**.

### Like Terms and Unlike Terms

**Like terms** share the **same** letter-number:
- 8m, 2m, and 15m are like terms (all involve m)
- 7x and -3x are like terms (both involve x)

**Unlike terms** have **different** letter-numbers:
- 9p and 4q are unlike terms

**Rule:** Like terms can be merged into one. Unlike terms cannot.

**Example:** 8m + 2m + 15m = (8 + 2 + 15)m = **25m** (using the distributive property)

### Visualising Like Terms with Area

A rectangular plot is divided into two smaller plots, both with height **h**, and widths 5 and 3.

- Combined area of smaller plots: 5h + 3h
- Area of the whole plot: (5 + 3) x h = 8h

So: **5h + 3h = 8h** — a visual demonstration of combining like terms!

For subtraction: **15k - 6k = (15 - 6)k = 9k**

### Simplifying with Brackets

**Example:** A dhaba charges Rs 50 per plate and Rs 30 per drink. A deposit of Rs 5 per plate and Rs 3 per drink is returned when the customer leaves.

For **p** plates and **d** drinks:
- Amount charged: 50p + 30d
- Deposit returned: 5p + 3d
- Net cost = (50p + 30d) - (5p + 3d)

Simplifying:
1. Open the bracket (preceded by minus, so signs flip): 50p + 30d - 5p - 3d
2. Group like terms: (50p - 5p) + (30d - 3d)
3. Combine: **(50 - 5)p + (30 - 3)d = 45p + 27d**

### Adding Scores Across Rounds

Ritu's scores in three rounds of a quiz: 5a - 2b, 9a - 3b, and 4a - b, where **a** is the reward for a correct answer and **b** is the penalty for a wrong one.

Her total score:
(5a - 2b) + (9a - 3b) + (4a - b)

= 5a + 9a + 4a - 2b - 3b - b

= **(5 + 9 + 4)a - (2 + 3 + 1)b**

= **18a - 6b**

If a = 5 and b = 2: Total = 18 x 5 - 6 x 2 = 90 - 12 = **78**

### Who Scored More?

Her friend Kavya scored **20a - 4b** in total. The difference:

20a - 4b - (18a - 6b) = 20a - 4b - 18a + 6b = **2a + 2b**

Since both a and b are positive, Kavya always scores higher!

## The Distributive Property with Variables

The distributive property applies to variables just as it does to plain numbers:

**Example:** Simplify 3(p + q) - q

= 3p + 3q - q = 3p + (3 - 1)q = **3p + 2q**

## When Are Two Expressions Equal?

Not all similar-looking expressions are equal. Always verify by plugging in values.

**Are 4t and 4 + t equal?**

| t | 4t | 4 + t |
|---|-----|-------|
| 3 | 12 | 7 |
| 6 | 24 | 10 |
| 10 | 40 | 14 |

Clearly, **4t and 4 + t are different expressions** — they yield different values for the same t.

**Are 8y - 5 and 8(y - 5) equal?**

| y | 8y - 5 | 8(y - 5) |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 1 | 3 | -32 |
| 5 | 35 | 0 |
| 8 | 59 | 24 |
| 10 | 75 | 40 |

They are not equal. 8y - 5 means "5 less than 8 times y," while 8(y - 5) means "8 times the quantity 5 less than y."

## Grid Patterns and Sums

When values are arranged in a grid, there are multiple paths to find the total. Consider:

```
  5   5   5   5
  a   b   a   b
  a   b   a   b
  5   5   5   5
```

**Row-wise:** (4 x 5) + (a + b) + (a + b) + (4 x 5)
**By grouping:** (8 x 5) + (a + a) + (b + b)
**Using symmetry:** 2 x (4 x 5 + a + b)

All three simplify to: **2a + 2b + 40**

## Summary of Simplification Rules

1. **Like terms** combine: 8x + 3x = 11x
2. **Unlike terms** stay separate: 8x + 3y remains 8x + 3y
3. **Plus sign before brackets:** signs inside stay: +(4a - 2b) = 4a - 2b
4. **Minus sign before brackets:** all signs inside flip: -(4a - 2b) = -4a + 2b
5. **Distributive property:** 3(x + y) = 3x + 3y

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Letter-number (Variable)** | A letter representing a number that can take different values |
| **Algebraic expression** | A mathematical expression built with letter-numbers, numbers, and operations |
| **Formula** | An algebraic expression describing a general mathematical relationship |
| **Like terms** | Terms containing the same letter-numbers (e.g., 8x and 3x) |
| **Unlike terms** | Terms containing different letter-numbers (e.g., 8x and 3y) |
| **Simplification** | Merging like terms to write an expression more compactly |
| **Distributive property** | Multiplying by a sum equals the sum of individual products: a(b + c) = ab + ac |
