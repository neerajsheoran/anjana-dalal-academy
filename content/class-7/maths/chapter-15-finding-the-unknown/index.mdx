In previous chapters, we used letters to stand for numbers in expressions and formulas. Now comes the exciting part -- using them to **discover unknown values**. When we set two quantities equal to each other, we create an **equation**, and solving that equation reveals what the unknown must be. This is one of the most powerful ideas in all of mathematics.

## The Idea of an Unknown

### A Weighing Scale Analogy

Picture a traditional balance scale at a kirana shop. The shopkeeper places a bag of lentils on one side and keeps adding known weights on the other until the two sides are perfectly level. The moment they balance, the total of the known weights equals the weight of the lentils.

**Example:** A packet of spices balances with a 1 kg weight and a 250 g weight. So the packet weighs 1 kg 250 g = **1.25 kg**.

This is the fundamental concept behind every equation: two sides that are **equal**.

### Rangoli Dots and Unknowns

Suppose Kavita arranges dots in a pattern of connected squares:

- 1 square needs 4 dots
- 2 squares (sharing a side) need 7 dots
- 3 squares need 10 dots
- **n** squares need **3n + 1** dots

Now suppose she asks: **How many squares can I draw with exactly 76 dots?**

We need to find the value of **n** such that:

**3n + 1 = 76**

This is an **equation** -- a statement that two expressions are equal. Solving it reveals the unknown.

## What Exactly is an Equation?

An **equation** is a mathematical sentence that declares two expressions are **equal**. It always features the **equals sign (=)**.

Every equation has two halves:
- **Left-Hand Side (LHS)** -- the expression to the left of the equals sign
- **Right-Hand Side (RHS)** -- the expression to the right of the equals sign

**Examples of equations:**

| Equation | LHS | RHS |
|----------|-----|-----|
| 3n + 1 = 76 | 3n + 1 | 76 |
| 4x = 28 | 4x | 28 |
| y - 5 = 18 | y - 5 | 18 |
| 2m + 6 = 4m - 8 | 2m + 6 | 4m - 8 |

> **Important Distinction:** An **expression** like 3n + 1 has no equals sign -- it simply represents a quantity. An **equation** like 3n + 1 = 76 makes a claim that can be **true** or **false** depending on the value of n.

### Expressions vs Equations

| Feature | Expression | Equation |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Has an equals sign? | No | Yes |
| Example | 4x + 3 | 4x + 3 = 19 |
| Can be simplified? | Yes | Yes, and can be **solved** |
| Has a specific solution? | No (takes different values for different x) | Yes (a particular value makes it true) |

## First Approach: Trial and Error

The most straightforward way to solve an equation is to **test different values** until one works.

**Example:** Solve 3n + 1 = 76

| n | LHS = 3n + 1 | RHS | Equal? |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 10 | 31 | 76 | No (too small) |
| 20 | 61 | 76 | No (still small) |
| 25 | 76 | 76 | **Yes!** |

So **n = 25**. Kavita can draw 25 squares with 76 dots.

**Example:** Solve x + 6 = 14

Try x = 5: LHS = 5 + 6 = 11 (not 14)
Try x = 8: LHS = 8 + 6 = 14 = RHS

So **x = 8**.

> Trial and error gets the job done, but it can be painfully slow for tricky equations. We need a smarter approach.

## The Balance Principle

Think of an equation as a perfectly balanced seesaw. If you do the **same thing to both ends**, it stays balanced.

> **The Balance Principle:** If we add, subtract, multiply, or divide **both sides** of an equation by the **same number**, the equation remains true.

This principle gives us a systematic technique for solving any equation.

### Undoing Addition and Subtraction

**Example:** Solve x - 5 = 14

The unknown x has 5 taken away from it. To **undo** this, we **add 5** to both sides:

x - 5 + 5 = 14 + 5

x = **19**

**Check:** LHS = 19 - 5 = 14 = RHS. Correct!

**Example:** Solve y + 9 = 25

To undo the addition of 9, we **subtract 9** from both sides:

y + 9 - 9 = 25 - 9

y = **16**

**Check:** LHS = 16 + 9 = 25 = RHS. Correct!

### Undoing Multiplication and Division

**Example:** Solve 4x = 28

The unknown x is multiplied by 4. To undo this, **divide both sides** by 4:

4x / 4 = 28 / 4

x = **7**

**Check:** LHS = 4 x 7 = 28 = RHS. Correct!

**Example:** Solve n / 6 = 5

The unknown n is divided by 6. To undo this, **multiply both sides** by 6:

(n / 6) x 6 = 5 x 6

n = **30**

**Check:** LHS = 30 / 6 = 5 = RHS. Correct!

## Two-Step Equations

Many equations need **two operations** to solve -- first undo the addition or subtraction, then undo the multiplication or division.

**Example:** Solve 4x - 7 = 21

**Step 1:** Add 7 to both sides (undo the subtraction):

4x - 7 + 7 = 21 + 7

4x = 28

**Step 2:** Divide both sides by 4 (undo the multiplication):

4x / 4 = 28 / 4

x = **7**

**Check:** LHS = 4 x 7 - 7 = 28 - 7 = 21 = RHS. Correct!

**Example:** Solve 2m + 9 = 31

**Step 1:** Subtract 9 from both sides:

2m + 9 - 9 = 31 - 9

2m = 22

**Step 2:** Divide both sides by 2:

m = **11**

**Check:** LHS = 2 x 11 + 9 = 22 + 9 = 31 = RHS. Correct!

**Example:** Solve 3y + 1 = 76

**Step 1:** Subtract 1 from both sides:

3y = 75

**Step 2:** Divide both sides by 3:

y = **25**

## When the Unknown Appears on Both Sides

Sometimes the unknown shows up on **both** sides of the equation. The strategy: gather all terms with the unknown on one side.

**Example:** Solve 4x + 3 = 2x + 15

**Step 1:** Subtract 2x from both sides (collect x terms on one side):

4x - 2x + 3 = 2x - 2x + 15

2x + 3 = 15

**Step 2:** Subtract 3 from both sides:

2x = 12

**Step 3:** Divide by 2:

x = **6**

**Check:** LHS = 4 x 6 + 3 = 27. RHS = 2 x 6 + 15 = 27. Correct!

**Example:** Solve 6n - 5 = 4n + 9

**Step 1:** Subtract 4n from both sides:

2n - 5 = 9

**Step 2:** Add 5 to both sides:

2n = 14

**Step 3:** Divide both sides by 2:

n = **7**

**Check:** LHS = 6 x 7 - 5 = 37. RHS = 4 x 7 + 9 = 37. Correct!

## Turning Word Problems into Equations

The real strength of equations lies in cracking **word problems**. The crucial skill is **converting words into mathematical symbols**.

**Example:** Sunita thinks of a number. She triples it and adds 7. The result is 34. What is the number?

Let the number be **x**.

Equation: **3x + 7 = 34**

Solving: 3x = 27, so x = **9**

**Example:** A number is multiplied by 5, then 11 is subtracted. The result is 24. Find the number.

Let the number be **n**.

Equation: **5n - 11 = 24**

Solving: 5n = 35, so n = **7**

**Example:** The sum of three consecutive numbers is 54. Find the numbers.

Let the smallest number be **x**. The three consecutive numbers are x, x + 1, and x + 2.

Equation: x + (x + 1) + (x + 2) = 54

Simplify: 3x + 3 = 54

Solve: 3x = 51, so x = **17**

The three numbers are **17, 18, and 19**.

**Check:** 17 + 18 + 19 = 54. Correct!

**Example:** Deepa is 6 years older than three times Arjun's age. If Deepa is 21 years old, how old is Arjun?

Let Arjun's age be **a**.

Equation: **3a + 6 = 21**

Solving: 3a = 15, so a = **5**

Arjun is **5 years old**.

## Always Verify Your Answer

After solving any equation, **plug your answer back** into the original equation to make sure both sides match. This simple step catches careless errors and confirms your solution is correct.

> **Golden Rule:** Substitute the solution back into the **original** equation. If LHS = RHS, you are right.

## A Step-by-Step Solving Recipe

To solve an equation systematically:

1. **Simplify** both sides if needed (combine like terms, expand brackets)
2. **Shift** all terms containing the unknown to one side using addition or subtraction
3. **Shift** all constant terms to the other side
4. **Divide** (or multiply) to isolate the unknown
5. **Check** by substituting back into the original equation

> **Historical Note:** The word "algebra" comes from the Arabic title of a book by the mathematician **al-Khwarizmi** (around 820 CE), called *Al-Kitab al-Mukhtasar fi Hisab al-Jabr wal-Muqabala*. The term "al-jabr" means "completion" -- referring to the technique of moving terms from one side of an equation to the other. Indian mathematicians **Brahmagupta** (628 CE) and **Aryabhata** (499 CE) independently developed powerful methods for solving equations, including techniques for what we now call linear and quadratic equations.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Equation** | A mathematical statement declaring two expressions equal, using the equals sign |
| **LHS (Left-Hand Side)** | The expression on the left of the equals sign |
| **RHS (Right-Hand Side)** | The expression on the right of the equals sign |
| **Solution** | The value of the unknown that makes the equation true |
| **Balance principle** | Doing the same operation on both sides of an equation keeps it true |
| **Trial and error** | Testing different values of the unknown to find the solution |
| **Unknown (Variable)** | The letter whose value we need to discover |
| **Consecutive numbers** | Numbers that follow one after another in order, like 8, 9, 10 |
