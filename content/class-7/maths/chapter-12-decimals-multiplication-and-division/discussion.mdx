## How to Multiply Decimals

**Child:** Maa, Anita bought 7 metres of ribbon at Rs 8.5 per metre. How do I find the total cost?

**Mother:** Multiply 7 by 8.5. Here is a neat trick: forget the decimal, multiply as whole numbers -- 7 times 85 gives 595. Now count how many decimal places were in the original numbers. 8.5 has 1 decimal place. So put the decimal point to give 1 digit after it: 59.5. The total cost is Rs 59.5.

**Child:** What about two decimals multiplied together, like 15.4 times 8.5?

**Mother:** Same approach. 154 times 85 = 13,090. Total decimal places: 1 + 1 = 2. Place the decimal to get 130.90, which is 130.9. You can verify using fractions: 154/10 times 85/10 = 13090/100 = 130.9.

**Child:** So the rule is always: multiply as whole numbers, then count and place decimal points?

**Mother:** That is the golden rule. For 4.32 times 17.5: multiply 432 times 175 = 75,600. Total decimal places: 2 + 1 = 3. Result: 75.600 = 75.6. Once you master this shortcut, you will breeze through calculations.

## When Multiplication Shrinks Instead of Grows

**Child:** Maa, something strange happened. I multiplied 0.6 times 0.3 and got 0.18. But 0.18 is smaller than both numbers! I thought multiplication makes things bigger.

**Mother:** That is only true when you multiply by a number greater than 1. When you multiply by a number less than 1, you are taking a fraction of something -- a part of a part. 0.3 of 0.6 is less than 0.6, just the way three-tenths of six-tenths gives a tiny piece.

**Child:** So when both numbers are between 0 and 1, the product is smaller than both?

**Mother:** Yes. When both are greater than 1, the product is bigger than both. When one is greater and the other less than 1, the product lands between them. This understanding is your safety net.

**Child:** Like a sanity check! If I multiply 0.4 by 0.2 and get 8, I know something went wrong because 8 is way bigger than both.

**Mother:** Exactly. The correct answer is 0.08. These quick checks catch the most common exam mistake -- misplacing the decimal point.

## Dividing with Decimals

**Child:** Maa, how do I divide 82.5 by 3?

**Mother:** Turn it into a fraction: 825/10 divided by 3 gives 825/30. Long division: 825 divided by 30 = 27.5. Each person gets Rs 27.5.

**Child:** What if I am dividing by a decimal, like 9 divided by 0.5?

**Mother:** The key move is to make the divisor a whole number. Multiply both numbers by 10: 90 divided by 5 = 18. So you can cut 18 strips of 0.5 m from a 9 m rope.

**Child:** And for decimal divided by decimal?

**Mother:** Same idea. For 7.68 divided by 1.6, multiply both by 10: 76.8 divided by 16 = 4.8. Always eliminate the decimal from the divisor first, then divide as usual.

**Child:** Why does multiplying both by the same number work?

**Mother:** Because it does not change the quotient. Think of equivalent fractions: 6 divided by 3 is the same as 60 divided by 30 is the same as 600 divided by 300. All give 2. We are just scaling both numbers equally.

## The Power of Known Products

**Child:** Maa, if I know that 24 times 15 equals 360, can I quickly figure out 2.4 times 1.5?

**Mother:** Absolutely! 2.4 has 1 decimal place and 1.5 has 1 decimal place, so the product needs 2 decimal places. Take 360 and place the decimal to get 2 digits after the point: 3.60 = 3.6. So 2.4 times 1.5 = 3.6.

**Child:** And 0.24 times 0.15?

**Mother:** 2 + 2 = 4 decimal places. Place 360 with 4 digits after the decimal: 0.0360 = 0.036. One multiplication gives you a whole family of results.

**Child:** That is like a superpower for exams! Calculate once, use it five different ways.

**Mother:** Precisely. Whenever you spot decimal versions of the same multiplication, compute the whole-number version once and just shift the decimal point for each variation.

## Decimals in Daily Life

**Child:** Maa, where do we actually need decimal multiplication and division?

**Mother:** Think about your own life. A two-rupee coin is 1.75 mm thick. Stack 24 of them and you get 24 times 1.75 = 42 mm, which is 4.2 cm. Or when Nisha cycles 1.35 km to school and back, 5 days a week -- that is 1.35 times 2 times 5 = 13.5 km every week.

**Child:** What about when we go to the sabzi mandi?

**Mother:** If 3.5 kg of tomatoes costs Rs 42 per kg, the total is 3.5 times 42 = Rs 147. And at the stationery shop, if Rohan buys 6 pencils at Rs 12.75 each and 2 geometry boxes at Rs 45.50 each, the bill is 6 times 12.75 plus 2 times 45.50 = 76.50 + 91.00 = Rs 167.50.

**Child:** So every time we handle money, we are doing decimal arithmetic!

**Mother:** Every single time, beta. From the chai stall to the bank, decimals are the language of money. Learning to handle them confidently is not just a school skill -- it is a life skill.
