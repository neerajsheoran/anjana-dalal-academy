We already know how to add and subtract decimal numbers. Now it is time to tackle the next pair of operations -- **multiplying** and **dividing** decimals. These skills connect directly to what we know about fractions, and they show up constantly in everyday life -- from shopping bills to measuring ingredients for a recipe.

## A Quick Look at Decimals Again

The decimal number **34.72** stands for:
- 3 Tens + 4 Units + 7 Tenths + 2 Hundredths
- As a fraction: 34 + 7/10 + 2/100

### Dividing by Powers of 10

Dividing any number by 10, 100, or 1000 is as simple as **shifting the decimal point to the left** by the number of zeroes in the divisor.

| Division | Result | Rule |
|----------|--------|------|
| 456 / 10 | 45.6 | Shift 1 place left |
| 37 / 100 | 0.37 | Shift 2 places left |
| 891 / 1000 | 0.891 | Shift 3 places left |
| 23456 / 1000 | 23.456 | Shift 3 places left |
| 9 / 1000 | 0.009 | Shift 3 places left (add leading zeros) |

## Multiplying Decimals

### A Decimal Times a Whole Number

**Example:** Anita buys 7 metres of ribbon at Rs 8.5 per metre.

Total cost = 7 x 8.5

Using fractions: 7/1 x 85/10 = 595/10 = **59.5**

Or: 7 x 85 = 595, then place the decimal to get 1 digit after the point = **59.5**

### One Decimal Times Another Decimal

**Example:** A plot of land measures 15.4 m by 8.5 m. What is its area?

15.4 x 8.5 = 154/10 x 85/10 = 13090/100 = **130.90 = 130.9 sq m**

### The Shortcut for Decimal Multiplication

> **Step 1:** Pretend the decimal points do not exist. Multiply the numbers as if they were whole numbers.
>
> **Step 2:** Count the total number of decimal places in both original numbers combined.
>
> **Step 3:** In the product, place the decimal point so that it has exactly that many decimal places.

**Example:** 4.32 x 17.5

- As whole numbers: 432 x 175 = 75,600
- Count decimal places: 4.32 has 2, and 17.5 has 1, total = 3
- Place the decimal: **75.600 = 75.6**

**Example:** 6.3 x 2.48

- 63 x 248 = 15,624
- Decimal places: 1 + 2 = 3
- Result: **15.624**

### Does Multiplication Always Make Things Bigger?

With decimals, not necessarily! Multiplying by a number less than 1 actually **shrinks** the result:

| Situation | Example | Product | What happens |
|-----------|---------|---------|------------|
| Both > 1 | 2.5 x 4.8 | 12.0 | Product > both numbers |
| Both between 0 and 1 | 0.6 x 0.3 | 0.18 | Product &lt; both numbers |
| One > 1, one &lt; 1 | 0.8 x 6 | 4.8 | Product falls between the two |

> **Remember:** Multiplying by a number less than 1 gives a **smaller** answer, not a larger one.

## Dividing Decimals

### A Decimal Divided by a Whole Number

**Example:** Three friends share Rs 82.5 equally.

82.5 / 3 = (825/10) / 3 = 825 / 30

Using long division: 825 / 30 = 27.5

Each friend gets **Rs 27.5**

### A Whole Number Divided by a Decimal

**Example:** How many strips of 0.5 m can be cut from a 9 m rope?

9 / 0.5 = 9 / (5/10) = 9 x (10/5) = 90/5 = **18 strips**

### One Decimal Divided by Another Decimal

**Example:** 8.4 / 2.1

As fractions: (84/10) / (21/10) = 84/21 = **4**

Or: multiply both by 10 to remove decimals, then divide:
84 / 21 = **4**

### The Shortcut for Decimal Division

> **Technique:** Multiply both the dividend and the divisor by whatever power of 10 makes the **divisor** a whole number. Then divide normally.

**Example:** 7.68 / 1.6

Multiply both by 10: 76.8 / 16

Now divide: 76.8 / 16 = **4.8**

**Example:** 0.3528 / 0.42

Multiply both by 100: 35.28 / 42

Now divide: 35.28 / 42 = **0.84**

## Everyday Uses of Decimal Operations

**At the Stationery Shop:** Rohan buys 6 pencils at Rs 12.75 each and 2 geometry boxes at Rs 45.50 each.
Total = 6 x 12.75 + 2 x 45.50 = 76.50 + 91.00 = **Rs 167.50**

**Stacking Coins:** A two-rupee coin is 1.75 mm thick. A stack of 24 coins:
Height = 24 x 1.75 = 42.0 mm = **4.2 cm**

**Travelling to School:** Nisha cycles 1.35 km to school and back, 5 days a week:
Weekly distance = 1.35 x 2 x 5 = 1.35 x 10 = **13.5 km**

**Buying Vegetables:** If 3.5 kg of tomatoes costs Rs 42 per kg:
Total cost = 3.5 x 42 = **Rs 147**

## Trailing Zeros Do Not Change Value

Adding zeros at the end of the decimal part does not alter the number:
- 42.50 = 42.5
- 3.700 = 3.7

So 42.50 x 3.700 = 42.5 x 3.7, and both give the same product.

## Building a Family of Products

If you know that 24 x 15 = 360, you can derive many related results:

| Product | Working | Result |
|---------|---------|--------|
| 24 x 1.5 | 360 with 1 decimal place | **36.0** |
| 24 x 0.15 | 360 with 2 decimal places | **3.60** |
| 2.4 x 1.5 | 360 with 2 decimal places | **3.60** |
| 0.24 x 0.15 | 360 with 4 decimal places | **0.0360** |
| 2.4 x 15 | 360 with 1 decimal place | **36.0** |

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Decimal** | A number expressed using place value extended to tenths, hundredths, etc. |
| **Decimal places** | The number of digits after the decimal point |
| **Product of decimals** | Multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal point so total decimal places equals the sum of decimal places in the factors |
| **Division of decimals** | Make the divisor a whole number by multiplying both dividend and divisor by a suitable power of 10, then divide normally |
| **Equivalent decimals** | Decimals that represent the same value, like 3.5 and 3.50 |
