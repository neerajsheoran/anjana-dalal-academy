## Key Concepts

- Dividing by 10, 100, or 1000: **shift the decimal point left** by 1, 2, or 3 places respectively
- **Multiplying decimals shortcut:**
  1. Ignore decimal points and multiply as whole numbers
  2. Count total decimal places in both original numbers
  3. Place the decimal point in the product so it has that many decimal places
- **Decimal x whole number:** treat the decimal as a fraction, multiply, then place the decimal (e.g., 7 x 8.5 = 59.5)
- **Decimal x decimal:** multiply numerators and denominators as fractions, or use the shortcut above
- **Dividing decimals shortcut:** multiply both dividend and divisor by a power of 10 to make the **divisor** a whole number, then divide normally
- Multiplying by a number **less than 1** makes the result **smaller**
- Multiplying by a number **greater than 1** makes the result **larger**
- Both numbers between 0 and 1: product is **smaller** than either number
- **Trailing zeros** after the last decimal digit do not change value: 42.50 = 42.5
- If you know one product (e.g., 24 x 15 = 360), you can derive related decimal products by adjusting decimal places

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Decimal places | Number of digits after the decimal point |
| Product of decimals | Multiply as whole numbers, then place decimal point for correct total decimal places |
| Division of decimals | Make divisor a whole number by multiplying both by a power of 10, then divide |
| Equivalent decimals | Decimals with the same value (e.g., 3.5 and 3.50) |

## Remember These

- **Multiplying by a number less than 1 gives a smaller answer** -- this catches many students off guard
- When multiplying two decimals: total decimal places in the product = **sum of decimal places** in the two factors
- For division: make the **divisor** (not dividend) a whole number by multiplying both by 10, 100, etc.
- 0.6 x 0.3 = 0.18 (not 1.8) -- count decimal places carefully: 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places in the answer
