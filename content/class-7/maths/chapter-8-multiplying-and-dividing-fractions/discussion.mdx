## Multiplying with Fractions

**Child:** Maa, Sunita cycles 2 km in one hour. Her brother Arun rides his tricycle at only 1/3 km per hour. How far does Arun go in 5 hours?

**Mother:** Think of it as repeating 1/3 five times: 1/3 plus 1/3 plus 1/3 plus 1/3 plus 1/3 equals 5/3 km, which is 1 and 2/3 km. The rule is simple — multiply the whole number by the numerator and keep the denominator.

**Child:** What if I need to multiply two fractions together, Maa? Like 1/2 times 1/3?

**Mother:** Picture a chapati, beta. Cut it into 3 equal pieces — each piece is 1/3 of the chapati. Now take one piece and cut it in half. You have split the whole chapati into 6 pieces, and your small piece is 1/6 of the whole. So 1/2 times 1/3 equals 1/6.

**Child:** And the general rule for any two fractions?

**Mother:** Multiply the numerators together for the new numerator, and the denominators together for the new denominator. For 3/4 times 2/5, you get (3 times 2) over (4 times 5) which is 6/20, and that simplifies to 3/10. A good shortcut is to cancel common factors before multiplying.

## Understanding "Fraction Of"

**Child:** Maa, when my teacher says "find 3/4 of 80," what does "of" mean?

**Mother:** "Of" simply means multiply! So 3/4 of 80 is 3/4 times 80 equals 240 divided by 4 equals 60.

**Child:** A colony has 480 families and 3/8 of them subscribe to a newspaper. So the subscribers are 3/8 of 480?

**Mother:** Yes. 3/8 times 480 equals 1440 divided by 8 equals 180 families. Notice that 180 is less than 480. When you multiply by a proper fraction — one that is less than 1 — the answer is always smaller than what you started with.

**Child:** That makes sense. Taking a fraction of something means taking a part, so the result should be smaller.

**Mother:** Exactly. But multiplying by an improper fraction — one greater than 1 — makes the number grow. For instance, 5 times 7/4 equals 35/4, which is 8 and 3/4. Bigger than 5! Knowing this helps you check whether your answers make sense.

## Dividing by Fractions

**Child:** Maa, how do I divide by a fraction? Like, how many glasses of 1/4 litre can I fill from a 2-litre bottle?

**Mother:** Think practically, beta. Each litre fills 4 quarter-litre glasses. Two litres fill 2 times 4 equals 8 glasses. Mathematically, 2 divided by 1/4 equals 2 times 4 equals 8.

**Child:** So dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal?

**Mother:** Exactly right. The reciprocal of a/b is b/a — you just flip it. Dividing by 1/4 is the same as multiplying by 4. The general rule is: a/b divided by c/d equals a/b times d/c.

**Child:** Let me try: 5/6 divided by 1/6. That is 5/6 times 6/1 equals 30/6 equals 5. So I can cut 5 pieces of 1/6 metre from a 5/6 metre ribbon!

**Mother:** Perfect! And remember — any number times its reciprocal equals 1. For example, 2/5 times 5/2 equals 10/10 equals 1. This is the property that makes the division rule work.

## Mixed Numbers and Everyday Problems

**Child:** Maa, how do I multiply 1 and 2/3 by 2 and 1/4?

**Mother:** First convert both to improper fractions. 1 and 2/3 equals 5/3, and 2 and 1/4 equals 9/4. Now multiply: 5/3 times 9/4 equals 45/12, which simplifies to 15/4, or 3 and 3/4.

**Child:** And for word problems?

**Mother:** Here is one from everyday life. An auto-rickshaw covers 31 and 1/2 km on 4 and 1/2 litres of CNG. What is its mileage?

**Child:** Mileage equals distance divided by fuel. So 63/2 divided by 9/2 equals 63/2 times 2/9 equals 126/18 equals 7 km per litre.

**Mother:** Excellent! See how the 2 in the numerator and denominator cancelled? Always look for opportunities to simplify — it saves time and avoids mistakes.

## Why the Rules Make Sense

**Child:** Maa, the fraction rules feel like they just appeared from nowhere. Why does "multiply numerators and denominators" actually work?

**Mother:** Let me show you with a paper square. To find 3/4 times 2/5, fold the square into 5 vertical strips and colour 2 of them. Then fold into 4 horizontal rows and colour 3 of them. The overlapping coloured region covers 6 small rectangles out of 20 total — that is 6/20, which is 3/10.

**Child:** So the denominator tells me the total small pieces, and the numerator tells me how many are in the overlap?

**Mother:** Exactly, beta. Total pieces = 4 times 5 = 20, and overlapping pieces = 3 times 2 = 6. This visual proof shows why the rule works. The division rule works similarly — dividing by c/d asks "how many groups of c/d fit into a/b?" and that turns into multiplying by d/c.

**Child:** Understanding why the rules work makes them much easier to remember, Maa.

**Mother:** That is the difference between memorising and truly learning. When you understand the reason, the rule stays with you forever.
