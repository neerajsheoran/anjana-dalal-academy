## Why We Need Decimals

**Child:** Maa, I was measuring a ribbon for the art project. It is longer than 7 cm but shorter than 8 cm. How do I write that?

**Mother:** That is exactly why we have decimals, beta! When whole numbers are not enough, we go "beyond the point." If you divide the gap between 7 and 8 into 10 equal parts, each part is one-tenth of a centimetre. If the ribbon reaches the 4th mark past 7, its length is 7.4 cm.

**Child:** So we chop the space between two whole numbers into 10 tiny pieces?

**Mother:** Yes, because our number system runs on the power of 10. And when even tenths are not precise enough, we split each tenth into 10 more parts to get hundredths. Think about Neeraj Chopra's javelin throw — it was measured as 87.58 metres. That precision comes from decimals.

**Child:** The word "decimal" must have something to do with ten?

**Mother:** Exactly! It comes from the Latin "decem" meaning ten, which shares a root with the Sanskrit "dasha." In Hindi we say "das," in Marathi "daha" — the connection runs through many of our languages.

## Adding and Subtracting Decimals

**Child:** Maa, Anu's dupatta has two pieces — one is 3.5 units long and the other is 2.8 units. How do I add them?

**Mother:** Add the units and tenths separately. Units: 3 + 2 = 5. Tenths: 5 + 8 = 13 tenths, which is 1 unit and 3 tenths. So the total is 5 + 1.3 = 6.3 units.

**Child:** And what about subtraction? Like 14.2 minus 8.5?

**Mother:** You cannot take 5 tenths from 2 tenths directly. So you borrow, just like in whole-number subtraction. Take 1 unit from 14, making it 13 units and 12 tenths. Now 12 minus 5 is 7 tenths, and 13 minus 8 is 5 units. The answer is 5.7.

**Child:** So decimal arithmetic follows the same rules as regular arithmetic — just with a decimal point keeping things organized?

**Mother:** Precisely! Line up the decimal points, work column by column, and carry or borrow as needed. The beauty of our place value system is that the same rules work whether you are dealing with hundreds or hundredths.

## The Decimal Point Changes Everything

**Child:** Maa, are 409, 40.9, and 4.09 related? They all use 4, 0, and 9.

**Mother:** They share the same digits but are very different numbers! 409 means 4 hundreds and 9 ones. 40.9 means 4 tens and 9 tenths. 4.09 means 4 units and 9 hundredths. Moving the decimal point completely changes the value of each digit.

**Child:** It is like how the same spices in different proportions make completely different dishes!

**Mother:** What a delicious analogy! Now, quick question — is 4.2 the same as 4.20?

**Child:** I think so... adding a zero at the end after the decimal should not change anything?

**Mother:** Correct! 4.2, 4.20, and 4.200 are all the same number. These are called equivalent decimals. But 4.2 is NOT the same as 4.02. That zero sits between the decimal point and the 2, pushing the 2 from the tenths place into the hundredths place. A small shift, but a huge difference in value!

## Comparing Decimals

**Child:** Which is bigger — 6.35 or 6.9? I feel like 35 is more than 9, so maybe 6.35?

**Mother:** That is a very common trap! You must compare place by place, starting from the left. Both have 6 units — so far equal. Now compare tenths: 3 tenths versus 9 tenths. Since 3 is less than 9, we know 6.35 is less than 6.9. We do not even need to look at hundredths.

**Child:** So I should never compare the digits after the decimal point as though they form a whole number?

**Mother:** Never! Always go place by place from left to right. Think of it like a cricket match — you compare runs in the first innings, then the second. You do not mash all the digits together. So 4.8 is greater than 4.08 because 8 tenths beats 0 tenths.

**Child:** Got it! Left to right, one place at a time.

**Mother:** That is the way. And this systematic approach is exactly what your exams will test, so practise it well.

## Decimals in Our Daily Life

**Child:** Where do I actually use decimals outside school, Maa?

**Mother:** Everywhere, beta! When I check the weather forecast — "Delhi will be 12.5 degrees tomorrow." When Papa fills petrol — the price is Rs 105.72 per litre. When you timed your race on Sports Day — the winner finished in 13.42 seconds.

**Child:** And in cricket! Virat Kohli's batting average is always a decimal, right?

**Mother:** Yes! An average of 49.35 means across all his innings, he scored about 49.35 runs per innings. That single decimal number summarises hundreds of matches. Even our currency uses decimals — when I pay Rs 325.50 for atta, the 50 paise is 0.50 of a rupee.

**Child:** So decimals are about being more precise than whole numbers allow?

**Mother:** Exactly. Whether you grow up to become a doctor measuring doses, an architect measuring walls, or even a homemaker measuring fabric — decimals will be your constant companion. Mastering them now means you will be comfortable with precision for the rest of your life.
