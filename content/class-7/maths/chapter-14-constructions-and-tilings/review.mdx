## Key Concepts

- The **perpendicular bisector** of a line segment passes through its midpoint at **90 degrees**
- Every point on the perpendicular bisector is **equally distant** from the two endpoints
- To construct a perpendicular bisector: draw arcs from both endpoints with equal radius (> half the segment); join the two intersection points
- An **angle bisector** divides an angle into two **equal halves**
- To bisect an angle: arc from vertex, then equal arcs from the two intersection points, join vertex to the new intersection
- Constructions are proved correct using **congruence** (SSS or SAS)
- **Standard angle constructions:**
  - **90 degrees:** perpendicular at a point on a line
  - **60 degrees:** equilateral triangle construction (compass radius = arc from base point)
  - **45 degrees:** bisect a 90-degree angle
  - **30 degrees:** bisect a 60-degree angle
  - **120 degrees:** two 60-degree angles side by side
- A **tiling (tessellation)** covers a flat surface with no gaps and no overlaps
- Only **3 regular polygons** tile a plane by themselves: equilateral triangle (60°), square (90°), regular hexagon (120°)
- For tiling, interior angles at each meeting point must sum to exactly **360 degrees**
- Different regular polygons can combine to tile: e.g., octagons + squares (135 + 135 + 90 = 360)
- A regular hexagon inscribed in a circle has side = **radius of the circle**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Perpendicular bisector | Line through the midpoint of a segment at right angles |
| Bisect | To divide into two equal parts |
| Angle bisector | A ray splitting an angle into two equal angles |
| Compass | Tool for drawing circles and arcs |
| Tiling (Tessellation) | Gap-free, overlap-free arrangement of shapes covering a surface |
| Regular polygon | A polygon with all sides and all angles equal |
| Interior angle | The angle formed inside a polygon at each vertex |
| Shulba-Sutras | Ancient Indian geometry texts describing construction methods |

## Remember These

- For perpendicular bisector construction, the compass radius must be **more than half** the segment length
- Only **equilateral triangles, squares, and regular hexagons** can tile a plane alone -- pentagons and octagons cannot
- Octagons tile only when combined with **squares** (135 + 135 + 90 = 360)
- The side of a regular hexagon inscribed in a circle **equals the radius** -- that is why stepping the compass around a circle lands exactly 6 times
