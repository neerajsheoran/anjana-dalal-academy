Every time you calculate the total bill at a shop or figure out how much pocket money you have left after buying a treat, you are working with arithmetic expressions. In this chapter, we will learn how to write, read, and evaluate these expressions correctly — making sure there is never any confusion about the answer.

## What Is an Arithmetic Expression?

An **arithmetic expression** is a mathematical phrase built from numbers and operations (+, -, x, /). Here are some everyday examples:

- 18 + 7 (read as "18 plus 7" or "the sum of 18 and 7")
- 35 - 9 (read as "35 minus 9" or "the difference of 35 and 9")
- 8 x 6 (read as "8 times 6" or "the product of 8 and 6")
- 42 / 7 (read as "42 divided by 7" or "the quotient of 42 and 7")

Every expression produces a single number called its **value**. The value of 18 + 7 is **25**, and we write **18 + 7 = 25**.

### Many Roads to the Same Destination

Different expressions can share the same value. All of these equal 20:

- 15 + 5
- 25 - 5
- 4 x 5
- 60 / 3

### Comparing Expressions Without Calculating

We can compare expressions using =, < and > based on their values:

- 14 + 3 **>** 9 + 5 (because 17 > 14)
- 20 - 6 **<** 3 x 5 (because 14 < 15)

> **Quick Thinking:** Sometimes you can compare without computing full answers. Consider 2045 + 318 vs 2048 + 312. The first number in the second pair is 3 more, but the second number is 6 less. Net effect: the second pair is 3 less. So 2045 + 318 is greater by 3.

## When Order Creates Confusion

When an expression mixes addition and multiplication, **which operation you do first changes the answer**. Consider this scenario:

Priya had 20 stickers. Her friend gave her 3 packets, each containing 6 stickers. How many stickers does she have now?

She wrote: **20 + 3 x 6**

- If we add first: (20 + 3) x 6 = 23 x 6 = 138 (Wrong!)
- If we multiply first: 20 + (3 x 6) = 20 + 18 = 38 (Correct!)

Just as commas and full stops prevent misunderstandings in language, **brackets** and the concept of **terms** prevent ambiguity in mathematics.

## The Power of Brackets

**Brackets** are like signboards that say "do this part first."

**Example:** Rohan bought a notebook for Rs 30 and a pen for Rs 18. He handed Rs 50 to the shopkeeper. What is his change?

- Without brackets: 50 - 30 + 18 = 38 (Nonsense — suggests he got Rs 38 back!)
- With brackets: 50 - (30 + 18) = 50 - 48 = **2** (Correct!)

> **Golden Rule:** Always evaluate the expression **inside the brackets first**, then handle the remaining operations.

## Understanding Terms

**Terms** are the building blocks of an expression, separated by the **+** sign. To identify terms correctly, first rewrite any subtraction as addition of a negative number.

For example:
- In **15 + 9**, the terms are **15** and **9**
- In **46 - 12**, rewritten as 46 + (-12), the terms are **46** and **-12**
- In **8 x 3 + 7**, the terms are **8 x 3** and **7**

Notice that 8 x 3 counts as a **single term** because it contains no + sign within it.

### Evaluating Step by Step

1. Identify all terms in the expression
2. Work out each term individually (do any multiplication or division inside the term)
3. Add all the evaluated terms together

**Example:** Evaluate 20 + 3 x 6

| Step | Working |
|------|---------|
| Identify terms | 20 and 3 x 6 |
| Evaluate each term | 20 = 20, and 3 x 6 = 18 |
| Add all terms | 20 + 18 = **38** |

**Example:** Evaluate 4 x (6 + 1) + 3 x 9 + 5

| Step | Working |
|------|---------|
| Identify terms | 4 x (6 + 1), 3 x 9, and 5 |
| Evaluate each term | 4 x 7 = 28, 3 x 9 = 27, and 5 = 5 |
| Add all terms | 28 + 27 + 5 = **60** |

## Swapping and Grouping

### The Commutative Property of Addition

In addition, **the order of terms does not matter**. This property is called the **commutative property**.

- 9 + (-3) = 6
- (-3) + 9 = 6

Both give the same result!

This holds for any number of terms, positive or negative:

**Term 1 + Term 2 = Term 2 + Term 1**

### The Associative Property of Addition

**How you group terms does not affect the sum.** This is the **associative property**.

Consider (-5) + 8 + (-9):
- Group first two, then third: [(-5) + 8] + (-9) = 3 + (-9) = -6
- Group last two, then first: (-5) + [8 + (-9)] = (-5) + (-1) = -6
- Group first and last, then second: [(-5) + (-9)] + 8 = (-14) + 8 = -6

All three approaches give **-6**.

> **Why This Matters:** Suppose your mother is totalling the monthly grocery bills and realizes she forgot to add the vegetable vendor's amount. She does not need to restart — she can simply add it to the running total. The associative property guarantees the answer will still be correct!

### When Order Matters — and When It Does Not

In daily life, some things can be swapped and others cannot. You can wear your watch and your ring in any order. But you must put on a kurta before the jacket — the sequence matters! In addition, however, the order **never** matters.

## Opening Brackets — Part I

### Minus Sign Before Brackets

When brackets follow a **minus sign**, removing the brackets **flips all signs** inside.

**Example:** 300 - (50 + 8)

To subtract 58 from 300, we subtract 50 first, then subtract 8:
- 300 - 50 - 8 = 242

So: **300 - (50 + 8) = 300 - 50 - 8**

The +50 became -50, and the +8 became -8.

**Example:** 400 - (180 - 60)

- 400 - (180 - 60) = 400 - 120 = 280
- Removing brackets: 400 - 180 + 60 = 280

The +180 became -180, and the -60 became +60.

> **Key Rule:** When removing brackets preceded by a **minus sign**, every sign inside the brackets gets reversed (+ becomes - and - becomes +).

### Plus Sign Before Brackets

When brackets follow a **plus sign**, removing them **changes nothing** about the signs inside.

**Example:** 35 + (22 - 8) = 35 + 22 - 8 = 49

The signs of 22 and -8 stay exactly as they were.

### Tinkering with Terms

What happens when we nudge one term in an expression?

| Expression | Value | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| 47 + (-12) | 35 | Given |
| **48** + (-12) | **36** | 48 is 1 more than 47, so the value rises by 1 |
| **46** + (-12) | **34** | 46 is 1 less than 47, so the value drops by 1 |
| 47 + (**-11**) | **36** | -11 is 1 more than -12, so the value rises by 1 |
| 47 + (**-13**) | **34** | -13 is 1 less than -12, so the value drops by 1 |

## Opening Brackets — Part II (The Distributive Property)

### Multiplying a Number by a Sum

Anita and her brother each ordered a dosa (Rs 55) and a lassi (Rs 30). The total bill is:

**2 x (55 + 30) = 2 x 55 + 2 x 30 = 110 + 60 = 170**

This is the **distributive property**: multiplying a number by a sum equals multiplying by each part and then adding.

### Multiplying a Number by a Difference

Similarly, for differences:

**(18 - 5) x 10 = 18 x 10 - 5 x 10 = 180 - 50 = 130**

> **In Simple Words:** The multiple of a sum (or difference) equals the sum (or difference) of the multiples.

### Smart Calculations with the Distributive Property

This property turns tough multiplications into easy ones:

**Example:** Find 98 x 15

98 x 15 = (100 - 2) x 15 = 100 x 15 - 2 x 15 = 1500 - 30 = **1470**

**Example:** Find 72 x 14, given that 62 x 14 = 868

72 x 14 = (62 + 10) x 14 = 62 x 14 + 10 x 14 = 868 + 140 = **1008**

### Factoring Out Common Factors

When two terms share a common multiplier, we can factor it out:

**7 x 6 + 3 x 6 = (7 + 3) x 6 = 10 x 6 = 60**

Both terms are multiples of 6, so we combine the other factors.

Similarly: **12 x 45 + 8 x 45 = (12 + 8) x 45 = 20 x 45 = 900**

## Real-World Expressions

### Paying with Notes and Coins
To pay Rs 567, you could use:
- 5 notes of Rs 100 + 1 note of Rs 50 + 1 note of Rs 10 + 1 coin of Rs 5 + 1 coin of Rs 2
- Expression: 5 x 100 + 1 x 50 + 1 x 10 + 1 x 5 + 1 x 2 = 567

### Cinema Tickets
A multiplex charges Rs 250 per adult ticket and Rs 150 per child ticket. For 3 adults and 2 children:
- Expression: 3 x 250 + 2 x 150 = 750 + 300 = Rs 1,050

### Annual Savings
Sunita earns Rs 25,000 per month. She spends Rs 8,000 on rent, Rs 6,000 on food, and Rs 3,000 on transport. Her yearly savings:
- Expression: 12 x [25,000 - (8,000 + 6,000 + 3,000)] = 12 x 8,000 = Rs 96,000

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Arithmetic expression** | A mathematical phrase combining numbers with operations (+, -, x, /) |
| **Value of an expression** | The single number that the expression equals when evaluated |
| **Terms** | Parts of an expression separated by the + sign (after rewriting subtraction as addition) |
| **Brackets** | Symbols ( ) that mark which part of an expression must be evaluated first |
| **Commutative property** | Changing the order of terms in addition does not change the sum |
| **Associative property** | Changing the grouping of terms in addition does not change the sum |
| **Distributive property** | Multiplying a number by a sum (or difference) equals the sum (or difference) of the individual products |
| **Inverse** | A number with the opposite sign; the inverse of 8 is -8, and the inverse of -8 is 8 |
