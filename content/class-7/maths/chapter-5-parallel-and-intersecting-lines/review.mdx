## Key Concepts

- **Intersecting lines** meet at a point, creating **4 angles** at the intersection
- A **linear pair** consists of two adjacent angles at an intersection that add up to **180 degrees**
- **Vertically opposite angles** sit across from each other at an intersection and are **always equal**
- **Perpendicular lines** intersect at exactly **90 degrees** (marked with a small square)
- **Parallel lines** lie on the same plane and never meet, no matter how far extended (marked with arrows >)
- A **transversal** is a line that crosses two or more lines at separate points, creating **8 angles** total
- **Corresponding angles** occupy matching positions at each crossing; they are **equal when lines are parallel**
- **Alternate angles** sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the two lines; **equal when lines are parallel**
- **Co-interior angles** (same-side interior) are between the lines on the same side; they add up to **180 degrees** when lines are parallel
- With parallel lines and a transversal, there are only **2 distinct angle values**, and they add up to **180 degrees**
- If you know **one angle**, you can find all 8 angles using the properties above
- To test if lines are parallel: draw a transversal and check if corresponding angles are equal
- To construct parallel lines: use a set square and ruler, or fold perpendiculars through paper

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Intersecting lines | Lines that meet at a point on a flat surface |
| Linear pair | Two adjacent angles at an intersection summing to 180° |
| Vertically opposite angles | Angles across from each other at an intersection; always equal |
| Perpendicular lines | Lines crossing at exactly 90° |
| Parallel lines | Lines on the same plane that never meet |
| Transversal | A line crossing two or more lines at distinct points |
| Corresponding angles | Angles in matching positions at each crossing; equal when lines are parallel |
| Alternate angles | Angles on opposite sides of the transversal, between the lines; equal when lines are parallel |
| Co-interior angles | Angles between the lines on the same side of the transversal; sum to 180° when lines are parallel |

## Remember These

- Two lines must be on the **same plane** to be called parallel -- lines on different planes (floor and wall) are not parallel even if they never meet
- Vertically opposite angles are **always** equal, whether or not lines are parallel
- Corresponding and alternate angle equalities hold **only when lines are parallel**
- From just **one angle** at a parallel-transversal setup, all 8 angles can be determined
