We already know how to add and subtract integers. Now we extend our toolkit to **multiplication** and **division** of integers, uncovering the clean and elegant sign rules that govern these operations.

## Integers at a Glance

Integers span the entire number line: ..., −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...

![Number line showing positive and negative integers with zero in the centre](integer-number-line.png)

A helpful model uses **coloured counters**:
- A **blue counter** stands for +1
- A **yellow counter** stands for −1
- One blue and one yellow counter together form a **zero pair** (they cancel out)

**Useful fact:** Subtracting a number is identical to **adding its additive inverse**.
- 5 − 14 = 5 + (−14) = −9
- 6 − (−10) = 6 + 10 = 16

The **additive inverse** of any number a is −a. The additive inverse of 14 is −14, and the additive inverse of −14 is 14.

## Multiplying Integers

### Positive × Positive

This is ordinary multiplication:
- 3 × 5 = 15 (place 5 blue counters into a bag 3 times — 15 blue counters total)

### Positive × Negative

**3 × (−5):** Place 5 yellow counters into the bag 3 times.
Result: 15 yellow counters = **−15**

So: **3 × (−5) = −15**

> A positive multiplier with a negative multiplicand produces a **negative** product.

### Negative × Positive

**(−3) × 5:** Remove 5 blue counters from the bag 3 times.

The bag starts empty, so we first add zero pairs (blue + yellow), then remove the blue counters. After removing 5 blue counters 3 times (15 blue counters gone), 15 yellow counters remain.

Result: **(−3) × 5 = −15**

> A negative multiplier with a positive multiplicand also produces a **negative** product.

### Negative × Negative

**(−3) × (−5):** Remove 5 yellow counters from the bag 3 times.

Start with zero pairs again. After removing 5 yellow counters 3 times (15 yellow counters gone), 15 blue counters remain.

Result: **(−3) × (−5) = +15**

> When both numbers carry the same sign, the product is **positive**.

### The Sign Rules — Summarised

| First Number | Second Number | Product |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| + | + | **+** |
| + | − | **−** |
| − | + | **−** |
| − | − | **+** |

> **Memory Aid:**
> - **Matching signs** yield a **positive** product
> - **Clashing signs** yield a **negative** product

The **magnitude** (absolute value) of the product is always the product of the individual magnitudes, regardless of signs.

### Seeing the Pattern

Watch what happens as the first number decreases by 1:

```
 3 × 4 = 12
 2 × 4 =  8    (dropped by 4)
 1 × 4 =  4    (dropped by 4)
 0 × 4 =  0    (dropped by 4)
-1 × 4 = -4    (dropped by 4)
-2 × 4 = -8    (dropped by 4)
```

Now with a negative second number:

```
 3 × (-4) = -12
 2 × (-4) =  -8    (rose by 4)
 1 × (-4) =  -4    (rose by 4)
 0 × (-4) =   0    (rose by 4)
-1 × (-4) =   4    (rose by 4)
-2 × (-4) =   8    (rose by 4)
```

The pattern confirms: **negative × negative = positive**.

### Instant Calculations

If you know 245 × 312 = 76,440, then without any calculation:
- (−245) × 312 = **−76,440** (clashing signs: negative)
- (−245) × (−312) = **76,440** (matching signs: positive)
- 245 × (−312) = **−76,440** (clashing signs: negative)

## Properties of Integer Multiplication

### Commutative Property

Order does not affect the product:

**a × b = b × a**

Examples: 4 × (−6) = −24 and (−6) × 4 = −24

### Associative Property

Grouping does not affect the product:

**(a × b) × c = a × (b × c)**

Example: (3 × (−4)) × 5 = (−12) × 5 = −60, and 3 × ((−4) × 5) = 3 × (−20) = −60

### Distributive Property

Multiplication distributes across addition:

**a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c**

Example: (−4) × (3 + (−5)) = (−4) × (−2) = 8, and (−4) × 3 + (−4) × (−5) = −12 + 20 = 8

### Special Multipliers

- **a × 0 = 0** for every integer a
- **a × 1 = a** for every integer a
- **a × (−1) = −a** for every integer a (multiplying by −1 flips the sign)

## Dividing Integers

Division reverses multiplication. If a × b = c, then c ÷ a = b (provided a is not zero).

### Sign Rules for Division

Since division undoes multiplication, the same sign rules apply:

| Dividend | Divisor | Quotient |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| + | + | **+** |
| + | − | **−** |
| − | + | **−** |
| − | − | **+** |

> **Matching signs** produce a **positive** quotient. **Clashing signs** produce a **negative** quotient.

### Worked Examples

| Division | Steps | Answer |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| (−15) ÷ 5 | 15 ÷ 5 = 3, clashing signs → negative | **−3** |
| (−24) ÷ (−6) | 24 ÷ 6 = 4, matching signs → positive | **+4** |
| 21 ÷ (−7) | 21 ÷ 7 = 3, clashing signs → negative | **−3** |
| (−45) ÷ (−5) | 45 ÷ 5 = 9, matching signs → positive | **+9** |

### Division Is Not Commutative

Unlike multiplication, swapping order changes the answer:
- 15 ÷ 3 = 5, but 3 ÷ 15 is not 5
- (−8) ÷ 4 = −2, but 4 ÷ (−8) is not −2

### Zero and Division

**Dividing by zero is undefined.** No number multiplied by 0 produces a non-zero result. Expressions like 7 ÷ 0 or (−4) ÷ 0 have **no answer**.

## Brahmagupta's Legacy

> **Did You Know?** The Indian mathematician **Brahmagupta** (598–668 CE) was among the first in history to write down systematic rules for arithmetic with negative numbers and zero. In his treatise *Brahmasphutasiddhanta* (628 CE), he stated principles equivalent to:
> - A fortune (positive) multiplied by a debt (negative) gives a debt
> - A debt multiplied by a debt gives a fortune
> - Zero multiplied by anything gives zero
>
> These rules, formulated in India roughly 1400 years ago, remain exactly the ones we use today!

## Integers in Everyday Life

**Weather:** If the temperature falls by 4 degrees every hour, the total drop after 6 hours is: 6 × (−4) = **−24 degrees**

**Finances:** If Anil borrows Rs 75 from each of 3 friends, his total borrowing is: 3 × (−75) = **−Rs 225**

**Ocean depth:** A deep-sea diver descends 6 metres per minute. After 7 minutes, the position relative to the surface is: 7 × (−6) = **−42 metres** (42 metres below the surface)

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Integer** | Any positive number, negative number, or zero |
| **Additive inverse** | The number that cancels a given number when added; the additive inverse of a is −a |
| **Positive product** | The result when two integers with matching signs are multiplied |
| **Negative product** | The result when two integers with clashing signs are multiplied |
| **Commutative property** | a × b = b × a (order does not affect the product) |
| **Associative property** | (a × b) × c = a × (b × c) (grouping does not affect the product) |
| **Distributive property** | a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c |
| **Undefined** | Division by zero produces no meaningful result |
