When two shapes are identical in every way — same form, same measurements — we call them **congruent**, like twins in geometry! This chapter explains what congruence means, and reveals the minimum conditions needed to confirm that two triangles are congruent.

## What Does Congruence Mean?

Two figures are **congruent** if one can be placed on top of the other so they overlap perfectly. They must share the same shape and the same size.

![Two congruent triangles superimposed, one rotated and flipped to match the other](congruent-triangles-superimposed.png)

When testing congruence, you are allowed to **rotate** or **flip** (mirror) one figure before laying it on the other.

> **Central Idea:** Congruent figures are perfect replicas of each other. If you trace one on butter paper, the tracing will fit exactly over the other.

### Testing Congruence for Common Shapes

Different shapes require different checks:

| Shape | What to Compare |
|-------|----------------|
| **Circle** | Radius (or diameter) |
| **Rectangle** | Length and breadth |
| **Square** | Side length |
| **V-shape (angle figure)** | The two arm lengths and the angle between them |

Two circles are congruent when they share the **same radius**. Two rectangles are congruent when their **length and breadth** both match.

## When Are Two Triangles Congruent?

A triangle carries 6 measurements — 3 sides and 3 angles. The key question is: do we need all 6 to confirm congruence, or will fewer suffice?

### SSS: Three Equal Sides

If two triangles share the **same three side lengths**, they are congruent.

**Why?** Constructing a triangle from three given sides (using compass arcs) produces a unique shape — the arcs from the base endpoints intersect in only one way above the base (and a mirror image below it).

> **SSS Rule:** When all three sides of one triangle match the three sides of another, the triangles are congruent.

**Example:** Triangle PQR has PQ = 7 cm, QR = 4 cm, RP = 5.5 cm. Triangle DEF has DE = 5.5 cm, EF = 4 cm, FD = 7 cm.

All three pairs of sides are equal (in some arrangement), so the triangles are congruent by SSS.

### Writing Congruence Correctly

When we write **triangle PQR is congruent to triangle XYZ**, we mean:
- P corresponds to X
- Q corresponds to Y
- R corresponds to Z

This implies:
- **Matching sides:** PQ = XY, QR = YZ, PR = XZ
- **Matching angles:** angle P = angle X, angle Q = angle Y, angle R = angle Z

The vertex order matters! Triangle PQR congruent to triangle XYZ also means triangle PRQ congruent to triangle XZY, but NOT triangle PRQ congruent to triangle XYZ.

### Congruent Triangles Inside a Rectangle

In rectangle PQRS, the diagonal QS splits it into two triangles: PQS and RQS. These are congruent by SSS:
- PQ = RS (opposite sides of a rectangle)
- PS = QR (opposite sides of a rectangle)
- QS = QS (shared diagonal)

## Do Matching Angles Guarantee Congruence?

**No!** Two triangles can have angles of 40, 60, and 80 degrees but differ in size. They share the same **shape** but not the same **size**. Such figures are called **similar** (a topic for later classes).

## SAS: Two Sides and the Angle Between Them

If two sides and the **included angle** (the angle sandwiched between those sides) of one triangle match the corresponding parts of another, the triangles are congruent.

> **SAS Rule:** Two sides plus the angle between them lock a triangle into a unique shape and size.

**Example:** If PQ = DE = 7 cm, PR = DF = 4 cm, and angle P = angle D = 45 degrees, then triangle PQR is congruent to triangle DEF by SAS.

### The Angle Must Be "Included"

The **SSA case** (two sides and a **non-included** angle) does **not** guarantee congruence. The construction can yield **two different** valid triangles because the arc from the third vertex may strike the base at two points.

**Example:** With PQ = 7 cm, PR = 3 cm, and angle Q = 25 degrees, two distinct triangles satisfy these conditions. So SSA is **not** a reliable congruence test.

## ASA: Two Angles and the Side Between Them

If two angles and the **included side** (the side between those angles) of one triangle match the corresponding parts of another, the triangles are congruent.

> **ASA Rule:** Two angles plus the side between them fix the triangle uniquely.

**Example:** If QR = YZ = 6 cm, angle Q = angle Y = 55 degrees, and angle R = angle Z = 40 degrees, the triangles are congruent by ASA.

### Using ASA to Discover Equal Parts

**Example:** In a figure, point M is the midpoint of both PQ and RS:
- PM = MQ (M is midpoint of PQ)
- RM = MS (M is midpoint of RS)
- angle PMR = angle QMS (vertically opposite angles)

By SAS, triangle PMR is congruent to triangle QMS. Therefore, PR = QS (matching sides of congruent triangles).

## AAS: Two Angles and a Non-Included Side

If two angles and a **non-included side** of one triangle match the corresponding parts of another, the triangles are still congruent.

> **AAS Rule:** Two angles plus any one side also guarantee congruence.

**Why?** Knowing two angles automatically determines the third (since angles total 180 degrees). This converts AAS information into ASA information.

**Example:** angle P = angle D = 40 degrees, angle R = angle F = 70 degrees, QR = EF = 5 cm.

Since angle Q = 180 − 40 − 70 = 70 degrees = angle E, we now have ASA: angle Q = angle E, QR = EF, angle R = angle F.

## RHS: For Right Triangles Only

For **right-angled triangles**, a special shortcut exists:

> **RHS Rule:** If the **hypotenuse** and one **other side** of a right triangle match those of another right triangle, the two triangles are congruent.

This is a special case where SSA does work — the right angle eliminates the ambiguity that normally plagues SSA.

## Summary of Congruence Tests

| Test | What Must Match | Guarantees Congruence? |
|------|----------------|----------------------|
| **SSS** | All three sides | Yes |
| **SAS** | Two sides and the included angle | Yes |
| **ASA** | Two angles and the included side | Yes |
| **AAS** | Two angles and a non-included side | Yes |
| **RHS** | Hypotenuse and one side (right triangles) | Yes |
| **AAA** | All three angles | **No** (same shape, possibly different size) |
| **SSA** | Two sides and a non-included angle | **No** (ambiguous case) |

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Congruent figures** | Figures with exactly the same shape and size |
| **Superimpose** | To place one figure exactly over another to test for a match |
| **Corresponding vertices** | Vertices that overlap when congruent triangles are superimposed |
| **Corresponding sides** | Sides that overlap when congruent triangles are superimposed |
| **Corresponding angles** | Angles that overlap when congruent triangles are superimposed |
| **SSS** | Side-Side-Side: three equal sides guarantee congruence |
| **SAS** | Side-Angle-Side: two sides with their included angle guarantee congruence |
| **ASA** | Angle-Side-Angle: two angles with their included side guarantee congruence |
| **AAS** | Angle-Angle-Side: two angles with a non-included side guarantee congruence |
| **RHS** | Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side: for right triangles, hypotenuse and one side guarantee congruence |
