## Key Concepts

- Two figures are **congruent** if one can be placed exactly on top of the other (same shape AND same size)
- You may **rotate** or **flip** (mirror) a figure when testing for congruence
- Two circles are congruent if they have the **same radius**
- Two rectangles are congruent if their **length and breadth** both match
- A triangle has **6 measurements** (3 sides + 3 angles), but you do not need all 6 to confirm congruence
- **SSS (Side-Side-Side):** all three sides match -- congruent
- **SAS (Side-Angle-Side):** two sides and the **included** angle match -- congruent
- **ASA (Angle-Side-Angle):** two angles and the **included** side match -- congruent
- **AAS (Angle-Angle-Side):** two angles and any side match -- congruent (knowing 2 angles fixes the third)
- **RHS (Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side):** for right triangles, hypotenuse + one side match -- congruent
- **AAA does NOT guarantee congruence** -- same shape but possibly different size (similar, not congruent)
- **SSA does NOT guarantee congruence** -- two different triangles may satisfy the same conditions (ambiguous case)
- When writing congruence, **vertex order matters**: triangle PQR congruent to triangle XYZ means P matches X, Q matches Y, R matches Z

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Congruent figures | Figures with exactly the same shape and size |
| Superimpose | Place one figure exactly over another to test for a match |
| Corresponding vertices | Vertices that overlap when congruent figures are matched |
| Corresponding sides | Sides that overlap when congruent figures are matched |
| Corresponding angles | Angles that overlap when congruent figures are matched |
| SSS | Three equal sides guarantee congruence |
| SAS | Two sides + included angle guarantee congruence |
| ASA | Two angles + included side guarantee congruence |
| AAS | Two angles + any one side guarantee congruence |
| RHS | Right angle + hypotenuse + one side guarantee congruence |

## Remember These

- **AAA (all angles equal) is NOT enough** for congruence -- triangles can have the same angles but different sizes
- **SSA is ambiguous** -- it can produce two different triangles, so it is NOT a valid congruence test (except RHS)
- The **order of vertices** in a congruence statement tells you which parts correspond -- get it right
- In SAS, the angle **must be between** the two given sides (the included angle) -- a non-included angle is SSA, which fails
