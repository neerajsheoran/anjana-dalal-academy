## In One Paragraph

Long before money existed, humans traded by **barter** — exchanging goods directly. But barter has serious problems: you need a **double coincidence of wants** (the other person must want what you have AND have what you want), there is no **common measure of value**, goods can't always be **divided** or **carried** easily, and many goods don't **last**. So humans invented **money** — a universally accepted medium of exchange. Money does four jobs: medium of exchange, measure of value, store of value, and standard of deferred payment. The journey of money in India runs from **cowrie shells** to **karshapana coins** (600 BCE — the word "pana" still means money in Tamil/Telugu/Malayalam/Kannada) to **Roman gold coins** (showing ancient global trade) to **Chola** and **Chalukya** coins to **Mughal calligraphic coins** to **paper money** (RBI today), and finally to **digital money** — bank accounts, cards, and India's revolutionary **UPI** (launched 2016), now the world's leader in real-time digital payments. The **rupee symbol ₹** was designed by Udaya Kumar of IIT Bombay in 2010.

## Key Takeaways

- **Barter** = direct exchange of goods/services without money. Still seen at Assam's Junbeel Mela, in book-swap clubs, in old-clothes-for-utensils trades.
- Problems of barter: **double coincidence of wants**, no common measure, divisibility, portability, durability.
- **Money** solves all these problems by being universally accepted, divisible, portable, durable, and a store of value.
- **Four functions of money**: (1) medium of exchange, (2) measure of value, (3) store of value, (4) standard of deferred payment.
- **Coins** appeared in India around 600 BCE — called **karshapanas** or **panas** (in Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam — *panam*; in Kannada — *hana*).
- Ancient Indian coin makers: **Mahajanapadas** (punch-marked coins), **Cholas** (tiger emblem), **Chalukyas** (Varaha), **Guptas** (gold coins with kings), **Mughals** (Persian calligraphy).
- **Roman gold coins** found in Pudukkottai and Kerala — evidence of India's ancient global trade.
- **Paper money** invented in China; introduced in India late 18th century; issued today by **RBI**.
- The **rupee symbol ₹** was designed by **Udaya Kumar** of IIT Bombay in 2010.
- **Digital money** today: bank accounts, debit/credit cards, mobile wallets, **UPI** (launched 2016).
- India processes more **real-time digital payments** than the USA, Europe, and China combined.
- Money continues to evolve (CBDC, biometric payments) but its four functions remain the same.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Money is trust made portable.**

A piece of paper or a number on a phone screen has no value by itself. It works because we all trust that others will accept it tomorrow. Money is one of humanity's greatest social inventions — built entirely on shared trust.
