## Key Concepts

- **Age of Reorganisation** = roughly **185 BCE – 320 CE**
- Period between the **Mauryan Empire** and the **Gupta Empire**
- Featured smaller kingdoms, foreign invaders, and cultural blending

## End of the Mauryans

- Last Mauryan king assassinated by his commander **Pushyamitra Shunga** around 185 BCE
- Empire fragmented — many smaller kingdoms emerged

## Five Major Powers

### 1. Shungas (~185-73 BCE)
- Founded by Pushyamitra Shunga
- Capital: Pataliputra → Vidisha
- Built **Bharhut Stupa** — beautiful Buddhist art
- Patrons of Hindu traditions

### 2. Indo-Greeks (~190 BCE - 10 CE)
- Successors of Alexander the Great
- King **Menander I (Milinda)** — converted to Buddhism after debating Nagasena
- Created **Greco-Buddhist art** (Gandhara school)
- Introduced **portrait coins**

### 3. Shakas / Scythians
- Central Asian nomads who entered India
- Settled in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra
- King **Rudradaman I** — famous **Junagadh inscription** (one of the earliest in Sanskrit)
- Repaired the **Sudarshana Lake dam**

### 4. Kushans (~1st-3rd century CE)
- Central Asian dynasty, ruled huge empire from Central Asia into north India
- Capital: **Purushapura (Peshawar)** and Mathura
- Greatest king: **Kanishka the Great**
- Convened the **Fourth Buddhist Council**
- Promoted **Silk Route trade**

### 5. Satavahanas (Deccan, ~1st BCE - 3rd CE)
- Capital: **Pratishthana (Paithan)**
- Most famous king: **Gautamiputra Satakarni**
- Patrons of Buddhism AND Hinduism
- Major **Rome-Deccan trade** — Roman gold coins found in southern India

## Foreign Invaders → Indian

Many foreign groups invaded but **assimilated** into Indian society:
- Indo-Greek Menander became Buddhist
- Shaka king Rudradaman wrote in Sanskrit
- Kushans patronised Indian religions and art

India absorbed invaders rather than being destroyed by them.

## Cultural Confluence

| Achievement | What it was |
|---|---|
| **Gandhara Art** | First Buddha statues; Greek techniques + Indian themes |
| **Junagadh inscription** | Early Sanskrit inscription |
| **Silk Route trade** | Connected India to Rome and China |
| **Portrait coins** | Greek-introduced; widely adopted |
| **Religious tolerance** | Rulers patronised multiple religions |

## The Sangam Age (South India)

- **3rd century BCE – 3rd century CE**
- Three Tamil dynasties: **Cholas, Cheras, Pandyas**
- Capitals: Uraiyur/Kaveripattanam (Chola), Vanji (Chera), Madurai (Pandya)
- **Sangam literature** — over 2,000 Tamil poems
- Topics: love, war, kings, daily life, the **five tinai** (landscapes)
- Active **maritime trade** with Rome, Egypt, Southeast Asia

## Why This Period Matters

1. **Cultural blending** — multiple traditions
2. **Buddhism spread abroad** — to China, Tibet, Korea, Japan
3. **Global trade boom** — India connected to Silk Route + Indian Ocean
4. **First Buddha statues** appeared
5. **Sanskrit revival** in inscriptions
6. **Sangam literature** flourished in Tamil south
7. **Assimilation pattern** — India absorbing invaders

## Common Confusions

- **Indo-Greeks were NOT Indian Greeks.** They were Greeks ruling parts of India after Alexander. Their kings had Greek names but adopted Indian ways.
- **Shakas and Hunas are different.** Shakas (Scythians) came earlier and integrated. Hunas came later (after the Gupta period) and caused destruction.
- **Sangam Age is parallel to north India's reorganisation.** They were happening at the same time in different parts of the Subcontinent.
- **'Reorganisation' doesn't mean chaos.** Many great achievements happened — Gandhara art, Sangam poetry, global trade.
