## In One Paragraph

The 11th-12th centuries CE were a turning point in Indian history. **Mahmud of Ghazni** raided India 17 times (1000-1027 CE), destroying the Somnath temple in 1026 but never settling in India. With him came the brilliant scholar **Al-Biruni**, who learned Sanskrit and wrote a remarkable account of medieval India (**Kitab al-Hind**). A century later, **Muhammad of Ghor** defeated the Rajput hero **Prithviraj III** at the **Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE)** — beginning lasting Muslim rule in India. His general **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** founded the **Delhi Sultanate** in 1206 and built the **Qutb Minar**. Around 1200 CE, **Bakhtiyar Khilji** destroyed **Nalanda University** — one of the greatest cultural losses in human history. Meanwhile, India continued to flourish elsewhere: the **Cholas** (Rajendra Chola I) reached their peak; **King Bhoja** of the Paramaras wrote 84 books; the **Hoysalas** and **Kakatiyas** built magnificent temples; **Bhaskaracharya** produced the **Lilavati** and **Siddhanta Shiromani**; **Ramanujacharya** founded **Vishishtadvaita Vedanta**. **Angkor Wat** was built in Cambodia, showing how far Indian temple traditions had spread. The era was a time of strain — but not of collapse.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mahmud of Ghazni** (Sultan of Ghazni in Afghanistan) raided India 17 times between 1000-1027 CE; destroyed Somnath in 1026.
- Somnath has been **rebuilt seven times** — most recently after independence under Sardar Patel.
- **Al-Biruni** travelled with Mahmud, learned Sanskrit, wrote **Kitab al-Hind** — one of the great accounts of medieval India.
- **First Battle of Tarain (1191)**: Prithviraj III defeats Muhammad of Ghor.
- **Second Battle of Tarain (1192)**: Muhammad of Ghor defeats and kills Prithviraj III — a turning point in Indian history.
- **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** founded the **Delhi Sultanate** in 1206 CE; built the **Qutb Minar** in Delhi.
- **Bakhtiyar Khilji** destroyed **Nalanda University** around 1200 CE; its library reportedly burned for months.
- Khilji's army was defeated in **Kamrupa (Assam)** in 1206, showing Northeast India's resistance.
- **Rajendra Chola I** (1014-1044): northern expedition to the Ganga; **naval expedition to Srivijaya (Indonesia) in 1025 CE**; built Gangaikonda Cholapuram.
- **King Bhoja** of the Paramaras (1010-1055): warrior-scholar; wrote/sponsored 84 books on architecture, grammar, astronomy, yoga.
- **Hoysalas** of Karnataka: built Belur, Halebidu, Somnathpur (UNESCO).
- **Kakatiyas** of Warangal: built the Thousand Pillar Temple and Ramappa Temple (UNESCO).
- **Bhaskaracharya** (1114-1185): wrote **Lilavati**, **Bijaganita**, **Siddhanta Shiromani**; calculated sidereal year accurate to 3 minutes; anticipated calculus.
- **Ramanujacharya** (1017-1137): founded **Vishishtadvaita Vedanta**; opened liberation through bhakti to all castes.
- **Angkor Wat** built in Cambodia (12th c.) — the largest religious monument ever built; Hindu temple to Vishnu.
- The era saw both **destruction** and **continued creativity** — Indian civilisation was strained but not broken.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **History honours those who endure.**

The 11th-12th centuries brought painful invasions and the loss of great institutions. Yet Indian poets, scientists, sculptors, and philosophers kept creating. Civilisations are tested by hard centuries — and India's was tested and not broken. The light dimmed in places, but never went out.
