## A Country with Many Neighbours

If you stand at the right spot on the Indian map and stretch your hand, you can almost touch **seven different countries**. India has more direct neighbours than most countries in the world — and more types of neighbours.

When we think of "neighbours," we usually mean **land neighbours** — countries that share a border with us. But India has another kind of neighbour too: **maritime neighbours** — countries across the sea.

India's total land boundary is **15,100 km long**. Its coastline is **11,100 km long**. That gives it a unique position: a giant subcontinent that is a meeting point of continents and oceans.

In this chapter, we look at India's neighbourhood — who they are, what we share, what we struggle with, and what the future may hold.

## India's Land Neighbours

India shares **land borders** with seven countries:

| Country | Direction | Shared border |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Pakistan** | Northwest | Punjab, Rajasthan, Gujarat, J&K |
| **Afghanistan** | Northwest | A small strip in Ladakh/J&K (via PoK) |
| **China** | North | Across the Himalayas |
| **Nepal** | North | Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Uttarakhand, West Bengal, Sikkim |
| **Bhutan** | North | Sikkim, West Bengal, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh |
| **Bangladesh** | East | West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram |
| **Myanmar** | East | Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram |

These borders pass through deserts, plains, forests, mountains, marshes, and river valleys.

## India's Maritime Neighbours

Across the sea, India's closest neighbours are:

- **Sri Lanka** — just 30 km across the Palk Strait
- **Maldives** — a chain of islands southwest of India

Beyond these immediate maritime neighbours, India's wider sea neighbourhood includes:
- **Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand** to the east
- **Iran, Oman, UAE, Saudi Arabia** to the west
- **East African nations** across the Indian Ocean

The **Indian Ocean** itself is named after India, and India holds a strategic central position in it.

## Why the Indian Ocean Matters

The **Indian Ocean is the third-largest ocean** in the world. It is also one of the busiest:

- **Half of the world's container ships** pass through it
- **One-third of the world's bulk cargo**
- **Two-thirds of the world's oil shipments**
- **2.7 billion people** live in the countries bordering it

For India, the Indian Ocean is:
- A trade highway (we get most of our oil through it)
- A defence concern (we patrol it to keep shipping lanes safe)
- A cultural link (centuries of trade with Africa, Arabia, Southeast Asia)
- A source of regional influence

India's peninsular shape — like a giant arrowhead pointing south — puts it at the centre of this ocean. This was true 2,000 years ago when Romans, Chinese, Persians, and Indonesians all traded through India. It is true today when oil, electronics, and food move along the same routes.

## India and China — The Two Asian Giants

India and **China** are the two largest countries in Asia. Together, they have:
- **2.8 billion people** — over a third of humanity
- The world's **2nd and 5th largest economies** (or 1st and 3rd by some measures)
- Vast military and technological capabilities
- 5,000+ years of civilisational history each

The two countries share a **3,488 km border** through the Himalayas — passing through Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, and Ladakh.

### Ancient Cultural Ties

For 2,000 years, India and China were connected by deep cultural exchange:

- **Buddhism** travelled from India to China around the **1st century CE**, transforming Chinese spiritual life
- **Chinese pilgrims** visited Indian centres of learning:
  - **Faxian (Fa-Hien)** — visited India in the 5th century (Gupta period)
  - **Xuanzang** — visited in the 7th century, spent 17 years in India, returned with Buddhist texts
  - **Yi Jing** — visited later, studied at Nalanda
- **Indian monks** travelled to China:
  - **Bodhidharma** — went to China from Tamil Nadu, founded Zen Buddhism
  - **Dharmakshema, Kumarajiva** — translated Buddhist texts into Chinese
- **The Silk Road** connected India and China through Central Asia
- **Cultural items** moved both ways: silk, spices, tea, paper, gunpowder, mathematics

### Modern Relations

After India's independence (1947) and the founding of the People's Republic of China (1949), relations went through cycles:

- **1950s**: "Hindi-Chini bhai-bhai" — initial friendship
- **1962**: India-China war over border issues; India lost territory in Aksai Chin
- **1970s-80s**: Cool relations
- **1990s-2000s**: Slow normalisation; growing trade
- **2010s-2020s**: Border tensions (Doklam 2017, Galwan 2020 — 20 Indian soldiers martyred)
- **Today**: Complex — major trading partner but also strategic rival

### What's at Stake?

India and China have:
- **Disputed borders** — both claim parts of each other's territory (Aksai Chin, Arunachal Pradesh)
- **Trade** — about $130 billion annually (India imports much more than it exports to China)
- **Water** — Brahmaputra and several rivers originate in Tibet
- **Strategic competition** — over influence in South Asia, Africa, the Indian Ocean
- **Common interests** — climate change, regional stability, global trade rules

This is one of the most consequential relationships in the world. How India and China get along will shape the 21st century — for both countries and for the world.

## India and Pakistan — The Tragic Partition

**Pakistan** is India's western neighbour. The two countries share a complicated, often painful history.

Before 1947, what is now Pakistan and Bangladesh was **part of British India**. At independence, the country was partitioned along religious lines — Muslim-majority areas became Pakistan, Hindu/Sikh-majority areas remained in India. The Partition was one of the **bloodiest events of the 20th century**:

- **10-20 million people displaced** — the largest migration in human history
- **1-2 million people killed** in communal violence
- Families divided forever
- Property, businesses, identities — all uprooted

The wounds of Partition are still felt today.

Since 1947, India and Pakistan have fought **four wars** (1947-48, 1965, 1971, 1999) and many smaller conflicts. The **Kashmir** issue — over which both countries claim sovereignty — remains unresolved.

In 1971, Pakistan's eastern wing rebelled against the western Pakistani army. India supported the rebels, and **Bangladesh** was born as a separate country.

Today, India-Pakistan relations remain difficult — limited trade, suspended sports ties, occasional military skirmishes, periodic peace overtures.

Yet there is also much that connects the two:
- **Shared language** (Hindi-Urdu are very close)
- **Shared culture** (Punjabi food, music, poetry on both sides)
- **Shared history** (mahajanapadas, Mughal Empire, freedom movement)
- **Family ties** (millions have relatives across the border)
- **Cricket** (the rivalry is fierce but the love is real)

Better India-Pakistan relations would benefit both countries hugely. Whether that happens depends on choices made by both sides.

## India and Bangladesh — Younger Neighbour, Deep Roots

**Bangladesh** is a relatively young country, born in 1971 after a struggle for independence from Pakistan. India played a key role in its birth.

India and Bangladesh share:
- A **4,096 km border** — India's longest
- **Bengali language and culture** — West Bengal (India) and Bangladesh share the same language
- **Rivers** — the Ganga becomes the Padma in Bangladesh; the Brahmaputra becomes the Jamuna
- **Trade** — over $14 billion annually
- **Cultural ties** — literature, music, food, festivals

Bangladesh is a major **trading partner** for India. Many Bangladeshi students study in India. India provides electricity, transit routes, infrastructure support.

There are also tensions — over migration, river water sharing, occasional border incidents — but overall, India-Bangladesh relations are among India's most positive.

## India and Nepal — Brothers in the Hills

**Nepal** is a small country tucked into the Himalayas, north of India.

India and Nepal have an **open border** — citizens can travel freely without visas. This is unique in modern international relations.

We share:
- **Hindu and Buddhist heritage** — Lord Rama married Sita from Janakpur (in Nepal); Lord Buddha was born in Lumbini (Nepal)
- **Family ties** — many Indian and Nepali families intermarry
- **Trade** — Nepal is landlocked and depends heavily on Indian ports
- **Religion** — Nepal was the world's only officially Hindu kingdom until 2008 (now a secular republic)
- **Military ties** — the famous **Gorkha regiments** in the Indian Army recruit from Nepal

Nepal has also been balancing between India and China. As Nepal develops, it faces choices about which direction to lean.

## India and Bhutan — The Friendly Mountain Kingdom

**Bhutan** is a small Himalayan kingdom east of Nepal. It is one of India's closest friends.

What is special about Bhutan:
- Officially measures **"Gross National Happiness"** instead of (or alongside) GDP
- About **70% forest cover** — a true carbon-negative country
- Buddhist culture preserved beautifully
- Constitutional monarchy with a popular royal family

India and Bhutan share:
- A **special friendship treaty** (since 1949, updated 2007)
- India helps train Bhutanese military officials
- India is Bhutan's largest trading partner
- India helps build hydroelectric projects in Bhutan
- Bhutan does NOT have diplomatic relations with China (unique in the region)

The Indo-Bhutan relationship is one of the most stable bilateral relationships in the world.

## India and Sri Lanka — Brothers Across the Sea

**Sri Lanka** is an island nation, just 30 km across the **Palk Strait** from India.

Cultural ties go back to ancient times:
- **Ramayana** — Lord Rama's army built a bridge to Sri Lanka to rescue Sita from Ravana
- **Buddhism** — spread from India to Sri Lanka in the 3rd century BCE through Emperor Ashoka's son Mahinda and daughter Sanghamitta. Buddha's teachings have flourished there ever since
- **Tamil culture** — northern Sri Lanka has long had Tamil people, sharing culture with Tamil Nadu
- **Trade** — Sri Lankan spices and Indian textiles have traded for 2,000+ years

Modern relations:
- India is Sri Lanka's largest trading partner
- India provided massive aid during Sri Lanka's 2022 economic crisis
- Indian films and music are extremely popular
- Tourism flows both ways

There are also issues — Tamil rights, fishing in the Palk Strait, port construction by China — but overall, India-Sri Lanka relations are warm and growing.

## India and Maldives — Tiny Islands, Big Importance

**Maldives** is a chain of about 1,200 small islands southwest of India. It is one of the world's smallest countries by population (about 500,000 people).

But for India, Maldives is strategically important — it controls key shipping lanes in the Indian Ocean. India and Maldives have:
- **Defence cooperation** — India trains Maldivian forces, provides equipment
- **Tourism** — Indians are a major source of tourists
- **Disaster relief** — India helped after the 2004 tsunami and during the 2014 water crisis
- **Trade and aid** — billions of dollars in development help

Maldives has been a focus of India-China competition for influence. India typically maintains close ties with Maldives, though political shifts there sometimes create tensions.

## India and Myanmar — The Eastern Gateway

**Myanmar** (formerly Burma) is India's eastern land neighbour. It connects India to Southeast Asia.

Ties include:
- Shared Buddhist heritage
- Trade and energy projects
- Strategic position for India's **Act East policy**
- A long, complex history (Burma was part of British India until 1937)

Myanmar has gone through long periods of military rule. The democratic government of Aung San Suu Kyi was overthrown by the military in 2021, plunging Myanmar into civil conflict. India watches the situation carefully and supports stability.

## SAARC — South Asian Cooperation

In 1985, the South Asian countries formed the **South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC)**. Members:
- India
- Pakistan
- Bangladesh
- Nepal
- Bhutan
- Sri Lanka
- Maldives
- Afghanistan (joined 2007)

SAARC aimed to:
- Promote regional trade
- Coordinate on shared challenges (poverty, climate, terrorism)
- Build peace
- Share technology and education

But SAARC has been **paralysed** by India-Pakistan tensions. Many summits have been cancelled. Trade between SAARC countries is among the lowest of any region in the world (about 5% — compared to 25%+ for EU or ASEAN).

To work around this, India has built newer groupings:
- **BIMSTEC** — Bay of Bengal countries (India, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand)
- **BBIN** — Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal cooperation
- **IORA** — Indian Ocean Rim Association

These exclude Pakistan and have been more successful in actual cooperation.

## Why India's Neighbourhood Is Difficult

India faces a unique challenge: it is **8 times larger than its second-largest neighbour** (Pakistan), with a bigger economy than all its neighbours combined. This creates discomfort. Smaller countries fear domination. Some lean toward China as a balance.

India has tried various approaches:
- **Gujral Doctrine** (1996-97) — give more, expect less from smaller neighbours
- **Neighbourhood First** policy — special priority for South Asia
- **Connect Central Asia** — looking westward
- **Act East** policy — strong focus on Southeast Asia

The relationships are works in progress.

## What India and Its Neighbours Share

Despite the difficulties, India and its neighbours share enormous amounts:

- **Civilisation** — Hindu, Buddhist, Islamic cultures cross borders
- **Languages** — many languages are spoken across borders (Bengali, Punjabi, Tamil, Pashto)
- **Festivals** — Diwali, Eid, Buddha Purnima celebrated everywhere
- **Food** — biryani, samosa, halwa, dal — recognisable across the region
- **Films and music** — Bollywood reaches all neighbours; Pakistani serials are watched in India; Nepali artists work in Mumbai
- **Sports** — cricket is the regional passion
- **Pilgrimage** — Hindus visit Pashupatinath (Nepal), Sikhs visit Nankana Sahib (Pakistan), Muslims visit Ajmer (India), Buddhists visit Bodh Gaya (India), Tamil Hindus visit Sri Lankan temples

In many ways, **South Asia is one cultural region** with several political states. The borders are recent (mostly 1947); the connections are ancient.

## Before We Close

- India has **7 land neighbours** (Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar) and immediate **maritime neighbours** (Sri Lanka, Maldives).
- India's land border is **15,100 km**; coastline is **11,100 km**.
- The **Indian Ocean** is among the most important shipping highways in the world.
- **China**: Two ancient civilisations with deep Buddhist links; modern relations are complex (border disputes, trade, strategic rivalry).
- **Pakistan**: Born from Partition of 1947, the bloodiest migration in history; four wars; persistent tensions over Kashmir.
- **Bangladesh**: Born 1971 with India's help; deep cultural and trade ties.
- **Nepal**: Open border, Hindu-Buddhist heritage, Gorkha regiments, balancing India and China.
- **Bhutan**: Special friendship; first to measure Gross National Happiness; carbon-negative.
- **Sri Lanka**: Tamil cultural ties, Buddhism, recent economic crisis where India provided major help.
- **Maldives**: Tiny but strategically important; India's defence and aid partner.
- **SAARC** (founded 1985) aimed at regional cooperation but has been hindered by India-Pakistan tensions.
- Newer groupings like **BIMSTEC** and **BBIN** have been more functional.
- Despite tensions, India and its neighbours share deep **civilisational, cultural, linguistic, and economic** ties.
