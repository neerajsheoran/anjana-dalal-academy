## In One Paragraph

The **Constitution of India** is the country's basic rulebook — adopted on **26 November 1949** and brought into effect on **26 January 1950** (Republic Day). It was written by a **Constituent Assembly** of 299 members (15 of them women) over nearly three years, with **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** chairing the Drafting Committee. It draws on three sources: the **ideals of the Indian freedom movement**, India's own **civilisational heritage** (from Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam to rajadharma to the Uttaramerur tradition), and **lessons from other countries' constitutions**. At 25 parts and 12 schedules, it is the **longest written constitution in the world**. It declares India a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic**, guarantees **six fundamental rights** to citizens, sets out **directive principles** for the state, divides power between the **Centre and the States**, and establishes an **independent judiciary**. The Supreme Court has further ruled that even Parliament cannot alter the **basic structure** of the Constitution. The original document is preserved in a **helium-filled glass case** in Parliament — a national treasure.

## Key Takeaways

- A **constitution** is a country's basic rulebook — government structure, citizen rights, national values.
- The Indian Constitution came into effect on **26 January 1950** — Republic Day.
- The Constituent Assembly worked from **December 1946 to November 1949**.
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** chaired the Drafting Committee; he is honoured as the chief architect.
- **15 women** members included Sarojini Naidu, Vijayalakshmi Pandit, Hansa Mehta, Durgabai Deshmukh.
- The Constitution drew from (1) the freedom movement's ideals, (2) Indian civilisational heritage, (3) other countries' constitutions.
- It has **25 parts** and **12 schedules** — the **longest written constitution in the world**.
- The **Preamble** declares India a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic committed to Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.
- **Six fundamental rights**: equality, freedom, against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural & educational rights, constitutional remedies.
- **Directive principles** are non-justiciable goals (welfare, environment, education).
- **Fundamental duties** added in 1976 (42nd Amendment).
- **Federal structure**: power divided between Union, State, and Concurrent lists.
- **Independent judiciary**: Supreme Court and High Courts can strike down unconstitutional laws.
- **Universal adult franchise** from day one — revolutionary in 1950.
- **Basic structure doctrine** (1973 Kesavananda Bharati case): Parliament cannot amend the core features.
- The original Constitution is preserved in a **helium-filled glass case** in Parliament.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **The Constitution is the people's promise to themselves.**

It is not imposed from above. It begins with "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA." Every right, every freedom, every limit on government comes from this self-given covenant. Honouring the Constitution is honouring ourselves.
