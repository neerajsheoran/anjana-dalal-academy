## A Golden Age

After centuries of regional kingdoms, a powerful new dynasty rose in north India — the **Guptas**. Their rule (~320 CE to ~550 CE) is often called India's **"Classical Age"** or **"Golden Age."**

It was a period of extraordinary achievement in:
- **Mathematics** — the decimal system, the concept of zero
- **Astronomy** — accurate calculations of Earth's rotation, eclipses
- **Medicine** — Ayurveda's foundational texts written
- **Literature** — Kalidasa, considered the greatest Sanskrit poet
- **Art and architecture** — temples, sculptures, cave paintings
- **Metallurgy** — the rust-resistant Iron Pillar of Delhi

This was not the largest Indian empire (the Mauryans were larger), but it was perhaps the most **culturally productive** period in Indian history.

## The Gupta Rulers

### Chandragupta I (~320-335 CE)

The founder. Married a Licchavi princess, gaining influence in northern India.

### Samudragupta (~335-375 CE)

The empire's great conqueror. Often called the **"Napoleon of India"** for his military success. His exploits are recorded on the **Allahabad Pillar inscription** — a remarkable historical document.

He extended Gupta rule across most of northern India and as far as south India, but was unique in his approach — he often **defeated southern kings and then reinstated them as tributaries**, rather than absorbing them.

He was also a **poet, musician, and scholar** — coins show him playing the veena.

### Chandragupta II / Vikramaditya (~375-415 CE)

The greatest of the Guptas. Under him, the empire reached its zenith. Famous for:
- Defeating the **Western Shakas** and adding Gujarat-Saurashtra to the empire
- Patronising the **navaratnas** ("nine gems") of his court — including **Kalidasa** the poet
- Promoting science, art, and trade
- His title **Vikramaditya** ("Sun of Valour") became legendary

### Kumaragupta and Skandagupta (~415-470 CE)

Maintained the empire but faced rising threats from the **Hunas** — Central Asian invaders.

### Decline (~470-550 CE)

The **Huna invasions** weakened the Guptas. The empire fragmented in the 6th century CE. Independent kingdoms rose in its place.

## Achievements of the Gupta Age

### 1. Mathematics

The Gupta period saw breakthroughs that **changed mathematics worldwide**:

- **Aryabhata** (476-550 CE) wrote the **Aryabhatiya** at age 23. He:
  - Calculated the value of **π (pi)** to four decimal places
  - Stated that Earth **rotates on its axis**
  - Calculated the **length of a year** to within minutes
  - Used **place-value notation** for numbers
- The **concept of zero (shunya)** as a number was developed and used systematically
- The **decimal system** that we use today — digits 0 through 9 — originated in this period

These ideas later spread to the Arab world, then to Europe, transforming global mathematics. Modern computing, banking, science — all depend on the decimal system India gave to the world.

### 2. Astronomy

- **Aryabhata** correctly explained that the Earth rotates and causes day and night
- Calculated **eclipses** accurately as natural events (not divine wrath)
- **Varahamihira** wrote the **Pancha-Siddhantika** synthesising Indian and foreign astronomy

### 3. Medicine — Ayurveda

The classical Ayurvedic texts were systematised:
- **Sushruta Samhita** — described over **300 surgeries** and **120 surgical instruments**, including procedures for cataract surgery and plastic surgery
- **Charaka Samhita** — comprehensive treatise on internal medicine
- Indian medicine influenced Arab medicine, which later shaped European medicine

### 4. Literature

- **Kalidasa** — the greatest Sanskrit poet. Wrote:
  - **Abhijnanashakuntalam** — a romantic play that Europeans later called "the perfect drama"
  - **Meghaduta** ("The Cloud Messenger") — a poem of longing
  - **Kumarasambhava, Raghuvamsa** — epic poems
- **Vishakhadatta** wrote **Mudrarakshasa** — a political drama
- The Puranas were compiled in this period

### 5. Art and Architecture

- **Ajanta and Ellora caves** — paintings and sculptures continued from earlier periods, with peak achievements in this era
- **Stone temples** developed — early **Nagara style** in north India
- **Bhitargaon Temple** — earliest surviving brick temple
- Beautiful **Buddha statues** in the Mathura and Sarnath styles

### 6. The Iron Pillar of Delhi

A famous **iron pillar at Mehrauli in Delhi** dates from the Gupta period. **Over 1,600 years old**, it has remained **rust-free** through centuries — a marvel of ancient Indian metallurgy. Modern scientists still study it to understand the technology.

The pillar bears an inscription praising a king named **Chandra** — likely Chandragupta II.

## Society and Daily Life

The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** visited India in the early 5th century CE and wrote detailed observations:
- People were prosperous
- Roads were safe
- The state did not interfere much with daily life
- Buddhism and Hinduism coexisted
- Vegetarianism was widespread
- Hospitals provided free care

His account, called **"Records of Buddhist Kingdoms,"** is one of the best foreign sources on Gupta India.

## Other Powers of the Period

The Gupta Empire ruled the north, but other kingdoms ruled elsewhere:

- **Vakatakas** in central India (Nandivardhana, near Nagpur)
- **Kamarupa** in modern Assam
- **Pallavas** in south India (capital Kanchipuram) — patrons of Tamil literature and temple architecture
- **Kadambas, Western Gangas, and others** in the Deccan

These regional powers also produced great art and literature. The Gupta period was a peak across the Subcontinent.

## Why is This Called India's Classical Age?

The Gupta Age is called **"classical"** because:

1. **Sanskrit literature** reached its peak with Kalidasa
2. **Hindu temple architecture** developed its classical forms
3. **Mathematics and astronomy** reached high points
4. **Indian art** achieved standards that influenced all later periods
5. **Religious tolerance** and prosperity allowed cultural flowering
6. Many of the **patterns we associate with "Indian culture"** were established or perfected here

For Europeans, ancient Greece is the classical age. For India, the Gupta period is similar — the time when many enduring achievements crystallised.

## The Decline

By the late 5th century CE, the **Hunas** (Central Asian nomads, related to the Huns who invaded Europe) entered India through the northwest. They were destructive — sacking cities, burning temples, killing scholars.

The Guptas resisted but were weakened. **Skandagupta** managed one great victory against the Hunas, but subsequent invasions broke the empire's strength. By 550 CE, the Gupta dynasty was over.

But the **cultural achievements** of the Gupta Age outlasted the political collapse. Kalidasa's plays are still performed. Aryabhata's mathematics still shapes the world. The decimal system runs every computer. The Gupta age ended; its legacy did not.

## Before We Close

- The **Gupta Empire** (~320-550 CE) is called India's **Classical Age** or **Golden Age**
- Major rulers: **Chandragupta I, Samudragupta** (the conqueror), **Chandragupta II / Vikramaditya** (the greatest)
- **Mathematics** — Aryabhata, the decimal system, the concept of zero, π calculation
- **Astronomy** — Aryabhata explained Earth's rotation; eclipses understood naturally
- **Medicine** — Sushruta and Charaka Samhita texts of Ayurveda
- **Literature** — **Kalidasa** the greatest Sanskrit poet
- **Art and architecture** — Ajanta paintings, classical temples
- The **Iron Pillar of Delhi** — rust-free after 1,600 years
- **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** the Chinese pilgrim described prosperous Gupta India
- Other kingdoms (Vakatakas, Pallavas, Kamarupa) flourished alongside
- The empire fell to **Huna invasions** by 550 CE
- The cultural legacy — math, literature, art — outlasted the empire
