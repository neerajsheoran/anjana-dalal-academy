## In One Paragraph

A **state** is a political organisation with four essential parts: **people**, **territory**, **government**, and **sovereignty** — and India has all four. The word "state" refers to the permanent political entity; "**government**" refers to the changing rulers who run it. A **democracy** is rule by the people; a **republic** has an elected (not hereditary) head of state. **India is both** — a democratic republic, which is why we celebrate Republic Day on 26 January. India has **three levels of government** that share work: **Central (Union)** handles defence, foreign affairs, currency, railways; **State** handles police, public health, agriculture, education (partly); **Local** (panchayats and municipalities) handles water, garbage, local roads, primary schools. Some subjects are on the **Concurrent List** — handled by both Centre and State. As citizens, we engage with government through **voting**, **RTI applications**, **writing to MPs/MLAs/councillors**, **public hearings**, **peaceful protests**, and **volunteering**. Even children can be informed citizens by reading, asking questions, and being responsible (not littering, following rules, encouraging family to vote). Democracy works only when citizens are **active** — and the government is not "them," it is **us**.

## Key Takeaways

- A **state** has 4 essential parts: people, territory, government, sovereignty.
- A **state** is permanent (the country); a **government** is the changing rulers.
- **Democracy** = rule of the people. **Republic** = elected head of state (not hereditary).
- **India is a democratic republic** — both. Republic Day = 26 January (Constitution took effect).
- **Britain** is a democracy but not a republic (still has a king).
- India has **three levels of government**: Central (Union), State, Local.
- **Central Government** = President (ceremonial), Vice President, Prime Minister (real head), Council of Ministers — handles Union List (defence, currency, foreign affairs, etc.).
- **State Government** = Governor (ceremonial), Chief Minister (real head), Council of Ministers — handles State List (police, health, agriculture).
- **Local Government** = panchayats (rural) and municipal bodies (urban) — handles water, garbage, local roads, primary schools.
- **Concurrent List** = subjects handled by both Centre and State (education, criminal law, marriage).
- A law passes through Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, and is signed by the President.
- **Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005** — citizens can demand info from any government office; reply within 30 days.
- Citizen engagement tools: vote, RTI, write to representatives, attend public hearings, peaceful protest, petition, volunteer.
- Even as a child: be informed, follow rules, encourage family to vote, report problems, learn how government works.
- Democracy needs active citizens. Government is not "them" — it is **us**.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **A democracy is only as good as its citizens.**

Apathy weakens democracy. Informed engagement strengthens it. Whether you write a letter, file an RTI, attend a hearing, or just vote thoughtfully — you are doing the daily work of keeping democracy alive.
