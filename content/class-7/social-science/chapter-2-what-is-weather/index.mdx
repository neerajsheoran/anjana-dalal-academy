## Weather All Around Us

You wake up one winter morning and shiver. You reach for thick clothes. In summer, you wear something light and cotton. Your body is reading the **weather** and adjusting.

**Weather** is the **state of the atmosphere at a particular place and time**. It changes every day — sometimes every hour. Today might be sunny and warm; tomorrow stormy and cold; the day after, foggy and chilly.

## What is the Atmosphere?

The **atmosphere** is the layer of gases — what we call **air** — surrounding the Earth.

It's organised in layers, like a cake:
- **Troposphere** (closest to the ground) — where all land-based plants, animals, and humans live. This is where almost all weather happens. It extends 6-18 km up.
- **Stratosphere** — above the troposphere, contains the protective ozone layer
- And several higher layers studied in Science

For weather, we mostly care about the **troposphere**.

## The Five Elements of Weather

Weather has five basic ingredients:

### 1. Temperature
How hot or cold the air is. Measured in **degrees Celsius (°C)** or Fahrenheit (°F). India officially uses Celsius.

### 2. Precipitation
Any form of water falling from the sky:
- **Rain** — water drops
- **Snow** — frozen crystals (in cold regions like Ladakh, Shimla)
- **Sleet** — partly frozen rain
- **Hail** — small balls of ice (sometimes during thunderstorms)

### 3. Atmospheric Pressure
The weight of the air pressing down on the Earth. Measured by **barometers**. Low pressure often brings storms; high pressure often brings clear weather.

### 4. Wind
The movement of air, including its **speed** and **direction**. Measured with **anemometers** (for speed) and **wind vanes** (for direction).

### 5. Humidity
The amount of **water vapour** in the air. High humidity makes you feel sticky and uncomfortable; low humidity makes the skin feel dry.

## Why We Need to Measure Weather

Imagine **Krishnan** in Chennai tells **Amir** in Kashmir that it's "chilly" after last night's rain. Amir replies: "How cold is it exactly?" Without a number, Krishnan can't say. What feels chilly to Krishnan in Chennai might feel quite pleasant to Amir in Kashmir.

That's why we need **standard measurements**. So a temperature of 12°C means the same thing in Chennai, Kashmir, or anywhere else.

Knowing the weather precisely helps us:
- **Plan our day** — what to wear, whether to carry an umbrella
- **Schedule activities** — cricket match, outdoor wedding, school trip
- **Prepare for danger** — heavy rains, storms, droughts, heat waves
- **Help farmers** — when to sow, when to harvest, when to irrigate
- **Help transport** — flights, ships, trains adjust to weather
- **Save lives** — early warnings save thousands during cyclones

## Reading Nature's Clues

Long before instruments, humans **read the signals of nature** to predict weather. Many of these signs still work today:

- **Ants moving their eggs to higher ground** — heavy rain coming
- **Frogs croaking loudly** in unison — rain expected
- **Birds flying low** — storm approaching
- **Squirrels gathering more nuts** — colder season ahead
- **Pine cones opening or closing** — change in humidity
- **Smoke rising straight up** — fair weather; smoke spreading low — bad weather
- **Red sky at sunrise** — possible storm; red sky at sunset — fair weather likely

In many Indian villages, **traditional ways of predicting the monsoon** are still used. Farmers watch the behaviour of cattle, the flowering of certain trees, the cries of peacocks, and the migration patterns of birds.

These traditions are not magic. They are **thousands of years of careful observation** turned into practical wisdom.

## Modern Weather Measurement

Today, scientists use:

- **Thermometers** for temperature
- **Rain gauges** for measuring rainfall
- **Barometers** for atmospheric pressure
- **Anemometers** for wind speed
- **Hygrometers** for humidity
- **Weather satellites** in space for cloud patterns
- **Doppler radars** for tracking storms
- **Ocean buoys** for sea conditions

The **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** combines all this data to predict weather across India. They issue daily forecasts, cyclone warnings, monsoon predictions, and special alerts for fishermen and farmers.

## Aryabhata and Indian Weather Science

India has a long tradition of weather science. **Aryabhata** (5th century CE), **Varahamihira** (6th century CE), and other Indian astronomers studied the seasons, monsoon, and celestial movements that affect weather.

Varahamihira's text **Brihat Samhita** has detailed sections on:
- Rainfall prediction
- Earthquakes
- Eclipses
- Crop forecasts

Some of his observations — about cloud types, wind patterns, and the relationship between celestial bodies and seasons — were ahead of his time.

## Why Weather is Different from Climate

Many students confuse **weather** with **climate**. Don't.

- **Weather** = state of the atmosphere at a **specific time and place**. "It's raining in Mumbai right now."
- **Climate** = the **average pattern** of weather over **many years** in a region. "Mumbai has a tropical monsoon climate."

A single rainy day doesn't change the climate. But a region that consistently has rain over decades has a rainy climate.

We'll explore India's climates in detail in the next chapter.

## Weather and Disasters

When weather behaves extremely, it can cause disasters:

- **Cyclones** — violent rotating storms with strong winds and heavy rain. Common on India's east coast (Bay of Bengal). The 2020 **Cyclone Amphan** caused massive damage in West Bengal.
- **Floods** — too much rain in a short time. Causes Mumbai, Chennai floods periodically.
- **Droughts** — too little rain over many months. Affects farming in Rajasthan, Vidarbha.
- **Heat waves** — extreme high temperatures. Several Indian summers have set records.
- **Cold waves** — extreme low temperatures, especially in north India during December-January.

**Accurate weather forecasting** has helped reduce deaths from these disasters. The IMD's improved cyclone predictions have saved thousands of lives in recent decades.

## Before We Close

- **Weather** = state of the atmosphere at a specific time and place
- **Climate** = average weather pattern over many years
- Weather happens in the **troposphere** (the lowest layer of the atmosphere)
- Five elements: **temperature, precipitation, atmospheric pressure, wind, humidity**
- We need **standard measurements** so weather can be compared across places
- **Traditional Indian observation** of nature (ants, birds, trees) has long been used to predict weather
- Modern science uses **satellites, radars, instruments, and computer models**
- The **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** forecasts weather for India
- Accurate weather predictions help farmers, fishermen, travellers, and save lives during disasters
