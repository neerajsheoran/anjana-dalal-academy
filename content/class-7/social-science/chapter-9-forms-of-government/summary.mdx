## In One Paragraph

Every country has a government, but governments differ in **who holds power**, **how they get it**, and **what they do with it**. **Democracy** ("rule of the people") gives power to citizens through elections — directly in small communities, or through elected representatives in large ones. India and the UK are **parliamentary democracies** (head of government from the legislature); the USA is a **presidential democracy** (head of government elected separately). A **monarchy** is rule by a hereditary king or queen — absolute (Saudi Arabia) or constitutional/ceremonial (UK, Japan). A **theocracy** is rule by religious law (Iran, Vatican City). A **dictatorship** is rule by one person or a small group with no limits — the 20th century saw terrible examples in Hitler, Stalin, and Mao. India's own tradition includes **rajadharma** (kings bound by moral duty), the **Vajji republic** (6th century BCE), and the remarkable **Uttaramerur inscription** in the Chola territory (10th century CE) with sealed-ballot village elections — 1,000 years before modern democracy.

## Key Takeaways

- A government's job: law and order, defence, services (education, health), economy, welfare, foreign relations.
- Four organs of any government: **legislature** (makes laws), **executive** (carries them out), **judiciary** (decides cases).
- **Democracy** = rule of the people, through direct voting (rare) or elected representatives (common).
- **Parliamentary democracy** — PM from the legislature, accountable to it (India, UK, Canada, Australia).
- **Presidential democracy** — President elected separately, fixed term, not in the legislature (USA, South Korea).
- Core democratic principles: equality, freedom, universal adult franchise, free elections, fundamental rights, independent judiciary, accountability.
- India gave **universal adult suffrage from 1951–52** — Switzerland gave women the vote only in 1971!
- **Monarchy** — absolute (king has all power, e.g., Saudi Arabia) or constitutional (king is symbolic, e.g., UK, Japan).
- Indian **rajadharma**: even kings must rule by moral law; power without dharma is illegitimate.
- **Theocracy** — religious law is supreme; clerics hold ultimate authority (Iran, Vatican City).
- **Dictatorship** — one person/group has absolute power. 20th-century dictators (Hitler, Stalin, Mao, Pol Pot) caused mass atrocities.
- India's own ancient democratic roots: **Vajji republic** (6th c. BCE) and **Uttaramerur** Chola village elections (10th c. CE) with sealed ballots and anti-corruption rules.
- India is the **largest democracy in the world** with ~970 million voters.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Power without limits corrupts; democracy is the limit.**

Whatever its frustrations, democracy gives ordinary citizens the right to question, to vote, to change governments peacefully. Dictatorship strips all this away. Democracy is precious.
