## In One Paragraph

After the fall of the Guptas around 550 CE, India entered four centuries (600–1000 CE) without a single empire — but with **many strong regional kingdoms** that produced extraordinary art, literature, and architecture. **Harshavardhana** (606–647 CE) ruled the last great North Indian empire from **Kannauj** and hosted the Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang** for 17 years. After Harsha, the **Tripartite Struggle** for Kannauj raged between the **Pratiharas** (north-west, builders of Khajuraho), **Palas** (Bengal, patrons of Nalanda and Vikramashila), and **Rashtrakutas** (Deccan, builders of the rock-cut **Kailasa Temple at Ellora**). In South India, the **Pallavas** built the wonders of **Mamallapuram**, and the **Chalukyas** built the temples of Badami, Aihole, and Pattadakal. Then the **Cholas** rose — **Rajaraja Chola I** built the **Brihadeeswara Temple**, and **Rajendra Chola I** launched naval expeditions reaching **Southeast Asia**. The **Bhakti movement** began in Tamil Nadu with the **Alvars** and **Nayanars**. In 712 CE, Arabs conquered Sindh — the first lasting Muslim presence in India. Despite political fragmentation, this was a period of **brilliant decentralised creativity**.

## Key Takeaways

- 600–1000 CE: no single empire in India, but many strong regional kingdoms.
- **Harshavardhana** (606–647 CE) — last great North Indian emperor; capital **Kannauj**; hosted Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang** for 17 years.
- **Xuanzang** brought back 600+ Sanskrit texts to China; major source for Indian Buddhism.
- **Tripartite Struggle** (8th-9th c.) — Pratiharas vs Palas vs Rashtrakutas — all competing for Kannauj.
- **Pratiharas**: built **Khajuraho** temples (UNESCO); repelled early Arab invasions.
- **Palas**: ruled Bengal-Bihar 750-1150 CE; supported **Nalanda, Vikramashila, Somapura**; spread Buddhism to Tibet and SE Asia.
- **Rashtrakutas**: Deccan; built the **Kailasa Temple at Ellora** — carved from a single rock.
- **Pallavas** (3rd-9th c.): South India; built **Mamallapuram** wonders; pioneered Dravidian temple style.
- **Chalukyas**: Deccan; built **Badami, Aihole, Pattadakal** (UNESCO); King **Pulakeshin II** defeated Harshavardhana.
- **Cholas**: rose from 9th c. **Rajaraja Chola I** built **Brihadeeswara Temple**. **Rajendra Chola I** led naval expeditions to **Sri Lanka, Maldives, Srivijaya** (Indonesia).
- Chola **bronze sculptures** (especially **Nataraja**) are among the greatest art in world history.
- **Bhakti movement** began in Tamil Nadu (6th-7th c.) with **Alvars** (Vaishnavite) and **Nayanars** (Shaivite); composed devotional poems in Tamil.
- **712 CE** — Arabs conquered **Sindh**: first lasting Muslim presence in South Asia.
- Indian astronomy, mathematics (with zero) reached the Arab world during this period.
- Religious pluralism: Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Muslims, Christians (Kerala since 1st c.), Jews, Zoroastrians (Parsis arrived 8th-10th c.) all present.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **India is most creative when its regions are most free.**

When there was no central empire, regional kings competed to patronise the best artists, scholars, and temple-builders. The "post-Gupta" centuries are not a dark age — they are one of India's most creative periods. Empires are not the only measure of greatness.
