## Weather, Seasons, Climate — What's the Difference?

Three words people use interchangeably but really shouldn't:

- **Weather** — what we experience hour to hour or day to day
- **Seasons** — patterns that repeat year after year (summer, monsoon, winter, etc.)
- **Climate** — the long-term weather pattern of a region over many decades

A single rainy day doesn't change climate. A region that consistently has heavy rain for centuries has a wet climate.

## The Six Indian Seasons (Ritus)

While most of the world divides the year into **four seasons** (spring, summer, autumn, winter), traditional India divides it into **six ritus**:

1. **Vasanta** — Spring (Feb-Apr)
2. **Grishma** — Summer (Apr-Jun)
3. **Varsha** — Monsoon / Rainy (Jun-Aug)
4. **Sharad** — Autumn (Aug-Oct)
5. **Hemanta** — Pre-winter (Oct-Dec)
6. **Shishir** — Winter (Dec-Feb)

Each ritu has its own:
- **Festivals** — Vasanta Panchami in spring, Sharad Purnima in autumn, Holi at the end of winter
- **Foods** — mangoes in grishma, lassi in summer heat, til-gud in shishir winter
- **Clothes** — light cotton in summer, woollens in winter
- **Crops** — different sowings and harvests in different ritus

The six-ritu system shows how deeply Indian culture has observed seasonal change. Music has **ragas for seasons**, poetry has **verses for each ritu**, and Ayurvedic medicine prescribes **different foods and habits by season**.

## Why India Has So Many Climates

India is huge and geographically diverse. Within one country, you can find almost every climate type that exists in the world.

### 1. Alpine Climate — High Himalayas

- **Cold and snowy winters, cool summers**
- Found in Ladakh, parts of Himachal, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal
- Thickest clothing seen anywhere in India

### 2. Temperate Climate — Lower Himalayas and Hill Stations

- Moderate winters, mild summers
- **Hill stations** like Shimla, Manali, Mussoorie, Darjeeling, Ooty, Kodaikanal
- Popular escapes from hot Indian summers

### 3. Subtropical Climate — Northern Plains

- **Very hot summers (40-45°C)** and **cold winters (5-10°C)**
- Punjab, Haryana, UP, Bihar, parts of MP
- Most of India's **wheat** is grown here

### 4. Arid Climate — Thar Desert

- **Extremely hot days, cool nights, very little rain**
- Western Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat
- Unique water-saving traditions: tankas, baolis

### 5. Tropical Wet — Western Coast and Northeast

- **Heavy monsoon rains**, warm year-round
- Western Ghats, Kerala, Konkan, Mangalore, Goa
- Also: Northeast India (Assam, Meghalaya)
- **Cherrapunji and Mawsynram** in Meghalaya are among the **wettest places on Earth**
- Ideal for **rice, coconut, spices, tea**

### 6. Semi-Arid — Deccan Plateau

- Hot summers, mild winters, moderate rainfall
- Most of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, parts of Andhra
- Cotton-friendly black soil

### 7. Tropical Wet and Dry — Most of the Subcontinent

- Distinct **wet season (monsoon)** and **dry season**
- Most of central and northern India
- This is the climate most Indians live in

## The Monsoon — India's Heartbeat

For Indians, the **monsoon** is more than weather. It's a national event. It's the reason crops grow, dams fill, festivals happen.

### What is the Monsoon?

The word **"monsoon"** comes from the Arabic **mausim** meaning "season." It refers to **seasonal winds** that reverse direction.

There are two monsoons:

**1. Southwest Monsoon (June–September)**
- The big one. Brings about **75% of India's annual rainfall**.
- Winds blow from southwest to northeast (from sea to land)
- Hits Kerala first (around June 1), then sweeps across India over 4-6 weeks
- Splits into two branches: Arabian Sea branch (waters western India) and Bay of Bengal branch (waters eastern and northern India)

**2. Northeast Monsoon (October–November)**
- The smaller one. Mainly affects Tamil Nadu and parts of Andhra
- Winds blow from northeast to southwest (from land to sea)
- Brings most of Tamil Nadu's rainfall

### How is the Monsoon Formed?

In summer, the Indian landmass heats up rapidly. The Tibetan Plateau especially gets very hot. **Hot air rises** and creates **low pressure** over land.

Meanwhile, the **Indian Ocean** is relatively cooler. Cooler air = higher pressure.

Air moves from high pressure to low pressure. So **moist winds from the Indian Ocean** rush toward the land. As these winds rise over the Western Ghats and the Himalayas, the moisture **condenses and falls as rain** — the monsoon.

In winter, the pattern reverses. Land cools down faster than the ocean. The winds blow from land toward sea, bringing dry weather — and the Northeast Monsoon takes a small detour to bring some rain to Tamil Nadu.

### Why is the Monsoon So Important?

- **Farming** — ~60% of Indian farmland depends on rain (not irrigation). Without good monsoon, crops fail.
- **Drinking water** — most lakes and rivers refill during monsoon
- **Economy** — a poor monsoon can shrink India's GDP by 1-2%
- **Power** — many dams generate hydroelectric power from monsoon-fed water
- **Culture** — countless songs, poems, and films celebrate the rains. Hindi film industry has its monsoon ragas.

A good monsoon means a good year. A bad one means hardship for millions of farmers.

## Climate and Indian Life

Climate shapes almost everything in India:

**Food**:
- **Wheat** in dry, cool north (subtropical climate)
- **Rice** in wet, warm regions (tropical wet)
- **Millets** in semi-arid plateau regions
- **Coconut, banana, spices** in tropical coastal areas

**Clothing**:
- Cotton in hot dry regions
- Wool and pashmina in cold Himalayan regions
- Light fabrics in humid coastal areas

**Houses**:
- **Thick mud walls** in deserts (cool inside)
- **Sloping roofs** in heavy rain areas (Kerala, Konkan)
- **Wood-and-stone** in cold mountains (Himachal kath-kuni)
- **Flat roofs** in dry areas (terraces for drying clothes, sleeping in summer)

**Festivals**:
- **Harvest festivals** in winter (Pongal, Lohri, Bihu, Makar Sankranti) — celebrating winter crop harvest
- **Onam** in autumn — Kerala's harvest festival
- **Holi** at end of winter — welcoming spring

## Climate Change — A Threat to All of This

In the last few decades, India's climate has been changing:

- **Hotter summers** — record temperatures every few years
- **Erratic monsoon** — late starts, heavy bursts, then long dry spells
- **Glacier loss** in Himalayas — threatening future river water
- **Stronger cyclones** because warmer oceans give them more energy
- **More floods AND more droughts** — extremes both ways

This is **climate change**, and humans are causing most of it through burning coal, oil, and gas.

For India, climate change isn't theoretical. Glacier loss threatens our water security. Monsoon disruption threatens farming. Stronger cyclones threaten coastal cities. Heat waves threaten outdoor workers.

This is the challenge of the next generation — to find ways to grow our economy while reducing the damage to our climate.

## Before We Close

- **Weather** changes daily; **climate** is the long-term pattern over decades
- India traditionally has **six seasons (ritus)** — vasanta, grishma, varsha, sharad, hemanta, shishir
- India has **almost every climate type** — alpine, temperate, subtropical, arid, tropical wet, semi-arid
- The **monsoon** (southwest in summer, northeast in winter) is India's most important weather event
- **75% of India's rain** comes from the southwest monsoon
- Climate shapes Indian food, clothing, houses, and festivals
- **Climate change** is altering India's traditional climate patterns — a major challenge for the future
