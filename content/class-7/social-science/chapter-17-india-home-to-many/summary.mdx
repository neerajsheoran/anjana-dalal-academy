## In One Paragraph

For over 2,000 years, India has welcomed refugees fleeing persecution from around the world. The **Bene Israel Jews** were shipwrecked on the Konkan coast around 175 BCE and became Marathi-speaking oil-pressers while keeping their faith. **Cochin Jews** were welcomed by the Raja of Kochi "as long as the world, sun, and moon endure." **Syriac Christians** of Kerala trace their faith to Apostle Thomas in 52 CE — making them one of the oldest Christian communities in the world. **Parsis** fled Persia in the 8th-10th centuries CE; their famous "sugar in milk" story symbolised their promise to add sweetness to Indian society. They later produced legendary families like the **Tatas, Godrejs, Wadias**, scientists like **Homi Bhabha**, and political leaders like **Dadabhai Naoroji**. **Tibetans** fled Chinese occupation in 1959 — the **Dalai Lama** has lived in Dharamsala for over 65 years. India has also welcomed **Sri Lankan Tamils, Bangladeshis, Afghans, Bahais, Armenians**. This Indian tradition of welcoming refugees flows from deep cultural values like **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** ("the world is one family") and **Atithi Devo Bhava** ("the guest is god"). Most refugee communities followed the model of **integration without forced assimilation** — adopting Indian language and customs while keeping their distinct religion.

## Key Takeaways

- **Bene Israel Jews**: Came around 175 BCE; shipwrecked on the Konkan coast; integrated as "Shanwar Telis" (Saturday oil-pressers); preserved Judaism for 2,000 years.
- **Cochin Jews**: Came in waves between 9th-16th centuries; royal welcome by Raja of Kochi; built Paradesi Synagogue (1568).
- **Baghdadi Jews**: Came in 18th-19th centuries; **Sassoon family** built Mumbai's Sassoon Docks, library, hospitals, schools.
- **Syriac Christians**: Trace faith to Apostle Thomas (52 CE); one of the world's oldest Christian communities.
- **Parsis**: Fled Persia in 8th-10th centuries CE; "sugar in milk" story (dissolved into society, added sweetness); produced Tatas, Godrejs, Wadias, Dadabhai Naoroji, Homi Bhabha, Zubin Mehta.
- Parsi community is small (~60-70,000 worldwide) but disproportionately influential.
- **Tibetans**: Fled Chinese occupation in 1959; Dalai Lama based in **Dharamsala** since then; ~100,000 Tibetans live in India.
- **Dalai Lama** won the Nobel Peace Prize (1989) and is a global ambassador for peace.
- Other communities: Sri Lankan Tamils, Bangladeshis (1971), Afghans, Bahais (Lotus Temple in Delhi), Armenians, Burmese.
- **Swami Vivekananda's 1893 Chicago speech**: famously expressed pride in India's tradition of sheltering the persecuted of all faiths.
- Deep cultural values support this tradition: **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** (world is one family), **Sarve Bhavantu Sukhinah** (may all be happy), **Atithi Devo Bhava** (guest is god), religious pluralism.
- Model: **integration without forced assimilation** — adopt local language and customs, retain distinct religion and identity.
- India has been one of the world's most consistently welcoming countries to refugees.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Atithi Devo Bhava — The guest is god.**

This ancient Sanskrit teaching has shaped Indian behaviour toward refugees for thousands of years. Whether the guest stays for a meal, a night, or a lifetime — they are honoured. This is one of India's deepest gifts to the world.
