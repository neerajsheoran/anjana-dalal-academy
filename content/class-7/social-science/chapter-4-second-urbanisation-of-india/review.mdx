## Key Concepts

- **First Urbanisation** of India = Indus-Sarasvati civilisation (2600-1900 BCE)
- After 1900 BCE, **no major cities for ~1,000 years**
- **Second Urbanisation** began in the **1st millennium BCE** in the Ganga plains

## Janapadas

- **Janapada** = "the land where the people set foot"
- Each janapada = territory of a clan, led by a **raja**
- The raja consulted assemblies:
  - **Sabha** — council of senior members
  - **Samiti** — larger gathering of all people
- These words first appear in the **Vedas** — collective decision-making is ancient in India

## Mahajanapadas (~600 BCE)

By 7th century BCE, smaller janapadas merged into 16 **mahajanapadas**.

| Mahajanapada | Capital | Modern Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Magadha** | Rajagriha → Pataliputra | Bihar |
| **Kosala** | Shravasti | Eastern UP |
| **Vatsa** | Kaushambi | Near Allahabad |
| **Avanti** | Ujjayini | Madhya Pradesh |
| **Kuru** | Indraprastha | Near Delhi |
| **Vajji** | Vaishali | Bihar |
| **Gandhara** | Takshashila | Pakistan |
| **Shurasena** | Mathura | UP |

(Most were in the Ganga plains.)

## What Cities Looked Like

- Massive **walls and ramparts**
- **Moats** outside walls
- **Narrow gateways** to control entry
- Markets, workshops, palaces, religious centres
- Many capitals are **still living cities today**: Patna, Mathura, Ujjain, Rajgir

## Why Cities Returned

1. **Agricultural surplus** from Ganga plains
2. **Iron technology** — better tools, weapons, plough
3. **Trade networks** — rivers + roads
4. **Coinage** — punch-marked silver pieces, easier than barter
5. **Specialisation** — workers focused on specific crafts

## Gana-Sanghas — Early Republics

Most mahajanapadas had kings. But some — **gana-sanghas** — had council-based rule:
- A council of leading family heads governed together
- Decisions by **discussion and majority vote**
- No single king
- **Vajji** (Licchavi clan around Vaishali) was the most famous example

The **Buddha** praised this system and modelled his Buddhist sangha on it.

## Kautilya on Cities (Arthashastra, ~300 BCE)

A kingdom needs:
- **Fortified capital** and frontier towns
- **Cultivable land**, mines, forests, pastures
- **Reliable water** (not just rain)
- **Good roads and waterways**
- **Productive economy** with varied goods

## Magadha — The Greatest

- Most powerful mahajanapada
- Capitals: Rajagriha → Pataliputra (modern Patna)
- Advantages: fertile land, iron, trade routes, ambitious rulers (Bimbisara, Ajatashatru)
- Eventually became the **Mauryan Empire** under Chandragupta Maurya (~321 BCE)

## Common Confusions

- **Janapada and Mahajanapada are not the same.** Janapadas were smaller, earlier; mahajanapadas were larger, came later (some janapadas merged to form them).
- **Sabha and Samiti are not modern democracy.** They were council/assembly systems — early forms of consultative governance, not universal franchise.
- **Gana-sangha is not pure democracy either.** It was rule by a council of leading families — closer to oligarchy or aristocratic republic.
- **The Second Urbanisation never ended.** It started 2,500 years ago and continues today — modern Indian cities are part of the same long historical movement.
