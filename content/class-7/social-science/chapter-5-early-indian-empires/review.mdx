## Key Concepts

- **Empire** = collection of kingdoms under one supreme ruler ("imperium" = supreme power)
- Tributary kingdoms keep their own rulers but pay tribute to the emperor
- Sanskrit terms: **Samraj** (lord of all), **Adhiraja** (overlord), **Rajadhiraja** (king of kings)

## The Mauryan Empire (~321-185 BCE)

India's **first great empire**. Founded from Magadha.

### Three Major Rulers

| Ruler | Approximate dates | Key achievement |
|---|---|---|
| **Chandragupta Maurya** | 321-297 BCE | Founder; defeated Nandas and Greeks |
| **Bindusara** | 297-273 BCE | Extended into south India |
| **Ashoka the Great** | 268-232 BCE | Conquered Kalinga, then renounced violence; spread Buddhism |

## Chandragupta Maurya

- Came from humble background
- Guided by **Kautilya (Chanakya)** — his teacher and advisor
- Overthrew the unpopular **Nanda dynasty** in Magadha
- Defeated Greek successors of **Alexander the Great**
- Made peace with **Seleucus**, who gave his daughter in marriage

## Kautilya and the Arthashastra

- **Arthashastra** = ancient text on **statecraft, economics, and war**
- Covers administration, taxation, law, espionage, foreign affairs
- Some compare it to Machiavelli's *The Prince* — but **1,700 years earlier**
- Famous line: "There cannot be a country without people, and there is no kingdom without a country"

## Ashoka — The Most Famous Mauryan

### Early Reign
- Started as a ruthless conqueror

### The Kalinga War (~261 BCE)
- Ashoka invaded **Kalinga** (modern coastal Odisha)
- His own edict records: **100,000 killed, 150,000 captured**
- The horror of battle transformed him

### After Kalinga — Dhamma
- Embraced **Buddhism** and a philosophy he called **dhamma**
- **Ahimsa** (non-violence) toward all beings
- **Religious tolerance**
- **Compassion** for poor, elderly, helpless
- **Welfare of subjects** as the king's duty
- **Care for animals** — built animal hospitals

### Ashoka's Edicts
- 80+ edicts carved on rocks and pillars
- Written in **Prakrit** (people's language)
- Spread his message of dhamma across the empire

### The Sarnath Lion Capital
- Top of Ashoka's pillar at Sarnath (near Varanasi)
- Four lions back-to-back, with the **Dhamma Chakra** (wheel) below
- Today: **India's National Emblem**
- The **Ashoka Chakra** is on **India's national flag**

## How the Mauryans Governed

- **Capital**: Pataliputra (modern Patna)
- **Provinces** led by governors (often royal princes)
- **Districts** within provinces
- **Villages** as basic unit, with local headmen
- **Departments**: revenue, treasury, army, justice, public works, espionage, foreign affairs, welfare
- **Royal Highway** from Pataliputra to Taxila (1,500+ km) — basis of today's **Grand Trunk Road**
- **Punch-marked silver coins** widely used

## Megasthenes — The Greek Ambassador

- Sent by Greek king **Seleucus** to Chandragupta's court
- Wrote book **"Indika"** describing his observations
- Said Pataliputra was **one of the world's largest cities**
- Walls had **570 towers and 64 gates**
- Independent foreign confirmation of Mauryan greatness

## Why the Mauryans Matter

1. First **imperial unification** of the Subcontinent
2. **Arthashastra** = foundational political text
3. **Ashoka's dhamma** spread Buddhism across Asia
4. **National symbols** — Ashokan lions, Ashoka Chakra
5. **Idea of righteous rule** — king's duty is welfare of subjects

## Common Confusions

- **Kingdom vs Empire**: A kingdom is one state under one king. An empire is many kingdoms under one supreme ruler.
- **Ashoka's Dhamma is not just Buddhism**: It was a broader ethical code — including ahimsa, tolerance, welfare. Many non-Buddhists could follow it.
- **The Mauryans came BEFORE the Guptas**: Mauryans 321-185 BCE; Guptas 320-550 CE. Roughly 500 years apart.
- **Chanakya = Kautilya = Vishnugupta**: Same person, three names. The advisor of Chandragupta who wrote the Arthashastra.
