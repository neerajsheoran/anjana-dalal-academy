## In One Paragraph

Indian agriculture is **9,000 years old** (earliest barley and millet at Mehrgarh, ~7000 BCE) and still supports nearly half the country today — **46% of working Indians**, contributing **18% of GDP**, with **over 75% of rural farmworkers being women**. India has **seven climate types** and **15 agro-climatic zones**, so almost every crop grows somewhere — wheat in Punjab, rice in Bengal, tea in Assam, saffron only in Kashmir. The **southwest** (Jun-Sep) and **northeast** (Oct-Dec) **monsoons** decide success or failure of crops. Farming follows three seasons: **kharif** (monsoon), **rabi** (winter), and **zaid** (summer). India is the **world's largest producer of milk, pulses, sugarcane, cotton, and spices**, and **second-largest in rice, wheat, and tea**. The **Green Revolution** of the 1960s-70s made India self-sufficient in food but caused soil and water damage. Today, Indian farming is trying to combine tradition with technology — drip irrigation, solar pumps, organic methods, digital advisories — while solving deep problems: small farm size, debt, climate change, and falling groundwater.

## Key Takeaways

- Indian agriculture is **9,000+ years old** — Mehrgarh barley/millet, ~7000 BCE.
- Indus-Saraswati civilisation grew **wheat, barley, rice, cotton**, and practised **intercropping** (Kalibangan, 2800 BCE).
- Ancient texts on farming: **Krishi Parashara**, **Surapala's Vrikshayurveda**, **Brihatsamhita**, **Arthashastra**, **Sangam literature**.
- **46% of Indians** work in agriculture; it contributes **18% of GDP**.
- **75%+ of rural agricultural workers are women** (often invisible in stereotype images of farmers).
- India has **7 climate types** and **15 agro-climatic zones** — leading to crop diversity.
- **Southwest monsoon** (Jun-Sep) and **northeast monsoon** (Oct-Dec) are decisive for agriculture.
- Three cropping seasons: **kharif** (rice, maize, cotton — monsoon), **rabi** (wheat, gram — winter), **zaid** (melon, cucumber — summer).
- India is **world #1** in milk, pulses, sugarcane, cotton, spices.
- India is **world #2** in rice, wheat, tea.
- **Green Revolution** (1960s-70s) — Dr. M.S. Swaminathan; ended food shortage but caused soil/water damage.
- **White Revolution** — milk (Verghese Kurien, Amul) made India the world's largest milk producer.
- Modern Indian farming: **drip irrigation**, **solar pumps**, **organic farming** (Sikkim 100% organic), **FPOs**, **eNAM**, **PM-KISAN**.
- Problems: small landholdings, monsoon dependency, soil degradation, falling groundwater, debt, climate change.
- 2023 was the **International Year of Millets**, championed by India.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **No farmer, no food. No food, no civilisation.**

Every Indian meal — every chapati, every bowl of dal, every spoonful of curd — is the result of someone, somewhere, working the land. Honouring the farmer is honouring the foundation of our country.
