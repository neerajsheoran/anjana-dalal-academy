## In One Paragraph

A **bank** is a financial institution that takes deposits from savers and gives loans to borrowers — connecting the two groups and earning a small profit by intermediating. Banks offer different accounts (**savings, current, fixed deposit, recurring deposit, loan**) for different needs, paying interest on deposits and charging interest on loans. The **magic of compound interest** turns small regular savings into significant wealth over time — the **Rule of 72** says dividing 72 by your interest rate tells you how many years it takes to double your money. Above all Indian banks is the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** — issuing currency, regulating banks, setting interest rates. India has public sector banks (SBI), private banks (HDFC), foreign banks, cooperatives, payment banks, and more. India's banking revolution in the last decade has been transformative: **Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (2014)** opened 500+ million bank accounts; **DBT** sends government benefits directly to citizens; **UPI (2016)** made India the world leader in real-time digital payments; the **JAM Trinity** (Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile) created world-class digital financial infrastructure. Beyond banks: **stock markets** (BSE, NSE), **mutual funds**, **insurance**, **pensions**, and **regulators** (SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA).

## Key Takeaways

- A **bank** connects savers and borrowers — takes deposits, gives loans, earns interest spread.
- Account types: **savings** (low interest, limited withdrawals), **current** (no interest, no limits, for businesses), **fixed deposit** (locked period, higher interest), **recurring deposit** (monthly savings), **loan**, **demat**.
- **Interest**: bank pays on deposits, charges on loans.
- **Compound interest**: interest on interest; grows money dramatically over time.
- **Rule of 72**: 72 ÷ interest rate = years to double money.
- **Time** is the magic ingredient — starting saving early matters more than saving more later.
- **Loan types**: home, car, education, personal, business, credit card; repaid via **EMIs**.
- **Good debt** (home, education, business) vs **bad debt** (excessive consumption).
- **RBI** (Reserve Bank of India) — central bank since 1935; issues rupees, regulates banks, sets interest rates.
- **Public Sector Banks**: SBI (largest), PNB, Bank of Baroda, Canara Bank.
- **Private Sector Banks**: HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak Mahindra.
- **Specialised banks**: NABARD (agriculture), SIDBI (small industry), EXIM Bank, NHB (housing).
- **Payment Banks** (Airtel, Paytm, Jio): can accept deposits, can't lend.
- **Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (2014)**: 500+ million accounts opened; banking for all.
- **DBT** (Direct Benefit Transfer): government subsidies sent directly to bank accounts, reducing corruption.
- **UPI (2016)**: India leads world in real-time digital payments — more transactions than the entire rest of the world combined.
- **JAM Trinity** = Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile: digital financial infrastructure of world-class quality.
- Risks: bank failures (insured up to ₹5 lakh by DICGC), NPAs, fraud (phishing, OTP scams), digital exclusion.
- Beyond banks: **BSE/NSE** stock markets, **mutual funds**, **insurance** (LIC, HDFC Life), **pension funds** (EPFO, NPS).
- Regulators: **RBI** (banks), **SEBI** (markets), **IRDAI** (insurance), **PFRDA** (pensions).

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Time + Interest = Wealth.**

The most powerful financial lesson is to start saving early and let compound interest work over decades. ₹1,000/month starting at 18 can grow to far more than the same amount starting at 35. **Time** is the magic ingredient.
