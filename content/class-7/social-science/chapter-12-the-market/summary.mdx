## In One Paragraph

A **market** is any place — physical or online — where buyers and sellers meet to exchange goods, services, or money. Markets need three things to exist: **buyers**, **sellers**, and an **agreed price**. Prices arise from the interaction of **supply** (what sellers offer) and **demand** (what buyers want). When prices are too high, goods don't sell; when too low, sellers lose money; the price settles where both sides are reasonably satisfied. The famous **Hampi Bazaar** of the Vijayanagara Empire (16th century) was one of the world's great markets, with traders from Persia, Arabia, China, and Europe. Today, markets come in many forms: **physical** (mandis, malls) and **online** (Amazon, Flipkart), **domestic** (within India) and **international** (exports/imports). India exports software, pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, textiles, and gems (~$435 billion); imports crude oil, gold, electronics, and machinery (~$675 billion). Markets can fail through monopoly, hidden information, or inequality — which is why government regulation and consumer protection laws also matter.

## Key Takeaways

- A **market** = any place (physical or virtual) where buyers and sellers exchange goods, services, or money.
- Markets need **buyers**, **sellers**, and a **mutually agreed price** to function.
- Indian names for market: **bazaar**, **mandi**, **haat**, **markatte** (Kannada), **santhai** (Tamil), **vippanam** (Sanskrit).
- The **Hampi Bazaar** (Vijayanagara, 16th c.) was called "the best-provided city in the world" by Portuguese traveller Domingos Paes.
- Prices balance **supply and demand**: too high → unsold; too low → sellers lose; just right → both sides win.
- Vegetables get cheaper at night (perishables); woollens get cheaper in spring (off-season).
- **Bargaining** works in small markets; **fixed prices** work in large stores.
- **Physical markets**: mandis, kirana, malls. **Online markets**: Amazon, Flipkart, Swiggy, Uber.
- **Domestic** vs **International** markets — international means **exports** (selling abroad) and **imports** (buying from abroad).
- India exports: software, pharma, engineering, textiles, gems, agriculture (~$435B).
- India imports: crude oil, gold, electronics, machinery (~$675B).
- **Wholesale** = bulk sales to other businesses; **retail** = sales to final consumers.
- **Services markets** (haircuts, taxis, doctors) differ from **goods markets** — services can't be stored.
- Markets can **fail**: monopoly, hidden information, externalities (pollution), public goods, inequality.
- **Consumer rights** in India: safety, information, choice, redressal, education.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Markets connect strangers in cooperation.**

Every purchase you make involves dozens of strangers — farmers, factory workers, truck drivers, packers, shopkeepers — all coordinating through prices to get goods to you. The market is one of humanity's greatest cooperative inventions. Used wisely, it serves everyone.
