## Key Concepts

- The Earth **rotates** on its axis (from west to east) -- one full rotation takes about **24 hours**
- The **axis of rotation** is an imaginary line from the North Pole to the South Pole
- Earth's rotation causes **day and night** -- the sunlit half has day, the dark half has night
- The Sun appears to move east to west, but it is actually the **Earth spinning** west to east
- Dawn arrives in **eastern India** (Arunachal Pradesh) before western India (Gujarat) because of the Earth's rotation direction
- The **Pole Star (Dhruva Tara)** appears stationary because Earth's axis points nearly at it
- **Aryabhata** (499 CE) calculated Earth's rotation period as ~23 hours, 56 minutes, 4.1 seconds -- remarkably accurate
- The Earth **revolves** around the Sun in a nearly circular orbit, taking about **365 days and 6 hours**
- The extra 6 hours add up to one extra day every 4 years --> **leap year** (366 days)
- **Rotation** = Earth spinning on its axis (causes day/night); **Revolution** = Earth orbiting the Sun (causes seasons)
- **Seasons** are caused by the **tilt of Earth's axis** (23.5 degrees), NOT by distance from the Sun
- When Northern Hemisphere tilts **towards** the Sun --> summer in India, winter in Australia (and vice versa)
- **Summer solstice** (~21 June): longest day in Northern Hemisphere
- **Winter solstice** (~22 December): shortest day in Northern Hemisphere
- **Equinoxes** (~21 March, ~23 September): day and night are equal (12 hours each)
- At the poles: 6 months of continuous day followed by 6 months of continuous night
- **Solar eclipse**: Moon comes between Sun and Earth, blocking sunlight (Moon's shadow falls on Earth)
- **Lunar eclipse**: Earth comes between Sun and Moon, casting its shadow on the Moon
- During a total lunar eclipse, the Moon turns **reddish-copper** ("blood moon") due to Earth's atmosphere bending red light

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Rotation | The spinning of the Earth on its own axis (takes ~24 hours) |
| Revolution | The movement of the Earth around the Sun in its orbit (~365.25 days) |
| Axis of rotation | The imaginary line from North Pole to South Pole around which Earth spins |
| Pole Star (Dhruva Tara) | The star that appears nearly stationary because Earth's axis points towards it |
| Leap year | A year with 366 days, occurring every 4 years to account for the extra 6 hours per year |
| Solar eclipse | An event where the Moon blocks sunlight from reaching the Earth |
| Lunar eclipse | An event where the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon |
| Summer solstice | The day with the longest period of sunlight (~21 June in the Northern Hemisphere) |
| Winter solstice | The day with the shortest period of sunlight (~22 December in the Northern Hemisphere) |
| Equinox | A day when day and night are equal in length (12 hours each) |

## Key Dates

| Event | Date | Significance |

| Winter Solstice | ~22 December | Shortest day (Northern Hemisphere) |

## Remember These

- Seasons are caused by Earth's **axial tilt**, NOT distance from the Sun -- Earth is actually closest to the Sun in **January** (Northern Hemisphere winter!)
- **Never look directly at the Sun** during a solar eclipse -- it can cause permanent blindness
- Lunar eclipses are **safe to watch** with bare eyes; solar eclipses are **NOT**
- The Moon can cover the Sun during a solar eclipse because though the Sun is 400x wider, it is also 400x farther away (same apparent size)
- Aryabhata's rotation calculation from 1500+ years ago matches modern measurements almost exactly
