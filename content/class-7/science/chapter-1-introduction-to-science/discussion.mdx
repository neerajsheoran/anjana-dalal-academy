## What Even Is Science?

**Meera:** Arjun, our class teacher said yesterday that science is not just a subject — it is a way of looking at the world. I have been thinking about that all evening and I cannot decide if it makes sense.

**Arjun:** Yaar, that sounds like the kind of thing teachers say in a deep voice but it does not mean anything. Science is just a subject with chapters and exams. Like maths.

**Meera:** But hear me out. This morning I noticed that my mother's dahi sets faster in summer than in winter. Why does that happen?

**Arjun:** Bacteria? Heat? I don't know, who cares?

**Meera:** That is exactly what I mean. You just said two things in five seconds — bacteria, heat. You did not know but you tried to guess. That is science.

**Arjun:** Just guessing is science?

**Meera:** Not just guessing. Noticing. Wondering. Trying to explain. The teacher's point was that everyone does this — it is not a special activity for people in lab coats. We do it ourselves without realising.

**Arjun:** Hmm. So when I notice my phone heats up while charging and I wonder why — that is science too?

**Meera:** Now you are getting it.

## How Different Chapters Connect

**Arjun:** OK but here is a real problem. Look at our textbook. We have chapters on acids, electricity, metals, plants, even growing up. They feel like five totally different subjects shoved into one book.

**Meera:** That is what I thought too at first. But this morning I started spotting connections.

**Arjun:** Like what?

**Meera:** Like — acids. Your father grows tomatoes in the kitchen garden, right? Did you know if the soil is too acidic, tomatoes do not grow well?

**Arjun:** No way.

**Meera:** Yes! So an acid chapter is suddenly connected to growing food. Which connects to biology. Which connects to your dinner plate.

**Arjun:** OK that is one. But metals and electricity?

**Meera:** Why do you think the wire inside your charger is made of copper?

**Arjun:** Because copper conducts electricity. Everyone knows that.

**Meera:** Right. So the chapter on metals is also the chapter on electricity. And the chapter on heat connects to why Chennai feels hot and humid while Shimla feels cold. That is physics meeting geography.

**Arjun:** So all the chapters are connected.

**Meera:** Like roots of a banyan tree. They look separate above the ground but they all share the same trunk below.

**Arjun:** That is actually a nice image. I take back what I said.

## Why Asking Questions Is Powerful

**Meera:** We played a strange game in class today. Teacher gave us an ANSWER and we had to invent a question that could lead to it.

**Arjun:** Backwards? Sounds weird.

**Meera:** It was actually fun. The answer was "because the crow stole the roti". I came up with "Why was Radha chasing a crow across the terrace?"

**Arjun:** Ha. Mine would have been "Why is Daadi shouting?"

**Meera:** See? Same answer, totally different questions. That is the point teacher wanted to show. In science, asking the right question is often more important than finding the answer.

**Arjun:** I don't get how that helps me with exams.

**Meera:** It is bigger than exams. Have you heard of Jagadish Chandra Bose?

**Arjun:** Vaguely. The plant scientist?

**Meera:** Everyone in the 1800s assumed plants were just... things. Like rocks. Bose asked a question that no one had bothered to ask — "Do plants feel things? Do they respond to touch and sound?"

**Arjun:** And?

**Meera:** He invented an instrument to measure it, and proved that plants DO respond. They have a kind of sensitivity. That one question changed how the entire world thinks about plants. Over a century later, his work is still studied.

**Arjun:** So a single question can change the world.

**Meera:** And it usually starts with something that everyone else has stopped noticing.

## Science Cuts Both Ways

**Arjun:** Meera, I have been thinking. If science is so powerful, can it also cause damage?

**Meera:** Definitely.

**Arjun:** Like what?

**Meera:** Take pesticides. They help farmers protect crops, but if you spray too much, the chemicals go into the soil, into water, into our food. Many Indian rivers are now polluted because of factory waste.

**Arjun:** Delhi air in winter. My cousin in Gurugram says some days they cannot even see across the road.

**Meera:** Exactly. That smoke is partly stubble burning, partly vehicle emissions. Both come from real human needs — farmers clearing fields, people getting to work. The science that gave us these tools is the same science that has to fix what they damaged.

**Arjun:** So science is the problem AND the solution?

**Meera:** Science REVEALS things. Then humans decide what to do with them. Scientists are now developing electric buses, solar-powered homes, biodegradable packaging, new ways to dispose of stubble without burning. The fix is also coming from science.

**Arjun:** So when I save water at home — that is also kind of science in action?

**Meera:** Yes. Every time you choose to save water, walk instead of taking a lift, switch off a fan you are not using — you are applying scientific understanding responsibly. Class 7 science is not just for marks. It teaches you to think well enough to make smart everyday choices.

## Don't Just Read — Explore

**Arjun:** So how do we actually do well in this subject?

**Meera:** Don't just read the chapters. Do them.

**Arjun:** Meaning?

**Meera:** Touch things. Test things. When we study acids and bases, we will dip haldi paper in kitchen ingredients and watch the colour change. When we study circuits, we will build one with a battery and a bulb. When we study heat, we will time how long chai stays warm in a kulhad vs a steel glass.

**Arjun:** And if my experiment goes wrong?

**Meera:** Then you have learned something nobody else expected. Have you heard of Vikram Sarabhai — the father of India's space programme?

**Arjun:** ISRO guy, yes.

**Meera:** Before ISRO became what it is today, his team had many failed rocket launches. Each one taught them something they had not known. Failure was not the opposite of success — it was part of the journey to it.

**Arjun:** So there is no such thing as a failed experiment in science?

**Meera:** Just experiments with unexpected results. The unexpected ones often teach you the most.

**Arjun:** Got it. Be curious, try things, learn from mistakes. Don't just memorise.

**Meera:** That is the whole subject in one sentence. Now come, Daadi made pakoras. Let's eat before we discuss why oil heats up faster than water.

**Arjun:** Yaar, you are going to make me a scientist before the end of this term.
