## Key Concepts

- All materials can be broadly sorted into **metals** and **non-metals** based on their properties
- **Lustre**: metals have a characteristic shine (metallic lustre); non-metals are dull
- **Malleability**: metals can be hammered into thin sheets without breaking; non-metals are **brittle**
- **Ductility**: metals can be drawn into thin wires; non-metals cannot
- **Sonority**: metals produce a ringing sound when struck (sonorous); non-metals produce a dull thud
- Metals are good conductors of **heat** and **electricity**; non-metals are poor conductors (insulators)
- Metal oxides are **basic** in nature; non-metal oxides are **acidic** in nature
- **Rusting** occurs when iron reacts with both **oxygen and moisture** together -- removing either one prevents rust
- Rust is **iron oxide** -- a new substance, so rusting is a chemical change
- **Corrosion** is the gradual degradation of any metal surface (copper turns green, silver turns black)
- Rust prevention methods: **painting**, **oiling/greasing**, **galvanisation** (coating with zinc)
- **Sodium** and **potassium** are metals so soft they can be cut with a knife
- **Mercury** is the only metal that is **liquid** at room temperature
- Sodium is stored in **kerosene oil** (reacts violently with air and water)
- Phosphorus is stored **under water** (catches fire spontaneously in air)
- Non-metals vital for life: **oxygen** (breathing), **carbon** (basis of life molecules), **nitrogen** (fertilisers), **chlorine** (water purification), **iodine** (antiseptic)
- Metals and non-metals are categories of **elements** -- pure substances that cannot be broken down further
- Materials like plastic, glass, rubber are not elements and cannot be classified as metals or non-metals
- **118 elements** have been identified so far

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Lustre | The characteristic shine of metals |
| Malleability | The ability to be hammered or pressed into thin sheets |
| Ductility | The ability to be drawn into thin wires |
| Sonority | The ability to produce a ringing sound when struck |
| Rusting | Formation of iron oxide when iron reacts with oxygen and moisture |
| Corrosion | Gradual deterioration of metal surfaces due to reactions with air, moisture, or chemicals |
| Galvanisation | Coating iron with a layer of zinc to prevent rusting |
| Element | A pure substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means |

## Remember These

- Rusting requires **both** air (oxygen) AND moisture -- not just one
- The **Iron Pillar** near Qutub Minar (1600+ years old) has barely rusted -- proof of ancient India's metallurgical skill
- **Magnesium** burns with a brilliant **white flame** and produces a basic oxide; **sulfur** burns with a **blue flame** and produces an acidic oxide
- Mercury is the only metal that is **liquid** at room temperature
- Gold is the most **malleable** and **ductile** metal -- 1 gram can be stretched into a 2 km wire
