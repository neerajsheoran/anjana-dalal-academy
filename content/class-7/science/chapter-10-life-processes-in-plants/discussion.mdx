## Plants Are Self-Feeders

**Child:** Maa, animals eat food, but our tulsi plant just sits on the balcony. How does it get its food?

**Mother:** That is what makes plants so special, beta! They prepare their own food through a process called photosynthesis. They use sunlight as energy, water drawn up from the soil by their roots, and carbon dioxide absorbed from the air. With these three ingredients and the help of chlorophyll -- a green pigment in their leaves -- they produce glucose, which is their food. And as a bonus, they release oxygen into the air for us to breathe!

**Child:** So that is why leaves are green -- because of chlorophyll?

**Mother:** Exactly. Chlorophyll captures sunlight. Without it, photosynthesis simply cannot happen. You can prove this with a croton plant that has both green and white patches on its leaves. If you test the leaf with iodine solution, only the green patches turn blue-black, showing starch is present. The white patches -- which lack chlorophyll -- produce no starch at all.

**Child:** What if you keep a plant in the dark?

**Mother:** Then even the green parts produce no starch. Both chlorophyll and sunlight are essential. Have you noticed how the money plant near the window always stretches its stems toward the light? It is literally reaching for its energy source.

## How Scientists Proved Photosynthesis

**Child:** How do we know for sure that carbon dioxide is needed?

**Mother:** There is a neat experiment. You seal half a leaf inside a bottle filled with caustic soda, which absorbs all the carbon dioxide from the air inside the bottle. The other half of the leaf stays in normal air. After a few hours in sunlight, test both halves with iodine. The half that had normal air turns blue-black -- starch was produced. The half inside the bottle does not change colour at all -- no carbon dioxide means no photosynthesis.

**Child:** And how do we know oxygen is released?

**Mother:** Take a water plant like hydrilla, put it in a beaker of water in bright sunlight, and watch. Tiny bubbles stream upward from the leaves. Collect those bubbles in an inverted test tube. Then carefully insert a glowing matchstick into the tube -- it flares up into a bright flame, confirming the gas is oxygen.

**Child:** So the full equation is: carbon dioxide plus water, using sunlight and chlorophyll, gives glucose and oxygen?

**Mother:** Perfectly stated! The glucose serves as instant energy, and whatever the plant does not need immediately is stored as starch. Different plants store starch in different parts -- rice and wheat store it in seeds, carrots and sweet potatoes in roots, potatoes and ginger in stems, and mangoes and bananas in fruits.

## The Plant's Internal Highway

**Child:** Maa, our neighbourhood has a huge peepal tree. How does water from the roots reach leaves that are 15 metres above the ground?

**Mother:** Through an internal highway of tiny tubes called xylem. These tubes run from the roots all the way up through the trunk and branches to every leaf and flower. You can see this yourself at home -- put a white rajnigandha flower in a glass of water mixed with red food colour. Within a day, the petals will turn pinkish-red. The coloured water travelled up through the xylem, just like regular water and minerals do.

**Child:** And how does food from the leaves reach the roots?

**Mother:** Through a different set of tubes called phloem. Think of it as a two-lane highway -- xylem carries water and minerals upward, and phloem carries food downward and sideways to every part that needs it.

**Child:** Like a supply chain -- raw materials going up to the factory and finished goods going down to the warehouse!

**Mother:** What a great comparison! And the stomata on the leaves -- those tiny pores visible under a microscope -- are like the factory's ventilation system. Carbon dioxide enters through them, oxygen exits, and excess water vapour is released too. This entire system works seamlessly in everything from a tiny basil seedling to a giant banyan tree.

## Plants Breathe Too

**Child:** Maa, do plants also breathe like we do?

**Mother:** Yes! Every living cell in a plant respires -- roots, stems, leaves, flowers, everything. Respiration uses oxygen to break down glucose and release energy, producing carbon dioxide and water as waste. It happens all the time, day and night.

**Child:** But during the day, plants take in carbon dioxide for photosynthesis. Is that not the opposite?

**Mother:** Great observation. During daylight hours, both processes happen simultaneously. But photosynthesis produces far more oxygen than respiration consumes, so the net result is that the plant gives off oxygen during the day. At night, photosynthesis stops because there is no sunlight, but respiration continues. So at night, plants release only carbon dioxide.

**Child:** Is that why some people say you should not sleep directly under a big tree at night?

**Mother:** That is the traditional reasoning, though outdoors the amount of carbon dioxide a single tree produces is too small to be harmful. The important takeaway is that photosynthesis and respiration are partner processes. Photosynthesis captures sunlight energy and locks it into glucose molecules. Respiration unlocks that stored energy whenever the plant needs it for growing, repairing, or making flowers and fruits. Together, they keep the oxygen and carbon dioxide in our atmosphere balanced.

## Without Plants, There Would Be No Us

**Child:** Maa, what would happen if all the plants on Earth disappeared?

**Mother:** It would be the end of life as we know it. Plants produce the oxygen every animal breathes. They are also the first link in every food chain -- goats eat grass, and then we drink the goat's milk. Chickens eat grain, and then we eat eggs. Even fish in the ocean depend on tiny algae that do photosynthesis. Remove plants and algae, and there is no food and no oxygen for anything.

**Child:** So every meal I eat traces back to a plant somewhere?

**Mother:** Every single one. The rice on your plate, the dal made from pulses, the sabzi from vegetables, even the ghee that came from a cow that ate grass. And the air filling your lungs right now was produced by a plant or an ocean alga through photosynthesis.

**Child:** That makes me want to plant more trees!

**Mother:** And you should, beta. Every tree you plant is an investment in Earth's future. It will produce oxygen, absorb carbon dioxide, provide shade, give shelter to birds, and enrich the soil. That is why tree-planting drives across India are so important -- they are not just environmental gestures, they are essential acts of survival for all living things.
