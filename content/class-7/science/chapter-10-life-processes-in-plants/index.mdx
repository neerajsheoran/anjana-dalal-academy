## Introduction

![Introduction — life processes in plants](introduction.png)

Animals go out hunting or foraging for their meals, but have you ever seen a neem tree walk to a restaurant? Of course not! Plants have an extraordinary ability -- they **manufacture their own food** using just three simple ingredients: sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide from the air. This self-feeding ability makes plants unique in the living world and places them at the very foundation of life on Earth.

In this chapter, we will discover how plants prepare food, how they move water and nutrients through their bodies, and how they breathe to release energy.

> **Key idea:** Plants are the only organisms that can produce their own food through **photosynthesis**. This makes them the starting point of nearly every food chain on our planet.

## What Do Plants Need to Grow?

When a tulsi plant on your balcony grows, you can see visible changes -- new leaves unfurl, the stem stretches taller, and tiny branches appear. But what drives this growth?

### The Importance of Light and Water

![What do plants need to grow — the importance of light and water](what-do-plants-need-to-grow-the-importance-of-light-and-water.png)

Consider this simple experiment with three identical potted plants:

| Pot | Conditions | Observation after 2 weeks |
|-----|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Pot X** | Kept in sunlight, watered regularly | Grows vigorously with healthy green leaves |
| **Pot Y** | Kept in sunlight, not watered at all | Wilts, dries up, and may die |
| **Pot Z** | Kept in a dark cupboard, watered regularly | Grows weakly with pale, yellowish leaves |

**What this tells us:** Plants need **both sunlight and water** for healthy growth. Deprive them of either, and they struggle or perish.

> **Key idea:** Plants require **sunlight**, **water**, and **minerals from the soil** to grow. These are the raw materials for their food-making process.

## Photosynthesis: The Plant's Kitchen

### Leaves -- The Green Factories

![Photosynthesis the plant's kitchen — leaves, the green factories](photosynthesis-the-plants-kitchen-leaves-the-green-factories-1.png)

![Photosynthesis the plant's kitchen — chlorophyll inside the leaf](photosynthesis-the-plants-kitchen-leaves-the-green-factories-2.png)

If the plant is a food factory, then **leaves** are the production floor. Leaves are typically broad and flat -- shaped to capture as much sunlight as possible. Most leaves appear **green** because they contain a special pigment called **chlorophyll**.

**Chlorophyll** is the real hero of photosynthesis. It **absorbs sunlight** and channels that energy to power the food-making process.

The food plants produce is stored primarily as **starch** (a carbohydrate). You can detect starch in a leaf using the **iodine test** -- when iodine solution is applied to a decolourised leaf, a **blue-black colour** confirms the presence of starch.

### The Four Essentials for Photosynthesis

![Photosynthesis the plant's kitchen — the four essentials for photosynthesis](photosynthesis-the-plants-kitchen-the-four-essentials-for-photosynthesis.png)

Scientists have identified **four requirements** for photosynthesis:

1. **Sunlight** -- provides the energy
2. **Water** -- drawn up from the soil by roots
3. **Carbon dioxide** -- absorbed from the surrounding air
4. **Chlorophyll** -- the green pigment that captures light energy

### Proof from Experiments

**Experiment 1: Do chlorophyll and sunlight both matter?**

![Experiment 1 — do chlorophyll and sunlight both matter (variegated leaf setup)](experiment-1-do-chlorophyll-and-sunlight-both-matter-1.png)

![Experiment 1 — do chlorophyll and sunlight both matter (iodine test results)](experiment-1-do-chlorophyll-and-sunlight-both-matter-2.png)

Select a leaf that has both green portions and non-green (white or pale) portions -- a variegated leaf from a croton or money plant works well. Keep the plant in sunlight for several hours, then pluck the leaf and test it with iodine.
- The **green patches** turn **blue-black** (starch is present)
- The **non-green patches** show no colour change (no starch)
- A leaf from a plant kept entirely in the dark also shows **no starch** even in green areas

**Conclusion:** Both **chlorophyll** and **sunlight** are essential for photosynthesis.

**Experiment 2: Is carbon dioxide necessary?**

![Experiment 2 — is carbon dioxide necessary](experiment-2-is-carbon-dioxide-necessary.png)

Cover half a leaf with a sealed bottle containing caustic soda (a substance that absorbs carbon dioxide from the air). Leave the other half exposed to normal air. After several hours in sunlight, test both halves with iodine.
- The **exposed half** turns blue-black (starch present)
- The **covered half** shows no change (no starch)

**Conclusion:** **Carbon dioxide** is essential for photosynthesis.

**Experiment 3: Does oxygen come out?**

![Experiment 3 — does oxygen come out (hydrilla bubbles)](experiment-3-does-oxygen-come-out.png)

Place a water plant such as hydrilla in a beaker of water under bright sunlight. You will see **bubbles** rising from the plant. Collect these bubbles in an inverted test tube. Insert a glowing matchstick into the tube -- it **bursts into a bright flame**, confirming the collected gas is **oxygen**.

**Conclusion:** **Oxygen** is released during photosynthesis.

> **Key idea:** During photosynthesis, plants use **carbon dioxide** and **water** in the presence of **sunlight** and **chlorophyll** to manufacture **glucose** (food) and release **oxygen** into the air.

## The Photosynthesis Equation

The complete process can be written as:

![The photosynthesis equation — carbon dioxide and water becoming glucose and oxygen](the-photosynthesis-equation.png)

**Carbon dioxide + Water ---(Sunlight, Chlorophyll)---> Glucose + Oxygen**

- **Raw materials:** Carbon dioxide (from air through stomata) and water (from soil through roots)
- **Energy source:** Sunlight
- **Key agent:** Chlorophyll (the green pigment)
- **Products:** Glucose (food for the plant) and Oxygen (released into the atmosphere)

The glucose produced provides immediate energy. Excess glucose is converted into **starch** and stored in various plant parts -- leaves, stems, roots, seeds, and fruits.

## How Do Leaves Exchange Gases?

If you peel a thin transparent layer from the underside of a leaf and examine it under a microscope, you will spot thousands of tiny openings. These openings are called **stomata** (singular: stoma).

![How do leaves exchange gases — stomata and guard cells](how-do-leaves-exchange-gases.png)

**Stomata** serve as the plant's breathing pores:
- They allow **carbon dioxide** to enter the leaf for photosynthesis
- They release **oxygen** produced during photosynthesis
- They also let excess water escape as vapour -- a process called **transpiration**

Each stoma is flanked by two kidney-shaped **guard cells** that can open or close the pore depending on conditions.

> **Key idea:** **Stomata** are microscopic pores on leaf surfaces that enable gas exchange -- carbon dioxide enters for photosynthesis and oxygen exits as a by-product.

## Transport in Plants

### Moving Water and Minerals Upward

Roots absorb water and dissolved minerals from the soil. But how does this water travel upward -- sometimes 20 or 30 metres -- to reach the topmost leaves of a banyan tree or a coconut palm?

**Activity:** Place a white flower (like a rajnigandha or a white rose) in a glass of water tinted with red food colouring. After a day, observe that the stem, petals, and even leaf edges have turned reddish. If you slice the stem open, you can see red-coloured channels running through it.

![Transport in plants — moving water and minerals upward through xylem](transport-in-plants-moving-water-and-minerals-upward.png)

These channels are called **xylem**. Xylem acts like a network of thin pipes, transporting water and dissolved minerals **upward** from the roots to every part of the plant -- stem, branches, leaves, and flowers.

### Moving Food Downward and Sideways

![Transport in plants — moving food downward and sideways through phloem](transport-in-plants-moving-food-downward-and-sideways.png)

Photosynthesis produces food in the leaves. But roots, stems, flowers, and fruits -- all non-green parts -- also need nourishment. How does food reach them?

A second set of transport channels called **phloem** carries the food (sugars) prepared in the leaves **to all other parts** of the plant.

Plants also store food in specific organs:
- **Seeds** -- wheat, rice, chana, moong
- **Roots** -- carrot, radish, beetroot, shakarkandi (sweet potato)
- **Stems** -- potato, adrak (ginger), haldi (turmeric)
- **Fruits** -- mango, banana, amla

> **Key idea:** Plants have a dual transport system: **Xylem** carries water and minerals upward from roots to leaves. **Phloem** carries food from leaves to all other parts.

## Do Plants Breathe?

Absolutely! Every living cell in a plant -- in the roots, stem, leaves, flowers, and fruits -- performs **respiration**, day and night, just like animal cells.

### Evidence that Plants Respire

![Do plants breathe — evidence that plants respire](do-plants-breathe-evidence-that-plants-respire.png)

Take a handful of soaked, sprouting moong seeds and seal them in a glass bottle. Leave the bottle in a dark room for a day. Then carefully pass the air from the bottle through lime water using a tube. The lime water turns **milky**, proving the seeds released **carbon dioxide** during respiration.

### The Respiration Equation

**Glucose + Oxygen --> Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy**

During respiration:
- **Glucose** (the food made by photosynthesis) is broken down using **oxygen**
- **Carbon dioxide** and **water** are released as waste
- **Energy** is liberated, which the plant uses for growth, repair, and all life activities

### Photosynthesis vs Respiration -- Two Sides of One Coin

| Feature | Photosynthesis | Respiration |
|---------|---------------|-------------|
| **What happens?** | Food (glucose) is **produced** | Food (glucose) is **consumed** |
| **Raw materials** | Carbon dioxide + Water | Glucose + Oxygen |
| **Products** | Glucose + Oxygen | Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy |
| **Energy** | Sunlight energy is **stored** in glucose | Stored energy is **released** for use |
| **When?** | Only during daylight hours | Continuously, day and night |
| **Where?** | Only in green parts (mainly leaves) | In every living cell of the plant |

> **Key idea:** **Photosynthesis** builds food and releases oxygen using sunlight. **Respiration** breaks down food using oxygen to release energy. Both processes are vital for the plant, and together they help maintain the oxygen-carbon dioxide balance in Earth's atmosphere.

## Why Every Living Thing Depends on Photosynthesis

![Why every living thing depends on photosynthesis — the base of every food chain](why-every-living-thing-depends-on-photosynthesis.png)

Photosynthesis is not just a plant affair -- it sustains **all life on Earth**:

- Plants release **oxygen** during photosynthesis. This is the very oxygen that animals, including humans, need to breathe and survive.
- Plants are the **primary food producers**. Herbivores eat plants directly. Carnivores eat animals that ate plants. Remove plants, and the entire food web collapses.
- Without photosynthesising organisms (plants, algae, and certain bacteria), there would be no food and no breathable oxygen for any animal on the planet.

## Summary

- Plants need **sunlight**, **water**, and **minerals** for healthy growth.
- **Photosynthesis** is the process by which plants use carbon dioxide, water, sunlight, and chlorophyll to produce glucose (food) and release oxygen.
- **Leaves** are the main sites of photosynthesis, earning them the title of "food factories" of the plant.
- **Chlorophyll**, the green pigment, captures sunlight energy for photosynthesis.
- **Stomata** are tiny pores on leaf surfaces for gas exchange -- carbon dioxide enters, oxygen exits.
- **Xylem** transports water and minerals upward from roots to all plant parts.
- **Phloem** transports food from leaves to all other parts of the plant.
- Plants **respire** continuously (day and night) in all cells, breaking down glucose with oxygen to release energy.
- Photosynthesis and respiration together maintain the atmospheric balance of oxygen and carbon dioxide, supporting all life on Earth.
