## Key Concepts

- A **physical change** alters shape, size, or state but does **not** create any new substance
- A **chemical change** produces one or more **entirely new substances** through a chemical reaction
- Physical change examples: tearing paper, melting ghee, dissolving salt in water, evaporation, grinding wheat
- Chemical change examples: rusting, burning, curdling of milk, fermentation, cooking
- Signs of a chemical change: **colour change**, **gas bubbles**, **heat generation**, **change in smell**
- Lime water turns **milky** when carbon dioxide passes through it (classic test for CO2)
- Lemon juice + baking soda --> vigorous fizzing (CO2 gas released) -- a chemical change
- **Combustion** is a chemical reaction: substance + oxygen --> heat and/or light
- **Fire triangle**: fire requires three things -- **fuel** + **oxygen** + **heat** (ignition temperature)
- Remove any one element of the fire triangle and the fire goes out
- **Combustible substances**: materials that can burn (wood, paper, kerosene, LPG, cotton)
- A burning diya/candle shows **both** physical changes (wax melting) and chemical changes (wax burning) simultaneously
- **Reversible changes** can be undone (freezing water, dissolving salt, folding paper)
- **Irreversible changes** are permanent (burning, rusting, cooking, setting curd)
- Most **chemical changes are irreversible**; some physical changes are also irreversible (slicing onions)
- Same change can be helpful or harmful depending on context (decomposition: bad in fridge, good in compost)
- **Weathering**: gradual breakdown of rocks by temperature changes, root growth, freezing water, and chemical reactions
- **Erosion**: transport of rock fragments by wind and water to new locations
- Weathering + erosion over long periods creates **soil**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Physical change | A change in shape, size, or state without creating a new substance |
| Chemical change | A change that produces one or more new substances with different properties |
| Combustion | A chemical reaction where a substance combines with oxygen, releasing heat and/or light |
| Ignition temperature | The minimum temperature at which a substance catches fire |
| Fire triangle | The three requirements for fire: fuel, oxygen, and heat |
| Reversible change | A change that can be undone to get the original substance back |
| Irreversible change | A change that is permanent and cannot be undone |
| Weathering | Gradual breakdown of rocks by physical and chemical forces |
| Erosion | Transport of broken rock fragments by wind, water, or ice |

## Remember These

- A burning candle involves **both** physical and chemical changes at the same time
- The **fire triangle** is the key to fire safety: remove fuel, oxygen, or heat to extinguish fire
- Never use **synthetic fabric** to smother fire -- it can melt and stick to skin
- Lime water turning milky = presence of **carbon dioxide** (important lab test)
- Weathering creates rock fragments; erosion **moves** them -- they are different processes
