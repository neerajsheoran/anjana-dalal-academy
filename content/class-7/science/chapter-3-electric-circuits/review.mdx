## Key Concepts

- An **electric cell** stores chemical energy and converts it to electrical energy
- A cell has a **positive terminal (+)** (raised metal button) and a **negative terminal (-)** (flat metal plate)
- Two or more cells connected in series (positive to negative) form a **battery**
- An **incandescent lamp** has a thin coiled wire called a **filament** that glows when current passes through it
- An **LED** (Light Emitting Diode) has no filament, is more energy-efficient, and allows current in **one direction only**
- LED: **longer leg = positive (+)**, **shorter leg = negative (-)**
- An **electrical circuit** is a complete, unbroken loop that allows current to flow
- Current flows from the **positive terminal** through the circuit to the **negative terminal** (by convention)
- A **switch** opens or closes a circuit -- ON (closed circuit, current flows), OFF (open circuit, current stops)
- A switch can be placed **anywhere** in the circuit
- **Circuit diagrams** use standardised symbols to represent components (cell, battery, lamp, LED, switch, wire)
- **Conductors** are materials that allow current to flow through them -- mainly **metals** (copper, silver, iron, aluminium)
- **Insulators** block current -- plastic, rubber, wood, glass, ceramic, cloth
- **Copper** is used for wiring because it conducts well and is affordable (silver conducts best but is too expensive)
- **Direct Current (DC)**: flows in one direction, from cells/batteries (torches, remotes, phones)
- **Alternating Current (AC)**: reverses direction rapidly, from power stations (refrigerators, ACs, industry)
- The human body **can conduct electricity** -- household AC is extremely dangerous

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Electric cell | A portable device that converts chemical energy to electrical energy |
| Battery | Two or more cells connected in series |
| Filament | The thin wire inside an incandescent lamp that glows when heated by current |
| LED | Light Emitting Diode -- energy-efficient, one-direction current flow, no filament |
| Electrical circuit | A complete, unbroken loop providing a path for electric current |
| Switch | A device that opens or closes a circuit to control current flow |
| Circuit diagram | A drawing using standard symbols to represent an electrical circuit |
| Conductor | A material that allows electric current to pass through it |
| Insulator | A material that blocks the flow of electric current |
| Direct Current (DC) | Current that flows steadily in one direction |
| Alternating Current (AC) | Current that rapidly changes direction |

## Remember These

- LED works only when connected correctly: **longer leg to positive, shorter leg to negative**
- A lamp glows only when the circuit is a **complete, unbroken loop** with no gaps
- **Silver** is the best conductor, but **copper** is used in wiring because it is cheaper and abundant
- Never experiment with **wall sockets or household electricity** -- only use dry cells
- Graphite (pencil lead) is a **non-metal** that conducts electricity -- an exception!
