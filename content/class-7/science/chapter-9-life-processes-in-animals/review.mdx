## Key Concepts

- **Life processes** include nutrition, respiration, circulation, excretion, and reproduction
- The **alimentary canal** (digestive tract) runs from **mouth to anus**: Mouth --> Oesophagus --> Stomach --> Small Intestine --> Large Intestine --> Rectum --> Anus
- **Mouth**: teeth grind food (mechanical digestion); **saliva** converts starch to sugar (chemical digestion)
- Chewing plain roti for a minute produces a **sweet taste** -- saliva breaking starch into sugar
- **Oesophagus**: pushes food to stomach through wave-like muscular contractions
- **Stomach**: churns food; releases **digestive juice** (breaks proteins), **hydrochloric acid** (kills germs), and **mucus** (protects stomach wall)
- **Small intestine** (~6 metres long): completes digestion of all nutrients; absorbs them into blood through **finger-like projections** (villi)
- **Liver** produces **bile** (breaks fats into tiny droplets); **pancreas** produces **pancreatic juice** (digests carbs, proteins, fats)
- **Large intestine** (~1.5 m): absorbs water and salts, forms solid waste (faeces)
- Removal of undigested waste through the anus is called **egestion**
- **Ruminants** (cow, buffalo, goat) have a **rumen** and re-chew their food (**rumination** / chewing the cud)
- **Birds** have no teeth; they use a muscular **gizzard** (with grit/pebbles) to grind food
- **Breathing** is the physical process of inhaling/exhaling; **Respiration** is the chemical process of releasing energy from glucose
- **Respiration equation**: Glucose + Oxygen --> Carbon dioxide + Water + **Energy**
- Gas exchange occurs in the **alveoli** of the lungs -- oxygen enters blood, carbon dioxide exits
- **Inhalation**: ribs move up and out, diaphragm moves down, chest expands, air rushes in
- **Exhalation**: ribs move down and in, diaphragm moves up, chest contracts, air pushed out
- Exhaled air has more CO2 (~4-5%) and less O2 (~16-17%) compared to inhaled air (0.04% CO2, 21% O2)
- **Circulatory system**: heart, blood, and blood vessels deliver oxygen and nutrients to all cells
- Different animals breathe differently: **lungs** (land animals), **gills** (fish), **moist skin** (earthworms), **both lungs and skin** (adult frogs)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Alimentary canal | The continuous tube from mouth to anus through which food passes |
| Saliva | Fluid in the mouth that wets food and begins starch digestion |
| Bile | A greenish-yellow fluid from the liver that breaks fats into tiny droplets |
| Egestion | The removal of undigested solid waste from the body |
| Ruminant | A grass-eating animal that re-chews its food (e.g., cow, goat) |
| Rumen | A special stomach compartment in ruminants for initial food processing |
| Gizzard | A muscular organ in birds that grinds food using grit |
| Breathing | The physical act of inhaling and exhaling air |
| Respiration | The chemical process of breaking down glucose with oxygen to release energy |
| Alveoli | Tiny air sacs in the lungs where gas exchange occurs |
| Diaphragm | A dome-shaped muscle below the lungs that helps in breathing |

## Remember These

- **Breathing** (physical, in lungs) is different from **Respiration** (chemical, in every cell)
- The small intestine is the **longest** part of the alimentary canal (~6 m) -- this is where nutrients are absorbed
- The stomach's **mucus** lining protects it from its own hydrochloric acid
- Exhaled air turns **lime water milky** -- this proves it contains carbon dioxide
- Frogs undergo **metamorphosis**: gills as tadpoles --> lungs + moist skin as adults
