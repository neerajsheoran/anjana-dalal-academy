## Key Concepts

- Heat always flows from a **hotter region to a cooler region**
- Three modes of heat transfer: **Conduction**, **Convection**, **Radiation**
- **Conduction**: heat passes through a solid from particle to particle by vibration -- particles do **not move** from their positions
- Conduction is the main heat transfer method in **solids**
- **Good conductors** of heat: metals (copper, aluminium, iron, steel)
- **Poor conductors / Insulators**: wood, rubber, plastic, glass, clay, **air**
- **Air** is one of the best natural insulators -- trapped air in woollen clothes, quilts, and hollow bricks keeps us warm
- Two thin razais are warmer than one thick razai of the same weight (air trapped between them)
- **Convection**: heat transfers through **physical movement** of heated fluid particles -- warm fluid rises, cool fluid sinks
- Convection is the main heat transfer method in **liquids and gases**
- A **convection current** is the circular flow of rising warm fluid and sinking cool fluid
- **Sea breeze** (daytime): land heats faster --> warm air over land rises --> cool air from sea rushes in
- **Land breeze** (nighttime): land cools faster --> warm air over sea rises --> cool air from land flows to sea
- **Land heats up and cools down faster than water** -- this drives sea/land breezes
- **Radiation**: heat transfer **without any medium** -- works even through vacuum
- The Sun's heat reaches Earth through **radiation** across the vacuum of space
- **Light-coloured clothes** reflect heat (good for summer); **dark-coloured clothes** absorb heat (good for winter)
- In real life, all three modes often work **simultaneously** (e.g., boiling dal on a stove)
- The **water cycle**: Evaporation --> Condensation --> Precipitation --> Collection (powered by solar radiation)
- **Transpiration**: release of water vapour from plant leaves
- **Infiltration**: seepage of rainwater into the ground; speed depends on soil type (gravel > sand > clay)
- **Groundwater** is stored in underground rock formations called **aquifers**
- **Rainwater harvesting** and **recharge pits** help replenish groundwater

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Conduction | Transfer of heat through a material by particle-to-particle vibration without particle movement |
| Convection | Transfer of heat through physical movement of fluid (liquid/gas) particles |
| Radiation | Transfer of heat without any medium, through invisible rays |
| Convection current | The circular flow pattern of rising warm fluid and sinking cool fluid |
| Sea breeze | Cool air flowing from sea to land during the day |
| Land breeze | Cool air flowing from land to sea at night |
| Insulator | A material that resists the flow of heat |
| Water cycle | The continuous circulation of water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection |
| Infiltration | The seeping of water into the ground through soil and rock |
| Aquifer | An underground rock formation that stores groundwater |

## Remember These

- **Conduction** = solids (particles stay put), **Convection** = fluids (particles move), **Radiation** = no medium needed
- Land heats and cools **faster** than water -- this is why sea breeze blows during the day and land breeze at night
- Air is an excellent **insulator** -- this explains why woollen clothes, double-walled houses, and hollow bricks keep us warm
- Infiltration speed: **Gravel > Sand > Clay** (larger gaps = faster seepage)
- The Sun's heat reaches Earth by **radiation** -- the only mode that works through vacuum
