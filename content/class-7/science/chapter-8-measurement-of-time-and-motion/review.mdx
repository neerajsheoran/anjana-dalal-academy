## Key Concepts

- Ancient time-measuring devices: **sundial**, **water clock** (Ghatika-yantra), **hourglass**, **candle clock**
- All time-keeping devices rely on a **process that repeats at regular intervals**
- The **Samrat Yantra** at Jantar Mantar, Jaipur, is a giant sundial built by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II (~300 years ago), accurate to 2 seconds
- A **simple pendulum** consists of a heavy ball (**bob**) suspended from a fixed point by a thread
- **One complete oscillation**: mean position (O) --> extreme A --> extreme B --> back to O
- The time for one complete oscillation is called the **time period**
- Time period depends only on the **length** of the pendulum, **not** on the weight of the bob
- **Longer thread = longer time period** (slower swing); shorter thread = shorter time period (faster swing)
- Modern clocks: **pendulum clocks** (swing), **quartz clocks** (crystal vibrations), **atomic clocks** (atom vibrations)
- Atomic clocks lose only about **1 second in several million years**
- The **SI unit of time** is the **second (s)**; 60 s = 1 min; 60 min = 1 h
- **Speed = Distance / Time**; also **Distance = Speed x Time** and **Time = Distance / Speed**
- SI unit of speed: **m/s** (metres per second); common unit: **km/h**
- **Speedometer** shows the vehicle's current speed; **odometer** shows total distance travelled
- **Uniform linear motion**: constant speed, equal distances in equal time intervals (straight line)
- **Non-uniform linear motion**: changing speed, unequal distances in equal time intervals
- In real life, **non-uniform motion** is far more common
- **Average speed = Total distance / Total time** -- useful for journeys with varying speed

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Sundial | A device that tells time using the position of a shadow cast by sunlight |
| Simple pendulum | A bob suspended by a thread from a fixed point, swinging back and forth |
| Oscillation | One complete back-and-forth motion of a pendulum (O --> A --> B --> O) |
| Time period | The time taken for one complete oscillation |
| Speed | The distance covered per unit of time (Speed = Distance / Time) |
| Uniform linear motion | Motion in a straight line at constant speed |
| Non-uniform linear motion | Motion in a straight line with changing speed |
| Average speed | Total distance covered divided by total time taken |
| Speedometer | An instrument that displays a vehicle's current speed |
| Odometer | An instrument that records total distance travelled |

## Key Formulas

| Formula | Use |
|---------|-----|
| **Speed = Distance / Time** | Find speed when distance and time are known |
| **Distance = Speed x Time** | Find distance when speed and time are known |
| **Time = Distance / Speed** | Find time when distance and speed are known |
| **Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time** | Find overall speed for journeys with varying speed |

## Remember These

- The time period of a pendulum depends on **length only** -- changing the bob's weight has **no effect**
- Unit symbols are always lowercase and singular: **s** (not sec), **min** (not mins), **h** (not hrs)
- Always leave a space between number and unit: write **15 s**, not **15s**
- **Uniform motion** is rare in daily life; most motion is **non-uniform**
- The Ghatika-yantra sank every ~24 minutes and was announced by a drumbeat or bell
