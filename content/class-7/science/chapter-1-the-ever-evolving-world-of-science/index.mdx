## Your Journey into Class 7 Science

Have you ever watched your grandmother prepare pickles and wondered why she adds salt and oil? Or noticed how a puddle of water on your terrace vanishes by afternoon? These everyday moments hold the seeds of scientific discovery.

Science is not a collection of answers locked inside a textbook. It is a living, breathing adventure -- a way of looking at the world with wonder, asking bold questions, and searching for explanations through careful observation and experimentation.

In Class 7, your scientific thinking will grow deeper. You will move beyond "what" questions to tackle "how" and "why" -- How do certain substances behave differently? Why does a metal gate rust but a wooden door does not? What connects the food on your plate to the rain in the sky?

> **Key idea:** Science is a journey of exploration. It is not about memorising answers but about developing a habit of questioning, testing, and discovering the world around us.

## A Glimpse of What Awaits You

This year, you will travel through many fascinating territories of science -- from the chemistry of your kitchen to the physics of your torchlight, from the biology of your own growing body to the geology beneath your feet. Though each chapter appears to stand alone, they are all woven together like threads in a fabric.

Here is a preview of the adventures ahead:

### The Chemistry of Everyday Substances

![Preview — the chemistry of everyday substances](the-chemistry-of-everyday-substances.png)

- Why does lemon juice sting a cut on your finger but milk does not?
- What makes the **haldi** (turmeric) stain on your kurta change colour when you wash it with soap?
- You will learn to sort substances into **acidic**, **basic**, and **neutral** categories using simple tests.

### Powering Our World with Electricity

![Preview — powering our world with electricity](powering-our-world-with-electricity.png)

- How does pressing a switch on the wall light up a room?
- Why do electricians wear rubber gloves?
- You will construct your own electrical circuits and discover which materials let electricity pass through.

### The Nature of Metals and Non-Metals

![Preview — the nature of metals and non-metals](the-nature-of-metals-and-non-metals.png)

- Why are railway tracks made of iron but tyres are made of rubber?
- You will investigate the special properties that separate **metals** from **non-metals** and understand why each is used for specific purposes.

### Transformations All Around Us

![Preview — transformations all around us](transformations-all-around-us.png)

- When Amma makes dosa batter and it ferments overnight, can you get the rice and dal back? When ice cream melts in the summer heat, can you refreeze it?
- You will distinguish between changes that can be **undone** and those that are **permanent**.

### Understanding Heat and Its Movement

![Preview — understanding heat and its movement](understanding-heat-and-its-movement.png)

- Why does the steel handle of a pan get hot while its plastic grip stays cool?
- How does the warmth of the sun travel millions of kilometres to reach your skin?
- You will explore the fascinating ways heat travels from one place to another.

### Growing Up -- The Stage of Adolescence

![Preview — growing up: the stage of adolescence](growing-up-the-stage-of-adolescence.png)

- Around your age, the body begins a remarkable transformation.
- You will learn about the stage of **adolescence** -- the physical, emotional, and social changes that come with growing up.

### How Living Things Survive

![Preview — how living things survive](how-living-things-survive.png)

- Every animal needs food, air, and a way to move nutrients through its body. But how do plants manage without a mouth or lungs?
- You will uncover the clever strategies that plants and animals use to stay alive.

### Tracking Time and Motion

![Preview — tracking time and motion](tracking-time-and-motion.png)

- Centuries before smartphones and digital clocks, people in India used **sundials** and the positions of stars to track time.
- You will explore how we measure time and understand the speed of moving objects.

### The Behaviour of Light

![Preview — the behaviour of light](the-behaviour-of-light.png)

- Why can you see your reflection in a mirror but not in a brick wall?
- You will investigate how light travels, bounces, and creates shadows.

### Our Place in the Universe

![Preview — our place in the universe](our-place-in-the-universe.png)

- The Earth spins, the Moon orbits, and together they dance around the Sun. Sometimes, their shadows create breathtaking **eclipses**.
- You will understand the movements that give us day and night, months, and seasons.

> **Key idea:** Every branch of science connects to others in surprising ways. A lesson about acids and bases helps you understand soil chemistry, which connects to how plants grow. Keep your eyes open for these hidden links!

## The Power of Asking "Why?"

The greatest breakthroughs in science began not with answers but with questions. Sir C.V. Raman did not set out to explain why the ocean looks blue -- he simply asked the question, and that curiosity led him to the Nobel Prize.

To sharpen your questioning skills, here is a fun challenge:

### Activity: Invent the Question

Below are some answers. Your task is to come up with creative, funny, or clever questions that could lead to each answer:

1. **Answer:** "Because the crow stole the roti."
   - What unusual question might lead to this answer?

2. **Answer:** "Mix it with jaggery and warm water."
   - Can you think of a kitchen-related question for this?

3. **Answer:** "Only on a full moon night."
   - What mysterious question fits here?

There are no wrong questions -- the wilder your imagination, the better!

## Using Science Wisely

![Using science wisely — responsibility that comes with knowledge](using-science-wisely.png)

As you gain scientific knowledge this year, you will also realise that understanding comes with responsibility. The same chemistry that purifies drinking water can also pollute rivers if misused. The same electricity that powers hospitals can be dangerous if handled carelessly.

- Knowing how burning fuels affects our atmosphere helps us choose cleaner alternatives like solar and wind energy.
- Understanding how plastic breaks down (or fails to) motivates us to reduce, reuse, and recycle.
- Learning about soil health inspires better farming practices that feed millions across India.

> **Key idea:** Scientific knowledge is a powerful tool. Using it thoughtfully and responsibly helps us build a better future for ourselves and for generations to come.

## Ready, Set, Explore!

![Ready, set, explore — what makes a great young scientist](ready-set-explore.png)

Throughout this year, you will observe, question, experiment, and sometimes be surprised by what you find. Each chapter builds on what you already know and pushes you to think a little further.

Here is what will make you a great young scientist:

- **Stay curious** -- never stop asking "why" and "how"
- **Get your hands dirty** -- try experiments at home whenever safe to do so
- **Embrace surprises** -- when results are unexpected, you have found something worth investigating

Sometimes an experiment will confirm your guess. Other times, it will surprise you completely. Both outcomes are equally valuable in science, because every result -- expected or unexpected -- teaches you something new.

So sharpen your pencils, open your eyes wide, and step into a year of discovery!

## Apply What You Have Learned

### Where you see this in real life

**C.V. Raman -- a stubborn question on a ship.** In 1921, sailing home from England, Raman looked at the deep blue Mediterranean and refused to believe his textbook's explanation that the sea was just reflecting the sky. That one question led to seven years of work and a Nobel Prize. The "Raman Effect" he discovered is still used in Indian labs today to spot fake medicines and identify chemicals in food.

**Kalpana Chawla -- night skies over Karnal.** A small-town girl in Haryana spent her childhood wondering what the stars were made of. That wondering carried her to NASA and made her the first Indian-origin woman in space.

Scientists are not a different species. They are people who never stopped asking questions and learned to test their guesses carefully.

### Try it at home -- the Wonder Notebook

*⏱ 5 min a day for one week · 🧹 zero mess · 👨‍👩‍👧 no adult help needed*

**What you'll need:**

- A small notebook (any size, a half-used one is fine)
- A pen or pencil

**Steps:**

1. Each evening, spend five minutes thinking about your day.
2. Write down **one thing you noticed but did not fully understand**. Some examples to start:
   - Why does roti puff up on the tawa but not always?
   - Why does the moon look bigger on some nights than others?
   - Why does a balloon shrink overnight even when nothing has popped it?
3. Below each observation, write **two guesses** about what might be happening. Do not worry if you are wrong -- that is the whole point.
4. At the end of the week, pick your favourite question and look up the actual scientific explanation. Compare it to your guesses.

**What to look for:**

- How often is your guess partially right? Scientists are partially right far more often than they are fully right.
- Which questions cannot be answered by simply Googling -- the ones that need an experiment?

**Show what you found:** At the end of the week, show your notebook to a family member -- your dadi, your older cousin, your father. Read out the question that surprised you most and the explanation you found. Watch their face. You will see that your question made them curious too. That is how science spreads.

**A question to keep asking all year:** *When something around me does not behave the way I expect, what could I do to figure out why?*
