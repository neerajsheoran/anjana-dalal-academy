## Key Concepts

- Substances can be classified as **acidic**, **basic**, or **neutral** based on their chemical nature
- **Indicators** are substances that change colour in acids or bases -- used to identify the nature of a substance
- **Litmus** is the most common indicator, extracted from **lichens** (a fungus-alga partnership)
- **Blue litmus turns red** in acidic solutions; **red litmus turns blue** in basic solutions
- **Neutral substances** cause no change in either litmus paper
- Acidic substances taste **sour** (e.g., lemon -- citric acid, vinegar -- acetic acid, curd -- lactic acid, tamarind -- tartaric acid)
- Basic substances feel **slippery/soapy** and may taste bitter (e.g., soap, baking soda, toothpaste, lime water)
- **Hibiscus indicator**: stays red in acids, turns **green/bluish-green** in bases
- **Turmeric indicator**: stays yellow in acids and neutrals, turns **reddish-brown** in bases (detects only bases)
- Turmeric cannot distinguish between acidic and neutral substances
- **Olfactory indicators** detect acids/bases through changes in **smell** (e.g., garlic, onion)
- **Neutralisation reaction**: Acid + Base --> Salt + Water + Heat
- Neutralisation is used to treat **bee stings** (apply baking soda) and **wasp stings** (apply vinegar)
- Farmers add **lime** (basic) to acidic soil and **compost** to basic soil to correct pH
- Factories must **neutralise** acidic waste before releasing it into water bodies
- In paneer-making, citric acid in lemon juice causes milk proteins to **coagulate**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Acid | A substance that tastes sour and turns blue litmus red |
| Base | A substance that feels slippery and turns red litmus blue |
| Neutral substance | A substance that is neither acidic nor basic; no change on litmus |
| Indicator | A substance that changes colour in the presence of an acid or base |
| Litmus | A natural acid-base indicator obtained from lichens |
| Neutralisation | A reaction between an acid and a base producing salt, water, and heat |
| Olfactory indicator | A substance that indicates acid/base through a change in smell |

## Remember These

- Turmeric only detects **bases** (turns reddish-brown) -- it stays yellow in both acids and neutrals
- Bee sting is **acidic** (treat with baking soda); wasp sting is **basic** (treat with vinegar) -- opposite treatments!
- The neutralisation formula: **Acid + Base --> Salt + Water + Heat**
- Haldi stain on a white kurta turns red with soap because soap is **basic**
- Never taste unknown substances to test if they are acidic or basic -- always use indicators
