## The Curious Case of the Colour-Changing Stain

**Child:** Maa, today I spilled haldi on my white dupatta, and when I tried to wash it with soap, the stain turned reddish-brown! It looked even worse than before!

**Mother:** That is actually a perfect science demonstration happening right on your dupatta! Haldi -- turmeric -- is a natural indicator. It stays yellow under normal conditions, but when it meets a basic substance like soap, it turns reddish-brown.

**Child:** So turmeric can tell us if something is basic?

**Mother:** Yes, but with a limitation. Turmeric changes colour only in basic solutions. In both acidic and neutral solutions, it stays yellow. So if turmeric stays yellow, you cannot tell whether the substance is acidic or neutral -- you would need another indicator for that.

**Child:** Like litmus paper?

**Mother:** Exactly. Blue litmus turns red when it touches an acid. Red litmus turns blue when it touches a base. Neither changes in a neutral substance. Using both litmus and turmeric together, you can confidently identify any substance.

## Sour, Slippery, or Neither

**Child:** Maa, are all sour things acidic?

**Mother:** Almost always, yes. The sour taste of imli in your chutney comes from tartaric acid. The tanginess of aam panna comes from citric acid in raw mangoes. The mild sourness of dahi is from lactic acid. Sourness is nature's way of telling you that acids are present.

**Child:** What about basic substances? How do they feel?

**Mother:** Basic substances usually feel slippery or soapy -- like how soap feels between your fingers. Some taste bitter, like baking soda. But be very careful -- never taste anything in a science lab or any unknown substance. Some strong acids and bases can seriously burn your skin and mouth.

**Child:** And neutral substances?

**Mother:** They are the calm ones -- neither sour nor bitter, neither changing litmus paper. Drinking water, salt dissolved in water, and honey mixed in water are all neutral. They sit peacefully between acids and bases.

## Making Indicators from Kitchen Ingredients

**Child:** Maa, can I make my own indicator at home without litmus paper?

**Mother:** Of course! Take some red gudhal (hibiscus) flowers, crush them with a little warm water, strain the liquid, and you have a hibiscus indicator. Dip it in lemon juice -- it stays red or deepens. Dip it in soap water -- it turns greenish. You can also try beetroot juice or jamun juice.

**Child:** What about the haldi strips our teacher mentioned?

**Mother:** Easy to make. Mix haldi powder with a little water to make a paste, soak strips of paper in it, and let them dry. Now dip one strip in soapy water -- it turns reddish-brown. Dip another in nimbu paani -- it stays yellow. Your very own chemical testing kit from the kitchen!

**Child:** Are there indicators that work through smell instead of colour?

**Mother:** Yes, those are called olfactory indicators. If you soak cloth pieces in crushed onion paste and leave them overnight, the cloth absorbs the onion smell. When you add a basic substance, the smell may weaken or change. This is useful for people who have difficulty seeing colours.

## When Acids and Bases Come Together

**Child:** Maa, what happens if you mix an acid and a base?

**Mother:** They neutralise each other! It is like mixing hot and cold water -- the extreme qualities cancel out. An acid and a base combine to form salt, water, and a little heat. The result is a neutral substance.

**Child:** Does this happen in real life?

**Mother:** All the time! When a bee stings you, it injects an acidic substance. Applying baking soda paste -- which is basic -- neutralises the acid and eases the pain. Farmers add chuna to acidic soil so their bajra and jowar crops can grow properly. Factories treat their acidic waste with basic chemicals before releasing it into rivers.

**Child:** So neutralisation protects us and the environment?

**Mother:** Precisely. It is one of the most practical chemical reactions you will ever learn about. Doctors use it too -- antacid tablets contain a base that neutralises the excess acid in your stomach when you have acidity after eating too much spicy food!

## The Secret Message Trick

**Child:** Maa, at the school science exhibition, someone wrote an invisible message on white paper, and when they sprayed something on it, the words appeared in reddish-brown! How?

**Mother:** I bet I know how. The message was written using soap solution -- which is basic and looks colourless when it dries. The spray was turmeric water. Since soap is basic, wherever the soap was applied, the turmeric turned reddish-brown, revealing the hidden words.

**Child:** So the turmeric indicator reacted with the soap base!

**Mother:** Exactly. What looks like magic is simply chemistry at work. You can create your own secret messages at home -- write with a thin brush dipped in baking soda solution, let it dry, and then spray diluted haldi water. The message will appear as if by magic.

**Child:** I am going to try this for my project! But Maa, I will clean up the haldi stains this time -- I know they are persistent!

**Mother:** Good thinking. That is the mark of a true scientist -- curious enough to experiment, and responsible enough to clean up afterwards!
