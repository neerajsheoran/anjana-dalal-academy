## Introduction

Think of a mango tree. It starts as a tiny seed, sprouts into a delicate sapling, grows into a sturdy young tree, and eventually matures enough to bear flowers and fruit. Human beings follow a similar journey of growth, passing through distinct stages:

1. **Infancy** (birth to roughly 2 years)
2. **Childhood** (roughly 2 to 10 years)
3. **Adolescence** (roughly 10 to 19 years)
4. **Adulthood** (roughly 19 to 60 years)
5. **Old age** (60 years and beyond)

Every person moves through these stages at their own speed. The exact age at which each stage begins and ends varies from individual to individual -- and this variation is completely normal.

> **Key idea:** **Adolescence** is the bridge between childhood and adulthood. It typically begins around age 10 and extends to about age 19. During this period, the body undergoes significant transformations to prepare for adult life.

## The Physical Transformations of Adolescence

If you compare a Class 5 photograph of yourself with how you look now, the differences may surprise you. These changes are a perfectly natural part of growing up. Let us explore the most common ones.

### A Noticeable Jump in Height

The body grows continuously from birth, but during adolescence, the pace of height increase **accelerates dramatically**. You or your classmates may shoot up several centimetres within a single school year.

### Shifts in Body Shape and Weight

- **Boys:** Alongside growing taller, boys typically gain weight. Their shoulders often become broader and their chest may expand.
- **Girls:** Girls also experience changes in height and weight. They may notice **breast development**, which is a natural and expected part of maturation.

### The Voice Changes

In adolescent boys, the **voice box** (larynx) -- a structure in the throat responsible for producing sound -- enlarges significantly. This growth can cause the voice to **crack, waver, or deepen**. The enlarged voice box may be visible as a small bump on the throat, commonly called the **Adam's apple**. Not every boy develops a prominently visible one, and this is perfectly normal.

In girls, the voice box also grows during adolescence, but to a lesser extent, leading to only subtle changes in voice.

### Hair Appears in New Places

Both boys and girls notice the growth of hair in areas such as the **underarms** and **pubic region** during adolescence.

Boys may also develop **facial hair**, initially as light fuzz that eventually thickens into a moustache and beard as adulthood approaches. Some boys develop body hair on the chest and limbs as well. The amount and timing of hair growth differ widely among individuals -- all variations are normal.

### Skin Changes -- Pimples and Acne

One of the most common experiences during adolescence is **acne** -- a skin condition where small, inflamed **pimples** appear, typically on the face, forehead, and sometimes the back. Acne occurs because the skin's oil-producing glands become more active during this period. Excess oil can block skin pores, leading to bacterial growth and inflammation.

> **Key idea:** Physical changes during adolescence do not follow a fixed timetable. Every person's body develops at its own pace, and the wide variation from person to person is entirely normal.

## Secondary Sexual Characteristics and Puberty

Certain changes during adolescence help distinguish males from females physically but are **not directly related to reproduction**. These visible markers are called **secondary sexual characteristics**.

**In boys:**
- Voice becoming deeper
- Appearance of facial hair
- Shoulders growing broader

**In girls:**
- Breast development
- Hips becoming wider

These characteristics signal that the body is transitioning towards adulthood. Their appearance marks the beginning of **puberty**.

> **Key idea:** **Puberty** is the phase during which an adolescent's body undergoes both visible and internal changes, developing the capacity for reproduction.

## The Menstrual Cycle

A significant internal development in girls during puberty is the start of the **menstrual cycle**. Here are the essential facts:

- The cycle typically repeats every **28 to 30 days**, though cycles anywhere from 21 to 35 days are considered healthy.
- The phase involving **blood discharge** from the body is called **menstruation** (often referred to as "the period").
- Each menstrual period typically lasts between **3 and 7 days**.
- Some girls may experience **cramping or discomfort** in the lower abdomen during these days. This is a normal experience.
- Menstruation generally begins during puberty and naturally ceases between the ages of **45 and 55**.
- A regular menstrual cycle is a sign of **healthy reproductive functioning**.

### Clearing Away Misconceptions

Numerous false beliefs about menstruation persist in society, causing unnecessary fear, embarrassment, and restrictions. Some harmful myths include isolating menstruating girls from daily activities or treating them as impure. These beliefs have **absolutely no scientific basis**.

Menstruation is a **completely natural biological process**. Promoting scientific awareness helps ensure that girls approach this phase of life with confidence rather than shame, and supports better reproductive health for everyone.

> **Key idea:** Menstruation is a natural, healthy process. Myths and superstitions surrounding it have no scientific foundation and should be replaced with accurate knowledge and open conversation.

## Emotional and Behavioural Shifts

Adolescence involves more than physical changes. It also brings **shifts in emotions and behaviour** that can feel thrilling, bewildering, or both at once.

### Common Emotional Experiences

| What You May Feel | How It May Show Up | Healthy Ways to Respond |
|-------------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Mood swings** | Feeling elated one hour and irritable the next | Channel energy into sports, dance, music, or art |
| **Intense emotions** | Experiencing feelings with greater depth and force than before | Practise self-reflection; write in a journal or talk to someone |
| **Heightened sensitivity** | Being more affected by comments, criticism, or praise | Share your feelings with a trusted family member or teacher; recognise that this sensitivity is temporary |
| **Growing empathy** | Feeling deeply for others' struggles and challenges | Participate in community service, help classmates, volunteer |
| **Restless curiosity** | Wanting to explore new interests, ideas, and experiences | Pursue hobbies, join clubs, read widely, learn new skills |

Recognising that emotions influence our decisions helps us respond thoughtfully rather than react impulsively.

> **Key idea:** Adolescence brings emotions that are stronger and more varied than in childhood. Learning to understand and manage these feelings is a vital skill for growing up.

## Building a Healthy Adolescence

### Fuelling Growth with Good Nutrition

Since adolescence involves rapid growth and development, the body's demand for nutrients increases significantly. A **well-balanced diet** is crucial.

![Balanced diet plate showing food groups: proteins, carbohydrates, vitamins, minerals, calcium, and iron-rich foods](balanced-diet-adolescence.png)

| Food Sources | Key Nutrients | Role in the Body |
|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Milk, ragi, dahi, paneer, cheese | Calcium, proteins | Strengthen bones during the growth spurt |
| Moong dal, chana, eggs, fish, almonds | Proteins | Build and repair muscles and tissues |
| Palak, chana (chickpeas), dates, dried apricots | Iron | Support healthy blood production and energy |
| Seasonal fruits, salads, green vegetables | Vitamins, minerals | Boost immunity and overall development |
| Roti, rice, sweet potatoes | Carbohydrates | Provide energy for study and activity |

Adolescents -- particularly girls -- may develop health issues from insufficient **iron** or **vitamin B12**. Consuming foods rich in these nutrients helps prevent anaemia and fatigue.

### Keeping Clean -- Personal Hygiene

Maintaining good **personal hygiene** becomes especially important during adolescence:

- Bathe daily and wear freshly washed clothes.
- Keep underarm and pubic areas clean to prevent bacterial infections and body odour.
- For girls, maintaining **menstrual hygiene** is essential. Use sanitary pads, reusable cloth pads, or menstrual cups as appropriate.
- Dispose of used sanitary products by wrapping them in newspaper and placing them in a waste bin. Never flush them.
- Eco-friendly biodegradable sanitary pads are increasingly available as a sustainable option.

The Government of India runs several programmes to support adolescent hygiene:
- **Menstrual Hygiene Scheme (MHS)** -- distributes free or subsidised sanitary pads in rural areas
- **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK)** -- focuses on improving the overall health of young people
- **Suvidha Sanitary Napkin Initiative** -- makes affordable biodegradable pads available through Jan Aushadhi Kendras

### Staying Active -- Physical Exercise

Regular physical activity is a cornerstone of healthy adolescence. Activities such as playing cricket or kabaddi, running, practising yoga or surya namaskar, swimming, or cycling help to:

- Maintain physical fitness and build strength
- Improve concentration and academic performance
- Elevate mood and reduce feelings of stress or anxiety

> **Key idea:** Nutritious food, consistent personal hygiene, and regular physical activity form the three foundations of a healthy, happy adolescence.

## Navigating Social Life

As adolescents interact more independently with peers and the wider world, social skills become increasingly important.

- Treat everyone with **courtesy and respect**, regardless of differences
- Be **mindful and responsible** in all interactions, whether face-to-face or online
- Use social media **thoughtfully** -- once something is posted online, it can be difficult to erase
- When confused or uncertain, seek guidance from parents, elders, or teachers

### Dealing with Cyberbullying

**Cyberbullying** is the use of phones, computers, or social media to deliberately intimidate, harass, or humiliate another person through hurtful messages, rumours, or sharing private information without consent.

**If you face cyberbullying:**
- Do not panic or blame yourself
- Save evidence (screenshots) and report the behaviour
- Talk to your parents or a trusted teacher immediately
- Never retaliate online -- it often makes things worse

**Responsible Online Behaviour:**

| Do This | Avoid This |
|---------|-----------|
| Think carefully before posting anything | Never share personal photos with strangers |
| Be kind and supportive online | Never spread rumours or unverified information |
| Guard your personal data and passwords | Never bully, mock, or harass anyone digitally |
| Report abusive content or behaviour | Never share someone's private information without their permission |

## Staying Away from Harmful Substances

During adolescence, you may encounter pressure -- from peers, older students, or media -- to try substances like **tobacco, gutka, cigarettes, beedi, alcohol**, or illegal drugs.

### Why These Substances Are Dangerous

- They cause **serious damage** to physical and mental health
- They are **highly addictive** -- once a person begins using them, the brain develops a powerful craving that is extremely difficult to overcome
- Continued use leads to **substance abuse**, a condition where the person loses control over their consumption
- Long-term effects include breathing disorders, memory impairment, liver disease, and **permanent lung damage**

> **Key idea:** Every person who struggles with addiction today began with a single "first time." Saying NO firmly and clearly -- the very first time and every time after -- is the most effective protection.

### Where to Find Help

If you or someone you know is dealing with substance use:
- Reach out to family members and close friends for support
- Confide in a trusted teacher, counsellor, or doctor
- Call the **National De-addiction Helpline: 14446** for professional guidance

The **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** (Drug-Free India Campaign), launched by the Government of India, works to spread awareness about the dangers of substance abuse, with special emphasis on protecting young people.

## What Drives All These Changes?

The physical, emotional, and behavioural changes of adolescence are primarily orchestrated by **hormones** -- specialised chemical messengers produced within the body.

- **Hormones** are produced by various glands located in different parts of the body.
- They travel through the blood to reach their target organs.
- The **brain** sends signals that trigger the release of specific hormones at the right times.
- Different hormones govern different changes -- some control growth, others regulate mood, and still others trigger the development of secondary sexual characteristics.

> **Key idea:** **Hormones** are chemical substances produced inside the body that direct and regulate the physical, emotional, and behavioural transformations of adolescence.

## Summary

- **Adolescence** spans approximately ages 10 to 19 and represents the transition from childhood to adulthood.
- It brings notable physical, biological, and emotional changes.
- **Secondary sexual characteristics** (such as deepening voice in boys, breast development in girls) distinguish males from females but are not directly involved in reproduction.
- **Puberty** is the developmental phase when the body gains the capacity for reproduction.
- The **menstrual cycle** in girls involves a regular blood discharge approximately every 28-30 days. It is a natural and healthy process, and myths about it should be replaced with scientific understanding.
- Emotional changes during adolescence include mood swings, heightened sensitivity, and growing curiosity.
- Three pillars support a healthy adolescence: **balanced nutrition**, **good personal hygiene**, and **regular physical activity**.
- Substances like tobacco, alcohol, and drugs are addictive and cause severe health damage. The safest approach is to refuse them completely.
- All adolescent changes are primarily governed by **hormones** -- chemical messengers produced by the body.
