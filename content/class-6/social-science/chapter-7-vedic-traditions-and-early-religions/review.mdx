## Key Concepts

Indian culture has many **roots** that go back thousands of years. The major early roots include:
- The **Vedas** and Vedic schools of thought
- **Buddhism**
- **Jainism**
- **Folk and tribal traditions**

## The Vedas

- **Veda** = "knowledge" (from Sanskrit *vid*)
- **Four Vedas**: Rig, Yajur, Sama, Atharva
- **Rig Veda** is the oldest
- Composed in **early Sanskrit** by **rishis** (male seers) and **rishikas** (female seers)
- Preserved **orally** for thousands of years
- **UNESCO** recognised Vedic chanting as a masterpiece of intangible heritage

### Important Vedic Idea

> **ekam sat vipra bahudha vadanti**
> "The Existent is one, but sages give it many names."

The divine is **ultimately one**, though approached through many forms.

## The Upanishads

Took Vedic ideas deeper. Key concepts:

- **Atman** — the self inside every being
- **Brahman** — the ultimate reality / divine essence in everything
- **Karma** — actions and their results
- **Rebirth** — the soul takes new bodies
- **aham brahmasmi** — "I am brahman"
- **tat tvam asi** — "You are That"

### Famous Upanishadic Stories

- **Shvetaketu** and his father Uddalaka — the banyan seed lesson on brahman
- **Nachiketa** — meets Yama and learns the atman is immortal
- **Gargi** (a rishika) — debates the rishi Yajnavalkya in King Janaka's court (women were great philosophers too!)

## Vedanta and Yoga

- **Vedanta** — everything is one divine reality (brahman)
- **Yoga** — methods (postures, breath, meditation, ethics) to realise brahman in oneself
- Together with other Vedic schools, these became the foundations of **Hinduism**

## Buddhism

- Founded by **Siddhartha Gautama**, born in **Lumbini** (now Nepal), ~560 BCE
- Saw old age, sickness, death, and an ascetic — left palace to find the cause of suffering
- Attained enlightenment under a **pipal tree** at **Bodh Gaya** (Bihar)
- Became **The Buddha** — "The Awakened One"

### Buddhist Teachings

- **Ahimsa** — non-violence in body, speech, and thought
- **The Middle Path** — neither luxury nor extreme self-denial
- **Inner discipline** and **self-conquest**
- **Compassion** for all living beings
- Founded the **Sangha** — community of monks (bhikkhus) and nuns (bhikkhunis)

Buddhism spread to Sri Lanka, Tibet, China, Korea, Japan, Southeast Asia.

## Jainism

- Founded by **Vardhamana**, born near **Vaishali** in Bihar (6th century BCE)
- After 12 years of asceticism, became **Mahavira** — "the Great Hero"
- **Jain** comes from **jina** = "conqueror" — but of ignorance, not enemies

### Three Core Jain Ideas

- **Ahimsa** — non-violence in the strictest sense
- **Anekantavada** — "not just one perspective"; truth has many sides
- **Aparigraha** — non-possession; don't cling to material things

## Other Schools of Thought

- **Charvaka / Lokayata** — believed only the material world exists; no afterlife
- Many schools coexisted — ancient India had **intellectual freedom**

## Shared Concepts Across All Schools

- **Dharma** — right conduct
- **Karma** — actions have consequences
- **Rebirth** — soul takes new forms
- **Liberation** from suffering and ignorance

These shared ideas are the **trunk** of the cultural tree.

## Folk and Tribal Traditions

- India has about **705 tribal communities** and **10.4 crore** tribal people (2011 Census)
- Famous tribal traditions:
  - **Todas** of Nilgiris worship 30+ mountain peaks
  - **Mundas, Santhals** worship **Singbonga** (creator)
  - Tribes of Arunachal worship **Donyipolo** (Sun-Moon)
- Constant exchange with mainstream Hinduism over millennia
- **Jagannath of Puri** — originally a tribal deity
- Many forms of the **Mother Goddess** are of tribal origin

## Why "Schools of Thought" not "Religions"?

These traditions include philosophy, spirituality, ethics, art, science, and society — far more than what "religion" usually means. So scholars prefer **"schools of thought"** or **"belief systems."**

## Common Confusions

- **Brahman ≠ Brahma:** Brahman is the ultimate divine reality (everywhere). Brahma is one specific deity (the creator god). Easy to mix up because they sound similar.
- **Buddhism and Jainism are separate:** Both emerged around the same time, share some ideas (ahimsa, dharma), but were founded by different teachers and have distinct paths.
- **The Vedas were not 'written' for thousands of years** — they were memorised and recited orally. Only much later were they written down.
- **Tribal traditions are not 'primitive':** Older books called them so; modern study shows they are rich and complex, with their own deep philosophies.
