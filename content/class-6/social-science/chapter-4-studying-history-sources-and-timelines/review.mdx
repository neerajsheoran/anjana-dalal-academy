## Key Concepts

- **History** = the study of the human past
- A **historian** is like a detective — collects clues, weighs evidence, builds the most likely story
- We don't always have complete information; some "jigsaw puzzle pieces" of the past are missing forever

## Who Studies the Past?

| Specialist | What they study |
|---|---|
| **Geologist** | Earth's physical features (soil, rocks, hills) |
| **Palaeontologist** | Fossils — ancient plants and animals |
| **Archaeologist** | Buried objects — tools, pottery, bones, ruins |
| **Anthropologist** | Human societies and cultures |
| **Historian** | Connects evidence to write the story of the past |
| **Epigraphist** | Reads inscriptions on stone, metal, coins |

## Measuring Time

- **Decade** = 10 years
- **Century** = 100 years
- **Millennium** = 1,000 years
- **BCE** (Before Common Era) — earlier called BC
- **CE** (Common Era) — earlier called AD
- **No Year Zero** — 1 BCE is immediately followed by 1 CE
- Today (2025) is the **21st century CE** / **3rd millennium CE**

### Calendar Note

To find years between BCE and CE: **add them and subtract 1**.
Example: From 560 BCE (Buddha) to 2024 CE = 560 + 2024 − 1 = **2,583 years**.

- **Chinese**
- **Panchanga** — Indian almanac, lists sunrise/sunset, eclipses, auspicious dates

## Sources of History (Eight Types)

1. **Written Sources** — manuscripts, inscriptions, copper plates, coins
2. **Literary Sources** — Vedas, epics (Ramayana, Mahabharata), plays, scientific texts
3. **Oral Sources** — folk songs, family genealogies, legends
4. **Archaeological** — excavated tools, pottery, bones, grains
5. **Structures** — monuments, temples, forts, mounds (buried cities)
6. **Artistic** — sculptures, paintings (Ajanta, Bhimbetka)
7. **Foreign Accounts** — travelogues like Xuanzang (China), Ibn Battuta
8. **Modern Science** — carbon dating, genetics, climate study, chemical analysis

## Early Human Life

- **Hunter-gatherers** for most of human history
- Lived in **rock shelters, caves**, small groups (bands)
- Used **fire**, made stone tools, painted cave walls
- **Bhimbetka** (Madhya Pradesh) has famous Indian rock paintings
- Buried their dead — had ideas of afterlife

## After the Ice Age (about 10,000 years ago)

- **Farming** began
- People **settled** near rivers (water + fertile soil)
- **Pottery** made for storing food and water
- **Copper** and later **iron** tools were developed
- Villages → towns → cities
- The first civilisations rose in **Mesopotamia** and the **Indus-Sarasvati** region

## Common Confusions

- **BC vs BCE / AD vs CE** — same dates, just different names. CE/BCE are now preferred globally because they are not tied to one religion.
- **Year zero doesn't exist** — going from BCE to CE always involves the "−1" adjustment.
- **Archaeologist vs Palaeontologist** — Archaeologists study **human** remains and artifacts. Palaeontologists study **non-human** fossils (plants, animals, dinosaurs).
- **History vs Pre-history** — History technically begins when writing was invented. Before that is pre-history (but archaeologists still tell us a lot about it).
