## Key Concepts

- India has **8,000 towns** and **4,000+ cities**
- About **one-third** of Indians live in urban areas
- Urban areas have their own local government called **Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)**

## Three Types of Urban Local Bodies

| Population | Name |
|---|---|
| Above **10 lakhs** | **Municipal Corporation** (Mahanagar Nigam) |
| **1 to 10 lakhs** | **Municipal Council** (Nagar Palika) |
| Smaller towns | **Nagar Panchayat** |

Examples:
- **Municipal Corporation**: Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Bengaluru
- **Municipal Council**: smaller cities
- **Nagar Panchayat**: small towns just being urbanised

## How Cities are Structured

- Cities are divided into **wards** (small neighbourhood units)
- Each ward elects a **Councillor** (or **Corporator**)
- All councillors form the **Corporation/Council**
- Head of the Corporation = **Mayor**

## Key Historic Facts

- **Oldest municipal corporation in India**: **Madras Corporation (now Greater Chennai Corporation)** — established **29 September 1688**
- **Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai**: established **1865** (originally Bombay Municipal Corporation)
- **74th Constitutional Amendment (1992)**: gave constitutional status to urban local bodies

## Services Provided by Urban Local Bodies

- Water supply
- Garbage collection and disposal (solid waste management)
- Property tax
- Business licences
- Marriage certificates
- Fire services
- Public health
- Building approvals
- Road maintenance
- Parks and public spaces
- Birth and death registration

## The Cleanest City — Indore

- **Indore, Madhya Pradesh**: India's cleanest city **7 years in a row** (Swachh Survekshan rankings)
- Success comes from BOTH the municipal corporation AND citizens cooperating
- Key practice: **waste segregation at home** (wet and dry separately)

## Ward Committee — Closest to Citizens

- Smallest unit of urban governance
- Conducts health camps, awareness campaigns
- Monitors local issues (water leaks, blocked drains, garbage)
- Reports problems to higher authorities

## Citizens' Duties

- Don't litter
- Save water and electricity
- Use public transport
- Report problems quickly
- Pay taxes honestly
- Vote in local elections
- Attend ward meetings
- Care for public spaces and respect neighbours

## Decentralisation

- Means **giving power to local levels** rather than keeping all power at the top
- Benefits: faster response, better fit with local needs, more citizen participation, more accountability

## Common Confusions

- **Mayor vs Municipal Commissioner:** The Mayor is the elected political head. The Municipal Commissioner is a senior appointed officer (often IAS) who runs the actual administration. Both are important — one represents the people, one runs the daily operations.
- **Different city, different ULB:** A small town has a Nagar Panchayat. A large town has a Municipal Council. A big city has a Municipal Corporation. The body changes based on population.
- **Urban Local Body vs Panchayati Raj:** Both are local government, but for different settings. Panchayati Raj is for rural areas; ULBs are for urban areas.
- **Citizens have duties, not just rights:** A city stays clean and works well only when citizens cooperate — paying taxes, segregating waste, not littering, reporting problems.
