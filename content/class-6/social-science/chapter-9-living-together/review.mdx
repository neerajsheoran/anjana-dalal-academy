## Key Concepts

- The **family** is the oldest and most fundamental unit of society
- A **community** is a wider circle of connected families
- Indian society is built on **family + community** working together

## Types of Families

- **Joint family** — several generations living together (grandparents, parents, uncles, aunts, cousins)
- **Nuclear family** — usually a couple with their children
- **Single-parent family** — one parent with children
- **Modified joint family** — live separately but stay closely connected

## Family Vocabulary in Indian Languages

Indian languages have many specific terms for relatives:

| Hindi term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bua | Father's sister |
| Mausi | Mother's sister |
| Chacha / Chachi | Father's younger brother / wife |
| Tau / Tai | Father's elder brother / wife |
| Mama / Mami | Mother's brother / wife |
| Nana / Nani | Mother's father / mother |
| Dada / Dadi | Father's father / mother |

**Important note:** Most Indian languages have **no word for "cousin"**. Cousins are simply brothers and sisters — showing how close those bonds are.

## Roles, Responsibilities, and Values

Family teaches important values:
- **Ahimsa** — non-violence
- **Dana** — giving and sharing
- **Seva** — service to others
- **Tyaga** — sacrifice for family
- **Dharma** — doing one's duty
- **Respect** — for elders, care for younger

Each member has roles that change with age. Children grow into responsibility over time.

## Joint vs Nuclear — Trade-offs

**Joint family pros:**
- More people to share work
- Grandparents to learn from
- Mutual support
- Continuity of traditions

**Joint family cons:**
- Complex decision-making
- Less privacy

**Nuclear family pros:**
- Independence and privacy
- Easy to move for work

**Nuclear family cons:**
- Loneliness, especially for elderly
- Less support in crises

## Two Family Stories

### Shalini (Kerala)
- 7-member joint family
- For Onam, parents bought clothes for Chittappa's family too (he had lost his job)
- Shalini accepted a simpler dress so everyone could get new clothes
- Lesson: families share what they have

### Tenzing (Northeast)
- Father took on housework when mother joined a handicraft cooperative
- Grandparents actively involved — telling stories, helping with homework, supporting community
- Family decisions are made together
- Lesson: roles are not fixed; everyone helps based on need

## Community

A community is families connected together. They share:
- **Festivals and celebrations** — weddings, festivals, naming ceremonies
- **Mutual help** — farming, illness, emergencies, repairs
- **Shared resources** — water, grazing land, forests
- **Unwritten rules** — passed down orally, enforced by elders

## Concentric Circles

Family → Community → Society → Country → World

Values learned at home (love, ahimsa, dharma) scale outward to all levels of life.

## Common Confusions

- **Joint family is not "outdated":** Many Indian families still live this way successfully. Joint and nuclear families both have benefits and challenges. No single form is "better."
- **"Cousin" in Indian languages:** The closeness suggested by calling cousins "brothers" and "sisters" is a cultural fact, not a translation error.
- **Community is not just "neighbours":** A real community shares values, traditions, mutual help — it is more than physical proximity.
- **Traditional gender roles are changing:** As shown in Tenzing's story, families today are increasingly redistributing housework, childcare, and earning between members.
