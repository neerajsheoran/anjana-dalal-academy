## Key Concepts

- The **Indian Constitution** opens with: *"India, that is Bharat..."*
- India has had **many names** across history
- The **Indian Subcontinent** = today's India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Maldives, and parts of Afghanistan

## Natural Boundaries of India

- **North** — the Himalayas
- **South** — the Indian Ocean (Cape Kanyakumari at the tip)
- **West** — Arabian Sea
- **East** — Bay of Bengal

## Indian Names for the Land

| Name | Source | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Sapta Sindhava** | Rig Veda | "Land of seven rivers" (northwest region) |
| **Bharatavarsha** | Mahabharata, Puranas | "Country of the Bharatas" |
| **Jambudvipa** | Mahabharata, Ashoka's inscriptions | "Island of the jamun tree" |
| **Bharata / Bharat** | Most Indian languages today | Lasting name in everyday use |

### Verse from the Vishnu Purana

> "The country that lies north of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains is called Bharata."

### Ancient Tamil Poetry

A king's fame is praised as extending "from Cape Kumari in the south, from the great mountain in the north." Shows ancient Indians knew the whole Subcontinent's geography.

### Emperor Ashoka

Around **250 BCE**, Ashoka used "Jambudvipa" in his stone inscriptions to refer to his entire empire — spanning today's India, parts of Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan.

## Foreign Names for the Land

All foreign names trace back to **"Sindhu"** — the Sanskrit name for the Indus River.

| Language | Name | Path |
|---|---|---|
| Persian | Hind, Hindu | Sindhu → Hindhu (S became H in Persian) |
| Greek | Indoi, Indike | Hindhu → Indoi (Greek dropped the H) |
| Latin / English | India | Greek → Latin → English |
| Chinese | Yintu, Tianzhu | Sindhu → Hindhu → Indu → Yindu |
| Medieval Persian/Arabic | Hindustan | "Land of the Hindus" |

## Important Note

In ancient Persian, the word **"Hindu" was purely geographical** — referring to people living around the Indus. It did **NOT** mean a religion. The religious meaning came much later.

## Foreign Visitors to India

- **Xuanzang** — Chinese Buddhist pilgrim, travelled to India in the 7th century CE, stayed 17 years, took back Buddhist texts and translated them. A vital source for Indian history.
- Many other foreign travellers came over the centuries — Persian, Arab, Greek, European — and wrote about India.

## Why So Many Names?

1. India was **geographically distinctive** — clear natural boundaries
2. India was a **great trading hub** for thousands of years
3. India was a **centre of learning** — Buddhism, mathematics, astronomy, medicine
4. India was **culturally diverse** — different regions had their own names

## Common Confusions

- **"Hindu" in ancient Persian** was geographical (land of the Indus), not religious. The religious meaning of "Hindu" came much later.
- **Bharata** the name comes from "Bharata" the Vedic people (and later, several kings) — not from "bhar" meaning weight or burden.
- **Indian Subcontinent ≠ India alone.** The Subcontinent includes neighbouring countries that share the same landmass and many cultural roots.
- **Hindustan vs India** — both refer to the same country. Hindustan is older in medieval usage; India is the official English name today.
