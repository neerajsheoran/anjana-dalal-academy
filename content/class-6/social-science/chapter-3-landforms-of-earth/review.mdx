## Key Concepts

- A **landform** is the natural shape of the land surface
- The three main landforms: **Mountains**, **Plateaus**, **Plains**
- Other important landforms: **Deserts**, **Valleys**, **Islands**, **Coasts**

## Mountains

- Tall, with a broad base, steep slopes, and a narrow summit
- **Young mountains** (Himalayas) — tall, pointy, still growing
- **Old mountains** (Aravallis) — short, rounded, worn down over time
- Famous Indian peaks: **Mount Everest** (highest in the world), **Kanchenjunga**, **Anamudi** (highest in south India)
- **Mountain ranges**: Himalayas (Asia), Andes (S. America), Alps (Europe), Rockies (N. America)
- Plant zones: **Montane forests** at lower altitudes (conifers like pine, deodar); grasses and lichens higher up

## Life in the Mountains

- **Terrace farming** on slopes
- **Herding** of sheep, goats, yaks
- **Tourism** — hill stations, trekking, skiing
- **Pilgrimages** — Char Dham, Vaishno Devi, Amarnath
- Hazards: landslides, avalanches, flash floods, cloudbursts

## Sacred Mountains

- **Mount Everest** — Tibetan: **Chomolungma** (Mother Goddess of the World); Nepali: **Sagarmatha** (Goddess of the Sky)
- **Mount Kailash** — sacred to Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Bon followers

## Indian Mountaineers

- **Bachendri Pal** — first Indian woman to climb Mount Everest (1984)
- **Arunima Sinha** — climbed Mount Everest in 2013 with a prosthetic leg

## Plateaus

- Raised land with a **flat top** and often **steep sides**
- **Tibetan Plateau** — largest and highest in the world (Roof of the World)
- **Deccan Plateau** — one of the oldest plateaus, formed by volcanic activity
- "Storehouses of minerals" — many of the world's largest mines are on plateaus
- **Chhota Nagpur Plateau** — rich in iron, coal, manganese (Jharkhand)
- Famous waterfalls: **Jog Falls** (Karnataka), **Hundru Falls** (Jharkhand), **Nohkalikai Falls** (Meghalaya, 340 m drop)

## Plains

- Wide, flat or gently sloping land
- Usually less than **300 m** above sea level
- **Floodplains** are formed when rivers deposit sediments (rock, sand, silt)
- Plains have **very fertile soil** — ideal for farming
- **Gangetic Plain** — one of the most populous areas on Earth; ~40 crore Indians live there
- Crops: rice, wheat, maize, barley, millets, cotton, jute, sugarcane
- Modern challenge: **groundwater depletion** due to over-irrigation

## Rivers and Culture

- River **sources** (origin in mountains) and **confluences** (meeting points) are often sacred
- **Triveni Sangam** at Prayagraj — meeting of Ganga, Yamuna and the mythical Saraswati
- **Kumbh Mela** — held at river confluences; one of the world's largest gatherings
- Rivers also serve as waterways for transport — still used today on the Ganga and Brahmaputra

## Deserts

- Hot deserts: **Sahara** (Africa), **Thar** (Rajasthan & Gujarat)
- Cold deserts: **Gobi** (Asia), **Antarctica**
- Despite harsh conditions, communities have adapted with rich culture (folk songs, camel trade, Pushkar Fair)

## The Five Tinais of Ancient Tamil Poetry

| Tinai | Landscape | Main occupation |
|---|---|---|
| Kurinji | Hills/mountains | Hunting, gathering |
| Mullai | Grassland and forest | Cattle rearing |
| Marudam | Farmland | Farming |
| Neydal | Coastal | Fishing, sailing |
| Palai | Dry, desert-like | Trading, travel |

A poetic Indian classification that connects landform to lifestyle.

## Common Confusions

- **Mountain vs Hill:** Hills are smaller and have rounder tops; mountains are taller with sharper peaks. There is no exact height cutoff — it depends on the local context.
- **Plateau vs Mountain:** A mountain rises to a peak; a plateau is flat on top.
- **Plain vs Floodplain:** A floodplain is a specific kind of plain formed by sediments deposited by a river. All floodplains are plains, but not all plains are floodplains.
- **Young vs Old mountains:** Young means **recently formed** (millions of years ago, but recent in Earth's timescale). Old mountains formed even earlier and have been worn down.
