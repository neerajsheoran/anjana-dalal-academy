## Key Concepts

A **civilisation** has these features:
- Government and administration
- Cities with town planning
- Specialised crafts
- Trade (local and long-distance)
- Writing
- Cultural ideas (art, religion, customs)
- Productive agriculture

## The Indus-Sarasvati Civilisation

- Also called **Harappan** or **Indus** civilisation
- **Time**: about **2600 BCE to 1900 BCE** (~700 years)
- **Region**: today's Pakistan, north-west India (Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat)
- One of the **oldest civilisations** in the world
- First discovered in **1920–21** at the city of Harappa

## Main Cities

| City | Modern location |
|---|---|
| **Harappa** | Punjab, Pakistan |
| **Mohenjo-daro** | Sindh, Pakistan |
| **Dholavira** | Gujarat, India |
| **Rakhigarhi** | Haryana, India |
| **Kalibangan** | Rajasthan, India |
| **Lothal** | Gujarat, India |

## Town Planning Features

- **Grid streets** — aligned with cardinal directions
- **Two zones**: Upper Town (Citadel) for elite + Lower Town for common people
- **Standardised bricks** of same size in all cities
- **Quality of houses similar** for big and small homes
- The **Great Bath** at Mohenjo-daro — 12×7 m tank, waterproofed with tar

## Water Management — Their Biggest Achievement

- **Wells** — about 700 brick-lined wells at Mohenjo-daro alone
- **Drainage** — covered drains under streets carried wastewater out of cities
- **Reservoirs** — Dholavira had 6 large reservoirs, biggest was 73 m long
- **Underground water channels** connected reservoirs to the city
- The most advanced ancient water system in the world at the time

## Farming and Food

- Crops: wheat, barley, rice, millets, pulses, vegetables
- **First in Eurasia** to grow **cotton** — used to weave cloth
- Domesticated cattle, sheep, goats; ate fish
- Used the **plough**
- Spices like **turmeric, ginger** detected in pottery
- Even **bananas** were eaten!

## Crafts and Trade

- **Carnelian beads** — reddish semi-precious stones from Gujarat; found as far as Mesopotamia and Egypt
- **Shell bangles** — many cover entire arm (style still in Rajasthan/Gujarat)
- **Bronze tools and figurines** (mix of copper and tin)
- **Lothal** had a 217 m brick basin — likely the world's first known **dockyard**
- Exported: beads, cotton, timber, ornaments
- Imported: copper, gold, tin, special stones

## The Harappan Script

- **Thousands of small stone seals** found
- Each shows an animal (bull, unicorn, tiger) and symbols
- **Still undeciphered** — one of the world's great unsolved puzzles
- Probably used as trade markers / stamps

## Cultural Glimpses

- The famous **"Dancing Girl"** bronze figurine from Mohenjo-daro (10.8 cm tall)
- The **"Priest King"** statue
- A **namaste figurine** in seated pose with joined hands
- **Swastika** symbols on seals
- A pot showing the **"Thirsty Crow"** story — 4,000 years old!

## Why Did the Civilisation Decline?

NOT because of invasion or war (no evidence found). The main causes:

1. **Climate change** — region became drier from about 2200 BCE
2. **Drying of the Sarasvati River** — cities along it (Kalibangan, Banawali) abandoned

People returned to rural village life. The culture survived and passed on to later India.

## Common Confusions

- **Indus Valley vs Indus-Sarasvati civilisation:** Same civilisation, different name. "Indus Valley" was used when scholars thought it was only in the Indus valley. We now know it spread far into India along the Sarasvati basin too — hence "Indus-Sarasvati" is more accurate.
- **Harappan vs Harappa:** Harappa is the **name of one specific city**. "Harappan" refers to the **entire civilisation** because that city was discovered first.
- **Why call it "First Urbanisation" of India?** Because cities of this scale and complexity had never existed before in the Subcontinent. A "second urbanisation" came much later, in the Gangetic Plain around 600 BCE.
- **Invasion theory is outdated** — older textbooks said Aryans invaded and destroyed Harappan cities. There is no archaeological evidence for this. The decline was caused by environmental factors.
