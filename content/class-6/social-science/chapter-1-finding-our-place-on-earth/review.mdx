## Key Concepts

- A **map** is a small picture of an area, drawn as if viewed from above
- An **atlas** is a book of maps
- Three types of maps:
  - **Physical maps** show natural features (mountains, rivers, oceans)
  - **Political maps** show countries, states, cities, borders
  - **Thematic maps** show one specific kind of information (rainfall, population, etc.)
- Every map needs **distance** (scale), **direction** and **symbols**
- A **scale** tells how much the real area has been shrunk — e.g. 1 cm = 100 km

## Directions

- **Four cardinal directions:** North, East, South, West
- **Four intermediate:** Northeast, Southeast, Southwest, Northwest
- Most maps mark **N** with an arrow at the top

## The Earth is a Sphere

- A round Earth cannot be flattened perfectly on paper
- A **globe** is a small round model of the Earth — more accurate than a flat map
- An **atlas** uses clever techniques to flatten the Earth onto pages

## Latitudes (East-West Lines)

- Measure **how far north or south** of the Equator a place is
- **Equator** is at **0°**
- **North Pole** is at **90°N**, **South Pole** is at **90°S**
- Climate is hot near the Equator, moderate in between, cold near the poles

## Longitudes (North-South Lines)

- Measure **how far east or west** of the Prime Meridian a place is
- **Prime Meridian (Greenwich Meridian)** is at **0°** longitude
- Longitudes go from 0° to 180° East and 0° to 180° West
- Together with latitude, longitude pins down any place on Earth

## Indian Connection

- Ancient India had its own prime meridian called **madhya rekha** passing through **Ujjayini (Ujjain)**
- **Varahamihira** was a famous Indian astronomer who lived there ~1,500 years ago

## Time Zones

- Earth spins 360° in 24 hours → that is **15° per hour**
- Moving east, local time goes up; moving west, local time goes down
- **Indian Standard Time (IST)** is **5 hours 30 minutes** ahead of Greenwich
- **International Date Line** is at about **180°** longitude — crossing it changes the date by one day

## Quick Facts to Remember

- Latitude lines are **parallel** (they never meet) — the Equator is the longest
- Longitude lines are **half-circles** — they all meet at both poles, so they are all roughly equal in length
- Delhi: about **29°N, 77°E**
- Mumbai: about **19°N, 73°E**
- India spans about **8°N to 37°N** and **68°E to 97°E**

## Common Confusions

- **Local time vs Standard time:** A place's local time depends on its exact longitude. Standard time is the *one* time chosen for the whole country, even if some parts of it are slightly ahead or behind by local time.
- **Latitude vs Longitude:** Remember — la**T**itude has a "T" like a sideways line (east-west). LonG**i**tude has an "I" like a vertical line (north-south).
- **0° latitude is the Equator**, but **0° longitude is at Greenwich** — they are not the same point. They cross at one specific spot in the Gulf of Guinea.
