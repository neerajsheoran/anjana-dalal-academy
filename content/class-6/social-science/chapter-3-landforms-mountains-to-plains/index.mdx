## What is a Landform?

A **landform** is the shape that the surface of the Earth takes in different places. Some areas are mountainous; others are flat; some are raised but flat on top. These differences are not random. They form over millions of years through processes like the movement of giant plates beneath the Earth, the flow of rivers, the wearing away by wind and water, and volcanic activity.

The three main landforms are:

- **Mountains** — tall and steep
- **Plateaus** — high but flat on top
- **Plains** — wide and gently sloping

Beyond these three, there are also **deserts**, **valleys**, **islands**, and **coastal regions**. Each one has its own climate, plant life, animal life, and way of human living.

If you drove from Chhota Nagpur in Jharkhand to Prayagraj in Uttar Pradesh and then up to Almora in Uttarakhand, you would cross all three main landforms — a plateau, then plains, then mountains. India is rich in this variety.

## Mountains

A **mountain** is a piece of land that rises sharply above its surroundings. Mountains usually have:
- A **broad base** at the bottom
- **Steep slopes** rising up
- A **narrow summit** (peak) at the top

Some mountains have **snow** at the top all year because the air at great heights stays freezing cold. Others have snow only in winter. Smaller, gentler hills with rounded tops are simply called **hills**.

### Young Mountains and Old Mountains

Not all mountains are the same age, though they all formed millions of years ago.

- **Young mountains** like the **Himalayas** are tall and pointy. They are still growing in height — yes, even today! The Indian landmass is slowly pushing into Asia, lifting the Himalayas up by a few millimetres every year.
- **Old mountains** like the **Aravalli Range** in Rajasthan are short and rounded. They have been worn down over hundreds of millions of years by wind, rain, and ice. They are some of the oldest mountains on Earth.

### Some Famous Mountains

- **Mount Everest** (between Tibet and Nepal) — at 8,849 m, the highest in the world
- **Kanchenjunga** (between Nepal and Sikkim, India) — the third highest in the world
- **Anamudi** (in Kerala) — the highest peak in southern India
- **Mont Blanc** (in Europe) — the highest in the Alps
- **Mount Aconcagua** (South America) — the highest in the Andes
- **Mount Kilimanjaro** (Africa) — a tall lone mountain, not part of any range

Most mountains do not stand alone. They form **mountain ranges** — long chains of peaks. The Himalayas, the Andes, the Alps, the Rockies — all are ranges that stretch for thousands of kilometres.

### What Grows on Mountains?

Mountains have their own special plants and animals. On the lower slopes, you find **montane forests** — thick forests with tall **conifer trees** like pine, deodar and fir. These trees have cone-shaped tops and thin pointed leaves to handle the cold.

Higher up, the trees give way to **grasses, mosses and lichens** — tougher plants that survive in the thin, cold air.

Mountain animals are also unique. The **snow leopard**, **Himalayan tahr**, **yak**, **ibex**, **mountain hare**, **golden eagle** and **black bear** are some Himalayan residents. Each is built for the cold and the steep slopes.

### Life in the Mountains

Living on a mountain has its share of challenges.

- The land is **rocky and steep** — regular farming is difficult.
- People use **terrace farming** — cutting steps into the slope so that crops can grow on the flat parts. This is common in Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim and the Nilgiris.
- Many mountain communities also keep **sheep, goats and yaks** — herding is often easier than farming.
- **Tourism** is a big source of income. Hill stations like Shimla, Manali, Mussoorie, Darjeeling, and Ooty are favourite destinations. Adventure activities like trekking, mountaineering, paragliding and skiing draw young travellers.
- For centuries, people have also made **pilgrimages** to mountain shrines — the Char Dham in Uttarakhand (Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, Badrinath), Vaishno Devi in Jammu, Amarnath in Kashmir.

But there are dangers too:
- **Landslides** — rocks and earth slipping down a slope
- **Flash floods** — sudden river floods from heavy rain or cloudbursts
- **Avalanches** — sudden falls of snow or ice
- **Cloudbursts** — short, very heavy bursts of rain
- **Bitter cold** at high altitudes

### Sacred Mountains

Many cultures, including ours, treat mountains as sacred. Tibetans call Mount Everest **Chomolungma** — "Mother Goddess of the World." Nepalis call it **Sagarmatha** — "Goddess of the Sky." **Mount Kailash** in Tibet is sacred to Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and followers of the ancient Tibetan Bon religion.

### Indian Mountain Climbers

India has produced remarkable mountaineers:
- **Bachendri Pal** — in 1984, the first Indian woman to climb Mount Everest. Awarded the Padma Shri the same year, and Padma Bhushan in 2019.
- **Arunima Sinha** — lost a leg in an accident at age 22 but climbed Mount Everest in 2013 using a prosthetic leg. She went on to climb the highest peak on every continent. Awarded the Padma Shri in 2015.

## Plateaus

A **plateau** is a landform that is raised above the surrounding land but is mostly **flat on top**. Imagine a giant table standing in the landscape — that is what a plateau looks like.

- The **Tibetan Plateau** in Asia is the largest and highest plateau in the world. Its average height is about 4,500 m — so high that it is called the **Roof of the World**.
- The **Deccan Plateau** in central and southern India is one of the oldest plateaus on Earth. It was formed by volcanic eruptions millions of years ago.

### What Plateaus Are Good For

Plateaus are often called **storehouses of minerals**. Many of the world's biggest mines are located on plateaus.

- The **East African Plateau** is famous for **gold and diamonds**.
- The **Chhota Nagpur Plateau** in Jharkhand has huge reserves of **iron, coal and manganese**. Much of India's steel industry is built on the wealth of this plateau.

Plateaus also have beautiful **waterfalls** because rivers fall sharply off their edges:
- **Jog Falls** on the Sharavati River (Western Ghats, Karnataka)
- **Hundru Falls** on the Subarnarekha River (Chhota Nagpur Plateau)
- **Nohkalikai Falls** dropping 340 m from the Cherrapunji Plateau in Meghalaya

Plateaus formed by old lava flows (like parts of the Deccan) have **rich black soil**, ideal for growing **cotton**. That is why Maharashtra and parts of Madhya Pradesh are major cotton producers.

## Plains

A **plain** is a wide, flat or gently rolling area of land. Plains are usually not very high — generally under 300 metres above sea level.

### How Plains Are Made

Many plains are formed by **rivers**. Mountain rivers carry tiny pieces of rock, sand, and soil called **sediments**. As the rivers flow into flatter land, they slow down and drop these sediments. Over thousands of years, layer after layer of sediment builds up, creating **floodplains** — very flat, very fertile farmland.

This is why **plains are the breadbasket of the world**. The soil is rich, the land is flat for ploughing, and rivers nearby give water.

### India's Most Important Plain

The **Gangetic Plain** in north India is one of the largest and most fertile plains on Earth. It stretches across Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, and beyond. About **40 crore people** — more than one in four Indians — live here.

Main crops grown:
- **Food grains:** rice, wheat, maize, barley, millets
- **Fibre crops:** cotton, jute, hemp
- **Sugarcane** in the western parts

Traditionally, farming in this plain depended on **rainfall** (especially the monsoon). In recent decades, farmers have shifted to **irrigation** — water carried through canals or pumped from below the ground. This has helped grow more food, but it has also caused **groundwater depletion** — the water under the ground is running out. This is one of India's biggest challenges for the future.

### Rivers Are More Than Water

Plains are usually built around rivers. In India, rivers are not just sources of water — they are deeply cultural.

- People perform rituals and ceremonies at **sources of rivers** (where they begin in the mountains) and at **confluences** (where two rivers meet).
- Sacred spots: **Gangotri** (source of Ganga), **Yamunotri** (source of Yamuna), **Triveni Sangam** at Prayagraj (where Ganga, Yamuna and the mythical Saraswati meet).
- Festivals like **Kumbh Mela** happen at river confluences and attract millions of pilgrims.

Because plains are flat, rivers also work as **highways**. Even today, boats carry goods and people along the Ganga and Brahmaputra. In olden days, before roads and railways, this was the main way to travel long distances.

## Deserts — A Special Landform

Beyond the three main landforms, **deserts** deserve a special mention. A desert is a large, very dry area where rainfall is rare.

- **Hot deserts:** The **Sahara** in northern Africa, the **Thar Desert** in Rajasthan and Gujarat
- **Cold deserts:** The **Gobi Desert** in Mongolia and parts of China. Surprisingly, **Antarctica** is also considered a cold desert because it gets very little precipitation.

Even though deserts are tough places to live, people have adapted. Communities in the Thar Desert have rich traditions of folk music, colourful clothes, and trade routes that used camels (the famous "ship of the desert") to move across the sand. **Pushkar Fair** in Rajasthan is one of India's most famous desert festivals.

## A Different Way of Looking at Landforms

Long ago, ancient Tamil poets in the **Sangam period** classified land into **five tinais** — five landscapes, each linked to a kind of life, mood, and god. The five tinais are:

| Tinai | Landscape | Main occupation |
|---|---|---|
| Kurinji | Hilly / mountainous | Hunting and gathering |
| Mullai | Grassland and forest | Cattle rearing |
| Marudam | Fertile farmland | Farming |
| Neydal | Coastal | Fishing and sailing |
| Palai | Dry, desert-like | Trading, travel |

This poetic classification shows that ancient Indians understood the deep link between **the land you live on** and **the life you can live**. The same insight is at the heart of geography today.

## Before We Close

- The three main landforms are **mountains**, **plateaus**, and **plains**. Each has its own climate, flora, fauna, and challenges.
- **Mountains**: tall and steep; people live there with effort but the views, the cool air, and the pilgrim sites are valuable.
- **Plateaus**: raised flat lands; rich in minerals and waterfalls; the Deccan and Tibetan plateaus are world famous.
- **Plains**: wide, fertile, and densely populated; India's Gangetic Plain is one of the most populated places on Earth.
- **Deserts** are special — dry, hot or cold, but home to unique cultures and animals.
- Indian culture has celebrated each landform — through pilgrimages, poetry, festivals, and traditions for thousands of years.
