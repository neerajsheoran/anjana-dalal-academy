## Key Concepts

- Geometric constructions use only a **compass** and **straightedge** (ruler without markings)
- A compass draws circles/arcs and transfers lengths exactly -- no reading error
- The **Sulba Sutras** (800-200 BCE) are ancient Indian texts with precise geometric construction methods
- To draw a **circle**: fix compass to desired radius, press needle at centre, sweep pencil around
- To **copy a segment** without measuring: pick up the length with compass, transfer to a new ray
- A **perpendicular bisector** splits a segment into two equal halves at 90°
  - Method: arcs from both endpoints (radius > half the segment), join intersection points
- An **angle bisector** splits an angle into two equal parts
  - Method: arc from vertex cuts both arms; arcs from those points intersect inside; join vertex to intersection
- To **drop a perpendicular** from an external point to a line: arc from the point cuts the line at two points; arcs from those points intersect on the other side; join
- To draw a **parallel line** at a given distance: construct perpendiculars at two points on the line, mark equal distances, join
- **60° angle**: draw an arc from the vertex, use same radius from the arc's intersection to cut another arc -- equilateral triangle principle
- **120°**: two consecutive 60° steps on the arc
- **90°**: perpendicular bisector method, or bisect the gap between 60° and 120°
- **30°**: bisect 60°; **45°**: bisect 90°
- **SSS construction**: draw base, arcs from both endpoints with the other two side lengths, join intersection
- **SAS construction**: draw one side, use protractor for the angle, mark the second side, join
- **Triangle Inequality**: any side must be shorter than the sum of the other two sides
- A **regular hexagon** is constructed by stepping the radius around a circle exactly 6 times

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Compass** | Hinged tool for drawing circles, arcs, and transferring lengths |
| **Construction** | Drawing a figure using only compass and straightedge |
| **Perpendicular bisector** | A line that halves a segment at 90° |
| **Angle bisector** | A ray that divides an angle into two equal parts |
| **Equilateral triangle** | Triangle with three equal sides and three 60° angles |
| **SSS construction** | Triangle built when all three side lengths are known |
| **SAS construction** | Triangle built when two sides and the included angle are known |
| **Triangle inequality** | Any side < sum of the other two sides |
| **Sulba Sutras** | Ancient Indian geometric construction texts (800-200 BCE) |

## Remember These

- A 60° angle uses the **equilateral triangle** property: all sides equal to the compass radius
- SSS construction **fails** if one side ≥ sum of the other two (triangle inequality violation)
- The perpendicular bisector gives you both the **midpoint** and a **90° angle** in one construction
- The side of a regular hexagon inscribed in a circle equals the circle's **radius**
- Always set compass radius **greater than half** the segment when constructing a perpendicular bisector
