## Key Concepts

- A **factor** divides a number exactly with zero remainder; factors are always ≤ the number
- A **multiple** is obtained by multiplying a number by 1, 2, 3, ...; multiples are always ≥ the number
- If A is a factor of B, then B is a multiple of A
- Every number has a **finite** number of factors but **infinitely many** multiples
- **1** is a factor of every number; every number is a factor of itself
- A **prime number** has exactly two factors: 1 and itself (e.g., 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13)
- A **composite number** has more than two factors (e.g., 4, 6, 9, 12, 15)
- **1 is neither prime nor composite** (it has only one factor)
- **2 is the only even prime** number
- **Co-prime numbers** share no common factor other than 1 (HCF = 1); they need not be prime themselves
- The **Sieve of Eratosthenes** finds all primes up to a limit by eliminating multiples of each prime starting from 2
- **Prime factorisation**: expressing a number as a product of primes (use **factor tree** or **division ladder**)
- **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic**: every number > 1 has a unique prime factorisation
- **HCF** (Highest Common Factor): largest factor common to two or more numbers; use common primes with **lowest** powers
- **LCM** (Lowest Common Multiple): smallest multiple common to two or more numbers; use all primes with **highest** powers
- **HCF(A, B) x LCM(A, B) = A x B** (works only for two numbers)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Factor** | A number that divides another exactly, leaving no remainder |
| **Multiple** | Product of a number and any natural number |
| **Prime number** | A number > 1 with exactly two factors: 1 and itself |
| **Composite number** | A number > 1 with more than two factors |
| **Co-prime** | Two numbers whose only common factor is 1 |
| **Sieve of Eratosthenes** | Algorithm to find all primes up to a given limit |
| **Prime factorisation** | Writing a number as a product of its prime factors |
| **Factor tree** | Branching diagram to decompose a number into primes |
| **HCF** | Largest factor shared by two or more numbers |
| **LCM** | Smallest multiple shared by two or more numbers |
| **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic** | Every number > 1 has a unique prime factorisation |

## Remember These

- **1 is NOT prime** -- it has only one factor, not two
- For HCF: pick **common** primes with **lower** powers; for LCM: pick **all** primes with **higher** powers
- HCF x LCM = product of the two numbers (does NOT work for three or more numbers directly)
- To test if a number is prime, check divisibility only up to its **square root**
- Co-prime does not mean prime -- 8 and 15 are both composite but co-prime as a pair
