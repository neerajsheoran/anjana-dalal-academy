## Key Concepts

- **Negative numbers** are numbers below zero, written with a minus sign (-1, -2, -3, ...)
- **Integers** = { ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ... } -- all whole numbers including negatives and zero
- **Zero** is neither positive nor negative
- On the number line: right = larger, left = smaller; **-4 < -1** because -4 is further left
- Among negative numbers, the one **closer to zero** is **larger** (e.g., -3 > -7)
- **Absolute value** |n| = distance from zero, always non-negative; |8| = |-8| = 8
- **Opposites**: numbers equally far from zero on opposite sides (e.g., 7 and -7); their sum = 0
- **Brahmagupta** (598 CE, Rajasthan) first formalised rules for negative numbers and zero in the Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE)
- Adding two positives: add normally, result is positive
- Adding two negatives: add absolute values, result is **negative**
- Adding a positive and negative: subtract absolute values, keep sign of the **larger** absolute value
- **Subtracting an integer** = adding its opposite: **a - b = a + (-b)**
- **Subtracting a negative** = adding a positive: **a - (-b) = a + b**
- Negative x negative = positive (shown by extending multiplication patterns)
- Real-life uses: temperature below 0°C, depth below sea level, bank overdrafts, basement floors, BCE dates

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Negative number** | A number below zero, shown with a minus sign |
| **Integer** | Any whole number: positive, negative, or zero |
| **Opposite** | A pair of integers equally far from zero on opposite sides (e.g., 4 and -4) |
| **Absolute value** | Distance of an integer from zero -- always non-negative |
| **Number line** | A line where integers are placed in order from left to right |
| **Ascending order** | Smallest to largest (left to right) |
| **Descending order** | Largest to smallest (right to left) |
| **Brahmagupta** | Indian mathematician (598-668 CE) who first formalised rules for negative numbers and zero |

## Integer Addition & Subtraction Rules

| Operation | Rule | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| (+) + (+) | Add, result positive | 6 + 5 = 11 |
| (-) + (-) | Add absolute values, result negative | -6 + (-5) = -11 |
| (+) + (-) | Subtract, keep sign of larger | 8 + (-3) = 5 |
| (-) + (+) | Subtract, keep sign of larger | -8 + 3 = -5 |
| a - b | Rewrite as a + (-b) | 7 - 10 = -3 |
| a - (-b) | Rewrite as a + b | 7 - (-10) = 17 |

## Remember These

- **Subtracting a negative = adding a positive** (removing a debt increases wealth)
- Among negatives, the number with the **smaller** absolute value is actually the **larger** number (-3 > -10)
- Zero is an integer but is NEITHER positive NOR negative
- Temperature range = higher temp - lower temp (use subtraction rules: e.g., 6 - (-10) = 16°C range)
- Brahmagupta's rules from 628 CE are the SAME rules used in classrooms today -- 1400 years old
