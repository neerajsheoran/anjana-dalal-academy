## Key Concepts

- A **point** has no size -- it marks an exact position; labelled with capital letters (P, Q, R)
- A **line segment** has two endpoints and a fixed, measurable length
- A **ray** has one endpoint (initial point) and extends infinitely in one direction
- A **line** extends infinitely in both directions with no endpoints
- An **angle** is formed when two rays share a common starting point (the **vertex**)
- Angles are measured in **degrees (°)** using a **protractor**
- Full rotation = **360°**, half = **180°**, quarter = **90°**
- **Acute angle**: between 0° and 90°; **Right angle**: exactly 90°; **Obtuse angle**: between 90° and 180°
- **Straight angle** = 180°; **Reflex angle**: between 180° and 360°; **Complete angle** = 360°
- **Complementary angles** add up to **90°** (only acute angles can be complementary)
- **Supplementary angles** add up to **180°**
- A **linear pair** = two adjacent angles on a straight line; always add to 180°
- **Vertically opposite angles** are always **equal**
- **Perpendicular lines** meet at 90° (symbol: ⊥)
- **Parallel lines** never meet and stay equally spaced (symbol: ∥)
- A **transversal** crossing two parallel lines creates 8 angles with special relationships
- **Corresponding angles** are equal (F-shape); **Alternate interior angles** are equal (Z-shape); **Co-interior angles** add to 180° (C-shape)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Line Segment** | Straight path between two endpoints; finite length |
| **Ray** | Starts at one point, extends infinitely in one direction |
| **Line** | Extends infinitely in both directions |
| **Vertex** | The point where two arms of an angle meet |
| **Acute angle** | More than 0° and less than 90° |
| **Right angle** | Exactly 90° |
| **Obtuse angle** | More than 90° and less than 180° |
| **Reflex angle** | More than 180° and less than 360° |
| **Complementary** | Two angles whose sum is 90° |
| **Supplementary** | Two angles whose sum is 180° |
| **Linear pair** | Adjacent angles on a straight line (sum = 180°) |
| **Vertically opposite** | Angles across from each other at an intersection; always equal |
| **Transversal** | A line that crosses two or more other lines |

## Remember These

- Complement = **90° - given angle**; Supplement = **180° - given angle**
- When two lines cross, they form **two pairs** of equal vertically opposite angles (e.g., 42°, 138°, 42°, 138°)
- Diagonals of a rectangle are NOT lines of symmetry -- do not confuse with perpendicular bisectors
- Memory tricks: **C**omplementary = **C**orner (90°); **S**upplementary = **S**traight (180°)
- A transversal with parallel lines: if one angle is known, all 8 angles can be found
