## Key Concepts

- **Symmetry** means a figure looks unchanged after a reflection (flip) or rotation (turn)
- **Line symmetry** (mirror symmetry): a line divides the figure into two identical mirror halves
- The **folding test**: fold along the line of symmetry -- both halves must land perfectly on each other
- A **scalene triangle** has 0 lines of symmetry; **isosceles** has 1; **equilateral** has 3
- A **rectangle** has 2 lines of symmetry; a **square** has 4; a **circle** has unlimited
- A regular polygon with n sides has exactly **n** lines of symmetry
- **Reflection** maps each point to the opposite side of the mirror line at equal distance; the mirror line is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining a point and its image
- **Rotational symmetry**: the figure looks the same after rotating by less than 360° around a centre point
- **Order of rotational symmetry** = 360° / angle of rotation
- Equilateral triangle: order **3** (120°); Square: order **4** (90°); Regular hexagon: order **6** (60°)
- Rectangle (non-square): order **2** (180°); Circle: **infinite** order
- Some figures have only rotational symmetry (e.g., parallelogram, letters N, S, Z)
- Some figures have only line symmetry (e.g., isosceles triangle, arrow shape)
- Regular polygons have **both** line and rotational symmetry
- Only three regular polygons **tessellate** by themselves: equilateral triangle (6 at vertex), square (4 at vertex), regular hexagon (3 at vertex)
- **Tessellation** = covering a flat surface with no gaps and no overlaps
- Hexagons tessellate most efficiently -- maximum area for minimum boundary (honeycomb)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Symmetry** | Looking the same after a reflection or rotation |
| **Line of symmetry** | A line dividing a figure into two mirror-image halves |
| **Reflection** | Flipping a figure across a line to create its mirror image |
| **Rotational symmetry** | Figure looks identical after turning by less than 360° |
| **Centre of rotation** | The fixed point around which the figure rotates |
| **Order of symmetry** | Number of times a figure matches itself in a full 360° turn |
| **Angle of rotation** | Smallest turn that makes the figure look the same |
| **Tessellation** | Gap-free, overlap-free covering of a surface using shapes |

## Lines of Symmetry -- Quick Reference

| Figure | Lines of Symmetry | Rotational Order |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Equilateral triangle | 3 | 3 |
| Square | 4 | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 | 2 |
| Regular hexagon | 6 | 6 |
| Circle | Infinite | Infinite |
| Parallelogram | 0 | 2 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 | 1 |

## Remember These

- Rectangle diagonals are NOT lines of symmetry -- only the vertical and horizontal centre lines work
- Letters with NO symmetry: F, G, J, L, N, P, Q, R, S, Z
- A figure has rotational symmetry only when order is **2 or higher** (every figure trivially matches at 360°)
- Parallelograms have rotational symmetry but ZERO lines of symmetry
- For tessellation: interior angles meeting at a vertex must sum to exactly **360°**
