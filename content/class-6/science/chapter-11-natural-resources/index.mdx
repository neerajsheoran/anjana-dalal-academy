## The Wealth Beneath, Around, and Above Us

Step outside your home and take a deep breath. The air filling your lungs, the water in your glass, the soil in the garden, the sunlight warming your face — none of these was manufactured in a factory. They all come from nature.

![What are natural resources](natural-resources.png)

**Natural resources** are the materials and energy sources that nature provides and that all living organisms depend upon.

> **Central thought:** Natural resources sustain every form of life on Earth. Whether future generations can enjoy them depends entirely on how wisely we use them today.

---

## Air — The Unseen Essential

We are constantly submerged in a sea of air, yet we seldom think about it. Without air, no animal could survive beyond a few minutes, and no plant could manufacture food.

### Composition of Air

Air is a **mixture of gases**:

| Gas | Approximate share in dry air |
| --- | ---------------------------- |
| Nitrogen (N₂) | ~78% |
| Oxygen (O₂) | ~21% |
| Argon and other inert gases | ~0.9% |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | ~0.04% |
| Water vapour | Varies by location and season |

![Why is air important](air-importance.png)

### Why Air Matters

- **Oxygen** powers respiration in nearly all organisms — releasing energy from food.
- **Carbon dioxide** fuels photosynthesis — enabling plants to produce food and oxygen.
- **Nitrogen** is essential for building proteins in living cells — soil bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into a form plant roots can absorb.
- The **atmosphere** acts as a protective shield, filtering out dangerous ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the Sun.
- Wind — moving air — drives weather patterns, disperses seeds, aids pollination, and dries harvested crops.

### Capturing Wind Energy

![Harnessing wind energy — traditional windmills and modern turbines](wind-energy.png)

Wind carries **kinetic energy** — the energy of motion. People have harnessed wind for centuries:

- **Traditional windmills** in Rajasthan and Gujarat have long been used to pump water from underground for irrigation and drinking.
- **Modern wind turbines** — towering structures with enormous blades — convert wind into electricity. India operates major wind farms along the coasts of Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Karnataka, and Rajasthan.

> **Noteworthy:** India ranks among the world's top five producers of wind energy, with an installed capacity exceeding 46 GW.

### When Air Becomes Polluted

![Air pollution — harming the invisible treasure](air-pollution.png)

Air pollution occurs when harmful gases and particles contaminate the atmosphere:
- Exhaust from cars, buses, and trucks releases carbon monoxide (CO), sulphur dioxide (SO₂), and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ).
- Industrial chimneys emit toxic fumes and particulate matter.
- Burning of paddy straw (parali) in Punjab and Haryana after harvest season blankets northern India in thick smog every winter.

*Consequences:* Respiratory diseases like asthma and bronchitis, stinging eyes, acid rain that damages crops and monuments, and thinning of the ozone layer.

**Electric vehicles (EVs)** produce zero exhaust emissions and are growing rapidly in Indian cities as a cleaner transport option.

## Water — The Elixir of Life

Roughly **71%** of Earth's surface is covered by water. Yet **97%** of it is **salty ocean water** — unfit for drinking or farming. Only **3%** is freshwater, and the bulk of that lies frozen in glaciers and polar ice caps. The freshwater easily accessible in rivers, lakes, and underground aquifers is astonishingly small.

This makes **freshwater one of the most valuable natural resources** on the planet.

### Where India Gets Its Freshwater

![Sources of freshwater in India](freshwater-sources.png)

- **Rivers** — Ganga, Yamuna, Narmada, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Brahmaputra
- **Lakes** — Dal Lake (Jammu & Kashmir), Loktak Lake (Manipur), Chilika Lake (Odisha)
- **Groundwater** — accessed through wells, borewells, and tubewells across rural India
- **Glaciers** — the mighty Himalayan glaciers feed most of North India's rivers
- **Rain** — the ultimate source of all freshwater on land, driven by the water cycle

### The Water Cycle — Nature's Recycling System

![The water cycle — evaporation, condensation, precipitation](water-cycle.png)

Water continuously moves between the Earth's surface and the atmosphere:

1. The Sun heats water in oceans, rivers, and lakes → water **evaporates** (changes to vapour).
2. Water vapour rises, cools at higher altitudes, and **condenses** into tiny droplets → clouds form.
3. Droplets merge and grow heavy → they fall as **rain, hail, or snow** (precipitation).
4. Water flows into rivers, seeps into the ground, or collects in lakes.
5. The cycle begins again.

> This cycle has been running for billions of years. The glass of water you drank this morning may contain molecules that once flowed in a Himalayan glacier or floated in a monsoon cloud over the Bay of Bengal!

### Ancient Indian Water Wisdom

For centuries, Indian communities developed ingenious methods to conserve water:

- **Baoli / Stepwell (Vav)** — grand stepped wells where people could descend to the water level even during droughts. The stunning **Rani ki Vav** in Patan, Gujarat (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) is a masterpiece of engineering and art.
- **Kund** — cylindrical underground tanks in Rajasthan that collected and stored rainwater for the dry months.
- **Johad** — crescent-shaped earthen check dams in Rajasthan that captured rainwater and recharged underground aquifers. Activist Rajendra Singh revived hundreds of johads in Alwar district, earning him the title "Waterman of India."
- **Bamboo drip irrigation** — an age-old system in Meghalaya where hollow bamboo channels guide spring water directly to plant roots on steep hillside farms.

### Rainwater Harvesting Today

![Rainwater harvesting — collecting and storing rain](rainwater-harvesting.png)

**Rainwater harvesting** collects rain from rooftops and directs it into storage tanks or into the ground:

1. Rain falls on a building's roof.
2. Gutters and pipes channel the water.
3. A filter removes debris.
4. Filtered water is stored in a tank for household use, or directed into a recharge pit to replenish the water table.

Several Indian cities — including Chennai, Bengaluru, and Hyderabad — have made rooftop rainwater harvesting **compulsory** for new constructions.

### Water Pollution — A Growing Crisis

![Water pollution — harmful substances entering water bodies](water-pollution.png)

Water bodies become polluted when harmful substances enter them:
- Untreated industrial effluents containing heavy metals and chemicals.
- Sewage and domestic waste flowing directly into rivers.
- Pesticides and chemical fertilisers washing off farmlands during rain.
- Plastic waste and garbage dumped into rivers and lakes.

*Impact:* The Yamuna in Delhi and the Ganga in several stretches carry dangerously high pollution loads. Contaminated water causes diseases such as cholera, dysentery, typhoid, and hepatitis.

Initiatives like **Namami Gange** aim to restore the health of the Ganga through sewage treatment, industrial regulation, and riverbank conservation.

## Energy from the Sun

The Sun is the **primary energy source** for nearly all life on Earth.

- Plants harness sunlight for **photosynthesis** → herbivores eat plants → carnivores eat herbivores → energy cascades through the food chain.
- **Wind** is a product of the Sun — uneven heating of Earth's surface creates pressure differences, producing air currents.
- **Rain** depends on the Sun evaporating ocean water.
- Even **fossil fuels** — coal, petroleum, natural gas — are ancient solar energy stored in the remains of organisms that lived millions of years ago.

### Harnessing Solar Energy Directly

![Solar panels — converting sunlight directly into electricity](solar-panels.png)

**a) Solar panels (photovoltaic cells)**
- Convert sunlight directly into electricity with no moving parts.
- Installed on rooftops of homes, schools, hospitals, and factories.
- India's **Bhadla Solar Park** in Rajasthan, spread over 56 square kilometres, is one of the largest solar installations on the planet.

![Solar cooker — cooking with free sunlight energy](solar-cooker.png)

**b) Solar cooker**
- A reflective box that focuses sunlight onto cooking vessels.
- Completely free energy — no fuel, no smoke, no cost after initial purchase.
- Can prepare rice, dal, sabzi, and even boil water for drinking.
- Widely promoted in rural India where LPG supply may be unreliable.

![Solar water heater — heating water using sunlight](solar-water-heater.png)

**c) Solar water heater**
- Roof-mounted panels absorb sunlight and heat water for bathing, washing, and cleaning.
- Common across Indian cities and towns — reducing electricity or gas consumption.

> **India's ambition:** The country aims to generate 500 GW of energy from renewable sources by 2030, with solar power as the cornerstone of this plan.

## Forests — The Green Lifeline

A **forest** is far more than a cluster of trees. It is a dynamic **ecosystem** — a web of trees, shrubs, herbs, mosses, fungi, bacteria, insects, birds, reptiles, and mammals, all interdependent.

### Why Forests Are Indispensable

| Benefit | How forests provide it |
| ------- | ---------------------- |
| **Fresh air** | Trees absorb CO₂ and release O₂ during photosynthesis |
| **Clean water** | Forest soil acts as a natural filter for rainwater percolating to rivers and aquifers |
| **Soil protection** | Tree roots bind the soil, preventing erosion by rain and wind |
| **Climate stability** | Forests moderate local temperatures and influence rainfall patterns |
| **Biodiversity haven** | Forests shelter millions of species — many still undiscovered |
| **Livelihoods** | Tribal and rural communities rely on forests for food, fuel, fodder, and traditional medicine |

### Products We Obtain from Forests

![Forest products — timber, bamboo, honey, medicinal plants](forest-products.png)

- **Timber** (furniture, construction, railway sleepers)
- **Firewood** (cooking fuel in millions of rural homes)
- **Bamboo** (building material, furniture, baskets, edible bamboo shoots)
- **Gums and resins** (adhesives, paints, varnishes)
- **Wild honey** (collected by forest-dwelling communities)
- **Medicinal plants** — neem (antiseptic), tulsi (immunity booster), amla (vitamin C), ashwagandha (stress reliever)
- **Wild fruits and nuts** — mahua, tendu, sal seeds — vital for tribal nutrition
- **Paper** (manufactured from wood pulp)

### The Forest Floor — Nature's Compost Factory

![The forest floor — nature's recycler](forest-floor.png)

Fallen leaves, dead branches, and animal droppings pile up on the forest floor. **Decomposers** — bacteria, fungi, termites, and earthworms — break this organic matter into dark, nutrient-rich **humus**, which enriches the soil and nourishes new plant growth. Nothing goes to waste in a forest.

> **Earthworms** are often called the **"farmer's best friends"** because their tunnelling aerates the soil, and their castings (excretions) are among the richest natural fertilisers.

### Deforestation — Losing the Green Cover

![Deforestation — losing the green treasure](deforestation.png)

Forests are being destroyed at alarming rates for:
- Expanding agricultural land
- Constructing highways, dams, and urban settlements
- Logging for timber and paper
- Mining operations

![Effects of deforestation — habitat loss, soil erosion, climate change](deforestation-effects.png)

*Consequences of deforestation:*
- Loss of animal habitats → species become endangered or go extinct.
- Soil erosion → rivers silt up → flooding intensifies.
- Fewer trees → less rainfall → droughts become more frequent.
- Carbon stored in trees enters the atmosphere → accelerates global warming.

### Indian Forest Conservation Movements

**Chipko Movement (1973)**
In Chamoli district of the Garhwal Himalayas (now Uttarakhand), local women led by Gaura Devi literally **hugged the trees** to prevent contractors from felling them. The word "Chipko" means "to cling" or "to embrace." This brave grassroots action sparked a nationwide movement and eventually influenced India's forest conservation policies. It became an inspiration for environmental movements around the world.

**Van Mahotsav**
Launched in 1950 by K.M. Munshi, this annual tree-planting festival is held during the first week of July. Schools, colleges, government offices, and community groups across India plant millions of saplings. "Van" means forest, and "Mahotsav" means festival — a celebration of forests.

## Soil, Rocks, and Minerals

### Soil — The Skin of the Earth

**Soil** is the thin, life-supporting layer covering Earth's rocky crust. It forms over **hundreds to thousands of years** through the **weathering** of rocks — the gradual crumbling of rock by wind, rain, frost, and biological activity — combined with the decomposition of plant and animal remains.

![Layers of soil — topsoil, subsoil, weathered rock, bedrock](soil-layers.png)

*Soil layers (top to bottom):*
1. **Topsoil** — the darkest layer, rich in humus and minerals; home to roots, worms, insects, and microorganisms.
2. **Subsoil** — lighter in colour, containing more minerals and clay; reached by deeper roots.
3. **Weathered rock fragments** — partially broken pieces of the parent rock.
4. **Bedrock** — solid, unbroken rock.

![Types of soil — sandy, clayey, and loamy](soil-types.png)

*Common soil types:*
- **Sandy soil** — large, coarse particles; drains water very fast; found in Rajasthan's Thar desert; suitable for watermelons, bajra (millet).
- **Clayey soil** — fine, closely packed particles; holds water tightly; can become waterlogged; ideal for paddy (rice) cultivation.
- **Loamy soil** — a balanced mixture of sand, silt, and clay; retains moisture without waterlogging; the most fertile type; best for wheat, vegetables, and most crops.

### Rocks — The Skeleton of the Earth

The Earth's crust is made of **rocks**, and rocks are composed of one or more **minerals**.

| Rock | Type | Found in India | Common use |
| ---- | ---- | -------------- | ---------- |
| Granite | Igneous | Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Rajasthan | Flooring, countertops, building facades |
| Sandstone | Sedimentary | Rajasthan (Red Fort and Qutub Minar were built using it) | Construction, monuments |
| Limestone | Sedimentary | Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh | Cement manufacturing, glassmaking |
| Marble | Metamorphic | Makrana, Rajasthan (the Taj Mahal's marble comes from here) | Flooring, sculptures, decorative work |
| Slate | Metamorphic | Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan | Roofing tiles, old-style writing slates |
| Coal | Sedimentary (fossil fuel) | Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal | Power generation, steel production |
| Laterite | Sedimentary | Kerala, Goa, Karnataka | Building blocks for traditional houses |

### Minerals — Nature's Hidden Riches

**Minerals** are naturally occurring, non-living substances with a definite chemical composition, found within rocks and soil.

*Key minerals and their Indian sources:*

| Mineral | Primary use | Major Indian sources |
| ------- | ----------- | -------------------- |
| Iron ore | Steel production — buildings, bridges, vehicles | Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Goa |
| Bauxite (aluminium ore) | Lightweight metal — aircraft bodies, cooking utensils, electrical wires | Odisha, Gujarat, Jharkhand |
| Copper | Electrical wiring, plumbing pipes | Rajasthan (Khetri), Jharkhand |
| Gold | Jewellery, electronics, dentistry | Karnataka (Kolar Gold Fields — among the deepest mines in the world) |
| Coal | Thermal power plants, industrial fuel | Jharkhand (Jharia), West Bengal (Raniganj) |
| Petroleum (crude oil) | Petrol, diesel, kerosene, plastics | Mumbai offshore (Mumbai High), Gujarat, Assam |
| Natural gas | Cooking gas (LPG), vehicle fuel (CNG) | Gujarat, Rajasthan, Krishna-Godavari basin |

> **Surprising fact:** A typical smartphone contains more than 60 different minerals — including gold, silver, copper, tin, lithium, and cobalt. Recycling old phones recovers these precious minerals and reduces the need for fresh mining.

## Fossil Fuels — Energy from the Deep Past

![Fossil fuels — ancient stored energy from millions of years ago](fossil-fuels.png)

**Fossil fuels** — coal, petroleum, and natural gas — are the transformed remains of organisms that lived hundreds of millions of years ago. Immense heat and pressure deep within the Earth converted these ancient remains into concentrated energy sources.

### Coal

- Originated from vast ancient forests that were buried under sediment in swampy regions.
- Appears as a hard, black, combustible rock.
- Burned in **thermal power stations** to boil water, produce steam, and spin turbines that generate electricity.
- Also essential in **steel production** (as coke) and various industries.
- India's major coal belts lie in Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and West Bengal.

### Petroleum (Crude Oil)

- Formed from ancient marine microorganisms (plankton, algae) buried under ocean sediments.
- Extracted as a thick, dark liquid by drilling deep wells on land or under the sea.
- Sent to **refineries** where it is separated into various products:
  - **Petrol** (for cars, scooters, autorickshaws)
  - **Diesel** (for trucks, buses, trains, generators)
  - **Kerosene** (for lamps and cooking stoves in some rural areas)
  - **LPG** (Liquefied Petroleum Gas — the familiar cooking gas cylinder in Indian kitchens)
  - **Bitumen** (for surfacing roads)
  - Raw material for **plastics, synthetic fibres, and medicines**
- India's petroleum comes from Mumbai High (offshore), Gujarat (Ankleshwar), and Assam (Digboi — one of Asia's oldest oilfields).

### Natural Gas

- Often found alongside petroleum deposits or in independent underground pockets.
- Primarily composed of methane (CH₄).
- Delivered to homes as **PNG (Piped Natural Gas)** for cooking.
- Compressed into cylinders as **CNG (Compressed Natural Gas)** for vehicles — significantly cleaner than petrol or diesel.
- Also used in electricity generation and chemical manufacturing.

### The Trouble with Fossil Fuels

| Concern | Explanation |
| ------- | ----------- |
| **Finite supply** | Fossil fuels required millions of years to form; at current consumption rates, reserves may be exhausted within decades to a few centuries |
| **Air pollution** | Combustion releases CO₂, SO₂, CO, and fine particulate matter — causing smog, respiratory illness, and acid rain |
| **Climate change** | CO₂ and methane are greenhouse gases; their rising concentration traps heat, warming the planet |
| **Environmental accidents** | Oil spills during transport or drilling contaminate oceans and devastate marine ecosystems |

## Renewable vs Non-Renewable Resources

![Renewable and non-renewable resources comparison](renewable-nonrenewable.png)

### Non-Renewable Resources

Resources that formed over **millions of years** and **cannot be replenished** once consumed.

*Examples:* Coal, petroleum, natural gas, iron ore, copper, gold, and most other minerals.

Once exhausted, these resources are gone for good within any human timescale.

### Renewable Resources

Resources that nature **continuously regenerates**, making them available indefinitely — provided we use them responsibly.

| Resource | How it renews |
| -------- | ------------- |
| **Sunlight** | Continuously radiated by the Sun — available every day |
| **Wind** | Generated by the Sun's uneven heating of Earth — never stops globally |
| **Water** | Recycled endlessly through the water cycle |
| **Forests** | Trees regrow — but only if replanted and protected from over-harvesting |
| **Soil** | Slowly rebuilt through weathering and decomposition — but takes centuries |

> **A word of caution:** Even renewable resources can be degraded beyond recovery if misused. Forests can be cleared faster than they regrow. Rivers can be polluted beyond self-cleaning capacity. Topsoil can be eroded in a single monsoon if left unprotected. Responsible use is essential for every resource.

## Protecting Nature's Treasures — What You Can Do

| Resource | Simple actions that make a difference |
| -------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Air** | Walk, cycle, or use public transport; switch to EVs when possible; plant trees; never burn garbage |
| **Water** | Repair dripping taps promptly; take shorter showers; harvest rainwater; never throw waste into rivers |
| **Forests** | Reduce paper waste; avoid products from illegal logging; participate in tree-planting drives |
| **Soil** | Compost kitchen scraps; avoid littering; support farmers who practise crop rotation and organic methods |
| **Fossil fuels** | Turn off lights and fans when leaving a room; use energy-efficient appliances (LED bulbs, 5-star rated ACs); embrace solar energy |
| **Minerals** | Recycle old electronics, aluminium cans, and scrap metal; avoid discarding gadgets carelessly |

## Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| ---- | ------- |
| **Natural resource** | A material or energy source found in nature and used by living organisms |
| **Renewable resource** | A resource that nature regenerates continuously and that will not run out if used wisely |
| **Non-renewable resource** | A resource that took millions of years to form and cannot be replaced once consumed |
| **Fossil fuel** | An energy-rich substance — coal, petroleum, or natural gas — formed from ancient organisms |
| **Photovoltaic (solar) cell** | A device that converts sunlight directly into electrical energy |
| **Deforestation** | The large-scale removal or clearing of forest cover |
| **Humus** | Dark, nutrient-rich material in soil formed by the decomposition of organic matter |
| **Weathering** | The gradual breakdown of rocks by wind, water, temperature changes, and biological activity |
| **Rainwater harvesting** | The collection and storage of rainwater for later use or groundwater recharge |
| **Chipko Movement** | A landmark Indian environmental movement in which villagers embraced trees to prevent logging |
| **CNG** | Compressed Natural Gas — a cleaner-burning vehicle fuel |
| **LPG** | Liquefied Petroleum Gas — widely used for cooking in Indian households |

## Apply What You Have Learned

### Where you see this in real life

**1. The Delhi Metro at peak hour.** Every weekday morning, the Delhi Metro carries around 60 lakh passengers. If even half of those people took cars or autos instead, the air over Delhi would become unbreathable within a week — more than what stubble burning does in November. The Metro runs largely on electricity, and a growing share of that electricity now comes from solar parks in Rajasthan. Every Metro ride is a tiny vote for cleaner air, whether the rider realises it or not.

**2. The rainwater harvesting pit at Chennai bus stops.** Walk near any modern Chennai bus terminal during monsoon. You will spot small concrete pits with grates — quietly catching rainwater and sending it deep underground. After Chennai's terrible water crisis of 2019, the city made rainwater harvesting mandatory. The result: groundwater levels are slowly rising again. One pit by itself is small. Thousands of pits across the city is a revolution.

**3. The Bhadla Solar Park, Rajasthan.** In the Thar desert, where the sun beats down all year on cracked earth, India built one of the largest solar farms on the planet. Spread over 56 square kilometres, millions of panels silently turn sunlight into electricity that lights up homes, hospitals, and schools far away. The same scorching sun that once made Rajasthani villages suffer in summer is now powering the country.

### DIY: Track your home's water, electricity, and waste for one week

*⏱ 15 minutes per day for 7 days  ·  🧹 Zero mess  ·  👨‍👩‍👧 Adult help needed for reading the electricity meter*

You will need: a notebook divided into 3 sections, a pen, the curiosity of a detective.

**Section 1 — Water (every evening)**

1. Count how many times the kitchen tap was running today.
2. Count how many buckets were used (bathing, washing clothes, mopping, plants).
3. Was the tap left running while someone brushed teeth? Note it.
4. Spot any leaks — dripping taps, leaking flush. Mark each.

**Section 2 — Electricity (Sunday vs Saturday meter reading)**

1. With an adult, find the electricity meter (usually outside or in the meter box). Note the reading on Day 1.
2. Note it again on Day 7. The difference = units of electricity your house used in one week.
3. Each day, list: was the fan left on in an empty room? AC running with windows open? Phone charger plugged in but not charging?

**Section 3 — Waste (daily)**

1. Look at the kitchen dustbin every evening. Count: plastic items, vegetable peels, paper, leftover food.
2. Could the vegetable peels go to a compost pot on the balcony instead?
3. Could the plastic items have been avoided (cloth bag instead of polythene)?

**End of week:** Total it all up. Where is the BIGGEST leak? Pick ONE habit to change next week. Just one. Track for another week and see if numbers change.

---

> **Show what you found:** Snap a photo of your before-and-after numbers in the notebook. Sketch a small bar chart comparing Week 1 and Week 2 use. Show your daadi (or dadu/mummy/older cousin). Tell them one new thing you understood — and ask her how her family managed water and electricity when SHE was your age. You might find your family was already saving by default, before "save the planet" was a slogan.

> **A question to keep asking all year:** *Every time I use something — water, electricity, paper, plastic, fuel — where did it come from, and where will it go after I am done with it?*
