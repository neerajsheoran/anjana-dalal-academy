## What Counts as Science?

**Meera:** Yaar, my cousin keeps saying "science proves this, science proves that" whenever we argue about anything. But how is science different from just saying what you think?

**Arjun:** I think it is the testing part. If I just say "mangoes are the best fruit," nobody can prove that wrong or right. It is only my opinion.

**Meera:** But if I say "a mango seed takes about ten days to sprout"?

**Arjun:** Then anyone can plant one and check. That part is science, because you can test it.

**Meera:** Oh, so the difference is whether someone else can check what I am saying?

**Arjun:** Exactly. And not just one person. If a hundred people plant a seed and it sprouts in about ten days each time, then it becomes real knowledge. One person's word is not enough.

**Meera:** That is actually a nice way to think about it. Science is what holds up when many people test it.

## Everyday Experiments You Already Do

**Meera:** Our class teacher said something funny today. She said everyone does the "scientific method" without realising it. But I have never done a method, Arjun. I do not even have a lab coat!

**Arjun:** Hmm, I think she meant something else. Did the TV remote at your place stop working yesterday? You told me at recess.

**Meera:** Haan! I pressed the button and nothing happened.

**Arjun:** That was observation. Then what did you do?

**Meera:** I thought maybe the batteries were dead, so I changed them. And the TV came on.

**Arjun:** See! You observed, you guessed, you tested, you got an answer. That is the whole method right there at your sofa.

**Meera:** No way, yaar. I just changed batteries.

**Arjun:** But that is exactly the point. The sabziwali bhaiya near our gully does this too. He noticed his tomatoes go bad fast in the sun, so he keeps them in the shade now. Same logic.

**Meera:** So science is just careful thinking? It is not stuck in a lab somewhere?

**Arjun:** Exactly. Mummy does it when she checks if the dahi has set. Daadi does it when she figures out if the rice has enough water. We all do it.

## Why Does "Fair Testing" Matter?

**Meera:** Arjun, we did an experiment in school today. We wanted to check if sugar dissolves faster in hot water or cold water. I used hot water and stirred it. My friend Riya used cold water and did not stir. My sugar dissolved first, so we said hot water wins.

**Arjun:** Wait, wait. Was that even a fair test?

**Meera:** What do you mean? My sugar dissolved faster, na.

**Arjun:** But you stirred yours. Riya did not stir hers. So was it the hot water that helped, or the stirring?

**Meera:** Oh ho. I cannot tell because I changed two things at once.

**Arjun:** Exactly. A fair test means you change only one thing. Everything else has to be the same — same water amount, same sugar amount, same stirring.

**Meera:** So if we redo it properly and hot water still wins, only then can we trust it?

**Arjun:** Yes. Otherwise the answer might be wrong, and a wrong answer is worse than no answer sometimes.

**Meera:** That makes sense. Cricket would be unfair if my team got six balls and yours got only four, na?

**Arjun:** Haha, exactly. Same idea. Fair testing is the rule book of science.

## When Scientists Get It Wrong

**Meera:** Yaar, does science ever make mistakes? Or are scientists always right?

**Arjun:** Of course they make mistakes! Mummy told me once that doctors used to think washing hands before surgery was not needed. People kept dying.

**Meera:** Really? How did they figure it out?

**Arjun:** Some doctor noticed that fewer patients died when he washed his hands. He told other doctors. They tested it. They all started washing hands. The problem went away.

**Meera:** So one person was wrong, but many people checking together fixed it?

**Arjun:** That is the whole point. Science is built so anyone can be wrong, even the smartest person. Other people check, and the wrong ideas get caught.

**Meera:** Like when teacher checks our homework?

**Arjun:** Almost. Except imagine a hundred teachers from a hundred different schools all checking the same answer. If all of them say "yes, this is right," then you can be very, very sure.

**Meera:** What if a scientist cheats and writes fake results?

**Arjun:** They get caught eventually, because when other people try the same thing, the fake results do not show up. The system is designed to catch lies.

**Meera:** So science is honest because lots of people check each other. That is actually kind of nice.

## The Kitchen as a Science Lab

**Meera:** Arjun, can you give me one example of science happening right now in our houses?

**Arjun:** Easy. The pressure cooker. Mummy puts dal inside, the whistle goes off three times, and dal that takes forty minutes in an open pot is ready in ten.

**Meera:** Why does it cook faster?

**Arjun:** Because the steam cannot escape. The pressure builds up inside, and that makes the water hotter than normal boiling water. Hotter water cooks faster.

**Meera:** Whoa. So the cooker is doing physics?

**Arjun:** Every day, three times a day. And look at the dahi bowl on the counter. Last night Daadi added one spoonful of old dahi to warm milk. By morning, the whole bowl is dahi.

**Meera:** That is biology, na! Tiny bacteria multiplying overnight.

**Arjun:** Exactly. And when you squeeze lemon over cut apples in your tiffin so they do not turn brown — that is chemistry. The lemon stops the apple from reacting with air.

**Meera:** Yaar, our kitchen is basically a lab. I am never looking at it the same way again.

**Arjun:** Wait till you learn what is happening when chai boils over. That one Mummy still cannot fully explain.

## Science Is a Team Sport

**Meera:** In movies they always show one genius scientist alone in a secret lab with smoke coming out of bottles. Is that how it really works?

**Arjun:** Almost never. Look at Chandrayaan-3. Mummy showed me on the news — hundreds of scientists worked for years. Engineers built the rocket, coders wrote the software, geologists figured out where to land, communication people made sure signals reached. No one person could have done all that.

**Meera:** Even smaller things need teams?

**Arjun:** Yes. When a new medicine is made, chemists design it, biologists test it on cells, doctors try it on patients, statisticians look at the numbers, and safety people check everything. Each one does a piece.

**Meera:** It is like our school cricket team, na? You need batters, bowlers, fielders, a wicket-keeper. No single player can do every job.

**Arjun:** Perfect example. And just like our team watches old match videos to improve, scientists check each other's work to make the answers better.

**Meera:** I like that. You do not have to just believe what someone says. You can always ask "how do you know?"

**Arjun:** That is the best thing about science, actually. The question "why" is never wrong. Daadu once said the same thing — only people who stop asking questions stop learning. Now come, I think Mummy made pakoras. We can do more science over chai.
