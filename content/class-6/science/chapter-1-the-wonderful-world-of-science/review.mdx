## Key Concepts

- **Science** begins with asking questions about the natural world and finding reliable answers through observation and testing
- Science is present in everyday life — sound, light, chemical reactions, and biology happen all around us before we even leave for school
- **Curiosity** is the engine of every scientific discovery — noticing something odd and refusing to ignore it
- **C.V. Raman** wondered why the sea looks blue — his investigation earned him the Nobel Prize
- The **scientific method** is a structured, step-by-step process scientists use to find reliable answers
- The six steps: **Observe → Question → Hypothesise → Experiment → Analyse → Conclude**
- A **hypothesis** is a testable educated guess that explains an observation
- A **variable** is the one thing you change in an experiment to see its effect
- A **fair test** means changing only one variable while keeping everything else the same
- An unfair test gives unreliable results because you cannot tell which change caused the outcome
- If an experiment does not support your hypothesis, it is **not a failure** — it is progress
- **Verification** means other scientists must be able to repeat an experiment and get the same result
- **Peer review** is the process where experts check, question, and try to reproduce published findings
- Scientific thinking is used by everyone — farmers, mechanics, cooks, and students — not just lab scientists
- India's **Chandrayaan-3** succeeded because of a culture of repeated testing and peer verification

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Observation** | Carefully noticing something using your senses or instruments |
| **Hypothesis** | A testable educated guess that explains an observation |
| **Experiment** | A planned test to check whether a hypothesis is correct |
| **Variable** | The one thing you change in an experiment to see its effect |
| **Fair test** | An experiment where only one variable is changed; everything else stays the same |
| **Conclusion** | A statement of findings based on evidence from an experiment |
| **Scientific method** | Observe, question, hypothesise, experiment, analyse, conclude |
| **Peer review** | The process where other scientists check and verify a finding |
| **Verification** | Repeating an experiment to confirm the same result |

## Remember These

- The scientific method has **6 steps** — Observe, Question, Hypothesise, Experiment, Analyse, Conclude
- A fair test changes **only one variable** at a time — changing two things makes results unreliable
- A failed experiment is **not a failure** — it rules out one explanation and helps form a better hypothesis
- Science is a **method of thinking**, not just a subject in school — anyone who observes, tests, and learns from results is thinking scientifically
- Peer review protects us from **errors and false claims** — one experiment alone is never enough
