## Key Concepts

- Living things share six **characteristics of life**: growth, respiration, reproduction, response to stimuli, movement, and excretion
- **Respiration** is the process where food is broken down using oxygen to release energy
- **Excretion** is the removal of waste products from the body (sweat, urine, falling leaves in plants)
- Plants show movement too — sunflowers turn toward the sun, roots grow downward, shoots grow upward
- **Biodiversity** means the total variety of living organisms on Earth (from bacteria to blue whales)
- India is rich in biodiversity due to diverse landscapes — **Western Ghats**, **Sundarbans**, **Thar Desert**
- Every organism has a role in nature — bees pollinate, earthworms aerate soil, vultures clean carcasses, mangroves protect coasts
- **Endangered** species are at risk of disappearing; **extinct** species are gone forever
- A **habitat** is the natural environment where an organism finds food, water, shelter, and conditions to survive
- Three types of habitats: **terrestrial** (land), **aquatic** (water), **aerial** (air)
- **Adaptations** are special features that help organisms survive in their habitat (camel's wide feet, cactus spines, fish gills)
- Plants are grouped as **herbs** (soft stem), **shrubs** (many woody branches), **trees** (single trunk), **climbers**, and **creepers**
- **Reticulate venation** = net-like vein pattern (mango); **Parallel venation** = parallel veins (grass)
- **Taproot** = one main deep root (carrot); **Fibrous root** = many thin spreading roots (grass)
- **Monocot** = one seed leaf/cotyledon (rice); **Dicot** = two seed leaves (bean)
- Animals are grouped as **vertebrates** (with backbone) and **invertebrates** (without backbone)
- Over **95%** of animal species are invertebrates

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Biodiversity** | The total variety of living organisms on Earth |
| **Habitat** | The natural environment where an organism lives |
| **Adaptation** | A body feature or behaviour that helps an organism survive in its habitat |
| **Vertebrate** | An animal with a backbone (fish, frog, bird, human) |
| **Invertebrate** | An animal without a backbone (ant, spider, snail) |
| **Venation** | The pattern of veins in a leaf |
| **Cotyledon** | A seed leaf; monocots have one, dicots have two |
| **Endangered** | A species at risk of disappearing |
| **Extinct** | A species that no longer exists anywhere on Earth |
| **Respiration** | Breaking down food using oxygen to release energy |
| **Excretion** | Removing waste products from the body |

## Remember These

- Not all things that grow are living — a crystal grows but is not alive; living things show **all six** characteristics together
- Plants move but **cannot walk** — they show movement by turning toward light, folding leaves, or growing roots downward
- Not all metals are magnetic, and similarly, **not all animals are vertebrates** — over 95% are invertebrates
- **Reticulate venation goes with taproot and dicot**; **parallel venation goes with fibrous root and monocot** — these features are linked
- Adaptations are **habitat-specific** — move a camel to the ocean or a fish to the desert, and neither would survive
