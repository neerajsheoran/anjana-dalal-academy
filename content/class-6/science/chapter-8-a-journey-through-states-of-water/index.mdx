## One Substance, Three Avatars

> *"Water is the driving force of all nature."*
> — Leonardo da Vinci

Picture a winter morning in Mussoorie. Tiny ice crystals coat the railings. By noon, the sun melts them into water droplets that trickle down. By afternoon, those droplets vanish — absorbed into the air as invisible vapour. The same substance — H₂O — appeared as ice, as liquid water, and as gas, all within a few hours.

Water is the only substance we encounter every single day in all three states of matter — **solid (ice)**, **liquid (water)**, and **gas (water vapour)**. The magic that switches it between these forms? Simply adding or removing **heat**.

---

## Evaporation — The Silent Escape

Have you noticed these everyday occurrences?

- A wet towel hung on the balcony railing becomes dry by evening
- The rangoli made with wet rice paste dries up in a couple of hours
- Puddles on the road after a monsoon shower vanish once the clouds clear
- Your forehead feels dry a few minutes after you wipe it with a damp cloth

In each case, **liquid water quietly converts into water vapour** — an invisible gas that blends into the surrounding air. This process is called **evaporation**.

> **Evaporation** is the conversion of a liquid into vapour at the liquid's surface, happening at temperatures below the boiling point.

### What Speeds Up or Slows Down Evaporation?

| Factor | How It Affects Evaporation |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Temperature** | Hotter surroundings → water molecules gain energy faster → quicker evaporation |
| **Surface area** | More liquid surface exposed to air → more molecules can escape → faster drying |
| **Wind** | Moving air carries vapour away from the surface → makes room for more evaporation |
| **Humidity** | Air already loaded with moisture → fewer water molecules can join it → slower evaporation |

> **Quick experiment:** Take two identical steel plates. Pour one tablespoon of water on each. Keep one plate in the sun and the other in the shade. Check after 15 minutes. The plate in the sun dries first — proving that higher temperature accelerates evaporation.

## Condensation — When Invisible Becomes Visible

Pour yourself a glass of cold nimbu paani on a sticky July afternoon. Within minutes, you will notice **tiny water droplets forming on the outside of the glass** — even though you did not pour any water there.

Where did those droplets come from? The air around you contains invisible water vapour. When this vapour touches the chilled glass, it loses energy and turns back into **liquid water**. This reverse journey is called **condensation**.

> **Condensation** is the process by which water vapour (gas) converts into liquid water upon cooling.

### Condensation All Around Us

- **Dew on flowers** at dawn — overnight, surfaces cool down and water vapour in the air condenses on them as sparkling droplets
- **Fog blanketing the plains** in December — millions of tiny water droplets suspended in cold air near the ground
- **Your breath becoming a little cloud** on a chilly Shimla morning — warm moist air from your lungs meets cold outside air and condenses
- **The bathroom mirror going hazy** after a hot shower — steam from the shower condenses on the cooler mirror surface

### Humidity — The Moisture in Air

The amount of water vapour present in air at any given time is called **humidity**.

- **High humidity** (like during the monsoon): The air is already packed with moisture. Sweat on your body evaporates slowly, so you feel sticky and uncomfortable.
- **Low humidity** (like in a desert or on a dry winter day): Air has plenty of room for more vapour. Sweat evaporates quickly, cooling you efficiently.

## The Three States of Water

| State | Daily Life Example | Shape | Volume | How Particles Behave |
|-------|-------------------|-------|--------|---------------------|
| **Solid** | Ice cubes, frost on a car windshield | Definite shape | Definite volume | Particles locked in fixed positions, vibrating gently |
| **Liquid** | Tap water, rain, river water | Takes the shape of whatever holds it | Definite volume | Particles slide over each other freely |
| **Gas** | Steam from a kettle, invisible water vapour | Spreads to fill any space | No definite volume | Particles zoom around at high speed in all directions |

## How Water Changes State

Every change of state is caused by **adding or removing heat energy**.

### Melting — Solid Becomes Liquid
Heat ice and it turns into liquid water.
- **Melting point of ice:** 0°C

### Freezing — Liquid Becomes Solid
Cool liquid water and it turns into ice.
- **Freezing point of water:** 0°C (same temperature as melting point, just in reverse)

### Evaporation — Liquid Becomes Gas
Heat water further and it converts into water vapour. At **100°C** (boiling point), this happens rapidly throughout the liquid. But evaporation also occurs slowly at any temperature — that is how your clothes dry on a 30°C afternoon.

### Condensation — Gas Becomes Liquid
Cool water vapour and it turns back into liquid water — as seen in dew, fog, and droplets on a cold glass.

### Summary Diagram

```
         Add Heat           Add Heat
Ice  ──────────────► Water ──────────────► Water Vapour
(solid)   Melting   (liquid)  Evaporation    (gas)

         Remove Heat        Remove Heat
Ice  ◄──────────────  Water ◄──────────────  Water Vapour
(solid)  Freezing   (liquid)  Condensation    (gas)
```

## Why Evaporation Makes Things Cool

When a liquid evaporates, each escaping molecule carries energy away from the surface. This energy loss causes the remaining liquid and its surroundings to **cool down**. This is called the **cooling effect of evaporation**.

### Everyday Examples of Cooling by Evaporation

![An earthen pot with water droplets on its surface showing evaporation and cooling effect](earthen-pot-cooling.png)

**1. The clay pot (matka)**
In millions of Indian homes, drinking water is stored in a matka. The clay has microscopic pores through which water seeps to the outer surface and evaporates. Each evaporating molecule takes heat away from the pot, keeping the water inside pleasantly cool — no electricity needed!

**2. Why you shiver after a swim**
When you step out of a swimming pool, a thin film of water covers your skin. As this water evaporates, it pulls heat from your body, and you feel a sudden chill — even on a warm day.

**3. Sprinkling water on a hot terrace**
During peak summer, many Indian families sprinkle water on rooftops or verandahs. As the water evaporates, it absorbs heat from the surface, cooling it down noticeably.

**4. How a desert cooler works**
A desert cooler (air cooler) pulls hot air through water-soaked pads. The water on the pads evaporates, stealing heat from the passing air and sending out cooler air into the room. It is the matka principle, just scaled up with a fan and a pump.

> **Think about it:** A ceiling fan does not actually produce cold air. So why do you feel cooler under it? Because the breeze speeds up the evaporation of sweat from your skin, and that evaporation removes your body heat faster.

## Clouds, Rain, and the Water Cycle

![Diagram of the water cycle showing evaporation from ocean, condensation into clouds, precipitation as rain, and water flowing back to rivers and ocean](water-cycle-diagram.png)

### The Great Water Journey — Step by Step

1. **Evaporation** — The Sun heats the surface of oceans, rivers, lakes, and even wet soil. Water molecules escape as vapour and rise because warm moist air is lighter than dry air.

2. **Condensation** — As the vapour climbs higher into the atmosphere, the air grows colder. The vapour loses energy and condenses around tiny dust and pollen particles floating in the sky, forming minuscule water droplets. Billions of these droplets clustered together create a **cloud**.

3. **Precipitation** — Inside the cloud, tiny droplets bump into each other and merge into larger, heavier drops. When they become too heavy for the air to hold, they fall. If the air is warm, they fall as **rain**. In extremely cold conditions, they freeze and fall as **snow** or **hail**.

4. **Collection** — Rainwater flows into streams, rivers, and eventually back to the ocean. Some seeps underground to recharge wells and aquifers. And the cycle continues endlessly.

> This unbroken journey of water — ocean to sky to land and back — is called the **Water Cycle** (or **hydrological cycle**). It has been running for billions of years, recycling the same water over and over.

### AWG — Harvesting Water from Air

**Atmospheric Water Generators (AWGs)** are modern machines that draw humid air in, cool it to cause condensation, and collect the resulting water for drinking. They are particularly useful in coastal regions that have high humidity but limited freshwater — a clever technological application of the condensation principle.

## Key Words

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Evaporation | Conversion of liquid to vapour at the surface, below boiling point |
| Condensation | Conversion of water vapour back to liquid on cooling |
| Melting | Transformation of a solid into a liquid by adding heat |
| Freezing | Transformation of a liquid into a solid by removing heat |
| Solid | State with definite shape and definite volume |
| Liquid | State with no fixed shape but definite volume |
| Gas | State with neither fixed shape nor fixed volume |
| Humidity | The quantity of water vapour present in the air |
| Water cycle | Continuous movement of water through evaporation, condensation, and precipitation |
| Precipitation | Water falling from clouds as rain, snow, or hail |
| Dew | Tiny droplets formed when water vapour condenses on cool surfaces overnight |
| Boiling point | Temperature at which a liquid rapidly converts to vapour throughout (100°C for water) |
| Melting point | Temperature at which a solid converts to liquid (0°C for ice) |
