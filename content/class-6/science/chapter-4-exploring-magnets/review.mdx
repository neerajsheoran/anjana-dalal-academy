## Key Concepts

- Natural magnets were discovered near **Magnesia** in ancient Greece — these rocks are called **magnetite** or **lodestone**
- A **magnet** is any object that produces a magnetic force and attracts iron, steel, cobalt, and nickel
- Every magnet has two **poles** — **North Pole (N)** and **South Pole (S)**
- Magnetic force is **strongest at the poles** and weakest in the middle
- **Like poles repel** (N-N or S-S push apart); **unlike poles attract** (N-S pull together)
- A freely hanging magnet always settles in the **north-south direction** — this is how a compass works
- **Magnetic materials**: iron, steel, cobalt, nickel — these are attracted by magnets
- **Non-magnetic materials**: wood, plastic, glass, rubber, copper, aluminium, gold, silver — not attracted
- Not all metals are magnetic — **copper, aluminium, gold, silver** are metals but are non-magnetic
- A **compass** has a free-spinning magnetic needle that always points north-south
- The **Earth behaves like a giant magnet** — its molten iron core generates a magnetic field
- Earth's geographic North Pole is near its **magnetic south pole** (that is why the north end of a compass points north — unlike poles attract)
- **Magnetisation** = turning a non-magnetic iron object into a magnet (by stroking or using electric current)
- An **electromagnet** is a temporary magnet made by passing electric current through a coil around an iron core — it can be switched on and off
- Magnets are used in speakers, motors, MRI scanners, maglev trains, compasses, cranes, and fridge seals

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Magnet** | An object that attracts iron, nickel, cobalt, and steel |
| **Magnetite / Lodestone** | A naturally occurring magnetic rock |
| **Poles** | The two ends of a magnet where force is strongest (N and S) |
| **Magnetic material** | A substance attracted by a magnet (iron, steel, cobalt, nickel) |
| **Non-magnetic material** | A substance not attracted by a magnet (wood, plastic, copper) |
| **Compass** | A device with a magnetic needle used to find direction |
| **Magnetisation** | Turning a non-magnetic iron/steel object into a magnet |
| **Electromagnet** | A temporary magnet created by electric current through a coil around an iron core |
| **Magnetic field** | The invisible region around a magnet where its force acts |
| **Demagnetisation** | Loss of magnetism due to heating, dropping, or hammering |

## Poles Rule — Quick Reference

| Combination | Result |
|-------------|--------|
| North + North | **Repel** |
| South + South | **Repel** |
| North + South | **Attract** |

## Remember These

- **Not all metals are magnetic** — copper, aluminium, gold, and silver are metals but are NOT attracted by magnets
- A magnet's force is **strongest at the poles**, not in the middle
- Earth's geographic North Pole is actually near its **magnetic south pole** — that is why the north end of a compass needle points north (unlike poles attract)
- To make a magnet by stroking, always stroke in **one direction only** — at least 30-40 times
- Magnets lose strength when **dropped, hammered, or heated** — store them in pairs with keepers and keep them away from electronics
