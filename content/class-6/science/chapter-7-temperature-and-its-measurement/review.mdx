## Key Concepts

- Our **sense of touch is unreliable** for measuring temperature — skin judges by comparison, not actual value
- **Temperature** is a numerical measure of how hot or cold a body is
- A **thermometer** is the instrument used to measure temperature accurately
- **SI unit of temperature** is **kelvin (K)**; in daily life we use **degrees Celsius (°C)**; USA uses **degrees Fahrenheit (°F)**
- **Freezing point of water:** 0°C = 32°F = 273.15 K
- **Boiling point of water:** 100°C = 212°F = 373.15 K
- **Normal human body temperature:** 37.0°C = 98.6°F; fever is above 38°C
- **Conversion formulas:** K = °C + 273.15; °F = (°C x 9/5) + 32
- **Absolute zero** = -273.15°C = 0 K — the lowest possible temperature in the universe
- **Clinical thermometer** — range 35°C to 42°C, has a **kink** to hold the reading, used for body temperature
- **Laboratory thermometer** — range -10°C to 110°C, **no kink**, must be read while bulb is in the liquid
- **Parallax error** — wrong reading from viewing the scale at an angle; always read at **eye level**
- **Heat and temperature are different:** temperature is intensity (how hot), heat is total energy
- **Heat** is measured in **joules (J)**; **temperature** is measured in **kelvin (K)**
- Heat is measured with a **calorimeter**; temperature with a **thermometer**
- **Stevenson Screen** — white slatted box at weather stations that houses thermometers for accurate outdoor readings
- **Anna Mani** — the "Weather Woman of India," designed 100+ weather instruments at IMD

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Temperature | Numerical measure of how hot or cold a body is |
| Thermometer | Instrument designed to measure temperature |
| Celsius (°C) | Scale where water freezes at 0° and boils at 100° |
| Fahrenheit (°F) | Scale where water freezes at 32° and boils at 212° |
| Kelvin (K) | SI unit of temperature, starts at absolute zero |
| Absolute zero | Coldest possible temperature: -273.15°C (0 K) |
| Clinical thermometer | Body temperature instrument, range 35-42°C |
| Laboratory thermometer | Experiment instrument, range -10°C to 110°C |
| Kink | Narrow constriction in clinical thermometer that locks the reading |
| Parallax error | Wrong reading caused by viewing scale at an angle |
| Stevenson Screen | White slatted box housing outdoor weather thermometers |

## Remember These

- **Heat is not the same as temperature** — a large pot of warm water has more heat energy than a small flame, even though the flame has a higher temperature
- Clinical thermometer has a **kink**; laboratory thermometer does **not** — this is a very common exam question
- Always **shake down** a mercury clinical thermometer below 35°C before use
- Laboratory thermometer bulb must **not touch** the walls or bottom of the container
- **Celsius to Kelvin:** just add 273.15 — the easiest conversion to remember
