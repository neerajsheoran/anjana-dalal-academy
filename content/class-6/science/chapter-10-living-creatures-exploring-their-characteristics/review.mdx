## Key Concepts

- All living organisms share **seven characteristics**: Movement, Nutrition, Respiration, Growth, Excretion, Sensitivity, and Reproduction
- **Animals** show **locomotion** (whole body moves); **plants** show growth-based movements (bending toward light, roots growing downward)
- **Autotrophs** (plants) make their own food through **photosynthesis** using sunlight, water, CO₂, and **chlorophyll**
- **Heterotrophs** (animals, fungi) consume ready-made food — classified as **herbivores**, **carnivores**, **omnivores**, or **decomposers**
- **Respiration** is the chemical breakdown of glucose in cells to release energy — it is NOT the same as breathing
- **Aerobic respiration**: Glucose + Oxygen → CO₂ + Water + Energy
- **Anaerobic respiration** occurs without oxygen — yeast produces alcohol and CO₂ (makes idli/dosa fluffy); muscles produce **lactic acid** (causes cramps)
- **Growth** in plants continues throughout life at **meristems**; in animals, growth stops in adulthood
- **Excretion** removes metabolic waste — kidneys (urine), lungs (CO₂), skin (sweat) in humans; stomata (O₂), leaf shedding, gums/resins in plants
- **Stimulus** is an environmental change that triggers a response in a living organism
- **J.C. Bose** invented the **crescograph** to detect minute plant movements and proved plants respond to stimuli
- **Reproduction**: asexual (one parent, identical offspring — budding, binary fission, cuttings) or sexual (two parents, blended traits)
- **Viruses** are neither fully living nor non-living — they reproduce only inside a host cell
- **Cell** is the basic unit of life; **unicellular** = one cell (amoeba); **multicellular** = many cells (humans)
- Organisation: **Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism**

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Autotroph** | Organism that produces its own food (e.g., plants) |
| **Heterotroph** | Organism that depends on others for food (e.g., animals) |
| **Photosynthesis** | Process by which plants make food using sunlight, water, and CO₂ |
| **Respiration** | Chemical breakdown of glucose in cells to release energy |
| **Excretion** | Removal of metabolic waste from the body |
| **Stimulus** | An environmental change that triggers a response |
| **Locomotion** | Movement of the entire body from one place to another |
| **Unicellular** | Made of a single cell (amoeba, bacteria) |
| **Multicellular** | Made of many cells (humans, trees) |
| **Crescograph** | Instrument invented by J.C. Bose to detect tiny plant movements |
| **Lifespan** | Duration of an organism's life from birth to natural death |

## Remember These

- **Breathing is not respiration** — breathing is the physical act of inhaling/exhaling; respiration is the chemical reaction inside cells
- Plants are **not motionless** — they show movements like phototropism (bending toward light) and thigmonastic response (touch-me-not folding)
- **Viruses** can reproduce only inside a host cell — they cannot carry out nutrition, respiration, or excretion on their own
- Different organisms breathe through different organs: lungs (humans), gills (fish), spiracles (insects), stomata (plants), moist skin (earthworms)
- Birds excrete **uric acid** (semi-solid) instead of liquid urine to conserve water
