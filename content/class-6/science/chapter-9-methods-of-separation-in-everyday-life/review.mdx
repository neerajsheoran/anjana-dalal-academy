## Key Concepts

- Every separation method works by exploiting a **difference in properties** between components — size, weight, solubility, or magnetic behaviour
- **Hand-picking** removes large, visible unwanted pieces by hand (stones from dal)
- **Threshing** uses beating or shaking to detach grain from stalks after harvest
- **Winnowing** uses wind to separate lighter chaff from heavier grain based on **weight difference**
- **Sieving** separates particles by **size** — smaller particles pass through the mesh, larger ones stay
- **Sedimentation** is the settling of heavy insoluble particles to the bottom of a liquid
- **Decantation** is the careful pouring of clear upper liquid after sedimentation, without disturbing the sediment
- **Loading** with **alum (fitkari)** speeds up sedimentation by clumping fine particles together
- **Filtration** passes liquid through fine pores (filter paper) to trap **insoluble** solids; the liquid collected is the **filtrate**, the solid left behind is the **residue**
- Filtration cannot remove **dissolved** substances — only insoluble ones
- **Evaporation** heats a solution so the liquid becomes vapour, leaving the **dissolved solid** behind (e.g., salt from sea water)
- **Condensation** cools vapour back into liquid; evaporation + condensation together = **distillation**
- **Magnetic separation** uses a magnet to pull out magnetic materials (iron, nickel, cobalt) from a mixture
- Three reasons to separate mixtures: remove harmful parts, obtain a useful substance, recover multiple useful components

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Mixture** | Two or more substances combined without chemical bonding |
| **Soluble** | A substance that dissolves in a liquid (e.g., sugar in water) |
| **Insoluble** | A substance that does not dissolve (e.g., chalk in water) |
| **Filtrate** | The clear liquid that passes through a filter |
| **Residue** | The solid left behind on the filter |
| **Sedimentation** | Heavy particles settling to the bottom of a liquid |
| **Decantation** | Pouring off the clear upper liquid after sedimentation |
| **Evaporation** | Heating a solution so the liquid becomes vapour, leaving dissolved solid behind |
| **Loading** | Adding alum to muddy water to speed up sedimentation |

## Remember These

- Sedimentation and decantation are always used **together** as a two-step process
- Filtration removes only **insoluble** substances — dissolved salt passes right through filter paper
- Evaporation recovers the **solid** but the water is lost; use **distillation** to recover both
- Choose the method based on the **property difference**: size → sieving, weight → winnowing, solubility → evaporation, magnetism → magnetic separation
- Salt harvesting in the **Rann of Kutch** is a real-life example of large-scale evaporation
