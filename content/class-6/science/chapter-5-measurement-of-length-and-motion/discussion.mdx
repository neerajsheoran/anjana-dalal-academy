## Measurement and Standard Units

**Meera:** Arjun, you know what is so weird? In P.T. period today, Sir told us to measure the length of the basketball court using our feet — heel to toe. I got 48 foot-steps. My friend Priya got 41.

**Arjun:** That is because your foot is smaller than hers, yaar. Obvious.

**Meera:** Yes but here is the funny part — the court is the SAME court. The number changed just because the foot changed.

**Arjun:** Ohho. So if every kid in school measures it, we will get 30 different answers.

**Meera:** Exactly! And what if the principal said "build a new court the same size" to a worker in Bengaluru. His foot would be totally different. The new court would be completely wrong.

**Arjun:** Okay okay. That is why we use the ruler in geometry class. Centimetres are the same everywhere.

**Meera:** Right. My daadi told me in her village they used to measure cloth with the haath — elbow to fingertip. And every shopkeeper had a slightly different haath.

**Arjun:** Smart shopkeepers had a short haath then. Less cloth, same money.

**Meera:** Daadi laughed and said exactly that! That is why scientists made the metre. One metre is the same in Delhi, in Dubai, in Tokyo. Centimetre, millimetre, kilometre — all family.

**Arjun:** And the highway boards say "Agra 180 km", "Jaipur 240 km" — same km everywhere in India.

**Meera:** Same km everywhere in the world, yaar.

## Measuring Length Correctly

**Meera:** Today in science class my pencil came out as 14 cm. The boy next to me measured the same pencil and said 14.3 cm. We almost had a fight over it.

**Arjun:** Were you both starting from the same place?

**Meera:** I started from the edge of the ruler. He started from the zero mark.

**Arjun:** There you go. On most rulers there is a small gap before the zero. If you start from the edge, your answer is wrong.

**Meera:** I did not know that! How did you know?

**Arjun:** My dadu told me last summer when we were measuring the tomato plants in our balcony. He calls it "zero ki galti".

**Meera:** Cute. Sir called it zero error in class. There is another one called parallax error.

**Arjun:** What is that?

**Meera:** When you tilt your head and look at the ruler sideways, the reading shifts. Like in the auto-rickshaw, when you sit on one side, the meter looks like it shows a different number than what the driver sees.

**Arjun:** Hmm. So I should look straight down, right above the ruler?

**Meera:** Yes. Otherwise the number shifts a tiny bit and the answer comes wrong.

**Arjun:** Okay one more — how do we measure something round? Like the rim of my water bottle?

**Meera:** Take a thread, wrap it around once, mark where it meets, then straighten the thread on a ruler.

**Arjun:** Oho clever. Or use a measuring tape — it bends.

**Meera:** Yes, the tailor uses it when stitching kurtas. He wraps it around your chest and shoulders.

## Types of Motion

**Meera:** Arjun, I was watching the kids in the park yesterday. The merry-go-round was going round and round. A boy was on the swing going back and forth. Another boy was running straight across to grab his ball. Three completely different ways of moving!

**Arjun:** Sir told us scientists actually have names for each type. Based on the path the object follows.

**Meera:** Tell me, tell me.

**Arjun:** When something goes in a straight line — that is rectilinear motion. Like a train on a straight track. Or that boy running across to grab his ball.

**Meera:** Or the way a fielder runs in a straight line to catch the ball in gully cricket.

**Arjun:** Exactly. Then the merry-go-round — that is circular motion. Going around a centre point. Like the ceiling fan blades. Each blade tip traces a circle.

**Meera:** And the seconds hand on the wall clock!

**Arjun:** Yes. And the swing is oscillatory motion. Forward, backward, forward, backward, around a middle resting point. Like the pendulum inside Dadu's old wooden clock.

**Meera:** What about a top? You know, the wooden lattoo my cousin brings every Diwali?

**Arjun:** That is rotational motion — spinning around its own axis. Like a kumhar's pottery wheel.

**Meera:** Wait, Sir also said something about periodic motion. Is that the same as oscillatory?

**Arjun:** Close but not same. Periodic just means it repeats after a fixed time. The fan blade going around once and again and again — that is periodic. But it does not reverse direction, so it is not oscillatory.

**Meera:** Got it. So oscillatory is always periodic, but periodic is not always oscillatory.

**Arjun:** Tu toh science teacher ban gayi.

**Meera:** Shut up, yaar. One more — the butterfly that came to our balcony yesterday flew totally zigzag. What is that?

**Arjun:** Random motion. No pattern. Like dust dancing in a sunbeam coming through the window.

**Meera:** And can an object have two motions at the same time?

**Arjun:** Of course. Look at my cycle. The wheels rotate around the axle — that is rotational. But the whole cycle moves forward in a straight line — rectilinear. Two motions, same object.

## Speed — Connecting Distance and Time

**Meera:** Arjun, last weekend we went to my nani's house in Jaipur. The board on the highway said "Jaipur 240 km". Papa said we will reach in about 4 hours. Was that fast?

**Arjun:** Decent speed, yaar. In 4 hours you covered 240 km, so each hour roughly 60 km. That is highway speed.

**Meera:** So speed is just how much distance in how much time?

**Arjun:** Exactly. Bigger distance in same time = faster. Same distance in more time = slower.

**Meera:** Like in gully cricket — the kid who runs to the boundary in 3 seconds is faster than the kid who takes 6 seconds.

**Arjun:** Yes. A bullock cart in a village does maybe 5 km in one hour. A car does 60 km in one hour. An aeroplane does 800 km in one hour. All different speeds.

**Meera:** Now I noticed something on that trip. On the highway we just kept going steady. But inside the Jaipur city, papa kept braking, accelerating, stopping at red lights.

**Arjun:** Highway = uniform motion. Equal distance in equal chunks of time. City driving = non-uniform motion. Sometimes you move 100 metres in a minute, sometimes you don't move at all because the signal is red.

**Meera:** So almost nothing in real life is perfectly uniform?

**Arjun:** Almost nothing. But the idea is still useful. It is a simple starting point. Later we will study motion in more detail.

**Meera:** Sir said something like that. Build the basics first.

**Arjun:** Now come, my tiffin has gobi paratha. Let's eat before we calculate the speed of food disappearing.

**Meera:** Yaar, you ruin every nice moment with a bad joke.
