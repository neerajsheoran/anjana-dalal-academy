## Key Concepts

- **Non-standard units** (handspan, cubit, foot) vary from person to person and are unreliable for accurate measurement
- Scientists agreed on the **International System of Units (SI)** for universal, consistent measurement
- The **SI unit of length** is the **metre (m)**
- **Conversion chain:** 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm
- Always start measuring from the **zero mark** of the ruler, not the edge
- **Parallax error** occurs when you read a scale from an angle instead of keeping your eye directly above the reading point
- **Zero error** occurs when you start from the wrong mark on the ruler
- To measure **curved lengths**, use a thread along the curve, then straighten it against a ruler
- **Motion** = change in position of an object over time; **Rest** = no change in position
- **Rectilinear motion** — movement along a straight line (e.g., train on straight track)
- **Circular motion** — movement along a circular path (e.g., fan blades, clock hands)
- **Periodic motion** — repeats after equal time intervals (e.g., Earth's orbit)
- **Oscillatory motion** — to-and-fro about a central position (e.g., pendulum, swing); all oscillatory motion is periodic, but not all periodic motion is oscillatory
- **Rotational motion** — spinning around an axis through the object (e.g., potter's wheel)
- **Random motion** — unpredictable, no fixed path (e.g., butterfly, dust particles)
- **Speed** = how quickly an object covers distance; links distance with time
- **Uniform motion** — equal distances in equal time intervals; **Non-uniform motion** — unequal distances in equal time intervals

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| SI Units | Universally agreed standard units of measurement |
| Metre (m) | SI unit of length |
| Parallax error | Wrong reading caused by viewing a scale at an angle |
| Zero error | Mistake from not aligning object with the true zero mark |
| Rectilinear motion | Movement along a straight-line path |
| Circular motion | Movement along a circular path around a centre |
| Oscillatory motion | To-and-fro movement about a fixed central position |
| Periodic motion | Motion that repeats at regular time intervals |
| Rotational motion | Spinning around an axis passing through the object |
| Random motion | Unpredictable movement with no fixed path |
| Uniform motion | Equal distances covered in equal time intervals |
| Non-uniform motion | Unequal distances covered in equal time intervals |

## Remember These

- All oscillatory motion is periodic, but **not all periodic motion is oscillatory** (e.g., circular motion of a fan is periodic but not oscillatory)
- A single object can show **multiple types of motion** at once (bicycle wheel: rotational + rectilinear)
- Always read a ruler with your eye **perpendicular** to the scale to avoid parallax error
- Use a **thread** for measuring curved or irregular lengths — a rigid ruler will not work
- **1 km = 1000 m = 100,000 cm = 10,00,000 mm**
