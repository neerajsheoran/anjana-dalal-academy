## Key Concepts

- Water exists in **three states:** solid (ice), liquid (water), gas (water vapour)
- Changes between states are caused by **adding or removing heat energy**
- **Melting** — solid to liquid (ice to water) at **0°C**
- **Freezing** — liquid to solid (water to ice) at **0°C**
- **Evaporation** — liquid to gas at the **surface**, happens at any temperature below boiling point
- **Boiling** — rapid conversion of liquid to gas throughout the liquid at **100°C**
- **Condensation** — gas to liquid when water vapour is cooled (dew, fog, droplets on cold glass)
- Evaporation is faster with: **higher temperature, larger surface area, more wind, lower humidity**
- **Cooling effect of evaporation** — escaping molecules carry energy away, cooling the remaining liquid and surroundings
- Clay pot (matka) keeps water cool because water seeps through pores and evaporates, removing heat
- A desert cooler works on the same principle — hot air passes through water-soaked pads, water evaporates and cools the air
- **Humidity** = amount of water vapour in the air; high humidity slows evaporation (sticky feeling), low humidity speeds it up
- **Dew** — water vapour condenses on cool surfaces overnight
- **Fog** — tiny water droplets suspended in cold air near the ground
- **The Water Cycle:** Evaporation (sun heats water) → Condensation (vapour rises, cools, forms clouds) → Precipitation (rain, snow, hail) → Collection (water flows back to rivers/ocean)
- Vapour condenses around **dust and pollen particles** in the sky to form cloud droplets
- **AWG (Atmospheric Water Generator)** — machine that uses condensation to harvest drinking water from humid air

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Evaporation | Conversion of liquid to vapour at the surface, below boiling point |
| Condensation | Conversion of water vapour to liquid on cooling |
| Melting | Solid to liquid by adding heat |
| Freezing | Liquid to solid by removing heat |
| Boiling point | Temperature at which liquid rapidly converts to vapour throughout (100°C for water) |
| Melting point | Temperature at which solid converts to liquid (0°C for ice) |
| Humidity | Amount of water vapour present in the air |
| Water cycle | Continuous movement of water through evaporation, condensation, and precipitation |
| Precipitation | Water falling from clouds as rain, snow, or hail |
| Dew | Droplets formed when vapour condenses on cool surfaces overnight |

## Remember These

- **Melting point and freezing point are the same temperature (0°C)** — just opposite directions of change
- **Evaporation happens at any temperature**, not just at 100°C — that is how clothes dry on a 30°C day
- A ceiling fan does **not produce cold air** — it speeds up sweat evaporation, which cools your body
- The water cycle has been running for **billions of years**, recycling the same water over and over
- Four factors affecting evaporation rate: **temperature, surface area, wind, humidity** — memorise all four
