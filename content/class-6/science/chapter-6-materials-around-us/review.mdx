## Key Concepts

- **Matter** is anything that has **mass** and occupies **space** (volume)
- An **object** is a thing we use for a purpose; a **material** is the substance it is made from
- The same material can make many objects (wood → stump, door, rolling pin); the same object can be made from different materials (chair → wood, plastic, steel)
- **Hardness** — ability to resist scratching/denting (diamond is hardest natural substance); **Softness** — changes shape easily under pressure (cotton, wax)
- **Roughness** — uneven surface that provides grip; **Smoothness** — flat surface that is easy to clean
- **Lustre** — natural shine when light falls on a material; metals are lustrous, chalk and paper are non-lustrous
- **Transparent** — lets all light through (glass, clean water); **Translucent** — lets some light through but blurs it (frosted glass, butter paper); **Opaque** — blocks all light (wood, metal)
- **Soluble** materials dissolve in water (sugar, salt); **Insoluble** materials do not dissolve (sand, turmeric, iron filings)
- Objects **sink** if denser than water (steel spoon); **float** if less dense (wood, cork)
- **Conductors** allow heat/electricity to pass through easily (metals like copper, aluminium); **Insulators** resist the flow of heat/electricity (wood, plastic, rubber)
- **Natural materials** — obtained from plants (cotton, wood, rubber), animals (silk, wool, leather), or the earth (iron ore, clay, marble)
- **Synthetic materials** — manufactured by humans, often from natural raw materials (plastic, nylon, glass, cement, steel)
- **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle** — in order of priority — is the best approach to caring for materials
- Recycling aluminium uses roughly **95% less energy** than producing it from raw ore

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Matter | Anything that has mass and occupies space |
| Object | A thing made for a particular use |
| Material | The substance from which an object is made |
| Transparent | Allows light to pass through clearly |
| Translucent | Allows some light through but scatters it |
| Opaque | Does not let any light pass through |
| Soluble | Dissolves in a given liquid (usually water) |
| Insoluble | Does not dissolve in a given liquid |
| Conductor | Material that allows heat or electricity to pass easily |
| Insulator | Material that resists the flow of heat or electricity |
| Lustre | Natural shine of a material's surface |
| Recycling | Reprocessing used materials to make new products |

## Remember These

- A single object can use **two different materials** for different parts (electric plug: copper pins for conduction, plastic body for insulation)
- **Transparent vs Translucent vs Opaque** is a very common exam question — remember one example of each
- Oil does not dissolve in water — it **floats as a separate layer** (not the same as being insoluble like sand which sinks)
- Thermoset plastics like **Bakelite cannot be recycled** because they cannot be melted and reshaped
- The order is **Reduce > Reuse > Recycle** — reduce comes first
