## Gazing at the Night Sky

![Gazing at the night sky — thousands of stars stretching across the sky](gazing-night-sky.png)

On a cloudless night, step away from streetlights and look upward. A vast canvas of twinkling lights stretches from horizon to horizon. Thousands of years ago, shepherds in ancient India, Mesopotamia, and Egypt stared at the very same sky and asked: What are those lights? How far away are they? Are we alone?

That curiosity gave birth to **astronomy** — one of humanity's oldest sciences. Today we know that those pinpoints of light are **stars** — colossal spheres of blazing gas, many dwarfing our own Sun, some so distant that the light reaching your eyes began its journey before your great-great-grandparents were born.

> **Central thought:** The universe is vast beyond imagination. Studying it — from the Moon next door to galaxies billions of light years away — is among the greatest adventures of the human mind.

## Stars and the Night Sky

### What Exactly Is a Star?

![What is a star — enormous balls of hot gas generating light and heat](what-is-star-1.png)

![Stars in the night sky](what-is-star-2.png)

A **star** is an enormous ball of extremely hot gas — primarily hydrogen and helium — that generates its own light and heat through **nuclear fusion** reactions deep in its core.

Our **Sun** is a star. It looks large and dazzling because it is relatively close — roughly 150 million km away. Every other star you see at night is far, far more distant, which is why they appear as tiny specks.

### Measuring Cosmic Distances — The Light Year

Stars are so remote that measuring their distance in kilometres gives absurdly huge numbers. Instead, astronomers use the **light year**.

> **One light year** = the distance that light — travelling at about 3 lakh km per second — covers in one full year ≈ 9.46 lakh crore km (9.46 x 10¹² km).

The nearest star beyond the Sun, **Proxima Centauri**, is about 4.24 light years away. Even a beam of light would need more than four years to reach it!

### Constellations — Star Patterns in the Sky

Ancient stargazers noticed that certain groups of stars seemed to form recognisable shapes — a hunter, a bear, a scorpion. They named these patterns **constellations** and wove them into myths and stories.

The **International Astronomical Union (IAU)** officially recognises **88 constellations** that tile the entire sky.

Some notable constellations:

![Orion — The Hunter constellation](constellation-orion.png)

**Orion (The Hunter / Mriga)**
- One of the easiest constellations to spot, visible on winter nights in India.
- Features three bright stars in a neat row — **Orion's Belt**.
- Contains the red giant **Betelgeuse** (upper left) and the brilliant blue-white **Rigel** (lower right).
- In Indian tradition, stars in this region correspond to the **Mrigashira** nakshatra.

![Canis Major — The Great Dog constellation with Sirius](constellation-canis-major.png)

**Canis Major (The Great Dog)**
- Appears just below and left of Orion, like a faithful dog following the hunter.
- Home to **Sirius** — the **brightest star in the night sky** (apart from the Sun).
- Sirius is so luminous it can be spotted even from well-lit cities.

![Taurus — The Bull constellation with Pleiades](constellation-taurus.png)

**Taurus (The Bull / Rohini)**
- Located near Orion.
- Contains the **Pleiades** — a beautiful open cluster sometimes called the **Seven Sisters** (known as the **Krittika** nakshatra in Indian astronomy).
- Also hosts the orange-red star **Aldebaran**.

![Ursa Major — Saptarishi, the seven sages of Indian tradition](constellation-ursa-major.png)

**Ursa Major (The Great Bear) — Saptarishi**
- Among the most celebrated constellations in both Western and Indian traditions.
- Its seven brightest stars form the **Big Dipper** (a large ladle shape).
- In India, these seven stars are called the **Saptarishi** — named after seven legendary sages: Marichi, Atri, Angiras, Pulaha, Kratu, Pulastya, and Vashistha.
- Visible throughout the year from most parts of India.

**Ursa Minor (The Little Bear)**
- Contains the **Little Dipper**, a smaller ladle-shaped pattern.
- Its most famous member is **Polaris** — the **Pole Star (Dhruva Tara)**.

### The Pole Star — Dhruva Tara (Polaris)

Polaris holds a unique position in the sky:
- It sits almost exactly above Earth's **North Pole**.
- As the Earth rotates, every other star appears to arc across the sky during the night — but Polaris stays nearly **fixed** in place.
- For thousands of years, sailors and desert travellers used the Pole Star to find **north** — it was nature's own compass, requiring no batteries or satellites!

*Locating Polaris:*
1. Find the Big Dipper (Saptarishi) — seven bright stars shaped like a large ladle.
2. Identify the two stars at the outer edge of the ladle's bowl (the "pointer stars").
3. Draw an imaginary straight line through them and extend it roughly five times the gap between them.
4. You will arrive at a moderately bright star — that is Polaris.

> In Hindu mythology, the Pole Star is called **Dhruva Tara** after the young prince Dhruva, whose unwavering devotion earned him a permanent, unmoving place in the heavens.

### Indian Astronomy — Nakshatras and Jantar Mantar

Indian astronomers have charted the sky for millennia. The Indian system divides the sky along the Moon's path into **27 nakshatras** (lunar mansions). As the Moon orbits Earth in about 27 days, it passes through one nakshatra each night.

The **Jantar Mantar** observatories in Jaipur and Delhi, constructed by Maharaja Jai Singh II in the early 1700s, feature massive stone instruments that measured time, tracked celestial objects, and predicted eclipses with remarkable precision — all without a single telescope.

## The Solar System

![The Solar System — Sun and its family of planets](solar-system.png)

Our star — the Sun — holds a family of planets, moons, asteroids, and comets in its gravitational embrace. This entire family is called the **Solar System**.

### The Sun — Our Neighbourhood Star

- The Sun sits at the **centre** of the Solar System.
- Distance from Earth: ~150 million km (this distance defines **1 Astronomical Unit, or AU**).
- Diameter: roughly **139 lakh km** — about **109 times** Earth's diameter.
- Surface temperature: approximately **5,500 °C**; core temperature: about **15 million °C**.
- Light from the Sun reaches Earth in roughly **8 minutes and 20 seconds**.
- The Sun provides the light and warmth that make life on Earth possible.

### The Eight Planets

Eight planets orbit the Sun along well-defined paths called **orbits**. Listed from nearest to farthest:

| Planet | Memory aid | Distinctive feature |
| ------ | ---------- | ------------------- |
| **Mercury** | **M**eri | Smallest planet; nearest to the Sun; almost no atmosphere; wild temperature swings |
| **Venus** | **V**ery | Hottest planet (thick CO₂ atmosphere traps heat); brightest object after the Moon; known as the **Morning Star** or **Evening Star** |
| **Earth** | **E**nergetic | Only known planet harbouring life; called the **Blue Planet** (~70% water surface); has one natural satellite — the Moon |
| **Mars** | **M**aa | Called the **Red Planet** (iron oxide/rust gives its surface a reddish hue); home to Olympus Mons — the tallest volcano in the Solar System; India's **Mangalyaan** reached Mars in 2014 |
| **Jupiter** | **J**oyfully | **Largest planet**; its Great Red Spot is a storm bigger than Earth, raging for centuries; 95 known moons |
| **Saturn** | **S**aw | Famous for its stunning **ring system** (made of ice chunks and rocky debris); second largest planet; 146 known moons including Titan |
| **Uranus** | **U**ttar | **Tilted on its side** — its axis is nearly horizontal; faint rings; appears blue-green due to methane |
| **Neptune** | **Nakshatra** | **Farthest** planet; deep blue colour; possesses the fiercest winds in the Solar System |

**Memory sentence:** **Meri Very Energetic Maa Joyfully Saw Uttar Nakshatra**

### Rotation and Revolution

![Rotation — a planet spinning on its own axis](rotation.png)

![Revolution — a planet travelling around the Sun](revolution.png)

- **Rotation** = a planet spinning on its own axis. This creates **day and night**. Earth completes one rotation in **24 hours**.
- **Revolution** = a planet travelling around the Sun in its orbit. This defines a **year**. Earth takes **365.25 days** to complete one revolution.

| Planet | Time for one orbit around the Sun |
| ------ | --------------------------------- |
| Mercury | 88 Earth days |
| Venus | 225 Earth days |
| Earth | 365.25 days |
| Mars | 687 Earth days (~1.9 Earth years) |
| Jupiter | About 12 Earth years |
| Saturn | About 29.5 Earth years |
| Neptune | About 165 Earth years |

### Inner and Outer Planets

The **asteroid belt** between Mars and Jupiter divides the Solar System into two groups:

- **Inner (terrestrial) planets** — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Smaller, rocky, closer to the Sun.
- **Outer (giant) planets** — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. Much larger, mostly gas or ice, farther from the Sun. Often called **Gas Giants** or **Ice Giants**.

### Venus — Not Actually a Star

Venus is frequently called the **Morning Star** or **Evening Star**, but it is a planet, not a star.

- It appears as a dazzling white light low in the eastern sky before dawn or in the western sky after dusk.
- Its brilliance comes from its proximity to Earth and its thick cloud cover, which **reflects sunlight** extremely well.
- Venus is the **hottest planet** — even hotter than Mercury (which is closer to the Sun!) — because its dense CO₂ atmosphere traps heat in a runaway **greenhouse effect**.

### Pluto — The Reclassified World

For decades, Pluto was counted as the 9th planet. In **2006**, the International Astronomical Union redefined what qualifies as a planet. Pluto was reclassified as a **dwarf planet** because it has not gravitationally cleared its orbital neighbourhood of other objects. Five dwarf planets are now officially recognised, including Pluto, Eris, and Ceres.

## The Moon — Earth's Companion

![The Moon — Earth's natural satellite](moon.png)

A **natural satellite** is a celestial body that orbits a planet. Earth's only natural satellite is the **Moon**.

### Moon Facts

- Average distance from Earth: approximately **3.84 lakh km**
- Time to orbit Earth: about **27.3 days**
- The Moon rotates on its axis at the same rate it orbits Earth → we always see the **same face** from Earth.
- The Moon has **no atmosphere**, no liquid water, and no weather.
- It does **not produce its own light** — the moonlight we see is **reflected sunlight**.
- Its surface is scarred by countless **craters**, formed billions of years ago by meteorite impacts.
- Gravity on the Moon is about **one-sixth** of Earth's → a person weighing 60 kg on Earth would weigh only 10 kg on the Moon!

### Phases of the Moon

As the Moon circles Earth, the Sun illuminates different portions of its surface as seen from our vantage point. This gives rise to the **phases of the Moon**:

New Moon → Waxing Crescent → First Quarter → Waxing Gibbous → Full Moon → Waning Gibbous → Last Quarter → Waning Crescent → New Moon

This complete cycle takes about **29.5 days** — one **lunar month**. Indian festivals like Diwali (Amavasya — new moon), Holi, and Purnima celebrations are tied to these lunar phases.

### India's Chandrayaan Missions

India's space agency **ISRO** has sent three landmark missions to the Moon:

- **Chandrayaan-1 (2008)** — India's maiden lunar mission. Its Moon Impact Probe discovered evidence of **water molecules** on the lunar surface — a finding that reshaped our understanding of the Moon.
- **Chandrayaan-2 (2019)** — The orbiter continues to function successfully, providing high-resolution images. The lander and rover, however, experienced a hard landing near the south pole.
- **Chandrayaan-3 (2023)** — On **23 August 2023**, the **Vikram lander** and **Pragyan rover** touched down softly near the **Moon's south pole**. India became the **first nation to land near the lunar south pole** and the **fourth country** to achieve a controlled lunar landing (after the USA, Russia, and China). The landing site was named **Shiv Shakti Point**.

> Chandrayaan-3's success was celebrated across India and the world. It demonstrated that cutting-edge space exploration is achievable with ingenuity and determination, not just enormous budgets.

## Asteroids and Comets

### Asteroids — Rocky Leftovers

![Asteroids — rocky leftovers orbiting the Sun](asteroids.png)

**Asteroids** are rocky bodies orbiting the Sun, significantly smaller than planets.

- The vast majority reside in the **Asteroid Belt** — a broad zone between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter.
- Sizes range from pebble-sized fragments to objects nearly 1,000 km across.
- The largest asteroid, **Ceres**, has been reclassified as a dwarf planet.
- Scientists believe asteroids are remnants of material that never coalesced into a planet during the Solar System's formation about 4.6 billion years ago.

### Comets — Visitors from the Frozen Edge

![Comets — icy visitors with spectacular tails](comets.png)

**Comets** are icy bodies that follow highly elongated (elliptical) orbits, spending most of their time in the cold, distant reaches of the Solar System.

- Composed of ice (water, methane, ammonia), dust, and rock — often called **"dirty snowballs"**.
- When a comet swings close to the Sun, solar heat causes its ice to vaporise directly into gas (**sublimation**). This gas and freed dust stream away from the comet, forming a spectacular **tail** that can stretch millions of kilometres.
- A comet's tail always points **away from the Sun**, blown by the **solar wind** — regardless of the comet's direction of travel.

**Halley's Comet:**
- The most famous periodic comet, named after British astronomer Edmond Halley who accurately predicted its return.
- Visible from Earth approximately every **76 years**.
- Last appeared in **1986**; next expected around **2061**.
- Recorded in ancient Indian, Chinese, and Babylonian texts stretching back thousands of years.

> In ancient India, comets were called **ketu** — tail-bearing celestial objects. Their appearances were carefully documented by court astronomers and recorded in royal chronicles.

## Our Galaxy — The Milky Way (Akasha Ganga)

![The Milky Way — our home galaxy, Akasha Ganga](milky-way.png)

The Sun, along with all its planets, is just one member of a vast congregation of stars called the **Milky Way Galaxy**.

- The Milky Way is a **spiral galaxy** — it has a luminous central bulge surrounded by sweeping spiral arms.
- It contains an estimated **200 to 400 billion stars**.
- Its diameter is so immense that light needs roughly **1 lakh years** to cross it.
- Our Solar System occupies a position about **26,000 light years** from the galactic centre, along one of the spiral arms.
- On a truly dark, moonless night, far from city lights, you can see the Milky Way as a **soft, glowing band** arching across the sky — the combined light of billions of distant stars blending into a luminous river.

In Indian tradition, the Milky Way is beautifully named **Akasha Ganga** — the celestial river — linking the shimmering band of stars to the sacred Ganga flowing across the land.

## The Universe — Everything That Exists

The **universe** encompasses all of existence — every particle of matter, every form of energy, all of space, and all of time.

- It contains **hundreds of billions of galaxies**, each harbouring hundreds of billions of stars.
- The universe is approximately **13.8 billion years old**.
- It originated in the **Big Bang** — a sudden, rapid expansion from an unimaginably hot, dense state.
- The universe continues to **expand** — galaxies are drifting farther apart even now.

### Could There Be Life Beyond Earth?

This is one of science's most thrilling open questions: **Are we alone?**

- As of now, Earth remains the **only confirmed abode of life**.
- Scientists are actively searching for life — or signs of past life — on **Mars**, on **Europa** (a moon of Jupiter believed to have a liquid water ocean beneath its icy shell), and on **Enceladus** (a moon of Saturn with water geysers).
- Thousands of **exoplanets** — planets orbiting stars other than the Sun — have been discovered. Some lie within the **habitable zone** (the range of distances from a star where liquid water could exist on a planet's surface).
- The study of potential life beyond Earth is called **astrobiology**.
- ISRO's planned **Gaganyaan** mission will send Indian astronauts — called **Vyomanauts** — into Earth orbit, marking India's entry into human spaceflight.

> The universe contains more stars than all the grains of sand on all the beaches on Earth. With so many stars and so many planets, the question is not just whether life exists elsewhere — but how we will recognise it when we find it.

## Light Pollution — The Vanishing Night Sky

In ancient times, everyone — farmers, poets, sailors, scientists — could see thousands of stars on any clear night. Today, in most Indian cities, barely a few dozen are visible.

**Light pollution** is the excessive and misdirected artificial light from streetlamps, signboards, buildings, and vehicles that washes out the natural darkness of the night sky.

*Effects:*
- The Milky Way has become invisible to over 80% of the world's population.
- **Nocturnal animals** — owls, bats, moths, fireflies — are disrupted by artificial light, which confuses their navigation, feeding, and mating.
- Sea turtle hatchlings, which instinctively crawl toward the brightest horizon (normally the moonlit ocean), can be fatally disoriented by bright coastal lights.
- Astronomers must travel to remote, high-altitude locations to use their telescopes effectively.

*What can be done?* Use **shielded, downward-facing lights** that illuminate the ground without spilling light upward. Switch off unnecessary outdoor lights. Replace harsh white LEDs with warmer-toned alternatives. Visit a dark-sky location — such as **Hanle in Ladakh**, home to the Indian Astronomical Observatory — and experience the sky as our ancestors saw it.

## Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| ---- | ------- |
| **Star** | A massive, luminous sphere of hot gas producing its own light and heat through nuclear fusion |
| **Constellation** | A recognised pattern of stars in the sky, used to map and name regions of the celestial sphere |
| **Pole Star (Dhruva Tara)** | The star (Polaris) located nearly above Earth's North Pole; appears almost stationary |
| **Solar System** | The Sun and all objects — planets, moons, asteroids, comets — bound by its gravity |
| **Planet** | A large body orbiting a star, massive enough to be nearly spherical, that has cleared its orbital zone |
| **Orbit** | The curved path one object follows around another under the influence of gravity |
| **Rotation** | The spinning of an object around its own axis (produces day and night) |
| **Revolution** | The movement of one body around another (e.g., Earth around the Sun, producing a year) |
| **Natural satellite** | A natural body orbiting a planet (the Moon is Earth's natural satellite) |
| **Asteroid** | A rocky object orbiting the Sun, smaller than a planet; most are found in the Asteroid Belt |
| **Comet** | An icy, dusty body with a glowing tail that appears when it approaches the Sun |
| **Galaxy** | A vast system of billions of stars, gas, and dust held together by gravity |
| **Milky Way (Akasha Ganga)** | Our home galaxy — a spiral collection of hundreds of billions of stars |
| **Universe** | The totality of all matter, energy, space, and time |
| **Light year** | A unit of distance — the distance light travels in one year (~9.46 lakh crore km) |
| **Light pollution** | Excessive artificial light that obscures the night sky and harms ecosystems |
| **Exoplanet** | A planet orbiting a star other than our Sun |
