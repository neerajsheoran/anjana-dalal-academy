## What Makes Us Happy?

Think about the last time you felt really joyful. Maybe you scored well in a test, helped someone carry their groceries, or played lagori with your friends after school. Happiness comes in many forms -- sharing a joke with your sibling, drawing a picture you are proud of, or simply watching rain from your window.

Being happy is not just about having fun. It is closely connected to being **healthy**. When we feel good inside, our body stays strong too. And one of the biggest sources of happiness? A warm, delicious meal prepared with love!

But before that meal reaches your thali, it travels a very long journey. Let us find out how.

---

## The Journey of a Grain

Imagine a single grain of wheat sitting on your roti. That tiny grain went through a remarkable adventure before it reached you!

| Step | What Happens |
|---|---|
| 1. **Preparing the field** | The farmer loosens the soil with a plough so seeds can grow |
| 2. **Planting seeds** | Seeds are carefully placed into the soft soil |
| 3. **Giving water** | Fields are watered through canals, wells or rain |
| 4. **Nature does its work** | Sun, soil and water help the tiny plant sprout and grow tall |
| 5. **Guarding the crop** | Farmers scare away birds and protect plants from insects |
| 6. **Cutting the crop** | When the crop turns golden, farmers harvest it with sickles or machines |
| 7. **Separating the grain** | The grains are beaten out from the stalks -- this is called threshing |
| 8. **Packing and storing** | Grains are collected in big sacks and stored safely |
| 9. **Travelling to market** | Trucks carry the grain sacks to mandis and shops in cities |
| 10. **Buying and cooking** | Your family buys the grain, and it is cooked into tasty food at home |

Many **helpers from nature** make this journey possible:

- **Soil** holds the roots and feeds the plant with nutrients.
- **Water** keeps the plant alive and growing.
- **Sunlight** provides the energy the plant needs to make food.
- **Air** gives carbon dioxide, which plants breathe in.
- **Earthworms** dig tunnels in the soil, making it loose and rich.
- **Bullocks** pull the plough in many Indian farms.

> From the farmer who wakes up at dawn to the truck driver who delivers the grain -- so many people and natural forces work together for every bite you eat. That is why each grain is **precious** and deserves our respect.

## Gratitude for Food

Across India, families and communities come together to **say thank you** to nature for the food they receive. Many of our biggest festivals are actually harvest celebrations:

| Festival | What We Celebrate |
|---|---|
| **Makar Sankranti** | The harvest season and the warmth of the Sun |
| **Pongal** | Thanking the Sun, rain and farm animals for a good harvest (Tamil Nadu) |
| **Onam** | A grand feast celebrating the harvest (Kerala) |
| **Lohri** | Welcoming the end of winter with harvest songs (Punjab) |
| **Akshay Tritya** | Beginning of a new farming season |
| **Raja Utsav** | Honouring Mother Earth for giving us food (Odisha) |

When we think about all the effort behind our food -- the farmer's sweat, the rain, the sunshine -- we naturally feel **thankful**. And grateful people enjoy their meals even more!

## Mindful Eating

Have you ever gobbled down your lunch while watching a cartoon, and later could not even remember what you ate? That is the opposite of **mindful eating**.

Mindful eating means giving your full attention to your food. Here is a simple way to practise it:

1. **Sit down properly** at the dining table. No screens, no distractions.
2. **Look at your food** and notice its colours and shape.
3. **Take a small bite** and place it in your mouth gently.
4. **Feel the texture** -- is it crunchy, soft, chewy?
5. **Chew slowly** and notice how the flavours change.
6. **Swallow only after chewing well** -- this helps your stomach digest food easily.

When you eat mindfully, you actually **taste** your food better, you feel full at the right time, and you **waste less** because you take only what you need.

> Your **tongue** is covered with tiny taste buds that detect sweet, salty, sour and bitter flavours. Your **teeth** crush food into small pieces, and **saliva** in your mouth starts breaking it down even before it reaches your stomach!

## Do Not Waste Food

Since every grain has such a long journey behind it, wasting food means wasting the hard work of many people and gifts of nature. Here are some simple habits to follow:

- Put only as much food on your plate as you can finish.
- If you are not sure you will like something, take a tiny portion first.
- If someone serves you too much, say "Thank you, but this is enough for me" politely.
- Finish what is on your plate before asking for a second helping.

## Exercise and Physical Activity

Good food is only half the story. Our body is like a machine -- it needs to **move** to stay in good shape. Sitting all day makes us feel lazy and dull, but running, jumping and playing fill us with energy.

**Ways to get your body moving:**

- Traditional Indian games like kabaddi, kho-kho, gilli-danda and pitthu
- Cycling around your colony in the evening
- Skipping rope with friends during recess
- Swimming during summer holidays
- Practising **yoga** every morning

**Yoga poses that children can try:**

| Yoga Pose | What It Does |
|---|---|
| **Tadasana** (Mountain pose) | Makes you stand tall and improves balance |
| **Vrikshasana** (Tree pose) | Builds focus and strengthens your legs |
| **Bhujangasana** (Cobra pose) | Makes your back and spine stronger |
| **Balasana** (Child's pose) | Calms your mind and relaxes your body |

> Moving your body every day keeps your **muscles strong**, your **heart healthy** and your **mind sharp**. Children who play and exercise regularly do better in studies too!

## Sleep Well

After a day full of school, play and learning, your body needs to **recharge**. Sleep is the time when your body fixes itself and your brain sorts out everything you learned during the day.

**When you get good sleep:**
- You wake up feeling **fresh and cheerful**.
- Your body grows and repairs muscles while you sleep.
- Your brain stores new things you learned, like a computer saving files.

**When you do not get enough sleep:**
- You feel **grumpy, tired and cranky** the next day.
- It becomes difficult to pay attention in class.
- You catch colds and fevers more easily.

> Children your age need about **9 to 11 hours of sleep** every night. So if you need to wake up at 6:30 AM, you should be asleep by 8:30 or 9:30 PM!

## Feeling Safe

Being happy and healthy also means feeling **protected and safe**. It is important for every child to understand the difference between a **safe touch** and an **unsafe touch**.

- A **safe touch** feels warm and comforting -- like your mother stroking your hair, your grandfather holding your hand, or your teacher giving you a pat on the back for good work.
- An **unsafe touch** feels wrong, scary or uncomfortable. It makes you want to pull away.

**What to do if someone's touch makes you uncomfortable:**

1. Say **"No! Stop!"** in a loud, clear voice.
2. Get away from that person quickly.
3. Tell a **trusted grown-up** right away -- your parents, grandparents, teacher or any adult you trust.

> You have every right to say "No" to anyone -- even a grown-up -- if their touch makes you uncomfortable. **Your body belongs to you**, and keeping you safe is always the most important thing.

## Key Points to Remember

- A grain of wheat or rice travels through **many steps** and many people's hands before it reaches your plate.
- Festivals like **Pongal, Lohri and Onam** are ways of thanking nature for our food.
- **Mindful eating** means paying attention to what you eat, chewing slowly and enjoying every bite.
- **Never waste food** -- every grain represents the hard work of farmers and the gifts of nature.
- **Exercise and yoga** keep your body strong, your heart healthy and your mind sharp.
- Children need **9 to 11 hours of sleep** every night for good health.
- Know the difference between **safe and unsafe touch** -- always speak up and tell a trusted adult if you feel uncomfortable.
