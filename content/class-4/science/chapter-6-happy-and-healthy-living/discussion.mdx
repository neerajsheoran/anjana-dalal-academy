## The Journey of a Grain and Gratitude for Food

**Child:** Maa, today our teacher told us that a grain of rice travels a really long journey before it reaches us. Is that true?

**Mother:** Absolutely, beta! Think about the rice you ate for lunch today. A farmer somewhere in India -- maybe in West Bengal or Andhra Pradesh -- woke up before sunrise, walked to the fields and planted those rice seeds months ago. Then he watered the plants, protected them from birds and insects, and waited patiently for weeks.

**Child:** That sounds like so much hard work just for rice!

**Mother:** And it does not stop there. After harvesting, the grain has to be separated, cleaned, packed into big sacks, loaded onto trucks, and driven to the market. Then your Maa buys it, washes it, and cooks it. So many hands and so many helpers from nature -- soil, water, sunlight, air -- all come together for that one plate of food.

**Child:** No wonder Dadiji always says "Anna devo bhava" -- food is like God.

**Mother:** That is right! And that is exactly why we have harvest festivals. Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Lohri in Punjab, Onam in Kerala -- they are all our way of saying thank you to nature and to farmers for giving us food. Next time you sit down to eat, take a moment to feel grateful for everyone who made that meal possible.

**Child:** I will, Maa. And I promise I will finish everything on my plate from now on!

## Exercise, Yoga and Sleep

**Child:** Maa, I feel so tired after sitting in school the whole day. But when I play with my friends outside, I feel full of energy. Why is that?

**Mother:** That is because your body is designed to move, beta! When you run, jump and play, your muscles get stronger and your heart pumps blood faster. Your brain also releases something called "happy chemicals" that make you feel wonderful. Sitting all day is like keeping a bicycle locked up -- it starts getting rusty!

**Child:** So playing is actually good for my health?

**Mother:** Very good! Playing kabaddi, skipping rope, cycling -- all of these are exercise. And you know what else is great? Yoga! Remember how Nani does Tadasana every morning? It keeps her back straight and her balance perfect even at her age.

**Child:** I know Vrikshasana too -- the tree pose! I have to stand on one leg and focus.

**Mother:** Wonderful! And after all that activity, your body needs rest. Sleep is when your body fixes itself -- like a mechanic repairing a car at night so it is ready to run in the morning. Children your age need at least 9 hours of sleep.

**Child:** What happens if I stay up late watching TV?

**Mother:** You will feel cranky and sleepy the next day, you will not be able to concentrate in class, and you might even catch a cold more easily. Good food, daily exercise and proper sleep -- these three together are the secret to a happy, healthy life.

**Child:** Got it, Maa -- eat well, play hard, sleep early!

## Mindful Eating and Feeling Safe

**Child:** Maa, what does "mindful eating" mean? My teacher said we should eat mindfully.

**Mother:** It means paying full attention to your food while eating. Tell me, beta, do you remember what you ate for breakfast yesterday?

**Child:** Hmm... I think it was paratha? I was watching cartoons so I am not sure.

**Mother:** See! That is the opposite of mindful eating. When you watch TV or play games on a phone while eating, your brain does not notice the food. You end up eating too fast, not chewing properly, and sometimes even eating more than you need.

**Child:** So what should I do instead?

**Mother:** Simple things. Sit at the dining table, look at your food, take small bites, chew slowly and notice the flavours. Your tongue has thousands of tiny taste buds -- let them do their job! When you eat slowly, you also feel full at the right time, and you do not waste food.

**Child:** I will try it tonight at dinner, Maa!

**Mother:** Good. Now, there is something else I want to talk about. You know how Maa hugs you, Dadaji pats your head, and your teacher shakes your hand? Those are all **safe touches** -- they make you feel warm and happy.

**Child:** Yes, I like those.

**Mother:** But if anyone ever touches you in a way that feels wrong, scary or uncomfortable -- that is an **unsafe touch**. If that ever happens, you must say "No!" loudly, move away from that person, and come tell me or Papa or your teacher immediately.

**Child:** Even if it is a grown-up?

**Mother:** Even if it is a grown-up. Your body belongs to you, and nobody has the right to make you feel unsafe. You should never feel shy or afraid to tell me. I will always believe you and protect you.

**Child:** Thank you, Maa. I feel safe knowing I can always talk to you.
