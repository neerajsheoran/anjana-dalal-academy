## The Letter That Travelled Far

**Child:** Maa, today our teacher told us about how people used to send letters by pigeons! Is that really true?

**Mother:** Yes, beta! Long ago, there were no phones or internet. People would tie a tiny rolled-up message to a pigeon's leg and release it. The pigeon would fly all the way to the other person's home.

**Child:** But how did the pigeon know where to go?

**Mother:** Pigeons have an amazing sense of direction. They can remember the way back to their home from very far away. But you can imagine how slow and risky it was -- what if it rained heavily or the pigeon got lost?

**Child:** That is why phones are so much better! But Maa, do people still use post offices?

**Mother:** Of course! Even today, post offices are very important. When Maa orders a saree from a handloom weaver in another city, it comes as a parcel through the post. People also use post offices for money orders and savings accounts. India has more than one and a half lakh post offices -- even in tiny villages where there is no bank!

**Child:** Wow, that is a lot!

## The Day We Took the Metro

**Child:** Maa, remember when we took the metro to Rajiv Chowk? It was so fast! How does it go so quickly?

**Mother:** Metro trains run on special electric tracks. They can travel very fast because they have their own separate route -- no traffic jams, no red lights!

**Child:** I wish we could take the metro everywhere. Why do we still use buses and autos?

**Mother:** Because metro lines do not go to every place yet. Different types of transport work for different situations. If you need to cross a river in the Sundarbans, you take a boat. If you need to go from Mumbai to Goa quickly, you might fly in an aeroplane. And if you want to go to the market two lanes away, you just walk or cycle!

**Child:** So we need all types -- road, rail, water and air?

**Mother:** Exactly. But here is something to think about -- when we build new roads and metro lines, sometimes trees have to be cut. That is why it is important to plant new trees too, so we do not harm nature while making travel easier.

## Learning About Money

**Child:** Maa, why do we keep money in the bank? Can't we just keep it at home in our cupboard?

**Mother:** We could, but the bank is much safer. At home, money could get lost, or someone might take it. In a bank, it is locked safely and even earns a little extra called interest.

**Child:** Interest? What is that?

**Mother:** Think of it this way. If you lend your story book to your friend and she returns it with a thank-you chocolate, that chocolate is like interest! The bank uses your money carefully, and in return, it adds a little extra to your savings.

**Child:** That is nice! But Maa, our teacher said people used to exchange things instead of using money. Like giving wheat for milk?

**Mother:** Yes, that was called the barter system. But imagine you had rice and wanted shoes. The shoe-maker might not want rice -- he might want vegetables! Finding the right match was so confusing that people decided to use coins and notes instead. Everyone accepts money, so it made life simpler.

**Child:** Haha, imagine going to a shop with a bag of potatoes to buy a pencil box!

**Mother:** Exactly! That is why money was such a clever invention.

## Finding Our Way

**Child:** Maa, today our teacher showed us a map of our school area. North was on top!

**Mother:** That is the standard rule for all maps. North on top, South at the bottom, East on the right, West on the left.

**Child:** But how do I know which direction I am facing when I am outside?

**Mother:** Easy trick! Tomorrow morning, go to the terrace and look for the rising Sun. The Sun always rises in the East. Once you face East, your back is to the West, your left hand points North, and your right hand points South.

**Child:** Oh! So the Sun is like a compass!

**Mother:** Quite right. And when you want to tell someone a route, use landmarks. Like if Riya asks you how to reach our house, you would say -- "Walk past the Hanuman temple, turn right at Sharma ji's sweet shop, and our house is the blue gate on the left." Landmarks make directions easy to follow.

**Child:** I can be a map expert now!

**Mother:** Start by drawing a map of our street with all the shops and houses. That will be great practice!
