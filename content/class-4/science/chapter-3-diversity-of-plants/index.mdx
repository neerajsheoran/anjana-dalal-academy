## Nature is Full of Wonders

Close your eyes for a moment and think of a forest. Can you hear birds chirping? Can you picture a colourful butterfly landing on a flower? Can you imagine a spider spinning silk threads into a perfect web? Nature is packed with surprises, and each living thing -- big or tiny -- has something special about it. In this chapter, we go on an exciting **nature walk** to discover animals, birds, insects and the leaves around us.

---

## A Nature Trail at Pachmarhi

**Pachmarhi** is a green, misty hill station tucked inside the Satpura hills of **Madhya Pradesh**. Its thick forests are home to towering sal trees, chattering langurs and bright-winged birds. A **nature trail** is a planned walk through such a natural area, where you slow down and carefully observe every plant and creature around you.

**Important safety rules for a forest visit or zoo trip:**

- Never tease, chase or try to feed wild animals.
- Do not pluck flowers, break branches or scratch tree bark.
- Carry a cloth bag -- say no to plastic bags and bottles.
- Leave your pets at home; wild animals may get stressed.
- Walk quietly and stay on the marked paths.

> These rules are not just for your safety -- they also protect the plants and animals that call the forest their home.

## Animals on Land

Forests shelter an incredible variety of animals. Each one has **body features** designed to help it survive:

| Animal | How Its Body Helps It |
|---|---|
| **Elephant** | Its long, flexible trunk works like a hand -- it picks leaves, sucks up water, and even gives itself a mud bath |
| **Deer** | Slender, powerful legs help it sprint away from hunters in seconds |
| **Tiger** | Sharp curved claws and strong jaws make it a fierce predator |
| **Gaur** (Indian bison) | A massive, muscular body with thick short horns gives it strength in the dense forest |
| **Indian Giant Squirrel** | A large, rust-coloured squirrel that leaps between tall trees in the Satpura forests |

> **Interesting fact:** The **Phayre's Langur**, also called the Spectacled Monkey, lives in the forests of Tripura. The pale rings around its eyes look exactly like a pair of spectacles!

## Birds and Their Beaks

A bird's **beak** tells you a lot about its diet. Think of beaks as special tools -- each shaped for a particular job:

| Bird | Beak Shape | What It Eats |
|---|---|---|
| **Sparrow** | Short, thick, cone-shaped | Crushes hard seeds and grains |
| **Eagle** | Sharp, hooked, with strong curved claws | Tears flesh of mice, rabbits and fish |
| **Sunbird** | Long, slender, slightly curved | Reaches deep into flowers to sip nectar |
| **Hornbill** | Large, heavy, with a helmet-like casque on top | Picks fruits and catches small animals |
| **Peacock** | Small, pointed beak | Pecks at grains, worms and small insects on the ground |
| **Owl** | Short, hooked beak with excellent night vision | Catches mice and rats in the dark |

Just by looking at a bird's beak, you can guess its favourite food!

## Animals Near Water

Ponds, rivers and lakes are buzzing with life. The creatures that live here have special adaptations for a watery world:

| Animal | How It Moves | Special Body Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Fish** | Swims smoothly | Fins steer it, streamlined shape cuts through water |
| **Frog** | Hops on land, kicks through water | Strong back legs, moist skin for breathing |
| **Turtle** | Walks on land, paddles in water | Hard shell for protection, webbed feet for swimming |
| **Crocodile** | Glides and lunges in water | Powerful tail for swimming, strong jaws for gripping prey |
| **Crab** | Scuttles sideways | Tough outer shell, two strong pincers for defence |

> The **gharial**, found in Indian rivers, has an unusually long and narrow snout perfectly suited for snapping up slippery fish.

## Tiny Creatures -- Insects

**Insects** are everywhere -- in gardens, on walls, under leaves, even inside your house! Despite being small, they have some features in common:

- **Six legs** (three pairs) -- every insect has exactly six legs.
- **One pair of antennae** -- thin feelers that sense smell, touch and direction.
- **Wings** on most species -- butterflies, beetles, dragonflies and flies all have wings, though some insects like ants usually do not fly.

**Some insects you might spot in your garden:** ants marching in a line, a ladybug sitting on a rose leaf, a grasshopper leaping through the grass, a dragonfly hovering near a puddle, and a busy bee visiting flower after flower.

## Leaves and Their Features

Pick up two different leaves from your garden. Are they the same shape? The same colour? Probably not! Leaves vary in many ways:

- **Colour** -- Usually green, but sometimes purple, red, or even spotted.
- **Shape** -- Heart-shaped (peepal), long and thin (grass), fan-like (palm), oval (mango).
- **Texture** -- Smooth and waxy (banana leaf), rough (sandpaper-like teak leaf), hairy (tomato leaf).
- **Smell** -- Crush a curry leaf or a pudina leaf between your fingers -- the strong aroma comes from natural oils.
- **Veins** -- The thin lines running through a leaf are called veins. They carry water and nutrition, just like pipes carrying water through a building.

> **Try this at home:** Place a leaf vein-side up on a table. Lay a sheet of white paper on top. Rub a crayon sideways across the paper. The vein pattern will appear like a beautiful print!

## The Web of Life

Every living thing in nature is linked to others in an invisible chain. This is called the **web of life**.

- **Trees** produce fruits, seeds and oxygen. They give shade and shelter to birds and animals.
- **Birds** eat insects that might otherwise harm crops, and they carry seeds to new places in their droppings.
- **Rivers and ponds** provide drinking water to deer, elephants and countless smaller creatures.
- **Earthworms** tunnel through soil, mixing it and making it soft and fertile so plants can grow strong roots.

Now imagine if all the trees in a forest were chopped down. The birds would lose their nests, the animals would lose their food, the soil would dry out, and the rivers would shrink. One missing link can shake the whole web. That is why **protecting nature** is so important.

## State Symbols

Every Indian state proudly has its own **state animal, state bird and state tree**:

| State | State Animal | State Bird |
|---|---|---|
| Madhya Pradesh | Barasingha (swamp deer) | Indian paradise flycatcher |
| Tripura | Phayre's Langur (Spectacled Monkey) | Green imperial pigeon |
| Rajasthan | Chinkara (Indian gazelle) | Great Indian bustard |

These symbols celebrate the unique wildlife found in each region of India.

## Key Points to Remember

- A **nature trail** is a walk through a forest or natural area to observe plants and animals up close.
- Animals have **special body features** -- trunks, claws, fins, shells -- that help them survive.
- A bird's **beak shape** reveals what it eats -- seed-cracking, meat-tearing, or nectar-sipping.
- **Insects** always have six legs and one pair of antennae; most can fly.
- **Leaves** differ in colour, shape, texture, smell and vein patterns.
- The **web of life** connects every living thing -- harming one part affects the whole chain.
- Always follow **safety rules** during forest visits to protect yourself and nature.
