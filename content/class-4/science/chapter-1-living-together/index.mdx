## What is a Community?

Imagine you are standing on the terrace of your house and looking around. You can see rooftops of other homes, a grocery shop at the corner, children playing in the lane, and maybe an auto-rickshaw passing by. All these people and places together make up your **community**.

A community is a group of people who live in the same area, share common spaces, and look out for each other. Whether it is a small village in Rajasthan or a busy lane in Kolkata, every community has certain places that people depend on -- **schools** where children learn, **hospitals** where the sick get treated, **markets** where families buy vegetables and grains, and **parks** where people walk and relax.

> Picture a neighbourhood without a single medical shop or clinic. If someone falls ill at night, the family would have to travel far for help. That is why each place in a community is like a piece of a jigsaw puzzle -- take one away and the picture is incomplete.

---

## Public Places and Why They Matter

Some places belong to everyone. The **garden** next to the bus stand, the **library** near the school, the **playground** behind the temple, the **post office** on the main road -- these are called **public places**. Anyone can walk in and use them.

Because these places belong to all of us, it is our duty to keep them neat and tidy. Imagine if everyone threw wrappers in the park -- soon no one would want to sit there! Simple habits like putting waste in dustbins, not scribbling on walls, and handling shared equipment gently make a big difference.

## Van Mahotsav -- A Festival for Planting Trees

Every year, from **1st July to 7th July**, India celebrates **Van Mahotsav** -- the **Festival of Forests**. It falls during the monsoon, when rain-soaked soil is perfect for young saplings to take root.

Here is how a school in a small town near Bhopal celebrated Van Mahotsav:

| What They Did | Who Pitched In |
|---|---|
| Swept the school ground clean | Students of Class 4 and Class 5 |
| Dug small pits for saplings | The school gardener and a few parents |
| Brought neem and mango saplings | A local nursery owner donated them |
| Watered the saplings after planting | Every child carried a small bottle of water |
| Put name tags on each sapling | The art teacher made tags with students |

When everyone shares the work, even a big task feels light and joyful.

## Helping Hands in the Community

Behind the smooth running of any neighbourhood are people who work hard every single day. Let us meet some of them:

| Helper | Their Work | Tools They Rely On |
|---|---|---|
| **Mason** | Builds walls and repairs cracks in buildings | Trowel, plumb line |
| **Carpenter** | Crafts doors, windows and wooden furniture | Saw, chisel, hammer |
| **Electrician** | Sets up wiring, fixes fans, lights and switches | Pliers, wire cutter, tester |
| **Gardener** | Looks after plants, trims hedges, waters lawns | Spade, hose pipe, pruning shears |
| **Sanitation Worker** | Clears garbage and keeps roads clean | Broom, dustpan, cart |

Next time you see a freshly repaired road or a brightly lit street lamp, remember -- someone worked hard to make that happen.

## Working Together as a Team

Think about your school. The **principal** plans activities, **teachers** guide lessons, **bus drivers** bring you safely to school, **kitchen staff** prepare your mid-day meal, and the **watchman** keeps the gate secure. Remove any one person and the day does not go smoothly. That is the beauty of teamwork -- everyone has a role.

> Nature teaches the same lesson. Watch a line of **ants** marching towards a piece of sugar. Each ant carries a tiny share. Alone, one ant cannot lift a grain of rice. Together, they carry pieces much bigger than themselves! **Honeybees** work the same way -- some gather nectar, some guard the hive, some fan their wings to keep it cool.

## Community Celebrations and Togetherness

Nothing brings neighbours closer than celebrating together. Across India, different communities have their own special gatherings:

- **Lohri** in Punjab -- On a cold January night, families gather around a bonfire, toss peanuts and popcorn into the flames, and sing folk songs together.
- **Onam** in Kerala -- Neighbours join hands to lay a beautiful flower carpet called **Pookalam** in front of their homes and share a grand feast served on banana leaves.
- **Chhath Puja** in Bihar and Jharkhand -- Entire families stand together in rivers and ponds at sunrise and sunset, offering prayers to the Sun god.

Festivals remind us that joy grows bigger when it is shared.

## Sharing a Meal

After a community event -- whether it is tree planting, a clean-up drive, or a local festival -- people often sit together and share food. In many villages, women cook a big pot of **khichdi** or **dal-chawal** using rice and lentils brought by different families. Everyone eats together, sitting in rows, often on **banana leaves** or steel plates.

> **Fun fact:** Used banana leaves can be buried in the soil. Over time, they break down and turn into **natural compost**, which makes the earth richer for growing plants. So even the plate goes back to nature!

## A Real-Life Example of Teamwork

In **Punsari**, a small village in Gujarat, the villagers decided to solve their own problems instead of waiting for outside help. Together, they set up **CCTV cameras** for safety, installed **solar street lights**, started a **village bus service** for children, and even built a **water purification plant**. Today, Punsari is known across India as a model village -- all because its people worked as one team.

This shows us that when a community joins hands, ordinary people can do extraordinary things.

## Key Points to Remember

- A **community** is a group of people sharing the same neighbourhood, resources and experiences.
- **Public places** like parks, libraries and bus stops are for everyone -- we must keep them clean.
- **Van Mahotsav** (1-7 July) is India's tree-planting festival, held during the monsoon season.
- **Community helpers** such as masons, carpenters, electricians, gardeners and sanitation workers keep our neighbourhood running.
- **Teamwork** -- in schools, families, or villages -- makes every task easier and more enjoyable.
- **Festivals and celebrations** bring people together and strengthen bonds.
- We should always **respect nature** and take care of our surroundings.
