## The Sky Changes Through the Day

Step outside early tomorrow morning and look up. The sky will be painted in soft shades of orange and pink as the Sun peeks over the horizon. By lunchtime, the same sky will be a bright, dazzling blue. In the evening, as the Sun dips down, the sky turns orange and red again, like a beautiful rangoli. And at night? A dark velvet canvas sprinkled with glowing stars and the glowing Moon.

| Time of Day | How the Sky Looks |
|---|---|
| **Early morning** | Soft orange and pink as the Sun rises in the east |
| **Midday** | Bright blue with strong, warm sunlight overhead |
| **Evening** | Deep orange and red as the Sun sets in the west |
| **Night** | Dark sky lit by the Moon and twinkling stars |

Nature follows this rhythm too. Birds wake up chirping at dawn, fly out to hunt for seeds and insects, and return to their nests at dusk. Flowers like the sunflower turn to face the Sun as it crosses the sky. Even we humans follow this pattern -- working and playing during daylight, and resting when darkness falls.

---

## The Sun -- The Brightest Object in the Sky

The **Sun** is a gigantic ball of fire and light. It is so incredibly bright that during the daytime, you cannot see any other star in the sky -- the Sun's light drowns them all out. Without the Sun, there would be no light, no warmth and no life on Earth.

**How the Sun appears to travel across the sky:**

1. In the morning, the Sun appears to **rise in the East**.
2. Through the morning, it climbs **higher and higher** in the sky.
3. Around **noon**, it is almost directly above your head.
4. In the afternoon, it moves **lower** towards the **West**.
5. In the evening, it **sets in the West**, and darkness begins.

> Here is a surprising fact: the Sun is not actually moving around the Earth! It is our **Earth** that is spinning like a giant top. As the Earth rotates, different parts face the Sun at different times, creating day and night. But from where we stand, it looks like the Sun is moving across the sky.

## Shadows

Stand in the sunlight and look at the ground. You will see a dark shape that looks exactly like you -- that is your **shadow**! A shadow forms when your body blocks the sunlight from reaching the ground behind you.

### How Shadows Change During the Day

| Time of Day | Where Is the Sun? | Your Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning** | Low in the eastern sky | Long shadow stretching towards the west |
| **Noon** | Almost directly overhead | Very short shadow right under your feet |
| **Evening** | Low in the western sky | Long shadow stretching towards the east |

**The rules of shadows:**

- When the Sun is **low** in the sky (morning and evening), it sends light at a slant, creating **long** shadows.
- When the Sun is **high** overhead (noon), light comes straight down, making shadows **very short**.
- Your shadow always points in the **opposite direction** from where the Sun is.

## Experimenting with Shadows

You can play with shadows using a **torch** and different objects in a dark room:

- Hold an object **close** to the torch -- the shadow on the wall will be **large**.
- Move the object **far** from the torch -- the shadow becomes **smaller**.
- Move the torch to a different angle -- the shadow shifts direction too.

> **Shadow puppetry** is an ancient art practised in many parts of India. Artists cut out shapes of characters from leather or cardboard and hold them between a bright lamp and a white cloth screen. By moving the figures, they tell wonderful stories entirely through dancing shadows!

## The Sundial -- Telling Time with Shadows

Thousands of years ago, people noticed that shadows move and change length throughout the day in a predictable pattern. They used this clever observation to invent the **sundial** -- a device that tells the time by reading the position of a shadow cast by the Sun.

> **Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh** of Jaipur was fascinated by the sky. He built enormous stone instruments called **Jantar Mantar** in five Indian cities: Jaipur, Ujjain, Delhi, Varanasi and Mathura. The biggest instrument there is the **Samrat Yantra**, a massive sundial that can tell the time with remarkable accuracy just by reading the shadow of the Sun!

## The Night Sky -- Stars

When the Sun goes down and the sky turns dark, thousands of tiny lights appear above you -- **stars**! Stars are actually enormous balls of burning gas, just like our Sun. They appear tiny only because they are incredibly far away from Earth.

If you gaze at the night sky carefully, you will notice that some stars form recognisable **patterns**. One famous pattern is called **Saptarshi** (the Seven Sages) -- it looks like a large ladle or serving spoon hanging in the sky. It is made up of seven bright stars arranged in a distinctive shape.

> On a clear night away from city lights, try lying on a charpai and looking up at the sky. See how many star patterns you can spot!

## The Moon

After the Sun, the **Moon** is the brightest object we see in the sky. But here is something fascinating -- the Moon does not produce any light of its own! It shines because it acts like a mirror, reflecting the **light of the Sun** back towards us.

### Changing Shapes of the Moon

Have you noticed that the Moon does not look the same every night? Sometimes it is a perfect glowing circle, sometimes it is a thin curved sliver, and sometimes you cannot see it at all! These changing appearances are called **phases of the Moon**:

| Phase | How It Looks |
|---|---|
| **Poornima** (Full Moon) | A bright, perfectly round circle lighting up the entire sky |
| **Half Moon** | Exactly half the circle is bright, the other half is dark |
| **Crescent Moon** | A thin, curved sliver shaped like the letter "C" |
| **Amavasya** (New Moon) | The Moon is completely hidden and the night sky is at its darkest |

The Moon goes through a complete cycle from Full Moon to New Moon and back again in about **one month**. After the New Moon, a thin crescent appears and grows a little bigger each night until it becomes a Full Moon. Then it slowly shrinks again until the next New Moon.

![Phases of the Moon: New Moon, Crescent, Half Moon, and Full Moon in sequence](moon-phases.png)

> People in ancient times had no clocks or calendars. They observed the **changing shape of the Moon** to count days and track the passage of time. This is how the very first **lunar calendars** were born!

## Festivals Based on the Sun and Moon

Our ancestors watched the sky so carefully that many of India's most beloved festivals are timed to match the position of the Sun or the phase of the Moon:

| Festival | Its Connection to the Sky |
|---|---|
| **Diwali** | Falls on Amavasya (New Moon night) -- the darkest night, so we fill it with diyas and lights |
| **Rakshabandhan** | Celebrated on Poornima (Full Moon night) when the Moon shines brightest |
| **Guru Poornima** | Honours teachers on a Full Moon night |
| **Buddha Poornima** | Marks the birth of Lord Buddha on a Full Moon night |
| **Eid** | Begins when the thin crescent Moon is first spotted after the New Moon |
| **Chhath Puja** | Devoted to the Sun god -- people offer prayers to the rising and setting Sun |
| **Karva Chauth** | Women fast all day and break their fast only after sighting the Moon at night |
| **Pongal** | A harvest festival thanking the Sun for its warmth and energy |

## India's Chandrayaan Mission

For thousands of years, people could only gaze at the Moon from afar and wonder. But on **23 August 2023**, India made history! The **Vikram Lander**, part of the **Chandrayaan-3** mission by ISRO, touched down gently on the surface of the Moon. It carried a small rover that rolled out and studied the Moon's soil and rocks. India became the **fourth country** in the world to successfully land on the Moon -- and the **first** to land near the Moon's mysterious south pole!

## Key Points to Remember

- The **sky changes colour** throughout the day -- pink in the morning, blue at noon, orange in the evening, dark at night.
- The **Sun** appears to rise in the **East** and set in the **West**, but it is actually the Earth that rotates.
- **Shadows** are long in the morning and evening (when the Sun is low) and short at noon (when the Sun is overhead).
- A shadow always points in the **opposite direction** from the Sun.
- The **sundial** is an ancient device that tells time by reading the Sun's shadow. The famous Samrat Yantra at Jantar Mantar is a giant sundial.
- **Stars** form patterns in the night sky. The **Saptarshi** pattern looks like a large ladle.
- The **Moon** reflects sunlight and changes shape over a month -- from Poornima (Full Moon) to Amavasya (New Moon).
- Many Indian festivals like **Diwali, Eid and Chhath Puja** are connected to the phases of the Sun and Moon.
- India's **Chandrayaan-3** mission landed near the Moon's south pole on 23 August 2023, a proud achievement for the nation.
