## The Palash Tree in the Park

**Child:** Maa, today on the way to school I saw a tree covered in bright red-orange flowers. It looked like the tree was on fire! What tree is that?

**Mother:** That is the Palash tree, beta! People call it the "Flame of the Forest" because when all its flowers bloom together, the whole tree looks like it is glowing with flames.

**Child:** Flame of the Forest! That is such a cool name. Does it have other names too?

**Mother:** Oh yes! In Gujarat they call it Kesuda, in Bengal it is Palash, and in many Hindi areas people say Dhak. India is so big that the same tree gets different names in different places.

**Child:** Like how I call you Maa, but my friend Aditya calls his mother Amma, and my friend Fatima says Ammi!

**Mother:** Ha ha, exactly! Different words, same love. And the Palash is the same beautiful tree, no matter what you call it.

## Nani's Home Remedies

**Child:** Maa, I have a scratchy throat today. It hurts a little when I swallow.

**Mother:** Let me make you tulsi tea. I will boil a few fresh tulsi leaves with ginger and a spoonful of honey. Tulsi has been used for sore throats and colds in Indian homes for hundreds of years.

**Child:** Does it really work? Why don't we just take a tablet?

**Mother:** Sometimes a tablet is needed, and then we go to the doctor. But for small problems, plants like tulsi can help gently. Your Nani used to rub haldi paste on our cuts and scrapes. Turmeric has something in it that helps wounds heal faster.

**Child:** What about neem? Our teacher said neem oil keeps mosquitoes away.

**Mother:** That is true! Neem leaves are also put inside grain storage pots to keep insects out. And aloe vera gel from the leaf is wonderful for skin -- if you get a sunburn, rubbing a little aloe vera cools it right down.

**Child:** So our garden is like a mini hospital!

**Mother:** In a way, yes! Our ancestors figured out which plants help with what, and they passed that knowledge down through families. That is why we call it traditional knowledge.

## Storing Grains Without Chemicals

**Child:** Maa, how did people keep their rice and wheat safe before refrigerators and plastic containers were invented?

**Mother:** Very cleverly! In many villages, people stored grains in big earthen pots. They would line the inside of the pot with dried neem leaves. The bitter smell of neem keeps insects and beetles far away.

**Child:** Just neem leaves? That is so simple!

**Mother:** Simple but brilliant. In some tribal areas, people weave big baskets from bamboo and coat the outside with cow dung paste. When it dries, it becomes hard like a wall and seals the basket tightly.

**Child:** Cow dung? That sounds strange!

**Mother:** It may sound strange, but cow dung has been used in India for centuries. It hardens well, keeps moisture out, and even has mild germ-fighting properties. In Uttarakhand, people use a dried bottle gourd called Tumri to store small amounts of grain. Nature provides everything we need if we look carefully.

**Child:** No factories, no chemicals -- just natural things. That is smart!

## Solar Panels on Rooftops

**Child:** Maa, I saw shiny blue panels on the roof of the new building near our school. What are those?

**Mother:** Those are solar panels! They catch sunlight and turn it into electricity. Some villages in India now run entirely on solar power -- their fans, lights, televisions, even water pumps work on energy from the Sun.

**Child:** But what happens at night when there is no Sun?

**Mother:** Good thinking! Solar panels charge big batteries during the day. At night, the stored energy in those batteries keeps everything running.

**Child:** That is like charging a phone! And does solar power create smoke or pollution?

**Mother:** Not at all. That is the best part. Unlike burning wood or coal, solar energy is completely clean. No smoke, no ash, no dirty air. The Sun gives us light, warmth and now electricity too. It really is nature's greatest gift!

**Child:** I wish every house had solar panels.

**Mother:** Many people are starting to use them. Maybe one day our house will have them too!
