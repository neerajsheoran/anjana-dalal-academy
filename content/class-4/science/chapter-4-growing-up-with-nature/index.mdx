## Nature and Our Lives

In India, people have lived side by side with nature for thousands of years. The cotton in your clothes comes from a plant. The turmeric in your dal comes from a root. The wooden desk you study on was once a living tree. From festivals to food, from art to medicine -- nature is woven into every part of Indian life. In this chapter, we explore how villages in India use the gifts of nature wisely.

---

## The Flame of the Forest

Among India's most striking trees is the **Palash**. When spring arrives, its branches burst into clusters of **bright orange-red flowers**, turning the landscape into a sea of fire. This is why the Palash is called the **"Flame of the Forest"**.

In Gujarat, the same tree goes by the name **Kesuda**. In Bengal it is called **Palash**, in Maharashtra **Palas**, and in Hindi-speaking areas **Dhak**. One tree, many names -- that is the beauty of India's diversity.

![The Palash tree with bright orange-red flowers, also known as the Flame of the Forest](palash-flame-of-forest.png)

> India is a land of flowers -- golden marigolds decorate weddings, white jasmine scents the evening air, pink lotus floats in temple ponds, and red hibiscus is offered in prayers.

## Houses Made from Nature

Walk through a village in Madhya Pradesh or Chhattisgarh and you will see houses built with **mud walls**, **thatched roofs** made of dried grass, and floors smoothed with a mix of **cow dung and clay**. These natural materials keep the house pleasantly cool in the scorching summer and comfortably warm during chilly winters -- no air conditioner needed!

Village walls are often decorated with stunning paintings. The colours for these paintings come straight from nature:

- **White paint** from rice flour mixed with water
- **Red and yellow tones** from powdered coloured stones
- **Green and purple shades** from leaf and flower extracts

> **Gond art** is a centuries-old tradition from the tribal communities of central India. Gond artists use fine dots and lines in natural colours to paint images of peacocks, fish, trees and village scenes. Their walls become living canvases that tell stories of nature.

![A Gond art painting showing trees, animals and birds in colourful natural patterns](gond-art-painting.png)

## Making Natural Dyes

Want to try a science experiment at home? Make your own **natural dye**!

1. **Gather** colourful plant parts -- beetroot slices, dried marigold petals, or crushed hibiscus flowers.
2. **Simmer** them in one or two litres of water on low heat for about 45 minutes. The water will slowly absorb the colour.
3. **Filter** the liquid through a cloth strainer to remove all plant bits. The coloured water is your dye!
4. **Dip** a white cotton handkerchief into the dye and leave it soaking overnight.
5. **Wring out** the cloth and hang it to dry in the morning shade.

People in Indian villages have coloured their fabrics this way for centuries -- safe, beautiful, and completely chemical-free.

## Storing Grains the Traditional Way

After a good harvest, farmers need to store grains safely for months. In Indian villages, clever traditional methods keep grains dry and pest-free without any factory-made chemicals:

- **Earthen pots** are lined with dried **neem leaves** on the inside. Neem has a natural bitterness that drives insects away.
- **Bamboo baskets** are sealed on the outside with a thin layer of **cow dung paste**, which hardens into a protective coat.

| Container | Region | What It Is |
|---|---|---|
| **Tumri** | Uttarakhand | A hollow, dried bottle gourd used to store small amounts of grain |
| **Earthen pot** | Many states | A clay pot lined inside with neem leaves |
| **Bamboo basket** | Tribal regions | A woven basket coated outside with cow dung |

These time-tested methods show us that nature often provides the simplest and best solutions.

## The Jenu Kuruba Tribe

Deep in the forests of **Karnataka** lives a tribe called the **Jenu Kurubas**. The word **"Jenu"** means **honey** in the local language, and honey collection is their age-old skill.

> Here is what makes them truly special -- while collecting honey from wild beehives high up in the trees, they **sing gentle songs to calm the bees and ask for forgiveness** for taking their honey. This beautiful practice shows their deep bond with nature and their belief that all creatures deserve respect.

## Medicinal Plants

Your kitchen and garden are like a small pharmacy! Many common Indian plants have **healing powers** that families have trusted for generations:

| Plant | Part Used | How It Helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Tulsi** (holy basil) | Fresh leaves | Soothes sore throat, eases cough and cold |
| **Neem** | Leaves, bark, oil | Fights skin infections, repels mosquitoes naturally |
| **Ajwain** (carom seeds) | Dried seeds | Relieves stomach pain and gas |
| **Haldi** (turmeric) | Root powder | Heals cuts and scrapes, reduces swelling |
| **Aloe vera** | Inner leaf gel | Moisturises skin, soothes sunburn |

A dab of **neem oil** on exposed skin works as a natural mosquito repellent -- no chemical spray needed!

## First-Aid Box

Accidents can happen anywhere -- at home, in school, or on the playground. A well-stocked **first-aid box** helps you handle small injuries right away before seeing a doctor.

**What to keep inside:** a roll of bandage, cotton wool, a small bottle of antiseptic liquid, adhesive plasters (band-aids), a pair of scissors, a tube of pain-relief ointment, mosquito repellent cream, and a small torch for checking wounds.

## Solar Energy in Villages

Sunlight is free, clean and available every day. Across India, more and more villages are tapping into **solar energy**. Solar panels placed on rooftops capture sunlight and convert it into electricity that runs fans, lights, televisions and even water pumps.

Unlike coal or wood, solar energy produces **no smoke, no ash and no pollution**. It is one of the best gifts nature has given us -- and it never runs out!

## Festivals That Honour Nature

Indian culture has always celebrated the trees, rivers and animals around us. Here are some festivals that honour nature:

| Festival | What It Celebrates |
|---|---|
| **Vat Purnima** | Women worship the banyan tree, tying sacred threads around its trunk |
| **Hari Jiroti** | Communities plant fruit-bearing saplings together |
| **Kaziranga Elephant Festival** | Spreads awareness about protecting elephants in Assam |
| **Van Mahotsav** | Nationwide tree-planting week in July |
| **Pongal** | Tamil harvest festival thanking the Sun, earth and farm animals |

> **Sacred Groves** are patches of forest that village communities guard carefully. No one is allowed to cut trees or hunt animals inside them. Villagers gather in sacred groves to pray, sing and celebrate nature together.

## How to Care for Nature

You do not need to be a grown-up to make a difference. Here are simple steps every child can take:

- **Plant a sapling** in your colony or school and water it regularly.
- **Put out a bowl of water** on your terrace for birds, especially during hot summers.
- **Carry a cloth bag** instead of taking plastic bags from shops.
- **Use both sides** of your notebook paper to save trees.
- **Join or start** a clean-up drive in your neighbourhood with friends and cousins.
- **Talk to others** about why protecting plants and animals matters.

## Key Points to Remember

- Nature and Indian culture are deeply intertwined -- from housing materials to art to medicine.
- The **Palash** tree, called the "Flame of the Forest", lights up the landscape with its fiery flowers.
- Village homes use **natural materials** like mud, thatch, and cow dung, and are decorated with traditional **Gond art**.
- Grains can be stored safely in **earthen pots with neem leaves** and **bamboo baskets coated with cow dung**.
- Plants like **tulsi, neem, haldi and aloe vera** have medicinal properties used for centuries.
- **Solar panels** convert sunlight into clean electricity with no pollution.
- Indian festivals such as **Vat Purnima, Van Mahotsav and Pongal** celebrate our bond with nature.
