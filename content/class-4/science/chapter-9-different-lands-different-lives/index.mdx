## India -- A Land of Many Landscapes

If you travel from one end of India to the other, you will see landscapes changing completely -- vast green fields, golden sand dunes, blue ocean beaches and snow-topped mountain peaks. India has four major types of **landforms**: plains, deserts, coastal areas and mountains. The land, weather and water of each place shape **everything** about how people live there -- what they eat, what they wear, the houses they build and the festivals they celebrate.

---

## Life in the Plains -- Amritsar, Punjab

The **plains** of India are large stretches of **flat, fertile land**. Rivers flowing through the plains deposit rich soil over thousands of years, making this land perfect for growing crops. Punjab, in northern India, is one of the country's most important farming regions.

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Land** | Flat and covered with rich, fertile soil |
| **Main work** | Farming -- wheat, rice, corn and mustard are major crops |
| **Famous food** | Makki di roti (cornmeal flatbread) with sarson da saag (spiced mustard greens), tall glasses of frothy lassi |
| **Animals** | Cows and buffaloes with shorter fur suited to the warm weather |
| **Clothing** | Men wear colourful turbans called pagris; women wear bright salwar kameez |

> The **Golden Temple** (Sri Harmandir Sahib) in Amritsar is covered in real gold and sits in the middle of a shimmering pool called the **sarovar**. Inside the temple complex, a **langar** (community kitchen) runs round the clock, serving free meals to every visitor -- rich or poor, young or old. It feeds over 50,000 people every single day, making it one of the biggest community kitchens on Earth!

## Life in the Desert -- Rajasthan

A **desert** is a land that receives very **little rain**. Rajasthan is home to the **Thar Desert**, where summers are scorching hot, nights can be surprisingly cold, and water is very precious.

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Land** | Vast stretches of golden sand with rolling dunes |
| **Plants** | Cactus, khejri tree and babool -- all survive on almost no water |
| **Animals** | Camels, desert lizards, wild cats, pythons and golden eagles |
| **Famous food** | Dal-bati-churma (baked wheat balls with lentils and sweet crumble), ker sangri (a tangy desert vegetable dish) |
| **Clothing** | Women wear bright ghaghras (long skirts) with bandhini dupattas; men wear turbans to protect from sun and sand |
| **Music** | Folk instruments like khartal (wooden clappers) and sarangi (stringed instrument) |
| **Houses** | Built with mud and sand, topped with thatched roofs |

**How desert people and nature adapt:**
- The **camel** is nicknamed the "Ship of the Desert" because it can walk long distances across hot sand without drinking water for days. Its wide, flat feet stop it from sinking into the sand.
- **Cactus** plants store water inside their thick, juicy stems, surviving months of no rain.
- Families dig underground water tanks called **tankas** to collect and store precious rainwater during the monsoon.
- Women decorate their homes and clothes with mirror work, colourful embroidery and glass bangles.

> **Jaisalmer Fort** rises from the desert like a golden castle. It is made entirely of yellow **sandstone**, which glows in the sunlight. It is one of the very few forts in the world where families still live inside its walls! The famous filmmaker **Satyajit Ray** set his detective story "Sonar Kella" (The Golden Fortress) in this magical fort.

## Life on the Coast -- Puri, Odisha

**Coastal areas** are regions where the land meets the sea. Puri, in **Odisha**, is a vibrant coastal city famous for its long sandy beaches, ancient temples and delicious cuisine.

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Land** | Sandy beaches stretching along the Bay of Bengal |
| **Main work** | Fishing -- fishermen head out to sea in boats before dawn |
| **Plants** | Tall coconut palms and fan-shaped palm trees swaying in the sea breeze |
| **Famous food** | Dalma (lentils cooked with vegetables), pakhala (fermented rice in cool water), chhena poda (baked cheese dessert), rasagola |
| **Art and craft** | Stunning sand art sculptures on the beach, beautiful Sambalpuri saree weaving |
| **Sea creatures** | Olive Ridley turtles, crabs, dolphins, starfish and colourful coral |

**Life shaped by the sea:**
- Many families earn their living by **fishing**. Fishermen row out in wooden boats before sunrise and return with baskets of fresh fish.
- Children collect beautiful **sea shells** on the beach. The **shankh** (conch shell) is considered sacred and is blown during temple prayers.
- Artists create breathtaking **sand sculptures** on Puri's beaches, visited by tourists from around the world.

> The **Shree Jagannath Temple** in Puri is one of India's most sacred places. Every year, the grand **Rath Yatra** is held here, where massive, brilliantly decorated chariots carrying the idols of Lord Jagannath, Devi Subhadra and Lord Balabhadra are pulled through the streets by thousands of devotees.

### Challenges in Coastal Regions

Living near the sea has its dangers. Sometimes, powerful storms called **cyclones** form over the ocean and hit the coast. Cyclones bring violent winds, heavy rainfall and flooding that can destroy homes, uproot trees and damage crops.

> **Odisha** has become a world leader in protecting its people from cyclones. The state uses **early warning systems**, evacuates people before the storm hits, and builds strong shelters. It has won praise from countries around the world for saving lives during severe cyclones.

## Life in the Mountains -- Sikkim

**Mountains** are towering landforms made of rock and earth that rise steeply above the surrounding land. High up in the mountains, the air is thin and cold, and many peaks are covered in sparkling white snow. Sikkim, a small and beautiful state in north-east India, sits right in the lap of the Himalayas.

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Land** | Steep mountains, deep valleys and rushing streams |
| **Climate** | Cold and crisp with clean, fresh air |
| **Famous peak** | Mount Kangchenjunga -- the third tallest mountain in the entire world |
| **Trees** | Oak, pine, walnut and chestnut trees cover the hillsides |
| **Animals** | The sturdy **yak** is used to carry heavy loads up mountain paths |
| **Famous food** | Thukpa (a steaming hot noodle soup perfect for cold days), sel roti (sweet ring-shaped fried bread) |
| **Traditional clothes** | Bakhu (a long robe), dumdyam, gunyo cholo |
| **Houses** | Wooden houses with steep, slanting roofs |

**Why do mountain houses have slanting roofs?** Because rain and snow slide off a slanted surface easily. If roofs were flat, heavy snow would pile up and could cause the roof to cave in!

![Comparing four Indian landforms: plains, desert, coast and mountains with their features](four-landforms-comparison.png)

> **Loosong and Namsoong** is one of Sikkim's most joyful festivals. It marks the beginning of the **Sikkimese New Year** and honours the hard work of farmers. People feast on local delicacies, compete in traditional games, and watch a spectacular mask dance called **chaam**, where dancers wear colourful masks and costumes.

### Challenges in Mountain Regions

Heavy rain during the monsoon can loosen the soil on steep slopes, causing **landslides** -- when rocks, mud and earth suddenly slide downhill. Mountain communities stay alert during the rains and work together to keep everyone safe.

## Comparing Life Across Landforms

| Category | Plains | Desert | Coastal | Mountains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weather** | Moderate -- not too hot, not too cold | Extremely hot days, cool nights | Warm and humid from the sea | Cold, sometimes snowy |
| **Trees** | Mango, neem, peepal | Cactus, khejri, babool | Coconut palm, palm trees | Oak, pine, walnut |
| **Animals** | Cows, buffaloes | Camels, desert lizards | Fish, turtles, crabs | Yak |
| **Houses** | Brick and cement buildings | Mud walls with thatched roofs | Concrete houses near the shore | Wooden houses with slanting roofs |
| **Food** | Roti, rice, dal, lassi | Dal-bati-churma, ker sangri | Fish, rice, dalma, pakhala | Thukpa, sel roti |

## Key Points to Remember

- India has four major types of landforms: **plains, deserts, coastal areas and mountains**.
- The **land and weather** of a place shape the food, clothing, houses, plants and animals found there.
- **Plains** like Punjab have rich soil, making them ideal for farming wheat, rice and mustard.
- **Deserts** like Rajasthan are dry and hot. Plants like cactus store water, and people build tankas to collect rain.
- **Coastal regions** like Puri depend on fishing and face cyclones during the monsoon season.
- **Mountains** like Sikkim are cold and steep. Houses have slanting roofs to handle snow and rain.
- Every region of India has its own special **culture, festivals, food and way of life**, making our country incredibly diverse.
