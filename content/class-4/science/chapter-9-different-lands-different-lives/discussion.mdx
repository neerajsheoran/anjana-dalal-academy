## Life in the Plains and Desert

**Child:** Maa, my friend Harpreet is from Punjab. She told me that her Dadaji grows wheat on a huge farm! Why is Punjab so good for farming?

**Mother:** Punjab sits on flat plains, beta. Thousands of years ago, rivers like the Beas and Sutlej deposited rich, fine soil across these plains. This soil is incredibly fertile -- plants love growing in it. That is why Punjab is called the "Granary of India" -- it produces so much wheat and rice that it helps feed the whole country!

**Child:** But my other friend Kavya went to Rajasthan and she said there were only sand dunes and barely any farms. Why is Rajasthan so different?

**Mother:** Because Rajasthan has the Thar Desert. Deserts get very little rain -- sometimes just a few centimetres in a whole year. Without enough water, most crops simply cannot grow. The land there is sandy and dry, completely different from the soft, moist soil of Punjab.

**Child:** Then how do people survive in the desert?

**Mother:** People in Rajasthan are incredibly clever! They dig underground tanks called tankas to store every drop of precious rainwater. They grow special crops that need very little water. And they depend a lot on the camel -- the Ship of the Desert -- which can travel for days without drinking water. Even the plants have adapted. Have you seen a cactus?

**Child:** Yes! It is a funny-looking plant with thorns instead of leaves.

**Mother:** Those thorns are actually modified leaves! Normal leaves lose a lot of water through evaporation, but thorns do not. And the cactus stores water inside its thick stem, like a built-in water bottle. Nature is brilliant at finding solutions!

**Child:** Maa, I also heard that Jaisalmer Fort is made of golden stone. Is that real gold?

**Mother:** No, beta, it is yellow sandstone that glows golden in the sunlight. It is so beautiful that the filmmaker Satyajit Ray set his famous detective story "Sonar Kella" there. And here is something amazing -- people still live inside the fort walls, even today!

## Life on the Coast and in the Mountains

**Child:** Maa, what is it like to live near the sea? I have only seen the beach once when we went to Puri.

**Mother:** Do you remember the tall coconut trees, the sound of the waves, and the fishermen bringing in their boats?

**Child:** Yes! And we ate the most delicious rasagola there!

**Mother:** Life in Puri revolves around the sea. Many families are fishermen who wake up before dawn, row out in wooden boats, and return with baskets full of fish. The sea gives them their food and their livelihood. Children there grow up collecting sea shells, playing on the sand, and watching Olive Ridley turtles come ashore to lay eggs.

**Child:** But my teacher said the coast can be dangerous too. What are cyclones?

**Mother:** A cyclone is a massive, spinning storm that forms over the warm ocean water. When it hits the coast, it brings howling winds, torrential rain and flooding. It can flatten houses and uproot huge trees. But Odisha has become incredibly good at handling cyclones. They use satellites and weather stations to predict the storm days in advance, then evacuate lakhs of people to safe shelters before the cyclone arrives. Other countries come to learn from Odisha!

**Child:** That is brave. What about mountains? My cousin lives in Sikkim and she says it snows there!

**Mother:** Sikkim is in the Himalayas, where the air is cold and fresh. Have you noticed something about the houses in her photos?

**Child:** The roofs look slanted, not flat like ours!

**Mother:** Exactly! Mountain houses have slanting roofs so that snow and rain slide right off. If the roof were flat like ours, heavy snow would pile up and could crush the house. People there eat warm foods like thukpa -- a hot noodle soup -- to fight the cold. And they use yaks, which are like big hairy cows, to carry things up steep mountain paths.

**Child:** Every part of India is so different! Desert people deal with no water, coastal people deal with the sea, and mountain people deal with snow!

**Mother:** And that is what makes India so wonderfully diverse. The land shapes the life, the food, the clothes, the festivals -- everything. Whether it is a farmer in Punjab singing while harvesting wheat, or a fisherman in Puri pulling in his net, or a yak herder in Sikkim walking through misty mountains -- each life is beautiful in its own way.

## India's Four Major Landforms

**Child:** Maa, can you help me remember all four landforms for my test? I keep mixing them up.

**Mother:** Let us make it easy. Close your eyes and imagine you are on a train travelling across India. First, you pass through Punjab -- flat green fields stretching to the horizon in every direction. That is the **plains**.

**Child:** Flat and green, I can see it!

**Mother:** Now the train moves west into Rajasthan. The green disappears. You see golden sand dunes, a camel walking slowly, and a blazing hot sun. That is the **desert**.

**Child:** Sand everywhere!

**Mother:** Next, the train turns south and reaches Puri in Odisha. You hear the roar of ocean waves, see coconut trees bending in the breeze, and smell fresh fish being cooked. That is the **coast**.

**Child:** I love the beach!

**Mother:** Finally, the train chugs north-east into Sikkim. The land rises steeply. You see mountains covered with pine trees, streams rushing down the slopes, and snow-capped peaks touching the clouds. That is the **mountains**.

**Child:** Plains, desert, coast, mountains -- I will never forget now!

**Mother:** And remember, each landform has its own special weather, food, animals and culture. The flat fertile plains grow wheat and rice. The dry desert has camels and cacti. The coast has fishermen and coconut palms. The cold mountains have yaks and warm thukpa. It is like India has four completely different worlds inside one country!

**Child:** India is amazing, Maa!
