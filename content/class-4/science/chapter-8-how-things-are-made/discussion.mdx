## Paper and How It Is Made

**Child:** Maa, where does paper come from? My teacher said it comes from trees, but how can a hard tree become a thin, smooth sheet?

**Mother:** That is a wonderful question, beta! It is actually a long process. First, trees are cut down and the wood is collected. Then, in a big factory, the wood is broken into tiny tiny fibres -- imagine tearing a roti into the smallest crumbs possible. This mushy fibre is called wood pulp.

**Child:** Pulp? Like mango pulp?

**Mother:** Ha ha, not exactly, but the idea is similar -- it is a wet, mushy mixture! The wood pulp is mixed with water and chemicals, then spread out in thin layers. Giant machines press and dry these layers until they become the smooth sheets of paper we use in our notebooks.

**Child:** That sounds like it needs a lot of trees!

**Mother:** You are right. To make one tonne of paper, about 17 to 20 trees are cut down. And it also uses thousands of litres of water and a lot of electricity. That is why we should never waste paper.

**Child:** Did people always have paper?

**Mother:** No! Long ago, people in India wrote on dried palm leaves called talapatra. Some people carved words into stone or wrote on cloth. Paper as we know it was invented much later. And today, scientists are even finding ways to make paper from banana stems and sugarcane waste so that fewer trees are cut.

**Child:** Can we make paper at home, Maa?

**Mother:** Yes! We can recycle old newspapers. You tear them into tiny bits, soak them in water with some methi seeds overnight, mash it all into a paste, spread it flat, and let it dry in the sun. The methi seeds act like natural glue. You will have your own handmade paper! You can even colour it with haldi for yellow or chukandar juice for pink.

**Child:** That sounds like a fun art project! I want to try it this weekend.

## The 5Rs of Waste Management

**Child:** Maa, our teacher taught us about the 5Rs today -- Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repurpose and Recycle. But I am confused about the difference between Reuse and Repurpose. Are they not the same thing?

**Mother:** Good question! They are similar but not the same. When you **reuse** something, you use it for the same purpose again. Like when Priya finishes reading her Class 3 books and gives them to her younger cousin -- the cousin reads the same books. The books are used for the same job -- reading.

**Child:** Okay, so what is repurpose?

**Mother:** When you **repurpose** something, you give it a completely different job. Remember how Dadi took her old cotton saree that she could not wear anymore and cut it into kitchen dusters? The saree was meant for wearing, but now it cleans the kitchen counter. That is repurposing -- giving something old a brand-new purpose.

**Child:** Oh, I get it! Like when Riya ma'am painted an old Bournvita tin and planted tulsi in it. That tin was for keeping Bournvita, but now it is a flower pot!

**Mother:** Exactly! You understood it perfectly. Now tell me, which R comes first and why?

**Child:** Refuse! Because if we say no to things we do not need, like plastic straws or extra paper napkins, then the waste is never created in the first place.

**Mother:** Brilliant! And Reduce means using things carefully -- like writing on both sides of your notebook pages. Recycle is when old materials are sent to a factory to be made into something new, like the raddi-wala collecting old newspapers.

**Child:** So all five Rs help us make less waste and protect the Earth?

**Mother:** That is exactly right, beta. And the best part? You do not need to be a grown-up to follow the 5Rs. Even a child your age can refuse a plastic bag, reuse a water bottle, and give old textbooks to the next batch of students. Every small action counts!

**Child:** I am going to start a "5Rs Challenge" in our house, Maa. Let us see who follows the most Rs in one week!

**Mother:** I love that idea! Count me in.
