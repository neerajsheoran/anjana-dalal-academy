## Things Around Us Are Made of Materials

Pick up your notebook and flip through its pages. Now look at the newspaper on the table, the cardboard box in the corner, the tissue paper near the washbasin. All of these are made from **paper**. But where does paper come from? Believe it or not, paper starts its life as a **tree** standing tall in a forest!

---

## How Paper is Made

Paper is made from the **wood** of trees. Here is how it happens in a paper factory:

1. Trees are chopped down and their wood is collected.
2. The wood is broken and mashed into tiny fibres called **wood pulp**.
3. This pulp is mixed with water and special chemicals to make a thick, soupy mixture.
4. The mixture is spread out in thin layers on flat screens.
5. These wet sheets are pressed and dried using heavy rollers and heat, and they become paper!

Making paper needs a **huge number of trees** and a lot of **water and electricity**. Every time we waste paper, more trees need to be cut, which harms forests, animals and the air we breathe.

![Steps of paper making: from trees to wood pulp to wet sheets to dried paper](paper-making-process.png)

> **Did you know?** Thousands of years ago, there was no paper! People in India wrote on dried **palm leaves** called **talapatra**. In other parts of the world, people used animal skins, bamboo strips and even clay tablets. Today, scientists are finding ways to make paper from **banana stems**, **sugarcane waste** and other plant materials to save trees!

## Making Recycled Paper at Home

Instead of throwing away old newspapers, you can turn them into brand new handmade paper! Here is how:

**What you need:** Old newspapers, fenugreek seeds (methi dana) and water.

**Steps:**

1. **Tear** the old newspapers into tiny bits -- the smaller, the better.
2. **Soak** the torn pieces in a bowl of water. Add a spoonful of methi seeds and leave it overnight. The methi seeds act as natural **glue** that holds the paper together.
3. **Mash** everything into a smooth, thick paste. You can use your hands or a grinder with your parents' help.
4. **Spread** the paste thinly and evenly on a flat surface or a wire mesh.
5. **Let it dry** completely in the sunlight.

Your recycled paper is ready! Use it to make beautiful **greeting cards**, **bookmarks**, **decorative wall hangings** or fun **paper masks**.

## Colouring Paper with Natural Dyes

Want to add colour to your handmade paper? You do not need to buy any paint! Your kitchen has natural ingredients that make lovely dyes:

| Kitchen Ingredient | Colour It Gives |
|---|---|
| **Haldi** (turmeric) | Bright yellow |
| **Palak** (spinach) | Fresh green |
| **Chukandar** (beetroot) | Pink or red |
| **Mehndi** (henna) | Warm brown or orange |

These natural dyes come from different parts of plants -- their leaves, roots, flowers and seeds. They are completely **safe to use** and do not pollute the environment like chemical dyes do.

## Different Types of Paper

Not all paper is the same! Different types of paper are designed for different jobs:

| Type of Paper | What Makes It Special | Where It Is Used |
|---|---|---|
| **Writing paper** | Smooth surface that holds ink well | Notebooks, letters, exam sheets |
| **Newspaper print** | Thin, lightweight and inexpensive | Daily newspapers |
| **Art paper** | Thick, shiny and glossy | Magazines, calendars, posters |
| **Cardboard** | Very thick and rigid | Packing boxes, cartons |
| **Tissue paper** | Ultra-thin, soft and absorbent | Wiping hands, cleaning spills |
| **Chart paper** | Firm and available in many colours | School projects, display boards |

## Braille Paper

Not everyone can read printed letters with their eyes. People who are **visually challenged** (cannot see) use a special system called **Braille** to read and write. Braille paper has tiny **raised dots** arranged in patterns. Each pattern stands for a letter, number or punctuation mark. By running their fingertips over these dots, visually challenged people can read books, exam papers and even menus at restaurants!

## The 5Rs of Waste Management

Every family in India produces waste -- vegetable peels, plastic wrappers, old notebooks, broken toys. If all this waste just piles up, it harms our soil, water and air. The **5Rs** give us a smart plan to deal with waste:

![The 5Rs of waste management: Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repurpose, Recycle shown in a circle](five-rs-waste-management.png)

### 1. Refuse

**Say "No thanks!"** to things you do not really need, especially if they create waste.

*Example:* When the juice seller offers you a plastic straw, refuse it and drink directly from the glass.

### 2. Reduce

**Use less** so that less waste is created in the first place.

*Example:* Write on **both sides** of every page in your notebook instead of leaving one side blank.

### 3. Reuse

**Use an item again and again** instead of throwing it away after one use.

*Example:* Carry the same cloth bag every time your family goes to the vegetable market.

### 4. Repurpose

**Give an old item a brand-new job** -- use your creativity!

*Example:* Turn an old glass jar into a pencil holder for your study table.

### 5. Recycle

**Send old materials to a factory** where they are processed into new products.

*Example:* Collect old newspapers and give them to the raddi-wala. The paper goes to a recycling factory and becomes new paper or cardboard.

## Why Should We Save Paper?

- Saving paper means **fewer trees** get chopped down, and our forests stay green.
- It saves the **enormous amount of water and electricity** used to make paper in factories.
- It **reduces the waste** that ends up in landfills and garbage dumps.
- Forests are home to **thousands of birds, animals and insects** -- protecting trees protects them too.

> Small steps add up. Writing on both sides of a page, carrying a cloth bag, giving old books to younger children, and recycling newspapers -- each of these actions helps protect our planet.

---

## Key Points to Remember

- Paper is made from **trees**, so wasting paper leads to cutting more forests.
- You can make **recycled paper** at home from old newspapers, water and fenugreek seeds.
- Natural dyes from **haldi, palak, chukandar and mehndi** can colour paper safely.
- Different types of paper -- writing paper, cardboard, tissue paper -- serve different purposes.
- **Braille paper** with raised dots helps visually challenged people read using their fingertips.
- The **5Rs** -- Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repurpose, Recycle -- are a smart plan to manage waste.
- Every effort to **save paper** helps save trees, water, energy and wildlife.
