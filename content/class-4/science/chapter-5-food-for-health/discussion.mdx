## The Three Food Groups

**Child:** Maa, today our teacher asked us to sort our lunch into three groups -- energy-giving, body-building and protective. I did not know which food goes where!

**Mother:** It is actually quite simple, beta. Think of it this way. Energy-giving foods are like petrol for a car -- they power you up. Rice, roti, potatoes and bananas all give you energy to run, study and play.

**Child:** Okay, so my roti and rice are energy foods. What about dal?

**Mother:** Dal is a body-building food! It helps your body grow, build muscles and repair itself. Milk, paneer, eggs, chana and rajma are also body-building foods. Think of them as the bricks that build your body taller and stronger.

**Child:** And protective foods?

**Mother:** Those are your bodyguards! Fruits and vegetables like carrots, oranges, spinach and guava protect you from falling ill. They keep your eyes sharp, your skin healthy and your body strong against germs.

**Child:** So a good lunch should have all three -- like my roti with dal and sabzi?

**Mother:** Exactly! That is a balanced meal. If you eat only roti all day, you will have energy but your body will not grow strong or fight diseases well. You need all three groups working together.

## Why Junk Food is a Trap

**Child:** Maa, Rohan brings chips and cola to school every day. He says they taste much better than roti-sabzi.

**Mother:** They might taste good because they are loaded with salt, sugar and oil. Your tongue loves those flavours! But your body does not get any real nutrition from them.

**Child:** What happens if someone eats junk food every day?

**Mother:** Think about it -- one bottle of cola can have ten spoons of sugar in it! That much sugar is terrible for your teeth and your energy levels. You feel a quick burst, then crash and feel tired. Chips are fried in oil that is heated again and again, which makes it harmful. Over time, junk food makes you feel heavy, tired and can make you sick more often.

**Child:** Ten spoons of sugar! That is as much as we put in a whole pot of chai for everyone!

**Mother:** Exactly. And would you drink a glass of pure sugar water? No! But that is basically what a cold drink is. Fresh nimbu paani with a little honey is so much better -- tasty, cooling and healthy.

**Child:** I will tell Rohan to try nimbu paani tomorrow!

## Millets -- Nani's Super Food

**Child:** Maa, our teacher said millets are super foods. What are millets?

**Mother:** Millets are small grains like bajra, jowar and ragi. Your Nani used to eat bajra roti with a big dollop of white butter every winter. It kept her warm and strong.

**Child:** Why did people stop eating millets?

**Mother:** As rice and wheat became easier to buy in shops, people slowly forgot about millets. But now scientists are telling us that millets have more iron, more calcium and more fibre than rice or wheat. They keep your blood healthy, your bones strong and your stomach happy.

**Child:** So Nani was eating super food all along and did not even know it!

**Mother:** She knew very well, beta! Our grandparents ate what grew locally -- jowar in Maharashtra, ragi in Karnataka, bajra in Rajasthan. Each region had its own millet. Now the whole country is learning to go back to these wonderful grains.

**Child:** Can we make ragi dosa this weekend?

**Mother:** What a lovely idea! We will make ragi dosa with coconut chutney. Healthy and delicious!

## Reading the Label

**Child:** Maa, I was looking at the biscuit packet today and saw so many things written on the back. What does it all mean?

**Mother:** That is the food label, and it is very important. Let me show you three things to always check. First, the expiry date. See this line? It says "Best before 15 July 2026." If today's date is past that, we should not eat it because the food may have gone bad.

**Child:** Okay, what is the second thing?

**Mother:** The ingredients list. It tells you what is inside the packet. The first ingredient listed is what the product has the most of. If sugar is listed first, that means the biscuit is mostly sugar!

**Child:** And the third?

**Mother:** Net weight. This tells you how many grams of biscuits are actually in the packet. Sometimes a big packet looks full but has very little food inside and a lot of air!

**Child:** So the label is like a report card for the food!

**Mother:** That is a perfect way to think about it. Always read the label before you eat or buy packaged food. It helps you make smart choices.
