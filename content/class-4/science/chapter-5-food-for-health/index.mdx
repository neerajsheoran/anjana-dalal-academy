## Why Do We Need Food?

Think of your body as a machine that is always working -- your heart beats, your lungs breathe, your brain thinks, and your legs carry you around all day. To keep this machine running, you need **fuel**, and that fuel is **food**. Food gives us the energy to play cricket in the evening, the strength to grow taller, and the power to fight off a cold.

But here is the thing -- no single food item contains everything your body needs. A plate of only rice will give you energy but will not make your bones strong. That is why eating a **mix of different foods** every day is so important.

## The Six Tastes (Shad Rasa)

**Ayurveda**, the ancient Indian science of healthy living, teaches that there are **six tastes** called **Shad Rasa**. A truly balanced meal should touch all six:

| Taste | Hindi Word | Everyday Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweet** | Meetha | Gud (jaggery), banana, honey, boiled rice |
| **Sour** | Khatta | Nimbu (lemon), imli (tamarind), dahi (curd), raw mango |
| **Salty** | Namkeen | Namak (salt), achaar (pickle) |
| **Pungent** | Teekha | Hari mirch (green chilli), adrak (ginger), kali mirch (black pepper) |
| **Bitter** | Kadwa | Karela (bitter gourd), methi (fenugreek leaves), neem |
| **Astringent** | Kasaila | Kachcha kela (raw banana), haldi (turmeric), kachchi aam (raw mango) |

> **Ugadi Pachadi** is a festive dish prepared during the Ugadi new year in Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Telangana. It intentionally mixes all six tastes in one bowl -- jaggery for sweet, raw mango for sour, salt, pepper, neem flowers for bitter, and unripe banana for astringent. Each taste symbolises a different experience in life!

## Three Groups of Food

Scientists group all the food we eat into **three categories** based on the job each food does inside our body:

### 1. Energy-Giving Foods

These are the fuels that power your body through the day -- walking, running, studying and playing.

**Examples:** Chawal (rice), atta (wheat), aloo (potato), makka (maize), shakarkandi (sweet potato), honey, kele (bananas), seb (apples), ghee, cooking oil and millets.

### 2. Body-Building Foods

These foods help your body grow new cells, build muscles and repair injuries -- like tiny construction workers inside you.

**Examples:** Dal (lentils), soyabean, chana (chickpeas), moongfali (peanuts), paneer, doodh (milk), dahi (curd), rajma (kidney beans), badam (almonds), akhrot (walnuts), ande (eggs), machli (fish) and chicken.

### 3. Protective Foods

These are your body's soldiers -- they guard you against infections and diseases, and keep your skin, eyes and organs healthy.

**Examples:** Gajar (carrot), palak (spinach), tamatar (tomato), patta gobhi (cabbage), santara (orange), nimbu (lemon), papita (papaya), amla (Indian gooseberry), amrood (guava) and most fresh fruits and vegetables.

> Eating from only **one group** every day is like having only one leg of a three-legged stool -- it cannot stand properly! Your body needs food from **all three groups** to stay balanced.

## Millets -- The Super Food

**Millets** are tiny but mighty grains that your great-grandparents ate regularly. Types of millets include **jowar** (sorghum), **bajra** (pearl millet), **ragi** (finger millet) and **jau** (barley). They are packed with iron, calcium and fibre -- nutrients that keep your blood healthy, bones strong and digestion smooth.

In many Indian homes, dadi and nani remember eating thick bajra roti with a dollop of white butter in winter and light jau porridge in summer. Today, millets are making a big comeback as people discover how healthy they are.

## A Balanced Food Plate

Picture a **thali** placed in front of you. A well-balanced thali has foods from all three groups in the right amounts:

- **Largest share:** Sabzi, salad and fruits (protective foods)
- **Good share:** Dal, paneer, dahi or sprouts (body-building foods)
- **Moderate share:** Roti, rice or millet bread (energy-giving foods)

Every region in India has its own style of thali -- a Gujarati thali has dhokla and kadhi, a South Indian thali has sambar and rasam, a Bengali thali has machher jhol and shukto. Different dishes, but the same principle -- variety and balance.

## Say No to Junk Food

**Junk food** tastes tempting but offers very little nutrition. It is loaded with excess oil, sugar and salt, which can make you feel sluggish and fall sick more often.

| Junk Food | Why It Is Unhealthy |
|---|---|
| Chips and namkeen packets | Excess salt and oil, artificial flavours |
| Cold drinks and cola | Loaded with sugar -- one bottle can have 10 spoons of sugar! |
| Burgers and pizzas | Over-processed ingredients, low in vitamins |
| Samosa and bread pakoda | Deep-fried in oil heated again and again |
| Packaged biscuits and cakes | Contain preservatives and artificial colours |

**Fresh, home-cooked food** -- like dal-chawal, roti-sabzi, or a bowl of dalia -- is always a healthier and tastier choice than anything from a packet.

## Water for Health

**Water** does not give you energy or build muscles, but without it your body simply cannot function. Here is what water does:

- Helps your stomach **digest food** properly.
- Keeps your **skin soft** and your body temperature balanced.
- Flushes **waste** out of your body through urine and sweat.
- Carries **nutrients** from your food to every part of your body.

Fruits like **tarbooz** (watermelon), **kheera** (cucumber) and **narangi** (orange) are naturally full of water and make excellent snacks on a hot day.

Drinking **six to eight glasses** of water every day is one of the simplest healthy habits you can build.

## Different Methods of Cooking

The same ingredient can taste completely different depending on how it is cooked! Here are common cooking methods in Indian kitchens:

| Cooking Method | Example Dishes |
|---|---|
| **Steaming** | Idli, momos, dhokla |
| **Boiling** | Dal, rice, khichdi |
| **Frying** | Puri, samosa, pakoda |
| **Roasting** | Bhutta (roasted corn), papad on flame |
| **Grilling** | Paneer tikka, tandoori roti |
| **Baking** | Cake, bread, naan |
| **No cooking needed** | Fruit chaat, raita, cucumber salad |

> **Did you know?** A **solar cooker** uses sunlight instead of gas or electricity to heat food. It is a box with a glass lid and reflective mirrors that trap the Sun's heat inside. No smoke, no fuel cost -- just clean cooking powered by nature!

## Reading Food Labels

Whenever your family buys **packaged food** -- biscuits, juice, cereal, or chips -- flip the packet over and look at the label. Three things to check every time:

- **Expiry date** -- If the date has passed, the food may have gone bad. Never eat expired products.
- **Ingredients list** -- The first ingredient listed is what the product contains the most of. Fewer ingredients usually means less processing.
- **Net weight** -- This tells you how many grams of food are actually inside the packet.

Building the habit of reading labels makes you a smart and safe eater.

## Key Points to Remember

- Food gives our body **energy, building material and disease-fighting power**.
- Ayurveda's **Shad Rasa** (six tastes) encourages a balanced mix of sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter and astringent.
- Food falls into **three groups**: energy-giving, body-building and protective -- eat from all three daily.
- **Millets** like jowar, bajra and ragi are nutrient-packed super foods.
- A **balanced thali** has the right proportions of vegetables, proteins and grains.
- **Junk food** is high in oil, sugar and salt but low in nutrition -- home-cooked food is the better choice.
- **Water** is essential for digestion, healthy skin and removing waste from the body.
- Always **read food labels** on packaged items to check the expiry date and ingredients.
