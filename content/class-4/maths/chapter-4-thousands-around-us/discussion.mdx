## From Ones to Thousands

**Child:** Maa, today we started learning about thousands! Our teacher said 10 hundreds make a thousand.

**Mother:** That is right! Think of it like a staircase. 10 ones make 1 ten. 10 tens make 1 hundred. And 10 hundreds make 1 thousand. Each step is ten times bigger.

**Child:** So 1000 is actually 10 times 100?

**Mother:** Exactly! And here is something wonderful — this whole system of writing numbers using just ten symbols was invented in India thousands of years ago. The rest of the world borrowed it from us!

**Child:** Really? We invented numbers?

**Mother:** We invented the way of writing them! The digits 0 through 9, and the idea that a digit's position decides its value — that is India's gift to the world.

## Where Do We See Thousands?

**Child:** Maa, where do we see thousands in real life? A thousand seems like such a huge number.

**Mother:** It is big, but you encounter it more than you think! How many steps do you walk from home to school?

**Child:** I do not know... maybe a lot?

**Mother:** An average person takes about 1300 steps to walk one kilometre. Your school is about 1 km away, so you walk over a thousand steps each way!

**Child:** Wow! And grains of rice in my lunch box?

**Mother:** A single handful of rice has more than 1000 grains! And think about the number of tigers in India — thanks to Project Tiger, we now have about 3600 tigers in our forests.

**Child:** That is amazing. Thousands are everywhere!

**Mother:** Even the number of languages spoken in India is more than 1600. We are a country of thousands!

## Place Value — The Thousands Place

**Child:** Maa, I get confused when a number has a zero in the middle, like 6032.

**Mother:** Let us break it down. Write the places on top: Th, H, T, O. Now put each digit in its place: 6 in Thousands, 0 in Hundreds, 3 in Tens, 2 in Ones.

**Child:** So the zero means there are no hundreds?

**Mother:** Exactly! And that zero is very important. Without it, the number would become 632 — a completely different number!

**Child:** Because the 6 would drop from thousands to hundreds?

**Mother:** Yes! Zero is like a guard standing in the hundreds place saying, "This spot is taken — even though it is empty, it matters!" That is why we call zero a placeholder.

**Child:** I will never forget the zero again, Maa!

## Comparing 4-Digit Numbers

**Child:** Maa, how do I tell which of two big numbers is bigger?

**Mother:** Always start from the left — the thousands place. Whoever has more thousands wins. If thousands are the same, move to hundreds. Then tens. Then ones.

**Child:** Like a tiebreaker?

**Mother:** Exactly like a tiebreaker! Let me test you. Which is bigger — 4508 or 4580?

**Child:** Thousands are both 4... hundreds are both 5... tens are 0 and 8. 8 is bigger, so 4580 is bigger!

**Mother:** Brilliant! Now here is another one: 875 or 1003?

**Child:** Easy! 875 has only 3 digits and 1003 has 4 digits. Any 4-digit number beats a 3-digit number. So 1003 is bigger!

**Mother:** You are becoming a number champion!

## Ordering Numbers

**Child:** Maa, we had to arrange 3742, 3274, and 3427 from smallest to biggest today. I got confused.

**Mother:** All three start with 3 thousand, right? So the thousands cannot help us. Look at the hundreds: 7, 2, and 4.

**Child:** 2 is the smallest, then 4, then 7!

**Mother:** So which number is smallest?

**Child:** 3274, because it has 2 hundreds. Then 3427 with 4 hundreds. Then 3742 with 7 hundreds!

**Mother:** Perfect! When ordering numbers, just use the same comparison trick — start from the left and work your way right.

**Child:** And for decreasing order, I just flip it around — biggest first!

**Mother:** Exactly right, beta. You have got this!
