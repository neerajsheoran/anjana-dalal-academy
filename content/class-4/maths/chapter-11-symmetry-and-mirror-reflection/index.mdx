## What is Symmetry?

Look at a rangoli drawn during Diwali. If you imagine folding it down the middle, both halves would match perfectly. That beautiful balance is called **symmetry**.

A shape is **symmetrical** when you can split it along a line and the two halves are **perfect mirror copies** of each other. The splitting line is called the **line of symmetry**.

### Testing for Symmetry

1. **Fold** the shape along a line
2. If both halves **overlap exactly**, the shape has symmetry
3. The fold is the **line of symmetry**

> **Make your own:** Fold a plain sheet of paper in half. Drop a few blobs of paint near the fold. Press the two halves together and open up. You will see a lovely symmetrical design!

---

## Line of Symmetry

The **line of symmetry** is the imaginary line that divides a shape into two matching halves.

### How Many Lines of Symmetry?

| Shape | Lines of Symmetry |
|-------|-------------------|
| Circle | Endless (infinite) |
| Square | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 |
| Equilateral triangle | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 |
| Regular pentagon | 5 |
| Regular hexagon | 6 |

Some shapes have **no line of symmetry** at all. We call them **asymmetrical**.

> **Key point:** A square has 4 lines of symmetry — one horizontal, one vertical, and two diagonal. A rectangle only has 2 — horizontal and vertical. The diagonals of a rectangle do NOT create symmetry!

## Symmetry in Our Surroundings

Look around and you will spot symmetry everywhere:

- **In nature:** Butterfly wings, flower petals, most leaves along their centre vein
- **In buildings:** The Taj Mahal, many temples and gurudwaras are designed with perfect symmetry
- **In art:** Mehndi patterns, kolam designs, and traditional embroidery
- **In letters:** Some English letters are symmetrical: A, B, C, D, E, H, I, K, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y
- **In flying:** A symmetrical paper plane sails straight; a lopsided one spins and crashes

> **Try it:** Make two paper planes — one with equal wings and one with unequal wings. Fly both and watch the difference!

## Mirror Images (Reflections)

When you stand in front of a mirror, you see your **reflection**. The mirror creates a copy where left and right are swapped.

### Numbers in a Mirror

Some digits look the same when reflected:

| Digit | Same in mirror? |
|-------|----------------|
| 0 | Yes |
| 1 | Yes |
| 3 | No |
| 8 | Yes |
| 5 | No |

Numbers made only from **0, 1, and 8** will look unchanged in a mirror: **100, 118, 180, 801, 808, 1001, 1881...**

### Why AMBULANCE Is Written Backwards

On the front of an ambulance, the word **AMBULANCE** is printed in reverse. When a driver ahead looks in their **rear-view mirror**, the flipped writing appears normal — so they know to move aside quickly!

> **Fun challenge:** Write your name backwards on paper and check it in a mirror. It will look perfectly normal!

## Completing Symmetrical Shapes

If someone gives you **half a shape** and shows you the line of symmetry, you can draw the other half.

Rules to follow:
1. Every dot or corner on one side must have a matching dot or corner on the other side
2. Each matching pair must be the **same distance** from the line of symmetry
3. Use grid paper to count squares from the line and mark the mirror positions

> **Grid trick:** On squared paper, if a point is 3 squares to the left of the line, its mirror point is 3 squares to the right.

## Folding and Cutting Paper

When you fold paper and snip a shape, the result has symmetry:

### Single Fold
- Fold a sheet **once** and cut along the folded edge
- Open up: the cut appears on **both sides** of the fold (1 line of symmetry)

### Double Fold
- Fold a sheet **twice** (once vertically, once horizontally) and cut a shape
- Open up: the cut appears in **all four sections** (2 lines of symmetry)

> **Diwali craft:** Fold paper twice and cut a small diya shape. When you open it, you get four diyas — perfect for a decoration chain!

## Tiling — Covering Surfaces

**Tiling** means fitting shapes together on a surface with **no gaps** and **no overlaps**. Think of the floor tiles in your kitchen or bathroom.

### Which Shapes Can Tile?

| Shape | Tiles perfectly? |
|-------|-----------------|
| Square | Yes |
| Rectangle | Yes |
| Triangle | Yes |
| Regular hexagon | Yes (like a honeycomb) |
| Circle | No (leaves curved gaps) |
| Regular pentagon | No (leaves gaps) |

### Creating Your Own Tiles

Here is a creative way to make a new tile shape:

1. Start with a **square** piece of card
2. **Cut** a wavy piece from the top edge
3. **Tape** that piece to the bottom edge
4. This new shape tiles perfectly — the bump on the bottom of one tile fits the dip on the top of the next tile!

> **Artists use this trick** to create amazing patterns that look like animals, birds, or other shapes — but still tile without gaps!

## Symmetry in Nature

Take a walk in a park or garden and observe:

- **Leaves:** The main vein runs down the centre, and the two halves are near-perfect mirrors
- **Flowers:** Petals are arranged symmetrically around the centre
- **Butterflies:** Left wing and right wing are mirror images
- **Starfish:** Have 5 lines of symmetry

But not everything is perfectly symmetrical! A snail's shell spirals in one direction, and no two sides of a real person's face are exactly the same.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Symmetry** | A shape that can be split into two identical mirror halves |
| **Line of symmetry** | The line along which a shape divides into matching halves |
| **Symmetrical** | Having at least one line of symmetry |
| **Asymmetrical** | Having no line of symmetry at all |
| **Mirror image** | What a shape looks like when reflected in a mirror |
| **Reflection** | The mirror copy of a shape or object |
| **Tiling** | Fitting shapes on a surface with no gaps and no overlaps |
| **Tile** | A single piece used to cover a surface in a pattern |
| **Fold and cut** | Folding paper and cutting it to make symmetrical designs |
