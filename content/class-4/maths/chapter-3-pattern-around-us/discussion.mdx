## Counting in Groups

**Child:** Maa, today we learned how to count things really fast using patterns!

**Mother:** Oh? Tell me how!

**Child:** If there are 5 rows of chairs with 7 chairs in each row, I do not need to count every chair. I just say 5 times 7 equals 35!

**Mother:** That is so smart! Patterns and groups make counting much faster. Think about our egg tray at home — it has rows and columns of eggs. Instead of counting each egg, you can count how many rows and how many in each row.

**Child:** The tray has 2 rows of 6 eggs, so 2 times 6 = 12 eggs!

**Mother:** Perfect! Now imagine Balu Uncle's sweet shop during Diwali. He arranges gulab jamuns in trays — 3 rows of 4 in each tray. If he has 8 trays ready, how many gulab jamuns is that?

**Child:** One tray has 3 times 4 = 12 gulab jamuns. And 8 trays means 12 times 8... that is 96!

**Mother:** You are becoming a maths wizard!

## Patterns with Money

**Child:** Maa, I want to count my piggy bank savings. But there are so many coins all mixed up!

**Mother:** First step — sort them. Put all the ₹5 coins in one pile, all the ₹2 coins in another, and all the ₹1 coins separately.

**Child:** Okay... I have 4 coins of ₹5, 6 coins of ₹2, and 3 coins of ₹1.

**Mother:** Now count each pile. The ₹5 pile?

**Child:** 5, 10, 15, 20 — that is ₹20!

**Mother:** The ₹2 pile?

**Child:** 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 — that is ₹12!

**Mother:** And the ₹1 pile?

**Child:** 1, 2, 3 — that is ₹3!

**Mother:** Now add all three groups: 20 + 12 + 3?

**Child:** ₹35! Wow, I have ₹35 in my piggy bank!

**Mother:** See how sorting into groups and skip-counting made it so much easier? That is the power of patterns!

## Even and Odd Numbers

**Child:** Maa, how do I know if a number is even or odd? I keep forgetting!

**Mother:** Think of it like pairing up for a school dance. If every student finds a partner and nobody is left alone, the number of students is even. If one student is left without a partner, the number is odd.

**Child:** So 6 is even because 3 pairs and nobody left out?

**Mother:** Yes! And 7 is odd because 3 pairs and 1 child standing alone.

**Child:** But what about big numbers like 7,346? I cannot pair up that many people in my head!

**Mother:** You do not need to! Here is the golden rule — just look at the **last digit**. If the last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8, the number is even. If it is 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9, it is odd.

**Child:** So 7,346 ends in 6... it is even!

**Mother:** And 509?

**Child:** Ends in 9... odd!

**Mother:** You have got it!

## Interesting Patterns with Even and Odd Numbers

**Child:** Maa, I noticed something cool. On the number line, even and odd numbers take turns — like a see-saw!

**Mother:** That is a beautiful observation! 1 is odd, 2 is even, 3 is odd, 4 is even... they alternate forever.

**Child:** And I checked my storybook — the left pages are always even and right pages are always odd!

**Mother:** That is true for every book! Here is another fun pattern — what happens when you add two odd numbers together?

**Child:** Let me try... 3 + 5 = 8. That is even! Let me try another... 7 + 9 = 16. Also even!

**Mother:** Will you ever get an odd answer by adding two odd numbers?

**Child:** Hmm... 11 + 13 = 24, still even. I think the answer is always even!

**Mother:** You are absolutely right! When two odd numbers are added, the two leftover singles pair up with each other, making the answer even. Maths is full of these beautiful patterns.

**Child:** I love finding patterns! It is like being a detective!

**Mother:** That is exactly what mathematicians do, beta — they look for patterns and figure out why they work.
