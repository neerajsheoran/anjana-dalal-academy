## Multiplying by 10

When you multiply any number by 10, every digit shifts one place to the left, and a **0** fills the ones place.

| Multiplication | Picture it as | Result |
|---------------|--------------|--------|
| 3 x 10 | 3 groups of ten | 30 |
| 7 x 10 | 7 groups of ten | 70 |
| 10 x 10 | 10 groups of ten | 100 |
| 14 x 10 | 14 groups of ten | 140 |

> **Simple rule:** Multiplying by 10 means writing the number and putting a zero after it! 23 x 10 = 230, 48 x 10 = 480.

## Creating Bigger Times Tables

You already know the tables from 1 to 10. Now let us build tables for larger numbers like 12, 13, 15, and beyond using what you already know!

### Example: The 13 Times Table

Split 13 into **10 + 3**:

| Problem | Break it up | Answer |
|---------|------------|--------|
| 1 x 13 | 1x10 + 1x3 = 10 + 3 | 13 |
| 2 x 13 | 2x10 + 2x3 = 20 + 6 | 26 |
| 3 x 13 | 3x10 + 3x3 = 30 + 9 | 39 |
| 4 x 13 | 4x10 + 4x3 = 40 + 12 | 52 |
| 5 x 13 | 5x10 + 5x3 = 50 + 15 | 65 |
| 10 x 13 | 10x10 + 10x3 = 100 + 30 | 130 |

### Comparing Tables

| Times-3 | Times-13 | Difference |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| 1 x 3 = 3 | 1 x 13 = 13 | 13 - 3 = 10 |
| 2 x 3 = 6 | 2 x 13 = 26 | 26 - 6 = 20 |
| 3 x 3 = 9 | 3 x 13 = 39 | 39 - 9 = 30 |

The difference column gives the **times-10 table**! This is because 13 = 3 + 10.

## Doubling to Build Even Tables

For even numbers, you can split them into two equal halves and **double** a known table:

### Example: The 16 Times Table

Since 16 = 8 + 8, find any number times 16 by **doubling** the times-8 answer:

| Problem | Times-8 | Double it | Answer |
|---------|---------|-----------|--------|
| 4 x 16 | 4 x 8 = 32 | 32 + 32 | **64** |
| 5 x 16 | 5 x 8 = 40 | 40 + 40 | **80** |
| 7 x 16 | 7 x 8 = 56 | 56 + 56 | **112** |

> **This works for any even number:** Times-12 = double of times-6. Times-14 = double of times-7. Times-18 = double of times-9.

## Multiplying by Multiples of 10

When one number is a multiple of 10 (like 20, 30, 40...), use this approach:

| Problem | Strategy | Answer |
|---------|----------|--------|
| 15 x 10 | 15 tens | **150** |
| 15 x 20 | 15 x 2 tens = 30 tens | **300** |
| 18 x 30 | 18 x 3 tens = 54 tens | **540** |
| 25 x 40 | 25 x 4 tens = 100 tens | **1000** |

> **Shortcut:** Multiply the non-zero parts first, then attach the zero. 18 x 30: do 18 x 3 = 54, then add a zero to get 540.

## 2-Digit Times 2-Digit Multiplication

To multiply two 2-digit numbers, use the **grid method** (box method).

### Example: 18 x 12 = ?

Split: 18 = 10 + 8, and 12 = 10 + 2

|  x  | 10 | 2 |
|-----|-----|-----|
| **10** | 100 | 20 |
| **8** | 80 | 16 |

Add all four boxes: 100 + 20 + 80 + 16 = **216**

### Example: A Real Journey — Shatabdi Express

A Shatabdi Express train has **22 coaches** with **78 seats** in each coach.

Split: 22 = 20 + 2, and 78 = 70 + 8

|  x  | 70 | 8 |
|-----|------|-----|
| **20** | 1400 | 160 |
| **2** | 140 | 16 |

Total: 1400 + 160 + 140 + 16 = **1716 seats**

## Division with 2-Digit Divisors

When dividing by a number bigger than 10, the **repeated subtraction method** works well — subtract large chunks first.

### Example: 420 / 15 = ?

A school is booking buses that hold 15 students each for 420 students.

| Step | Students placed | Buses used | Remaining |
|------|----------------|------------|-----------|
| 1 | 150 (10 buses x 15) | 10 | 420 - 150 = 270 |
| 2 | 150 (10 buses x 15) | 10 | 270 - 150 = 120 |
| 3 | 120 (8 buses x 15) | 8 | 120 - 120 = 0 |

Total buses: 10 + 10 + 8 = **28 buses** (no remainder)

### Example: 500 / 12 = ?

A colony distributes 500 mangoes, with 12 mangoes per family.

| Step | Mangoes given | Families | Remaining |
|------|-------------|----------|-----------|
| 1 | 120 (10 families x 12) | 10 | 500 - 120 = 380 |
| 2 | 120 (10 families x 12) | 10 | 380 - 120 = 260 |
| 3 | 120 (10 families x 12) | 10 | 260 - 120 = 140 |
| 4 | 120 (10 families x 12) | 10 | 140 - 120 = 20 |
| 5 | 12 (1 family x 12) | 1 | 20 - 12 = 8 |

Total: 41 families served, with **8 mangoes remaining**

Since 8 mangoes are left but a full share needs 12, we need **42 families would get something, but only 41 get a full share**.

## Multiplying by 100

Multiplying by 100 shifts every digit **two places left** and adds **two zeros**:

| Multiplication | Think of it as | Result |
|---------------|---------------|--------|
| 3 x 100 | 3 hundreds | 300 |
| 8 x 100 | 8 hundreds | 800 |
| 12 x 100 | 12 hundreds | 1200 |
| 45 x 100 | 45 hundreds | 4500 |

### Multiplying by 200, 300, 500...

| Problem | Strategy | Answer |
|---------|----------|--------|
| 12 x 200 | 12 x 2 hundreds = 24 hundreds | **2400** |
| 9 x 300 | 9 x 3 hundreds = 27 hundreds | **2700** |
| 8 x 500 | 8 x 5 hundreds = 40 hundreds | **4000** |
| 6 x 700 | 6 x 7 hundreds = 42 hundreds | **4200** |

## Dividing by 10 and 100

Division by 10 and 100 is simply multiplication in reverse:

| Division | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| 90 / 10 | 9 |
| 350 / 10 | 35 |
| 800 / 10 | 80 |
| 500 / 100 | 5 |
| 2000 / 100 | 20 |
| 7000 / 100 | 70 |

> **Shortcut:** Dividing by 10 removes one zero. Dividing by 100 removes two zeros.

## Smart Relationships

Noticing patterns helps you solve faster:

| Observation | Why it works |
|------------|-------------|
| 12 x 1 = 12, 12 x 10 = 120, 12 x 100 = 1200 | Each step is 10 times bigger |
| 8 x 5 = 40, 8 x 50 = 400, 8 x 500 = 4000 | Same idea with multiples of 5 |
| 36 x 10 = 360 and 18 x 20 = 360 | Halving one number and doubling the other keeps the product the same |
| 25 x 100 = 2500 and 50 x 50 = 2500 | Different pairs can give the same product |

## Real-Life Transport Problems

### The Metro

A Delhi Metro train has **8 coaches**, each holding **52 standing and seated passengers**.
Total capacity = 8 x 52. Split: 8 x 50 = 400, 8 x 2 = 16. Total = **416 passengers**

### India's Heritage Railway

The **Darjeeling Himalayan Railway** (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) has a toy train with **6 coaches** carrying **18 passengers** each.
Capacity = 6 x 18 = 6 x 10 + 6 x 8 = 60 + 48 = **108 passengers**

### The Chundan Vallam of Kerala

The famous **snake boats** used in the **Nehru Trophy Boat Race** are over **30 metres long** and can carry up to **100 paddlers and singers**. This tradition is centuries old!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Multiply by 10** | Shift digits left, add one zero (17 x 10 = 170) |
| **Multiply by 100** | Shift digits left, add two zeros (17 x 100 = 1700) |
| **Grid method** | Breaking both numbers into parts and multiplying in a grid |
| **Repeated subtraction** | A division method where you subtract big groups step by step |
| **Remainder** | What is left over after dividing |
| **Double** | Multiply a number by 2 |
| **Half** | Divide a number by 2 |
| **Times table** | The list of products when a number is multiplied by 1 through 10 |
| **Factor** | A number being multiplied (in 6 x 9, both 6 and 9 are factors) |
| **Product** | The answer you get when two numbers are multiplied |
