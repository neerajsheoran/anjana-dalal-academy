## What Do We Measure?

**Child:** Maa, today we learned about measuring length. But our teacher said height, width, and depth are all kinds of length. How?

**Mother:** Great question! They are all about measuring distance, just in different directions. When we say "the coconut tree is 10 metres tall," we are measuring its height — distance from the ground going up. When we say "the saree is 6 metres long," we are measuring length — distance going sideways.

**Child:** And width?

**Mother:** Width is how wide something is — like the width of our front door. And depth is how deep — like how deep our well is.

**Child:** But they all use the same units — metres and centimetres?

**Mother:** Exactly! Whether you measure up, down, sideways, or into the ground, you use the same measuring units.

---

## The Metre

**Child:** Maa, how big is 1 metre exactly?

**Mother:** Stand up and stretch your arms out to both sides. The distance from one fingertip to the other is roughly 1 metre for someone your age. Also, one really big step is about 1 metre.

**Child:** So I am taller than 1 metre?

**Mother:** Yes! Most Class 4 students are between 120 cm and 140 cm tall — that is more than 1 metre but less than 1 and a half metres.

**Child:** And our classroom door?

**Mother:** About 2 metres tall. Most adults can walk through without bending!

**Child:** What about half a metre?

**Mother:** Half a metre is 50 cm. Your school ruler is only 30 cm, so half a metre is longer than your ruler. And a quarter metre is 25 cm — just a little shorter than your ruler.

---

## The Centimetre — Measuring Small Things

**Child:** Maa, my teacher said 1 cm is about the width of my fingernail. Is that true?

**Mother:** Let us check! Put your little finger on the ruler. See? The nail is almost exactly 1 cm wide. That is why your finger is such a handy measuring tool!

**Child:** And 100 cm makes 1 metre. That is a lot of fingernails in a row!

**Mother:** Ha! Yes, 100 fingernails in a line would be about 1 metre. Now tell me — which unit would you use to measure this eraser?

**Child:** Centimetres! It is too small for metres.

**Mother:** And to measure the length of our garden?

**Child:** Metres! It would take too many centimetres.

**Mother:** You are learning to pick the right unit for the job. That is an important skill!

---

## Using a Ruler (Scale)

**Child:** Maa, my ruler got chipped at the start. The first number I can see is 3. Can I still measure things?

**Mother:** Of course! Just place one end of the object at the 3 cm mark instead of 0. Then read where the other end is and subtract.

**Child:** So if my pencil goes from 3 cm to 18 cm, its length is 18 minus 3 equals 15 cm?

**Mother:** Perfect! The trick is to always subtract the starting number from the ending number.

**Child:** I wish I had not dropped my ruler though.

**Mother:** Well, now you know a trick that many adults do not even know! A broken ruler is still useful.

## Perimeter — The Length of the Boundary

**Child:** Maa, what is perimeter? It sounds like a big word.

**Mother:** It is actually simple! Imagine an ant walking around the edge of your notebook — all the way around the outside and back to where it started. The total distance the ant walks is the perimeter.

**Child:** So I just add up all the sides?

**Mother:** Exactly! If your notebook is 24 cm long and 16 cm wide, the ant walks 24, then 16, then 24, then 16. Total: 24 + 16 + 24 + 16 = 80 cm.

**Child:** That is easy! What about a square?

**Mother:** Even easier! A square has all four sides equal. If each side is 10 cm, perimeter = 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 40 cm. Or just 4 times 10!

**Child:** Maa, when would I need perimeter in real life?

**Mother:** Many times! If Dadi wants to stitch lace around a cushion cover, she needs to know the perimeter to buy the right length of lace. If we want to fence our garden, we need the perimeter to know how much fencing to buy.

**Child:** So perimeter helps us know how much material we need to go around something!

**Mother:** Exactly right, beta. Now go measure the perimeter of your study table with a measuring tape!
