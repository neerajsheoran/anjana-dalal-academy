## What Do We Measure?

Whenever we talk about how long, how tall, how wide, or how deep something is, we are talking about **length**. We use different words depending on what we are measuring:

| Word | What it describes | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Height** | How tall something is | Height of a coconut tree, a child |
| **Length** | How long something is | Length of a cricket pitch, a saree |
| **Breadth / Width** | How wide something is | Width of a river, a notebook |
| **Depth** | How deep something is | Depth of a well, a lake |

All of these are measured using the same length units!

---

## The Metre

The **metre (m)** is the standard unit for measuring length. Here are some handy benchmarks:

- Most Class 4 children are **more than 1 metre** tall
- Your school desk is usually **less than 1 metre** wide
- A classroom door is typically about **2 metres** tall

### Half Metre and Quarter Metre

A metre can be split into smaller fractions:

| Part | Fraction | What it equals |
|------|----------|--------|
| Half metre | 1/2 m | Half of 1 metre |
| Quarter metre | 1/4 m | One-fourth of 1 metre |

Key connections:
- **1 m = 1/2 m + 1/2 m** (two halves make a whole)
- **1 m = 1/4 m + 1/4 m + 1/4 m + 1/4 m** (four quarters make a whole)
- **1/2 m = 1/4 m + 1/4 m** (two quarters make a half)

## Estimating Lengths

Before picking up a measuring tool, try to **estimate** (make a smart guess). Comparing with familiar objects helps:

| Object | Approximate Length |
|--------|-------------------|
| An elephant's height | About 3 metres |
| A King Cobra (longest venomous snake) | About 5 metres |
| A school bus | About 10 metres long |
| A badminton court | About 13 metres long |
| A cricket pitch (end to end) | About 20 metres |

> **Tip:** Use your own body to estimate! Your outstretched arms span roughly 1 metre. So ask yourself: "Is this object wider than my arm span or narrower?"

## The Centimetre — Measuring Small Things

For tiny objects like a crayon, a coin, or a seed, the metre is far too big. We need a smaller unit called the **centimetre (cm)**.

Look at any ruler (scale) — the small lines divide it into centimetres. Each gap between two lines is exactly **1 centimetre**.

### The Key Relationship

**1 metre (m) = 100 centimetres (cm)**

From this we can figure out:
- **1/2 m = 50 cm**
- **1/4 m = 25 cm**

### How Big is 1 cm?

- The width of your little **fingernail** is about 1 cm
- A **mustard seed** is much less than 1 cm
- A **rubber eraser** is usually 3 to 5 cm long

> **Handy trick:** Press your thumb on a ruler. The width of your thumbnail is close to 1 cm. Use this as a quick estimator for small objects!

---

## Using a Ruler (Scale)

To measure an object with a ruler:

1. Line up one end of the object with the **0 mark** (not the edge of the ruler — the actual 0 line)
2. Look at where the other end of the object reaches
3. Read that number — it tells you the length in **centimetres**

### Measuring with a Broken Ruler

What if your ruler's starting part is chipped off and there is no 0 mark? No problem! Just subtract the starting number from the ending number.

For example, if the object starts at 4 cm and ends at 11 cm:
Length = 11 - 4 = **7 cm**

## Converting Between Metres and Centimetres

Since **1 m = 100 cm**, converting is simple:

| Metres | Centimetres |
|--------|-------------|
| 1 m | 100 cm |
| 2 m | 200 cm |
| 5 m | 500 cm |
| 8 m | 800 cm |

### Mixed Measurements

Often a measurement uses both metres and centimetres:

| Height | Same as |
|--------|---------|
| 1 m 15 cm | 115 cm |
| 1 m 35 cm | 135 cm |
| 2 m 50 cm | 250 cm |
| 4 m 75 cm | 475 cm |

> **Example:** Geeta says she is 128 cm tall. Sita says she is 1 m 28 cm tall. They are the same height! 100 cm + 28 cm = 128 cm = 1 m 28 cm.

## Perimeter — The Length of the Boundary

The **perimeter** is the total distance around the outside edge of a shape. To find it, simply add up all the side lengths.

### Example: A Rectangle

If a rectangular notebook cover is 24 cm long and 16 cm wide:

```
       24 cm
    ┌─────────┐
    │         │ 16 cm
    │         │
    └─────────┘
       24 cm
```

Perimeter = 24 + 16 + 24 + 16 = **80 cm**

### Example: A Square

If a square tile has sides of 10 cm each:

Perimeter = 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = **40 cm**

### Important Points About Perimeter

- Two very different-looking shapes can have the **same perimeter**
- To measure the perimeter of any shape, trace along its entire boundary and add all side lengths
- Perimeter uses the same units as length (cm or m)

> **Real-life use:** If Dadi wants to stitch lace around a square cushion cover, she needs to know its perimeter to buy enough lace!

## Measuring in Real Life

Here are some measuring activities you can try at home:

| Activity | What to measure | Best unit |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| Your height | Stand against a wall and mark | cm or m and cm |
| Length of your foot | Heel to longest toe | cm |
| Width of your maths textbook | Side to side | cm |
| Height of a dining chair | Floor to seat | cm |
| Length of a room | One wall to the opposite wall | m |
| Perimeter of a table top | Around all four edges | cm |

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Length** | How long something is from end to end |
| **Height** | How tall something is from bottom to top |
| **Breadth / Width** | How wide something is from side to side |
| **Depth** | How deep something goes from top to bottom |
| **Metre (m)** | A standard unit of length; roughly one big step |
| **Centimetre (cm)** | A smaller unit of length; 100 cm = 1 m |
| **Estimate** | A smart guess about a measurement before actually measuring |
| **Ruler / Scale** | A flat tool with markings used to measure length in centimetres |
| **Measuring tape** | A bendable tape used to measure longer or curved lengths |
| **Perimeter** | The total distance around the boundary of a shape |
| **Boundary** | The outer edge that goes all the way around a shape |
