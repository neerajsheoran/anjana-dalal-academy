## What is Data?

**Data** means information — facts, numbers, or answers that we gather about something.

Whenever you ask a question and write down the answers, you are collecting data!

For example:
- "What is your favourite sport?" — the answers your classmates give you is data
- "How many runs did each player score?" — the numbers on the scoreboard is data
- "Which flavour of kulfi does your family like best?" — each person's choice is data

> **In simple words:** Data is just a collection of answers or numbers. We collect data so we can understand things better, compare them, and make good choices.

---

## Asking a Good Question

The first step in collecting data is to ask a **clear, specific question**.

### Strong Questions vs Weak Questions

| Weak question | Strong question |
|--------------|----------------|
| "What do you like to eat?" (too open — too many answers) | "Which tiffin item do you like the most: paratha, idli, sandwich, or poha?" (specific choices) |
| "Do you play?" (yes/no is not very useful) | "Which sport do you play the most: cricket, football, kabaddi, or badminton?" (clear categories) |

When each person picks **one answer** from a clear list, counting and comparing becomes easy.

## Ways to Record Data

After gathering answers, you need to **write them down** neatly. Here are different methods:

### 1. Writing a Raw List

Simply write each response one after the other:
```
C, F, C, K, B, C, F, K, C, B, F, C...
```
Where C = Cricket, F = Football, K = Kabaddi, B = Badminton

This works for small amounts, but gets confusing with many responses.

### 2. Using Tally Marks

Draw one stroke for each response. After every 4 strokes, the 5th stroke goes **across** the bundle:

```
| = 1    || = 2    ||| = 3    |||| = 4    |||| = 5 (the 5th crosses the group)
                                            /
```

Tally marks let you count quickly in groups of 5.

### 3. Putting Data in a Table

A **table** arranges data in rows and columns:

| Sport | Number of Children |
|-------|--------------------|
| Cricket | 12 |
| Football | 9 |
| Kabaddi | 6 |
| Badminton | 8 |

> **Best practice:** Tables are the clearest way to present data. They make comparisons instant.

---

## Reading Data from a Table

Once your data is in a table, you can answer important questions:

- **Most popular?** Look for the biggest number
- **Least popular?** Look for the smallest number
- **Total responses?** Add all the numbers together
- **Difference between two?** Subtract the smaller from the bigger

### Example

Using our sports table:
- Most popular sport: **Cricket** (12 children)
- Least popular sport: **Kabaddi** (6 children)
- Total children surveyed: 12 + 9 + 6 + 8 = **35 children**
- How many more like cricket than badminton? 12 - 8 = **4 more**

## Pictographs

A **pictograph** shows data using **pictures or symbols**. Each picture stands for a certain number.

### Example: Snack Sales at the School Mela

Suppose each picture of a bowl stands for **10 bowls sold**:

**Chaat Sales:**

| Day | Symbols | Total Bowls |
|-----|---------|-------------|
| Saturday | 3 symbols | 30 |
| Sunday | 5 symbols | 50 |

**Samosa Sales:**

| Day | Symbols | Total Samosas |
|-----|---------|--------------|
| Saturday | 4 symbols | 40 |
| Sunday | 6 symbols | 60 |

What can we find out?
- **Sunday** had more chaat sales (50 vs 30)
- **Sunday** also had more samosa sales (60 vs 40)
- Total Saturday sales: 30 + 40 = **70**
- Total Sunday sales: 50 + 60 = **110** (Sunday was busier!)

> **Always check the key:** In every pictograph, look at what **each picture represents** — it might stand for 1, 2, 5, 10, or even more.

## Running Your Own Survey

You can collect data about anything you find interesting! Here is how:

### Step 1: Think of a Clear Question
- "Which school holiday activity do you enjoy: reading, drawing, playing outdoors, or watching TV?"

### Step 2: Gather Answers
- Ask each classmate and note their response

### Step 3: Organise in a Table

| Activity | Girls | Boys |
|----------|-------|------|
| Reading | | |
| Drawing | | |
| Playing outdoors | | |
| Watching TV | | |

### Step 4: Study the Results
- Which activity do most girls prefer?
- Which activity do most boys prefer?
- How many children chose playing outdoors in total?

## Choosing How to Show Data

| Method | What it looks like | When to use it |
|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Raw list** | C, F, C, K, B, C... | Only for very small data sets |
| **Table** | Rows and columns of numbers | Always works — clear and neat |
| **Tally marks** | Strokes grouped in fives | Great when counting responses live |
| **Pictograph** | Pictures representing numbers | When you want a visual, fun display |

> **Remember:** A table is almost always the best first step. You can always make a pictograph or chart from a table later.

## Data All Around Us

Data collection happens everywhere in India:

- **Schools** keep track of attendance, marks, and library books borrowed
- **Shopkeepers** note which items sell the most so they know what to stock
- **Doctors** record patient details like height, weight, temperature
- **Weather stations** measure rainfall, temperature, and wind speed
- **The Indian Census** counts every person in the country — over 140 crore people!
- **IPL cricket** tracks runs, wickets, strike rates, and economy rates for every player

> **You are already a data scientist!** Every time you count something, compare two things, or ask "which is more?", you are handling data.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Data** | Facts, numbers, or information collected for a purpose |
| **Survey** | Asking people questions to collect their responses |
| **Response** | A single answer given by one person |
| **Tally marks** | Lines grouped in fives used to count responses |
| **Table** | Information arranged in rows and columns |
| **Pictograph** | A chart that uses pictures to show numbers |
| **Most popular** | The item chosen by the greatest number of people |
| **Least popular** | The item chosen by the fewest people |
| **Total** | The sum of all the numbers in the data |
| **Compare** | Looking at two or more values to see which is bigger, smaller, or equal |
| **Organise** | Arranging data in a neat, useful way |
