## Addition of 4-Digit Numbers

**Child:** Maa, adding 4-digit numbers scares me. The numbers are so big!

**Mother:** They are bigger, but the method is exactly the same as 2-digit or 3-digit numbers. You still start from the ones place and move left. Want to try with some wildlife numbers?

**Child:** Okay. Tamil Nadu has 2761 elephants and Karnataka has 6395. What is the total?

**Mother:** Start from the right. Ones: 1 plus 5?

**Child:** 6. Easy. Tens: 6 plus 9 is 15. So I write 5 and carry 1.

**Mother:** Good! Hundreds?

**Child:** 7 plus 3 is 10, plus the carried 1 makes 11. Write 1, carry 1. And thousands: 2 plus 6 is 8, plus 1 makes 9. The answer is 9156!

**Mother:** See? No different from adding smaller numbers. The 4-digit part is just decoration. The method stays the same.

## Subtraction of 4-Digit Numbers

**Child:** Maa, subtraction is harder because of the borrowing. I always get confused about which number becomes what.

**Mother:** I understand. Let me give you a trick. Think of borrowing like breaking a Rs 10 note into ten Rs 1 coins. You are not losing money — you are just changing the form.

**Child:** So when I cannot subtract in the ones place, I break one ten into ten ones?

**Mother:** Exactly. And if you cannot subtract in the tens place, you break one hundred into ten tens. Same idea at every column. Let me give you a problem: 4684 minus 1889.

**Child:** Ones: 4 minus 9 — I cannot! So I borrow from tens. Now I have 14 minus 9 is 5. Tens: 7 left, but 7 minus 8 — still cannot! Borrow from hundreds. 17 minus 8 is 9.

**Mother:** Keep going!

**Child:** Hundreds: 5 left, minus 8 — borrow again! 15 minus 8 is 7. Thousands: 3 minus 1 is 2. Answer is 2795!

**Mother:** Three borrows in one problem, and you handled every one. That is real maths confidence.

## Reverse and Add Patterns

**Child:** Maa, we learned a cool trick today. You take a number, reverse its digits, and add them together!

**Mother:** Show me!

**Child:** Take 34. Reverse it to get 43. Then 34 plus 43 is 77. Now take 16. Reverse is 61. And 16 plus 61 is also 77!

**Mother:** Interesting! Why do you think both give 77?

**Child:** Hmm... the teacher said something about the digits adding up to the same number?

**Mother:** Right! 3 plus 4 is 7. And 1 plus 6 is also 7. Whenever the digits add up to the same total, the reverse-and-add answer is the same. Try a number whose digits add up to 10.

**Child:** 46! 4 plus 6 is 10. Reverse is 64. And 46 plus 64 is 110. Let me try 28. 2 plus 8 is 10. 28 plus 82 is 110! It works!

**Mother:** You just discovered a beautiful pattern. Maths is full of surprises like this.

## Mental Maths Strategies

**Child:** Maa, when I do maths in my head, I sometimes get lost with all the numbers.

**Mother:** The secret is to not do it the hard way. Use shortcuts! What is 5555 minus 99?

**Child:** Umm... that is hard to do in my head.

**Mother:** What if I said: subtract 100 instead, then add 1 back?

**Child:** 5555 minus 100 is 5455. Plus 1 is 5456. That was easy!

**Mother:** Exactly. Subtracting 99 is the same as subtracting 100 and adding 1. Similarly, adding 99 is like adding 100 and subtracting 1. These little tricks make mental maths so much faster.

**Child:** What about adding 7999 plus 125?

**Mother:** What is 7999 close to?

**Child:** 8000! So add 1 to make 8000, then add 124 instead of 125. That gives 8124!

**Mother:** Now you are thinking like a mathematician.

## Working with Indian Money

**Child:** Maa, I broke open my piggy bank today. I want to count how much I saved!

**Mother:** Great! Let us sort the notes and coins first. How many of each do you have?

**Child:** I have 2 notes of Rs 500, 3 notes of Rs 100, 1 note of Rs 50, and 5 coins of Rs 10.

**Mother:** Start with the biggest. 2 times 500?

**Child:** 1000. Plus 3 times 100 is 300. Plus 50. Plus 5 times 10 is 50. So 1000 plus 300 plus 50 plus 50. That is Rs 1400!

**Mother:** Would you like to deposit this at the bank? You would need to fill a deposit slip listing each type of note and coin.

**Child:** Can I go to the bank with Papa this Saturday?

**Mother:** Of course! You can fill the form yourself. It is a great way to practise maths in real life.
