## Addition — Putting Numbers Together

**Child:** Maa, today we practiced addition with big numbers. Sometimes the ones column goes past 10 and I have to carry!

**Mother:** Yes, that is called regrouping. Let me give you a real example. Say we are buying vegetables — tomatoes for ₹37 and onions for ₹45. What is the total?

**Child:** 7 + 5 in the ones column is 12. I write 2 and carry 1. Then 3 + 4 + 1 = 8 in the tens column. Total is ₹82!

**Mother:** Perfect! You regrouped beautifully. Now try a bigger one — 365 + 478.

**Child:** Ones: 5 + 8 = 13, write 3 carry 1. Tens: 6 + 7 + 1 = 14, write 4 carry 1. Hundreds: 3 + 4 + 1 = 8. Answer is 843!

**Mother:** Wonderful! And before you calculated, could you have estimated the answer?

**Child:** Hmm... 365 is close to 370 and 478 is close to 480. So about 850?

**Mother:** And your actual answer was 843 — very close! Estimating first helps you catch mistakes. If your answer had been 243, you would know something went wrong.

## Subtraction — Finding the Difference

**Child:** Maa, subtraction with borrowing is harder. Especially when there is a zero in the middle!

**Mother:** I understand. Let us work through one together. How about 503 - 267?

**Child:** Ones: 3 minus 7... I cannot do that. I need to borrow from tens. But tens is 0!

**Mother:** So what do you do?

**Child:** Go to hundreds! 5 hundreds becomes 4 hundreds and 10 tens. Then I take 1 ten to make ones: 9 tens and 13 ones.

**Mother:** Now subtract each column.

**Child:** Ones: 13 - 7 = 6. Tens: 9 - 6 = 3. Hundreds: 4 - 2 = 2. Answer is 236!

**Mother:** Excellent! The zero in the middle makes it tricky, but you handled it like a pro. Always remember — if the tens column is 0, go to hundreds first, then bring it down to ones.

**Child:** It is like borrowing sugar from a neighbour when our jar is empty!

**Mother:** Ha! That is a perfect way to remember it!

## Returning Change — A Real-Life Skill

**Child:** Maa, when we go to the market, how does the shopkeeper know how much change to give?

**Mother:** Simple subtraction! If your items cost ₹127 and you pay ₹200, the change is 200 - 127 = ₹73.

**Child:** Can I try? Yesterday we bought apples for ₹62 and grapes for ₹48. I paid ₹200.

**Mother:** First find the total cost.

**Child:** 62 + 48 = ₹110.

**Mother:** Now find the change.

**Child:** 200 - 110 = ₹90!

**Mother:** And that is exactly what the fruit seller returned to us. You can always check if the shopkeeper gives the right change by doing this subtraction in your head!

**Child:** I am going to check every time now, Maa!

## Quick Mental Strategies

**Child:** Maa, our teacher taught us cool tricks for adding and subtracting in our heads. Like adding 99 is the same as adding 100 and subtracting 1!

**Mother:** Those tricks are so useful! Try 725 + 99 in your head.

**Child:** 725 + 100 = 825, then minus 1 = 824!

**Mother:** Much faster than the long way! Now try subtracting 29 from 860.

**Child:** Hmm... subtract 30, then add 1. 860 - 30 = 830, plus 1 = 831!

**Mother:** Brilliant! These mental tricks work because 99 is just 1 less than 100, and 29 is just 1 less than 30. It is easier to work with round numbers.

**Child:** What about adding 98?

**Mother:** Same idea — add 100, subtract 2. So 456 + 98 = 556 - 2 = 554.

**Child:** I love these shortcuts! They make maths feel like a game.

**Mother:** That is because maths IS a game, beta — a game of patterns and clever thinking!

## Word Problems — Think Before You Calculate

**Child:** Maa, I find word problems confusing. Sometimes I do not know whether to add or subtract.

**Mother:** Here is my trick — look for key words. If the problem says "total," "altogether," or "combined," you probably need to add. If it says "how many more," "how many left," or "difference," you need to subtract.

**Child:** So if Sunita Aunty picked 318 tomatoes last month and 456 this month, and the question asks "how many more this month?"...

**Mother:** What operation?

**Child:** Subtract! 456 - 318 = 138 more tomatoes.

**Mother:** And if the question asked "how many tomatoes in total across both months?"

**Child:** Add! 318 + 456 = 774 tomatoes altogether.

**Mother:** See? Same numbers, but the question changes the operation. Always read carefully and decide: am I combining things or finding a difference?

**Child:** I will read word problems more carefully from now on, Maa. Thank you!

**Mother:** You are welcome, beta. And always estimate before you calculate — it helps you catch silly mistakes!
