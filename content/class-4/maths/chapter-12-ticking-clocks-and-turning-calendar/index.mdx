## The Calendar

We use a **calendar** to keep track of which day, month, and year it is. Whether it is a school holiday, a festival, or your birthday — the calendar helps us plan everything.

### Months and How Many Days Each Has

A year has **12 months**:

| Month | Number of Days |
|-------|---------------|
| January | 31 |
| February | 28 (or 29 in a leap year) |
| March | 31 |
| April | 30 |
| May | 31 |
| June | 30 |
| July | 31 |
| August | 31 |
| September | 30 |
| October | 31 |
| November | 30 |
| December | 31 |

An ordinary year has **365 days**. A special year called a **leap year** has **366 days** because February gets one extra day.

### The Seven Days

A week has **7 days**: Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday.

---

## Leap Years

Every **4 years**, we get a **leap year** where February has **29 days** instead of 28.

Recent and upcoming leap years: 2020, 2024, 2028, 2032...

> **Tanvi's birthday mystery:** Tanvi was born on 29 February 2020. Since that date only exists in leap years, her exact birthday appears on the calendar only once every 4 years! On other years, her family celebrates on 28 February or 1 March.

### Quick Check: Is It a Leap Year?

Divide the year by 4. If there is **no remainder**, it is a leap year.
- 2028 / 4 = 507 (no remainder) — leap year!
- 2027 / 4 = 506 remainder 3 — not a leap year

## Writing Dates

In India, we write dates as **DD/MM/YYYY**:

| Part | What it means | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| DD | Day of the month | 15 |
| MM | Month (as a number) | 08 (August) |
| YYYY | Year | 2026 |

So **15/08/2026** means **15th August 2026** — Independence Day!

> **Comparing ages:** To figure out who is older, first check the **year** of birth. If the year is the same, check the **month**. Same month? Check the **day**.

## Day Patterns in the Calendar

Here is something interesting: if you know what day of the week a date is this month, you can figure out the day for the same date next month!

Since July has 31 days, and 31 = 4 full weeks + 3 extra days, the day of the week **shifts forward by 3** from July to August.

So if 10 July is a Thursday, then 10 August will be a **Sunday** (3 days ahead).

The shift depends on the number of days in the month:
- 31-day month: shifts by 3
- 30-day month: shifts by 2
- 28-day month (February): shifts by 0
- 29-day month (February in leap year): shifts by 1

## Hours in a Day

One full day — from midnight to midnight — has **24 hours**.

We divide it into two halves:
- **First 12 hours:** midnight to noon
- **Next 12 hours:** noon to midnight

---

## AM and PM

Since a clock face only shows 1 through 12, we add **AM** or **PM** to say which half of the day we mean.

| Time of Day | Label | Examples |
|------------|-------|---------|
| Midnight to just before noon | **AM** | 6:00 AM, 10:30 AM, 11:45 AM |
| Noon to just before midnight | **PM** | 12:30 PM, 4:00 PM, 9:15 PM |

### Daily Routine in AM and PM

| Activity | Typical Time |
|----------|-------------|
| Waking up | 6:30 AM |
| Reaching school | 7:45 AM |
| Lunch break | 12:30 PM |
| Coming home from school | 2:00 PM |
| Playing with friends | 5:00 PM |
| Dinner | 8:00 PM |
| Sleeping | 9:30 PM |

> **Be careful:** 7:00 AM (morning, time for school) and 7:00 PM (evening, time for dinner) are completely different times even though the number is the same!

## The 24-Hour Clock

Railway stations, airports, and digital devices often use a **24-hour clock** where hours go from 00:00 to 23:59.

| 12-Hour Time | 24-Hour Time |
|-------------|-------------|
| 12:00 AM (midnight) | 00:00 |
| 6:30 AM | 06:30 |
| 12:00 PM (noon) | 12:00 |
| 2:15 PM | 14:15 |
| 6:00 PM | 18:00 |
| 9:45 PM | 21:45 |
| 11:50 PM | 23:50 |

> **Converting PM to 24-hour:** Add 12 to the hour. 5:30 PM = 5 + 12 = 17:30. Morning AM times stay the same (just add a leading zero if needed).

## Hours and Minutes

**1 hour = 60 minutes**

| Fraction of an Hour | Minutes |
|---------------------|---------|
| Full hour | 60 minutes |
| Half hour | 30 minutes |
| Quarter hour | 15 minutes |
| Three-quarter hour | 45 minutes |

### Finding How Much Time Passed

**Example:** Ananya starts her homework at 4:15 PM and finishes at 5:00 PM.
- From 4:15 to 5:00 = **45 minutes**

**Example:** Sahil's cricket practice:
- 3:30 PM: Warm-up begins
- 3:45 PM: Batting practice starts (15 minutes of warm-up)
- 4:30 PM: Batting ends, bowling begins (45 minutes of batting)
- 5:00 PM: Practice ends (30 minutes of bowling)

Total practice time: from 3:30 to 5:00 = **1 hour 30 minutes = 90 minutes**

## Reading an Analogue Clock

On a clock with hands:
- The **short fat hand** shows the **hour**
- The **long thin hand** shows the **minutes**

The minute hand moves past **small tick marks**. Each tick = 1 minute. Each number on the dial represents 5 minutes.

| Minute hand points to | Minutes past the hour |
|----------------------|----------------------|
| 12 | 0 (the hour exactly) |
| 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 10 |
| 3 | 15 (quarter past) |
| 4 | 20 |
| 5 | 25 |
| 6 | 30 (half past) |
| 7 | 35 |
| 8 | 40 |
| 9 | 45 (quarter to the next hour) |
| 10 | 50 |
| 11 | 55 |

> **Watch out:** If the short hand is between 4 and 5, the hour is still **4**. It becomes 5 only when the minute hand reaches 12 again.

## Expiry Dates on Food

Packaged food items show a **manufacturing date** (when they were made) and an **expiry date** (after which they should not be eaten).

**Example:** A packet of chips was made on 10/01/2026 and expires on 10/07/2026. That means it stays fresh for **6 months**.

> **Always look** at the expiry date before eating anything from a packet. If the date has passed, do not eat it!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Calendar** | A chart that shows days, weeks, and months of a year |
| **Leap year** | A year with 366 days; February has 29 days; happens every 4 years |
| **AM** | The hours from midnight to noon (morning time) |
| **PM** | The hours from noon to midnight (afternoon, evening, night) |
| **24-hour clock** | A time system using hours 00 to 23, common on trains and phones |
| **Hour** | A unit of time; 1 hour = 60 minutes |
| **Minute** | A smaller unit of time; 60 minutes make 1 hour |
| **Duration** | How much time passes between two events |
| **Manufacturing date** | The date a product was made |
| **Expiry date** | The last date a product is safe to use |
| **DD/MM/YYYY** | The Indian way of writing dates: Day/Month/Year |
