## Parts and Wholes

When we divide something equally among friends, each person gets a **part** of the whole. These equal parts are called **fractions**.

### What is a Half?

Split something into **2 equal pieces** and each piece is called a **half**.

We write half as **1/2**.

```

             1/2         1/2
```

Put both halves back together: **1/2 + 1/2 = 1 whole**

### What is a Quarter?

Split something into **4 equal pieces** and each piece is called a **quarter** (or one-fourth).

We write quarter as **1/4**.

```
Whole:        [████████████████]
Four quarters: [████] [████] [████] [████]
                1/4    1/4    1/4    1/4
```

Join all four quarters: **1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 1 whole**

> **Tricky question:** Neha asked her brother Aman, "Would you like half of this chocolate bar or two quarters?" Aman chose two quarters, thinking he would get more. But 1/2 = 2/4 — both amounts are exactly the same! Two quarters is just another way of saying one half.

## Sharing — The Paratha Story

Imagine a hot, round paratha being shared equally among friends at lunch:

| Number of friends | Each person's share | Fraction |
|-----------------|-------------------|----------|
| 2 friends | Half the paratha | **1/2** |
| 3 friends | One-third of the paratha | **1/3** |
| 4 friends | One-quarter of the paratha | **1/4** |
| 5 friends | One-fifth of the paratha | **1/5** |
| 6 friends | One-sixth of the paratha | **1/6** |
| 8 friends | One-eighth of the paratha | **1/8** |

> **Important rule:** The more friends you share with, the **smaller** each piece becomes. So 1/8 is a tinier piece than 1/4, and 1/4 is smaller than 1/2.

## Understanding Fractions

A fraction uses two numbers to describe a part of something:

- The **bottom number** (denominator) tells us into how many **equal pieces** the whole was cut
- The **top number** (numerator) tells us **how many of those pieces** we are talking about

### Examples

| Fraction | Top number | Bottom number | Meaning |
|----------|-----------|---------------|---------|
| **1/3** | 1 | 3 | 1 piece out of 3 equal pieces |
| **2/5** | 2 | 5 | 2 pieces out of 5 equal pieces |
| **3/4** | 3 | 4 | 3 pieces out of 4 equal pieces |
| **4/5** | 4 | 5 | 4 pieces out of 5 equal pieces |
| **5/5** | 5 | 5 | All 5 pieces out of 5 — the whole thing! |

## Meera's Rangoli Garden

Meera has a small garden in front of her house, divided into **5 equal plots** for different flowers.

She plants roses in 2 plots — that is **2/5** (two-fifths) of the garden.

| Flower | Plots used | Fraction |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| Jasmine | 1 plot | 1/5 (one-fifth) |
| Rose | 2 plots | 2/5 (two-fifths) |
| Sunflower | 1 plot | 1/5 (one-fifth) |
| Marigold | 1 plot | 1/5 (one-fifth) |

All plots together: 1/5 + 2/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 = **5/5 = 1 whole garden**

## Arjun's Pizza Party

At Arjun's birthday party, his Maa ordered a big pizza. Let us see how different cuts give different fractions:

A pizza cut into **4 equal slices**:
- 1/4 has corn topping
- 1/4 has paneer topping
- 1/4 has capsicum topping
- 1/4 has plain cheese

A pizza cut into **2 equal halves**:
- 1/2 with onion topping
- 1/2 with mushroom topping

A pizza cut into **3 equal slices**:
- 2/3 with extra cheese (2 slices out of 3)
- 1/3 with olives (1 slice out of 3)

> **Key idea:** No matter how you slice it, the pieces must add up to the whole! If a pizza is cut into 3 slices, then 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 3/3 = 1 complete pizza.

## Comparing Fractions

### When the Top Number is the Same (like 1)

When comparing fractions with **1 on top** (like 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5):

**The bigger the bottom number, the smaller the fraction.**

| Comparison | Which is bigger? | Why? |
|-----------|-----------------|------|
| 1/4 vs 1/5 | **1/4** is bigger | Splitting into 4 gives bigger pieces than splitting into 5 |
| 1/6 vs 1/9 | **1/6** is bigger | Splitting into 6 gives bigger pieces than splitting into 9 |
| 1/2 vs 1/8 | **1/2** is bigger | Half is much larger than one-eighth |

> **Picture it:** If you share one roti among 3 people, each gets more than if you share the same roti among 6 people!

## Fractions of a Group

Fractions work with collections of objects, not just single items.

### Example: 12 Laddoos Shared Equally

| Shared among | Each person gets | Fraction |
|-------------|-----------------|----------|
| 2 children | 6 laddoos | 1/2 of 12 = 6 |
| 3 children | 4 laddoos | 1/3 of 12 = 4 |
| 4 children | 3 laddoos | 1/4 of 12 = 3 |
| 6 children | 2 laddoos | 1/6 of 12 = 2 |

To find a fraction of a group, **divide** the total by the bottom number.

### Example: Finding 1/4 of 8 Bangles

Divide 8 bangles into 4 equal groups: each group has **2 bangles**.
So, 1/4 of 8 = **2 bangles**.

## Equal Fractions (Equivalent Fractions)

Sometimes two fractions that look different actually stand for the **exact same amount**. These twin fractions are called **equivalent fractions**.

Try this paper-folding experiment:
1. Fold a paper into **3 equal strips** and colour 1 strip — that is **1/3**
2. Now fold the same paper **in half** again — you now have **6 strips**, with **2 coloured** — that is **2/6**
3. Fold one more time — **12 strips**, with **4 coloured** — that is **4/12**

So: **1/3 = 2/6 = 4/12** — they all cover the same amount of paper!

Similarly:

| Fraction | Equivalent Fractions |
|----------|-------------------|
| 1/2 | 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8 = 5/10 |
| 1/3 | 2/6 = 3/9 = 4/12 |
| 1/4 | 2/8 = 3/12 |

## Fractions Must Have Equal Parts

For a fraction to be valid, every piece **must be the same size**.

If you cut a roti into 4 parts but one piece is big and another is tiny, you cannot say each piece is 1/4. Only when all 4 pieces are exactly the same size can you use the fraction 1/4.

> **Be careful!** Always check that the parts are truly equal before writing a fraction. If in doubt, use fold lines or a ruler to make sure.

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Fraction** | A number showing a part of a whole (e.g., 1/2, 3/4) |
| **Half (1/2)** | One of two equal parts |
| **Quarter (1/4)** | One of four equal parts |
| **Third (1/3)** | One of three equal parts |
| **Numerator** | The top number — how many parts we have |
| **Denominator** | The bottom number — how many equal parts the whole is divided into |
| **Whole** | The complete item or group before dividing |
| **Equal parts** | Pieces that are exactly the same size |
| **Equivalent fractions** | Different-looking fractions that represent the same amount (e.g., 1/2 = 2/4) |
| **Fraction chart** | A visual diagram comparing different fractions side by side |
