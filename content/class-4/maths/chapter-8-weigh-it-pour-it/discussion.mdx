## Measuring Weight

**Child:** Maa, why do we say kilograms sometimes and grams other times? Why not just one word?

**Mother:** Think about distance, beta. When we drive to Nani's house, we say "200 kilometres." But when an ant walks across the table, would you say "0.0003 kilometres"?

**Child:** Ha ha, no! That sounds so funny. I would say centimetres for the ant.

**Mother:** Exactly! Same idea with weight. For heavy things like a bag of rice, we say kilograms. For light things like your earring or a pinch of haldi, we say grams. It just makes the numbers easier to understand.

**Child:** So how many grams make one kilogram?

**Mother:** One thousand! 1 kg equals 1000 g. The sugar packet in our kitchen is exactly 1 kg, so that is 1000 grams of sugar.

**Child:** And half a kg?

**Mother:** 500 grams. Like when I ask the sabzi wala for "aadha kilo tamatar" — that is 500 grams of tomatoes.

## Estimating Weight

**Child:** Maa, can I guess the weight of something just by holding it?

**Mother:** Of course! That is called estimating. Pick up a rupee coin — it weighs about 5 grams. Now pick up your water bottle when it is full. That is about 1 kilogram. Use these as your mental measuring sticks.

**Child:** So if I hold a mango and it feels heavier than the coin but much lighter than the bottle...

**Mother:** Then you can guess it is somewhere between — maybe 200 to 300 grams. Professional vegetable sellers at the mandi are so experienced that their guesses are almost as accurate as a scale!

**Child:** Wow, they must have been practising for years!

**Mother:** Yes, and you can practise too. Next time we go shopping, try guessing the weight before the shopkeeper puts it on the scale.

## Capacity and Liquids

**Child:** Maa, today in class we learned about capacity. Is it the same as weight?

**Mother:** Not quite. Weight tells you how heavy something is. Capacity tells you how much liquid can fit inside a container. Your steel glass can hold about 250 ml of water. Our kitchen bucket can hold about 15 litres.

**Child:** So millilitres and litres are for liquids, just like grams and kilograms are for weight?

**Mother:** Exactly right! And just like weight, 1 litre equals 1000 millilitres.

**Child:** The numbers are the same! 1000 in both!

**Mother:** Good observation! That makes it easier to remember. When I give you cough syrup, I measure 5 ml using the little cap. When I fill the water filter, I pour in about 7 litres. Different amounts need different units.

## Saving Water

**Child:** Maa, our bathroom tap was dripping last night. Does that waste a lot of water?

**Mother:** More than you think! If a tap drips about 10 ml every minute, how much water is that in one hour?

**Child:** One hour has 60 minutes, so 10 times 60 is 600 ml.

**Mother:** And in a full day of 24 hours?

**Child:** 600 times 24... that is... 14,400 ml! Wait, that is more than 14 litres!

**Mother:** Now imagine that dripping for a whole month — over 400 litres wasted. That is enough to fill 20 buckets just from one leaky tap.

**Child:** We need to get it fixed, Maa!

**Mother:** Absolutely. I will call the plumber tomorrow. And remember, turning off taps properly is something even a child can do. Every drop you save matters.

## At the Kirana Store

**Child:** Maa, today I went with Papa to the kirana store. The shopkeeper used different-sized weights on his balance. Why does he have so many?

**Mother:** Think of it like coins. If you need to pay exactly 7 rupees, you might use a 5 rupee coin and a 2 rupee coin. Similarly, the shopkeeper combines weights to match exactly what you want.

**Child:** So if someone asks for 750 grams of chana daal?

**Mother:** He could put a 500 g weight and a 250 g weight on one side of the balance, and keep pouring daal on the other side until both sides are level.

**Child:** And 500 plus 250 is 750. Perfect!

**Mother:** You can also think about it the other way. If I ask for 2 kg of rice, how many 500 g packets is that?

**Child:** 2 kg is 2000 g. And 2000 divided by 500 is 4. So four packets!

**Mother:** See how maths helps us shop smartly? Next time, you can be my little calculator at the store.
