## Measuring Weight

Pick up a pencil in one hand and your school bag in the other. Which one feels heavier? That heaviness is called **weight**. To describe weight properly, we need special units.

### The Kilogram (kg)

The **kilogram (kg)** is the unit we use for things we carry, buy, or cook with every day.

- A packet of sugar from the shop usually weighs 1 kg
- Your Maths textbook weighs less than 1 kg
- A big watermelon can weigh 4 kg or 5 kg

### The Gram (g)

When something is really light, kilograms are too big a unit. We use **grams (g)** instead. A single biscuit, a coin, or a small pencil all weigh just a few grams.

### How They Connect

**1 kilogram (kg) = 1000 grams (g)**

| Weight | In grams |
|--------|----------|
| 1 kg | 1000 g |
| 1/2 kg | 500 g |
| 1/4 kg | 250 g |

Think of it this way:
- **2 packets of 500 g together weigh 1 kg**
- **4 packets of 250 g together weigh 1 kg**
- **10 packets of 100 g together weigh 1 kg**

> **Remember:** Half a kilogram is 500 g, and a quarter of a kilogram is 250 g. These numbers come up again and again in daily life!

## Shopping at the Kirana Store

At your neighbourhood kirana store, the shopkeeper uses a **weighing scale** to measure out lentils, sugar, and spices.

**Suppose:** A packet of chana daal weighs 500 g. How many such packets make 2 kg?

2 kg = 2000 g. Number of packets = 2000 / 500 = **4 packets**

### Balancing Both Sides

On an old-style balance (tarazu):
- The **heavier** pan drops **down**
- The **lighter** pan goes **up**
- When both pans are level, the weights on both sides are **equal**

> **Think about it:** If one pan has a 500 g weight and the other pan has two 250 g weights, the balance stays level. Both sides equal 500 g!

## Measuring Capacity

**Capacity** tells us how much liquid fits inside a container. A glass holds less liquid than a bucket. A bucket holds less liquid than a tanker. We measure capacity using **litres** and **millilitres**.

### The Litre (l)

The **litre (l)** is our everyday unit for liquids.
- A large water bottle holds about 1 litre
- A kitchen bucket holds about 15 litres
- A small drinking glass holds about 200 ml — less than 1 litre

### The Millilitre (ml)

For tiny amounts, we use **millilitres (ml)**.
- One drop from a medicine dropper is about 1 ml
- A chai spoon holds about 5 ml
- A cough syrup cap holds about 10 ml

### How They Connect

**1 litre (l) = 1000 millilitres (ml)**

| Capacity | In millilitres |
|----------|---------------|
| 1 l | 1000 ml |
| 1/2 l | 500 ml |
| 1/4 l | 250 ml |

So:
- **2 bottles of 500 ml fill up to 1 litre**
- **4 glasses of 250 ml fill up to 1 litre**
- **5 pouches of 200 ml fill up to 1 litre**

## Saving Water — Every Drop Matters

A dripping tap may seem harmless, but the waste adds up quickly!

- If a tap drips 10 ml every minute, that is **600 ml in just one hour**
- In one full day (24 hours), that wastes about **14 litres**
- Over a whole month, that is more than **400 litres** — enough to fill 20 buckets!

> **Pledge:** Turn taps off completely after use. Tell an adult about any leaking taps at home. Saving water is everyone's job!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Weight** | How heavy something is |
| **Kilogram (kg)** | The bigger unit of weight; 1 kg = 1000 g |
| **Gram (g)** | The smaller unit of weight; used for light things |
| **Capacity** | How much liquid a container can hold |
| **Litre (l)** | The bigger unit of capacity; 1 l = 1000 ml |
| **Millilitre (ml)** | The smaller unit of capacity; used for tiny amounts |
| **Balance scale** | A tool that compares weights on two pans |
| **Estimate** | A thoughtful guess before measuring |
| **Regroup** | Switching between units (like 1000 g = 1 kg) |
