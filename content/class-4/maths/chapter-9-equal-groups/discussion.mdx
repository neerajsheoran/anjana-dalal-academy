## Multiples and Skip Counting

**Child:** Maa, what exactly are multiples? My teacher said to skip count, but I got confused.

**Mother:** Let us use the stairs in our building. If you climb 3 steps at a time, which step numbers do you land on?

**Child:** Step 3, then 6, then 9, then 12...

**Mother:** Those are the multiples of 3! Now try 4 steps at a time.

**Child:** 4, 8, 12, 16, 20...

**Mother:** See, you already found something interesting. Both lists have the number 12. When a number appears in two different skip-counting lists, it is called a common multiple.

**Child:** So 12 is a common multiple of 3 and 4! That is pretty cool.

**Mother:** And there are more: 24, 36, 48... Common multiples keep going forever. You will find them very useful in bigger maths later.

## Multiplication as Equal Groups

**Child:** Maa, why do we even need multiplication? I can just keep adding.

**Mother:** Okay, try this. We are making garlands for the pooja. Each garland needs 6 flowers and we need 12 garlands. Add 6 twelve times.

**Child:** 6 plus 6 is 12, plus 6 is 18, plus 6 is... hmm, this is getting long.

**Mother:** Exactly! Multiplication is a shortcut for repeated addition. 12 times 6 equals 72. Done in one step!

**Child:** But how do I do 12 times 6 in my head?

**Mother:** Split it! 10 times 6 is 60. And 2 times 6 is 12. Add them: 60 plus 12 is 72. Break the hard part into easy parts.

**Child:** Oh! So I can always split the big number into tens and ones?

**Mother:** Yes, and that trick works for any multiplication. It is called the distributive property, but you can just think of it as the "break it up" trick.

## The Doubling Trick

**Child:** Maa, what does doubling mean?

**Mother:** It means having two of something. If you have 15 chocolates and I give you the same amount again, now you have?

**Child:** 30! Because 15 plus 15 is 30.

**Mother:** That is doubling — multiplying by 2. And here is a secret: the answer is always even. Always!

**Child:** Even if I double an odd number like 7?

**Mother:** 7 times 2 is 14. Even! Try 99.

**Child:** 99 times 2 is... 198. That is even too!

**Mother:** It works every single time. When you add any number to itself, the ones digits always pair up to make an even number.

**Child:** Maa, you make maths feel like magic.

## Division and Remainders

**Child:** Maa, today we were dividing in class and my answer had something left over. I thought I did it wrong.

**Mother:** Not at all! That leftover is called the remainder. Imagine you have 25 biscuits and 4 friends. You share equally.

**Child:** Each friend gets... 6 biscuits. That uses 24. One biscuit is left!

**Mother:** That one biscuit is the remainder. And notice — the remainder is always less than the number of friends. You cannot have 4 biscuits left over because then you could give one more to each friend!

**Child:** Oh, I see! If the remainder was 4 or more, I could still keep sharing.

**Mother:** Exactly. When dividing by 4, the remainder can only be 0, 1, 2, or 3. Zero means it divides perfectly with nothing left.

## Connecting Multiplication and Division

**Child:** Maa, are multiplication and division related?

**Mother:** They are best friends — one undoes the other! If 7 times 8 equals 56, then 56 divided by 7 equals?

**Child:** 8!

**Mother:** And 56 divided by 8?

**Child:** 7! I see — the same three numbers work together.

**Mother:** This is very useful. If you know your times tables well, division becomes easy. For 72 divided by 9, just ask yourself: 9 times what gives 72?

**Child:** 9 times 8 is 72! So the answer is 8!

**Mother:** You just did division using multiplication. That is the smartest shortcut there is.
