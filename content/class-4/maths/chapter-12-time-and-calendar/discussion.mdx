## The Calendar

**Child:** Maa, why does February have fewer days than the other months? It feels unfair!

**Mother:** It does seem a bit odd, does it not? The calendar we use today was designed by the Romans thousands of years ago. They gave some months 31 days, some 30 days, and February got the shortest share — 28 days.

**Child:** And in a leap year it gets 29?

**Mother:** Yes! Every 4 years, February gets one extra day. This happens because the Earth takes about 365 and a quarter days to go around the Sun. After 4 years, those quarter days add up to a full day, and we put that extra day in February.

**Child:** So Tanvi in my class, who was born on 29 February — her birthday date only shows up every 4 years?

**Mother:** Exactly. She celebrates on 28 February or 1 March in other years. But her real birthday date comes only in leap years!

**Child:** That is special and a little sad at the same time.

## AM and PM

**Child:** Maa, I always mix up AM and PM. When is which?

**Mother:** Think of it this way. AM is from midnight to noon. You are sleeping for part of it, then you wake up, get ready, and go to school — all in AM hours.

**Child:** So my alarm at 6:30 in the morning is 6:30 AM?

**Mother:** Right! And when the clock strikes 12 noon, we switch to PM. Lunch, afternoon play, homework, dinner — all happen in PM hours.

**Child:** What about 12 itself? Is noon 12 AM or 12 PM?

**Mother:** Noon is 12:00 PM. And midnight is 12:00 AM. This confuses many grown-ups too! Just remember: 12 PM is the bright sunny middle of the day, and 12 AM is the dark middle of the night.

**Child:** Got it! PM has the sunshine at 12, and AM has the darkness at 12.

## The 24-Hour Clock

**Child:** Maa, when Papa booked train tickets, the departure time said 16:30. What does that mean? There is no 16 on our clock!

**Mother:** That is the 24-hour system. Instead of using AM and PM, it uses numbers from 0 to 23 for hours. So 16:30 means 4:30 PM.

**Child:** How do I convert it?

**Mother:** For anything above 12, just subtract 12. So 16 minus 12 equals 4. That means 4:30 PM. For morning times, the number stays the same. 07:00 is just 7:00 AM.

**Child:** So 20:00 would be 20 minus 12 equals 8. That is 8:00 PM!

**Mother:** Perfect. Trains, flights, and army people all use this 24-hour system because it avoids any confusion between AM and PM.

**Child:** No one can mix up 8 in the morning and 8 at night if one is 08:00 and the other is 20:00!

## Finding How Much Time Passed

**Child:** Maa, I started my homework at 4:15 PM and finished at 5:00 PM. How long did I take?

**Mother:** Count forward from 4:15. From 4:15 to 4:30 is 15 minutes. From 4:30 to 5:00 is another 30 minutes. Total: 15 plus 30 equals 45 minutes.

**Child:** That is three-quarters of an hour! Is there a faster way?

**Mother:** You can also subtract: 5:00 minus 4:15. Think of 5:00 as 4:60 (since 1 hour = 60 minutes). Then 4:60 minus 4:15 is 45 minutes.

**Child:** Oh, that is like borrowing in subtraction! 5 hours becomes 4 hours and 60 minutes.

**Mother:** Exactly the same idea. Time subtraction uses borrowing just like number subtraction.

## Expiry Dates on Food

**Child:** Maa, I found an old packet of biscuits in the cupboard. The expiry date says 01/03/2026. Can I eat them?

**Mother:** What is today's date?

**Child:** Today is 30 March 2026. So 01/03/2026 means 1st March 2026. That was almost a month ago!

**Mother:** Then the biscuits have expired. They might look fine, but they could have lost their freshness or even become unsafe to eat. Always check the expiry date before opening any packet.

**Child:** How do they decide how long food lasts?

**Mother:** Companies test their products to see how long they stay safe and tasty. The time from the manufacturing date to the expiry date is called the shelf life. Different foods have different shelf lives — milk expires in days, biscuits in months, and honey can last for years!

**Child:** I will always check the date from now on, Maa.
