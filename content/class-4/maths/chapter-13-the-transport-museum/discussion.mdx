## Multiplying by 10

**Child:** Maa, my teacher says multiplying by 10 is the easiest thing in maths. You just add a zero. But why does that work?

**Mother:** Think about our number system. Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right. When you multiply by 10, every digit moves one place to the left — to a place worth 10 times more.

**Child:** So in 23 x 10, the 2 moves from tens to hundreds, and the 3 moves from ones to tens?

**Mother:** Exactly! The 2 becomes 2 hundreds (200) and the 3 becomes 3 tens (30). That is 230. The ones place is now empty, so we fill it with zero.

**Child:** And multiplying by 100 means everything moves TWO places left?

**Mother:** Right again! 23 x 100 = 2300. Each digit jumps two places. You are really getting the hang of this.

## Creating Bigger Times Tables

**Child:** Maa, I know the tables up to 10, but my teacher is asking for 13 times table. I do not know it by heart!

**Mother:** You do not need to memorise it. You can build it using what you already know. What is 13?

**Child:** 10 plus 3.

**Mother:** So 7 times 13 is the same as 7 times 10 plus 7 times 3. Can you do that?

**Child:** 7 times 10 is 70. And 7 times 3 is 21. So 70 plus 21 is 91!

**Mother:** Brilliant! You just did 7 x 13 = 91 without needing to memorise anything. This splitting trick works for any number.

**Child:** What about even numbers like 16?

**Mother:** Even easier! 16 is 8 plus 8. So instead of multiplying by 16, just multiply by 8 and double the answer.

**Child:** So 5 x 16 is... 5 x 8 = 40, doubled is 80. Cool!

## 2-Digit Times 2-Digit Multiplication

**Child:** Maa, today the teacher showed us the grid method. It looked like a box with four sections. Can you explain it again?

**Mother:** Sure. Say we want 18 x 12. We split both numbers: 18 becomes 10 and 8. And 12 becomes 10 and 2. Then we make a little grid and multiply each pair.

**Child:** So the four boxes are 10 times 10, 10 times 2, 8 times 10, and 8 times 2?

**Mother:** That is 100, 20, 80, and 16. Add them all up.

**Child:** 100 plus 20 is 120. Plus 80 is 200. Plus 16 is 216!

**Mother:** Perfect. The grid keeps everything organised so you do not miss any part. It is like packing a suitcase with compartments — nothing gets lost!

**Child:** I like this method. It makes big multiplications feel small.

## Division with 2-Digit Divisors

**Child:** Maa, how do I divide 420 by 15? I cannot do this in my head!

**Mother:** Nobody expects you to do it all at once. Take it step by step. How many times does 15 fit into 420? Start with a big, easy group.

**Child:** 10 times 15 is 150. I can subtract that from 420. That leaves 270.

**Mother:** Good. Take another 10 groups of 15.

**Child:** Another 150 off 270 leaves 120.

**Mother:** Now, how many times does 15 fit into 120?

**Child:** 15 times 8 is... let me check: 15 times 8 = 120! It fits exactly! So the total is 10 plus 10 plus 8 = 28, with nothing left over.

**Mother:** 420 divided by 15 is 28. And you did not need to do any scary long division. Just smart subtraction.

**Child:** This is like peeling a banana in sections instead of trying to bite through the whole thing!

## Smart Relationships

**Child:** Maa, I noticed something strange. 36 times 10 is 360. And 18 times 20 is also 360. How can two different problems have the same answer?

**Mother:** Beautiful observation! You halved the 36 (getting 18) and doubled the 10 (getting 20). When you halve one factor and double the other, the product stays exactly the same.

**Child:** Does that always work?

**Mother:** Always! Try it with 50 times 8.

**Child:** That is 400. Now halve 50 to get 25 and double 8 to get 16. 25 times 16... hmm, 25 times 10 is 250, 25 times 6 is 150. Total is 400! Same answer!

**Mother:** This trick is very useful when one of the numbers is hard to work with. You can swap it for an easier pair.

**Child:** Maths is like a puzzle with hidden shortcuts everywhere!
