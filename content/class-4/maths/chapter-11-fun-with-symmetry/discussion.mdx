## What is Symmetry?

**Child:** Maa, what does symmetry mean? My teacher says it is everywhere, but I cannot see it.

**Mother:** Let us start right here at home. Go bring me a fresh leaf from the tulsi plant.

**Child:** Got it!

**Mother:** Now look at it closely. See the line running down the centre of the leaf? That is the main vein. Look at the two halves on either side. Do they look the same?

**Child:** Yes! The left side and the right side look like copies of each other!

**Mother:** That is symmetry. When you can divide something into two halves that are mirror images of each other, it is symmetrical. The centre line is called the line of symmetry.

**Child:** So a butterfly has symmetry too? Its wings look the same!

**Mother:** Exactly right. Nature loves symmetry.

## Line of Symmetry

**Child:** Maa, can something have more than one line of symmetry?

**Mother:** Great question! Pick up that square roti I just rolled out. How many ways can you fold it so both halves match?

**Child:** I can fold it top to bottom. Or left to right. That is two!

**Mother:** What about corner to corner, diagonally?

**Child:** Oh yes! I can fold it from this corner to that corner. And the other diagonal too. That makes four ways!

**Mother:** So a square has 4 lines of symmetry. Now think about a circle — like the bottom of your steel glass. How many ways can you fold it in half?

**Child:** Hmm... I can fold it in any direction through the centre. That is... a lot!

**Mother:** A circle has infinite lines of symmetry — you can fold it through the centre in any direction and both halves match.

**Child:** A circle is the symmetry champion!

## Mirror Images (Reflections)

**Child:** Maa, today the teacher talked about the word AMBULANCE being written backwards on ambulances. Why do they do that?

**Mother:** Stand in front of the mirror in the hallway. What do you notice about the writing on your T-shirt?

**Child:** It looks backwards! The letters are all flipped!

**Mother:** Right. A mirror reverses left and right. Now imagine you are driving a car and you look in your rear-view mirror. You see the ambulance behind you. The writing on the ambulance is already reversed, so in the mirror it looks normal — you can read AMBULANCE clearly!

**Child:** Oh, so they flip it on purpose because they know drivers will read it in a mirror. That is so clever!

**Mother:** Exactly. Maths and design working together to save lives.

## Tiling — Covering Surfaces

**Child:** Maa, why are floor tiles always squares or rectangles? Why not circles?

**Mother:** Try arranging some round chapatis on the table so that they cover it completely with no gaps.

**Child:** I am trying... but there are always little spaces between the chapatis because they are round!

**Mother:** Now try with square biscuits.

**Child:** These fit perfectly! No gaps at all!

**Mother:** That is why tiles are squares, rectangles, or sometimes hexagons — these shapes fit together perfectly. Circles always leave gaps because of their curved edges.

**Child:** What about triangles?

**Mother:** Triangles work too! You flip one triangle upside down next to the other, and they fit. Many traditional Indian patterns use triangles. Look at the border on your dupatta — see those triangles?

**Child:** Yes! They fit together perfectly. Maths is hiding in my clothes too!

## Folding and Cutting Paper

**Child:** Maa, can we do a craft? The teacher said we can make patterns by folding and cutting paper.

**Mother:** Of course! Take this piece of paper and fold it in half. Now cut a small heart shape along the folded side.

**Child:** I cut it! Let me open it up... wow! It is a full, perfect heart! Both sides are exactly the same.

**Mother:** That is because the fold created a line of symmetry. Now try folding the paper twice before cutting.

**Child:** Okay, folded twice, cutting a small triangle... opening it up... there are four triangles! One in each section!

**Mother:** Two folds give you two lines of symmetry. The cut gets copied four times. This is how people make beautiful paper snowflakes and rangoli patterns for decorations.

**Child:** I want to make Diwali decorations this way!

**Mother:** Wonderful idea. We will make a whole garland of cut-out diyas this Diwali.
