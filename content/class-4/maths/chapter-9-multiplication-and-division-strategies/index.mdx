## Multiples and Skip Counting

When you count by jumping equal steps, the numbers you land on are called **multiples**.

Imagine hopping along a number line:

| Hop size | Numbers you land on (multiples) |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| By 3s | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24... |
| By 4s | 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32... |
| By 6s | 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48... |
| By 8s | 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56, 64... |

The numbers 3, 6, 9, 12... are called **multiples of 3** because you get them by multiplying 3 by 1, 2, 3, 4...

### Common Multiples

When two different hop sizes land on the **same number**, that number is a **common multiple**.

- Look at multiples of 3 and multiples of 4. Both include **12, 24, 36...**
- Look at multiples of 6 and multiples of 8. Both include **24, 48, 72...**

> **Notice:** To reach 24, you can hop by 3 (eight times) or hop by 4 (six times) or hop by 6 (four times) or hop by 8 (three times). One number, many ways to reach it!

## Multiplication as Equal Groups

**Multiplication** is a quick way to add the same number over and over.

### The Building Blocks

In 8 x 5 = 40:
- **8** is the number of groups
- **5** is how many are in each group
- **40** is the **product** (the total)

### Example: Flowers at a Pooja

Aarti is stringing marigold garlands. Each garland uses **6 flowers**. She has 12 garlands to make.

- Flowers for 10 garlands = 10 x 6 = 60
- Flowers for 2 garlands = 2 x 6 = 12
- Flowers for 12 garlands = 60 + 12 = **72 flowers**

> **Helpful trick:** Split the number of groups into tens and ones. Multiply each part separately, then add the results together.

## Patterns in the Times Table

The times table from 1 to 10 is full of interesting patterns:

1. The **2 times table** gives only even numbers: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10...
2. The **5 times table** always ends in 0 or 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25...
3. **Order does not matter:** 4 x 7 and 7 x 4 both give 28. So row 7 and column 7 in the table have the same numbers.
4. Multiplying by an **even** number always gives an even product.
5. The **9 times table** digits always add up to 9: 9, 18 (1+8=9), 27 (2+7=9), 36 (3+6=9)...

## The Doubling Trick

**Doubling** means multiplying by 2. It is one of the handiest tricks in maths!

| Number | Its double |
|--------|-----------|
| 18 | 36 |
| 35 | 70 |
| 48 | 96 |
| 75 | 150 |
| 125 | 250 |
| 250 | 500 |
| 499 | 998 |

> **Always true:** Doubling any number always gives an **even** answer. There is no way to get an odd number by doubling!

## Multiplying by Tens

When one of the numbers ends in zero, use this shortcut:

| Calculation | Think this way | Answer |
|------------|---------------|--------|
| 10 x 6 | 1 ten x 6 = 6 tens | 60 |
| 20 x 5 | 2 tens x 5 = 10 tens | 100 |
| 30 x 7 | 3 tens x 7 = 21 tens | 210 |
| 40 x 9 | 4 tens x 9 = 36 tens | 360 |

> **Shortcut:** Multiply the front digits first, then write a zero at the end. 30 x 7: do 3 x 7 = 21, then put a 0 to get 210.

## Multiplying 2-Digit by 1-Digit Numbers

Break the bigger number into **tens and ones**, handle each part, then combine.

### Example: 16 x 5 = ?

| Part | Working | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| 10 x 5 | Ten groups of 5 | 50 |
| 6 x 5 | Six groups of 5 | 30 |
| **Total** | 50 + 30 | **80** |

### Example: 27 x 4 = ?

| Part | Working | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| 20 x 4 | Twenty groups of 4 | 80 |
| 7 x 4 | Seven groups of 4 | 28 |
| **Total** | 80 + 28 | **108** |

## Multiplying by Hundreds

The same approach works with hundreds:

| Basic fact | Times 10 | Times 100 |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| 4 x 3 = 12 | 40 x 3 = 120 | 400 x 3 = 1200 |
| 5 x 6 = 30 | 50 x 6 = 300 | 500 x 6 = 3000 |
| 7 x 2 = 14 | 70 x 2 = 140 | 700 x 2 = 1400 |

> **Pattern:** Multiplying by a number in the hundreds? Do the basic multiplication first, then add two zeros at the end.

## Division — Splitting into Equal Parts

**Division** is the reverse of multiplication. It answers: "How many equal groups can I make?" or "How many in each group?"

### Example: 75 marbles shared among 4 friends

How many marbles does each friend get?

- Give 10 each: 10 x 4 = 40 marbles used. Left: 75 - 40 = 35
- Give 5 more each: 5 x 4 = 20 marbles used. Left: 35 - 20 = 15
- Give 3 more each: 3 x 4 = 12 marbles used. Left: 15 - 12 = 3

Each friend gets 10 + 5 + 3 = **18 marbles**, with **3 left over**

> **Important:** The amount left over is the **remainder**. It is always less than the number you divide by. Here, the remainder (3) is less than 4.

## Patterns in Division

Division has neat patterns with tens and hundreds too:

| Simple | Dividing bigger numbers |
|--------|----------------------|
| 8 / 2 = 4 | 80 / 2 = 40, 800 / 2 = 400 |
| 12 / 4 = 3 | 120 / 4 = 30, 1200 / 4 = 300 |
| 15 / 5 = 3 | 150 / 5 = 30, 1500 / 5 = 300 |

## Two Kinds of Division Questions

### Sharing (How many in each group?)

"Dadi has 84 laddoos to put equally into 7 boxes. How many laddoos per box?"
84 / 7 = **12 laddoos per box**

### Grouping (How many groups?)

"A factory has 65 wheels. Each auto-rickshaw needs 3 wheels. How many autos can be built?"
65 / 3 = **21 autos** (with **2 wheels remaining**)

## True, Sometimes True, or Never True?

| Statement | Answer |
|-----------|--------|
| Multiplying by 10 puts a 0 in the ones place | **Always true** |
| Doubling a number gives an odd answer | **Never true** |
| Multiplying by 5 gives a number ending in 5 | **Sometimes true** (only when multiplied by an odd number) |
| An even number comes right after every odd number | **Always true** |
| Halving a number gives a whole number | **Sometimes true** (only when the original is even) |
| Adding 0 to a number changes it | **Never true** |

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Multiple** | The result of multiplying a number by 1, 2, 3, 4... (e.g., multiples of 5 are 5, 10, 15, 20...) |
| **Common multiple** | A number that appears in the multiples of two or more numbers |
| **Multiplication** | Finding the total of equal groups |
| **Product** | The answer you get after multiplying |
| **Division** | Splitting a total into equal groups |
| **Remainder** | What is left over after dividing evenly |
| **Doubling** | Multiplying a number by 2 |
| **Halving** | Dividing a number by 2 |
| **Array** | Objects arranged neatly in rows and columns |
