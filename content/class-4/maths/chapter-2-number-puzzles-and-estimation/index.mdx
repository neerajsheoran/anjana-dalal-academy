## Seeing Objects from Different Views

Have you ever climbed to the terrace of your building and looked down at a parked auto-rickshaw? From up there, it looks completely different from how it looks when you stand next to it on the road! The same object appears different depending on **where you are looking from**.

There are three main ways to look at any object:

| View | What it means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Front view** | What you see standing right in front | Looking at your school bag from the front |
| **Side view** | What you see from the left or right side | Looking at your school bag from the side |
| **Top view** | What you see looking straight down from above | Looking at your school bag from above |

### A Tiffin Box from Three Views

Imagine three children drawing the same steel tiffin box:
- Rohan draws it from the **top** — he sees a rectangle (the lid)
- Meera draws it from the **front** — she sees a shorter, wider rectangle
- Kabir draws it from the **side** — he sees a narrower rectangle

All three drawings look different, but they are all the **same tiffin box**!

> **Try this:** Place your water bottle on the dining table. Sketch what it looks like from the front, then from the side, and finally look down from above and draw that too. Three different pictures of the same bottle!

## Views of Common Objects

Here are some everyday items from your home and how they look from different angles:

| Object | Front View | Top View |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Steel glass (tumbler)** | A rectangle | A circle |
| **Notebook** | A thin line (edge) | A big rectangle |
| **Cricket ball** | A circle | A circle |
| **Party cap (cone shape)** | A triangle | A circle |
| **Chalk piece** | A thin rectangle | A tiny circle |

> **Key idea:** A **cricket ball** looks the same no matter which direction you look at it — always a circle! But most objects change their appearance based on your viewing angle.

## Building Models and Drawing Views

You can stack empty matchboxes or small cardboard boxes to make a mini-building, then draw its views:

- **Front view** — stand facing the model and draw exactly what your eyes see
- **Back view** — walk behind the model and draw from there
- **Top view** — stand on a chair (carefully!) and look straight down

The front and back views of a building are often quite different — the front might have a grand entrance while the back has a small service door.

## Describing Positions

When we want to tell someone exactly where an object is placed, we use specific words:

- **Row** — a line going from left to right (like seats in a cinema hall)
- **Column** — a line going from top to bottom (like pillars standing in a line)
- **Top**, **bottom**, **left**, **right**, **middle**, **corner**

### Example: In a Classroom

Picture a classroom with 3 rows and 3 columns of desks:
- "Priya sits at the **second desk** in the **first row**"
- "The red pencil box is on the **third desk** in the **second row**"
- "The teacher's flower vase is at the **middle desk** in the **third row**"

> **Tip:** To describe any position clearly, always mention both the **row** and the **column** — or use helpful words like corner, middle, top, or bottom.

## Using a Grid

A **grid** is a set of squares arranged in rows and columns — just like a chess board or a calendar. We use grids to place objects and find them easily.

```
         Column 1    Column 2    Column 3
Row 3   [mango  ]   [          ] [kite   ]
Row 2   [          ] [star     ] [       ]
Row 1   [top    ]   [diya     ] [       ]
```

In this grid:
- The **top** (spinning top) is at Row 1, Column 1 (bottom left corner)
- The **kite** is at Row 3, Column 3 (top right corner)
- The **star** is at Row 2, Column 2 (the middle)

---

## Moving on a Grid — Steps and Paths

On a grid, you move in four directions:
- **Left** or **Right** (along a row)
- **Up** or **Down** (along a column)

You count how many **steps** it takes to travel from one spot to another.

### Example

To travel from the bottom-left corner to a toy placed 3 steps to the right and 2 steps up, you would say:
- "Move 3 steps to the right, then 2 steps up."

There are often **many different paths** to reach the same spot! Some paths are longer, some are shorter. The **shortest path** always uses the fewest total steps.

> **Rule:** In most grid games, you can only move up, down, left, or right — **no diagonal moves** allowed!

## Reading a Map

A **map** shows a place as if you are a bird flying above it, looking straight down. It helps you understand where things are located and how to travel between them.

### Using a School Map

Imagine a map of your school showing:
- Classrooms, the library, the assembly ground, the principal's office, the canteen, the sports room

To use a map:
1. **Spot your location** on the map
2. **Find your destination**
3. **Trace the route** along corridors and pathways
4. **Describe the path** using directions and landmark names

### Giving Directions

When guiding someone, use clear words like:
- "Walk straight from the main gate"
- "Take a right turn at the assembly ground"
- "Pass the canteen on your left"
- "The library is the second door on your right"

> **Activity:** Draw a simple map of your house showing all the rooms. Now write step-by-step directions from the front door to the kitchen!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Front view** | How an object looks when seen from the front |
| **Side view** | How an object looks when seen from the left or right |
| **Top view** | How an object looks when seen from above |
| **Row** | A horizontal line of items (going left to right) |
| **Column** | A vertical line of items (going top to bottom) |
| **Grid** | A pattern of rows and columns used for placing and locating objects |
| **Position** | The exact spot where something is (described by row and column) |
| **Path** | The route you take to travel from one place to another |
| **Steps** | The number of moves needed to travel between two spots on a grid |
| **Map** | A drawing of a place as seen from above, showing locations and paths |
| **Directions** | Words like left, right, straight, up, down that describe a route |
