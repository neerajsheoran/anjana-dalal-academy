## Shapes in Buildings

Take a walk around your neighbourhood and notice — shapes are hiding everywhere! When Aanya visited Jaipur with her grandmother, she was amazed by the beautiful **Hawa Mahal**, the grand **Jantar Mantar**, and the mighty **Amer Fort**. Each building told a story through shapes — arched doorways, square windows, round domes, and tall rectangular walls.

Even the famous **Charminar** in Hyderabad is full of shapes! It has **4 grand arches** and **4 tall towers** called minarets. If you look carefully, the arches are shaped like half-circles, and the towers are like long cylinders reaching up to the sky.

> **Did you know?** Long ago, builders in India used mud bricks and carved stones to create temples and forts. Today we use cement blocks, steel, and glass. But one thing stays the same — every building block is a **3D shape**, usually a rectangular block!

## 3D Shapes — Prisms and Pyramids

Three-dimensional (3D) shapes are shapes you can pick up and hold. They have **length**, **width**, and **height** — like a lunch box, a cricket ball, or a tin of biscuits.

### Prisms

A **prism** is a 3D shape that has two matching flat faces (called bases) joined by rectangular faces on the sides. Imagine slicing through a prism anywhere in the middle — you would always get the same shape!

| Prism | Base Shape | Number of Faces |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Triangular Prism** | Triangle | 5 |
| **Square Prism (Cube)** | Square | 6 |
| **Rectangular Prism (Cuboid)** | Rectangle | 6 |
| **Hexagonal Prism** | Hexagon | 8 |

> **Remember:** The name of a prism comes from its base. A prism with a triangular base is a triangular prism. A prism with a hexagonal base is a hexagonal prism!

### Pyramids

A **pyramid** has just one base at the bottom, and all its other faces are **triangles** that slope upward and meet at a single tip on top.

| Pyramid | Base Shape | Number of Faces |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Triangular Pyramid** | Triangle | 4 |
| **Square Pyramid** | Square | 5 |
| **Pentagonal Pyramid** | Pentagon | 6 |

> **Fun fact:** The ancient Egyptians built square pyramids, but did you know that many Indian temples also use pyramid-shaped towers called **vimanas**?

## Faces, Edges, and Corners

Every 3D shape has three key parts:

- **Face (F)** — a flat or curved surface you can touch
- **Edge (E)** — the line formed where two faces join together
- **Corner / Vertex (V)** — the sharp point where edges come together

| Shape | Faces (F) | Corners (V) | Edges (E) |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Cube** | 6 | 8 | 12 |
| **Cuboid** | 6 | 8 | 12 |
| **Triangular Pyramid** | 4 | 4 | 6 |
| **Square Pyramid** | 5 | 5 | 8 |
| **Triangular Prism** | 5 | 6 | 9 |

### Euler's Formula

A brilliant mathematician named Euler discovered a magical rule that works for every 3D shape with flat faces:

**F + V - E = 2**

This means: **Faces + Corners - Edges = 2**, always!

Let us check with a cube: 6 + 8 - 12 = **2** (It works!)
Let us check with a square pyramid: 5 + 5 - 8 = **2** (It works again!)

## Nets of 3D Shapes

A **net** is the flat pattern you get when you carefully cut open a 3D shape and spread it out. If you fold the net back along the lines, the 3D shape comes back together!

Picture cutting open an empty chai box along its edges and laying it flat on a table. That flat shape is the net of a cuboid!

- A **cube** net shows 6 squares connected together
- A **triangular prism** net shows 2 triangles and 3 rectangles
- A **square pyramid** net shows 1 square with 4 triangles attached to its sides

> **Activity:** Find an empty cardboard box at home — a medicine box or a toothpaste box works well. Cut it carefully along the edges and flatten it out. You have just made a net! Can you fold it back into a box?

## Sorting 3D Shapes

We can group 3D shapes based on different features:

**By their faces:**
- Shapes with all identical faces (like a cube — all 6 faces are squares)
- Shapes with different types of faces (like a triangular prism — it has both triangles and rectangles)

**By their edges:**
- Shapes with only straight edges (like a cube or pyramid)
- Shapes with curved surfaces (like a cone or cylinder)

**By the type of side faces:**
- Only rectangular side faces (prisms)
- Only triangular side faces (pyramids)
- A mix of both

## When Lines Meet — Angles

Whenever two straight lines meet at a point, they form an **angle**. Angles are all around you — in the corner of your notebook, in the hands of a clock, in the way a door opens, and even in the shape of the letter "L".

### Types of Angles

**1. Right Angle**

A **right angle** looks like a perfect corner — just like the corner of your textbook or the edge of a chalkboard. It measures exactly 90 degrees. You can make a right angle yourself — take a sheet of paper, fold it in half, then fold it in half once more. The folded corner gives you a perfect right angle!

**2. Acute Angle**

An **acute angle** is **smaller than a right angle**. Think of a pair of scissors that are barely open, or the pointy top of a samosa.

**3. Obtuse Angle**

An **obtuse angle** is **wider than a right angle**. Imagine opening a book until it lies almost flat, or think of a reclining chair leaning way back.

> **Quick test:** Place the corner of a folded paper against any angle. If the angle fits perfectly inside the paper corner, it is a right angle. If it is narrower, it is acute. If it is wider, it is obtuse.

## Shapes with Straws — Rigidity

Here is a fun experiment using drinking straws and small balls of clay:

- Build a **triangle** using 3 straws and 3 clay balls. Now push one of the sides gently. Does the shape bend or collapse? **No!** The triangle holds its shape firmly. It is **rigid**.
- Now build a **rectangle** using 4 straws and 4 clay balls. Push one side gently. What happens? The rectangle tilts and becomes a slanted shape (called a parallelogram). Its right angles change into acute and obtuse angles.

> **That is why engineers use triangles in bridges, electric towers, and rooftops** — triangles never lose their shape, no matter how much force is applied!

## Rectangles and Squares

A **rectangle** has:
- 4 sides (the two long sides are equal, and the two short sides are equal)
- 4 right angles (every corner is 90 degrees)

A **square** has:
- 4 sides (all four sides are exactly the same length)
- 4 right angles

> **Think about it:** Every square is actually a rectangle too — a special rectangle where all four sides happen to be equal!

## Circles — Centre, Radius, and Diameter

A **circle** is a perfectly round shape. Every point on the circle is at the **exact same distance** from one special point in the middle.

### Parts of a Circle

- **Centre** — the special point right in the middle
- **Radius** — the distance from the centre to any point on the circle
- **Diameter** — the distance from one side of the circle to the other, passing straight through the centre

### How Radius and Diameter are Related

The **diameter** is always **twice** the radius.

**Diameter = 2 x Radius**

And the **radius** is always **half** the diameter.

If the radius of a bangle is 3 cm, its diameter is 6 cm.

> **Try this at home:** Cut a circle out of paper. Fold it exactly in half — the crease line is a diameter! Now fold it in half again — the distance from the centre to the curved edge is the radius. Notice that every diameter passes through the centre!

You can draw perfect circles using a **compass**. The gap you set between the compass needle and the pencil tip is the **radius**.

### Circles in Real Life

Every **wheel** is a circle! A bullock cart's wheel has a bigger radius than a bicycle wheel, which is why it appears larger. The bangles on your wrist, the rim of a steel glass, and even the base of a diya — all circles!

## Sides and Angles — A Special Relationship

Flat shapes (2D shapes) follow a neat rule:

| Shape | Number of Sides | Number of Angles |
|-------|----------------|-----------------|
| Triangle | 3 | 3 |
| Rectangle | 4 | 4 |
| Pentagon | 5 | 5 |
| Hexagon | 6 | 6 |

The **number of sides** always **equals** the **number of angles** in any flat shape!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **3D shape** | A shape with length, width, and height that you can hold (e.g., cube, sphere) |
| **Face** | A flat or curved surface on a 3D shape |
| **Edge** | The line where two faces of a 3D shape meet |
| **Corner (Vertex)** | The point where edges come together |
| **Prism** | A 3D shape with two identical bases connected by rectangular side faces |
| **Pyramid** | A 3D shape with one base and triangular faces meeting at a tip |
| **Net** | A flat pattern that folds up into a 3D shape |
| **Euler's formula** | F + V - E = 2 (faces + corners - edges = 2) |
| **Angle** | The opening formed when two lines meet at a point |
| **Right angle** | An angle of exactly 90 degrees, like a notebook corner |
| **Acute angle** | An angle narrower than a right angle |
| **Obtuse angle** | An angle wider than a right angle |
| **Rigid shape** | A shape that keeps its form when pushed (e.g., triangle) |
| **Centre** | The middle point of a circle |
| **Radius** | Distance from the centre to the edge of a circle |
| **Diameter** | Distance across a circle through the centre (= 2 x radius) |
