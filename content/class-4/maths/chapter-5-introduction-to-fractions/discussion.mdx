## Parts and Wholes

**Child:** Maa, today we learned about fractions! Our teacher said when we share equally, we get fractions.

**Mother:** That is right! We use fractions every day without realizing it. When I cut a roti in half for you and your sister, each of you gets 1/2.

**Child:** And if Papa and Dadi also want a piece, we cut it into 4 parts and each person gets 1/4!

**Mother:** Exactly! The more people sharing, the smaller each piece becomes. That is why 1/4 is smaller than 1/2.

**Child:** Maa, Neha at school asked her brother whether he wanted half a chocolate or two quarters. He chose two quarters thinking it was more!

**Mother:** Ha! But 1/2 is the same as 2/4. Two quarters together make one half. He did not get any extra!

**Child:** I know! Our teacher showed us that on the board. Those are called... equivalent fractions!

**Mother:** Very good! You remembered the name too.

## Sharing — The Paratha Story

**Child:** Maa, if you make one paratha and 3 of us want to share it, what fraction does each person get?

**Mother:** If 3 people share equally, each gets 1/3 — one-third.

**Child:** And if 6 people want to share the same paratha?

**Mother:** Each gets 1/6 — one-sixth. See how the piece gets smaller?

**Child:** Yes! With more people, smaller pieces. So 1/6 is smaller than 1/3.

**Mother:** That is the golden rule of fractions — when the top number is the same, the bigger the bottom number, the smaller the fraction. Think of it this way: would you rather share a paratha with 2 friends or 10 friends?

**Child:** 2 friends! I would get a bigger piece!

**Mother:** And that is exactly why 1/3 is bigger than 1/11. Fewer people sharing means bigger portions!

## Understanding Fractions

**Child:** Maa, what do the top and bottom numbers of a fraction mean?

**Mother:** The bottom number — the denominator — tells you how many equal pieces the whole thing was cut into. The top number — the numerator — tells you how many of those pieces you are talking about.

**Child:** So in 3/4, the roti was cut into 4 pieces, and I am talking about 3 of them?

**Mother:** Perfect! And what would 4/4 mean?

**Child:** All 4 pieces out of 4... that is the whole roti!

**Mother:** Yes! Whenever the top number equals the bottom number, you have 1 whole. 5/5 = 1 whole. 10/10 = 1 whole.

**Child:** And what about 0/4?

**Mother:** Zero pieces out of 4 — that means nothing! 0/4 = 0. You have none of the pieces.

**Child:** Fractions make so much sense when you explain them with food, Maa!

## Fractions of a Group

**Child:** Maa, can fractions work with groups of things too? Like if I have 12 laddoos?

**Mother:** Absolutely! What is 1/2 of 12 laddoos?

**Child:** 12 divided by 2 equals 6. So half of 12 laddoos is 6 laddoos!

**Mother:** Now try 1/3 of 12.

**Child:** 12 divided by 3 equals 4. So one-third is 4 laddoos.

**Mother:** And 1/4 of 12?

**Child:** 12 divided by 4 equals 3. Three laddoos!

**Mother:** Notice the pattern? As the denominator gets bigger, the fraction of the group gets smaller. You get fewer laddoos each time!

**Child:** Yes! 6, then 4, then 3. It keeps getting smaller.

**Mother:** Now imagine your class has 24 students. If 1/4 of them like drawing, how many is that?

**Child:** 24 divided by 4 equals 6. Six students like drawing!

**Mother:** Wonderful! You can find a fraction of any group by dividing.

## Equal Fractions (Equivalent Fractions)

**Child:** Maa, how can 1/2 and 2/4 be the same thing? They look so different!

**Mother:** Let me show you with this roti. I will cut it in half — see, two equal pieces. Now I will cut each half again. How many pieces now?

**Child:** Four pieces!

**Mother:** And how many pieces were originally in one half?

**Child:** Two of the four pieces!

**Mother:** So 1 out of 2 halves is the same as 2 out of 4 quarters. 1/2 = 2/4. The amount of roti has not changed — only the number of cuts changed.

**Child:** Oh! So equivalent fractions are just the same amount shown with different numbers of cuts?

**Mother:** Beautifully said! You can make equivalent fractions by multiplying both the top and bottom by the same number. 1/3 becomes 2/6 (multiply by 2), or 3/9 (multiply by 3). All the same amount!

**Child:** That is so cool. Fractions are like disguises — same amount, different looks!
