## Measuring Length

**Length** tells us how long, short, or tall something is.

Before rulers were common, people measured things using parts of their body!

### The Tailor's Trouble

Raju the tailor in a village was making a kurta for the sarpanch. The sarpanch said, "Make it **6 handspans** long." Raju measured with his own hands and stitched the kurta. But when the sarpanch tried it on, it was too short!

Why? Because Raju's hands were **smaller** than the sarpanch's hands. When Raju counted 6 handspans, it was actually less cloth than what the sarpanch meant.

> **This shows us:** Different people have different-sized hands. That is why we need **standard units** like centimetres and metres. But first, let us practise measuring with everyday things!

### Ways to Measure Length

We can use things around us to measure:

| What We Use | How It Works |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Handspan** | Stretch your hand wide — measure from thumb tip to little finger tip |
| **Footstep** | Walk heel to toe and count your steps |
| **Eraser** | Place erasers end to end for small things |
| **Pencil** | Line up pencils for medium things |
| **Arm length** | Stretch your arm out for bigger things |

> **Important:** Your measurement may be different from your friend's. That is because your hands and feet are not the same size. Both answers are correct for your own body!

### Comparing Paths

Deepa walks to her friend Nisha's house. One road goes straight, and another road goes around the park. Which path is shorter?

The **straight path** is shorter. A **curved or winding path** always covers more distance than a straight one to the same place.

> **Try this at home:** Take two pieces of thread. Lay one along a straight line and the other along a curvy line. Now stretch both threads straight and compare. The curvy thread will be longer!

### Measuring Height with Blocks

You can find out how tall things are by stacking small blocks next to them.

Aarav measured his toys with blocks:
- His teddy bear is about **8 blocks** tall.
- His toy robot is about **6 blocks** tall.
- His toy duck is about **3 blocks** tall.

The teddy bear is the **tallest** and the toy duck is the **shortest**.

## Heavy and Light (Weight)

Some things are **heavy** and some things are **light**. We find this out by **lifting** or **weighing** them.

### A Trip to the Sabzi Mandi

Rani went to the vegetable market with her Maa. She tried picking up different vegetables:

- The **kaddu (pumpkin)** was so heavy she could not lift it alone!
- The **tarbuz (watermelon)** was also very heavy.
- The **gobhi (cauliflower)** was lighter than the pumpkin but still a bit heavy.
- The **shimla mirch (capsicum)** was easy to carry.
- The **gajar (carrot)** was light.
- The **hari mirch (green chilli)** was the lightest of all — as light as a feather!

| Vegetable | How Heavy? |
|-----------|-----------|
| Kaddu (Pumpkin) | Heaviest |
| Tarbuz (Watermelon) | Very heavy |
| Gobhi (Cauliflower) | Medium heavy |
| Shimla Mirch (Capsicum) | Medium |
| Gajar (Carrot) | Light |
| Hari Mirch (Green Chilli) | Lightest |

### Comparing Weight with Your Hands

Pick up one thing in your left hand and another in your right hand. The hand that goes **down** holds the **heavier** thing. The hand that stays **up** holds the **lighter** thing.

### The See-Saw Test

Think of a see-saw in a park. If a big watermelon sits on one side and a small mango on the other, the watermelon side goes **down**. That means the watermelon is **heavier**. To make both sides level, you would need **many mangoes** on the other side.

### Make Your Own Balance

You can build a simple balance at home!

**Things you need:** A clothes hanger, 2 small plastic bags (or paper cups), and some string.

1. Tie a piece of string to each bag.
2. Hang one bag from each end of the hanger.
3. Hold the hanger up or hang it on a hook.
4. Put one object in each bag.
5. The bag that drops lower has the **heavier** object.

> **Remember:** When both bags stay at the same level, the two objects have **equal weight** — they are **balanced**.

## Capacity (How Much Liquid)

**Capacity** tells us how much liquid a container can hold.

### Chai for the Family

Sunita's Maa is making chai for the family. For **1 cup** of chai, she uses:

| Ingredient | How Much |
|------------|----------|
| Water | 1 cup |
| Milk | Half a cup |
| Sugar | 1 spoon |
| Tea leaves | 1 pinch |

If she wants to make chai for **4 people** (4 cups), she needs:

| Ingredient | How Much |
|------------|----------|
| Water | 4 cups |
| Milk | 2 cups (4 halves) |
| Sugar | 4 spoons |
| Tea leaves | 4 pinches |

> **Tip:** When we make more servings, we multiply each ingredient by the number of cups we want.

### Which Holds More?

Different containers hold different amounts of water.

- A **matka (clay pot)** can hold about **10 glasses** of water.
- A **lota (small brass pot)** holds about **2 glasses** of water.
- A **bucket** holds much more than a lota.

The matka has **more capacity** than the lota. You can compare them by counting how many glasses of water each one holds.

### Jal Tarang — Music from Water

Here is a fun experiment! Take 5 to 7 similar steel glasses. Pour **different amounts** of water into each glass. Now tap each glass gently with a metal spoon. Every glass makes a **different sound**!

- A glass with **less water** makes a **higher-pitched** sound.
- A glass with **more water** makes a **deeper** sound.

This is called **Jal Tarang** — a musical instrument used in Indian classical music!

> **Good habit:** Water is precious. Do not waste water during experiments. Pour it back into the bucket when you are done!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Length** | How long or short something is |
| **Handspan** | Distance from thumb tip to little finger tip with hand stretched wide |
| **Measure** | To find out how long, heavy, or full something is |
| **Heavier** | Weighing more than another thing |
| **Lighter** | Weighing less than another thing |
| **Balance** | A tool to compare the weight of two objects |
| **Capacity** | How much liquid a container can hold |
| **Taller** | Having more height |
| **Shorter** | Having less height |
