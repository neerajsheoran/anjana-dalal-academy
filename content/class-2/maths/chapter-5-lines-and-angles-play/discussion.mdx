## Types of Lines

**Child:** Maa, teacher said there are different types of lines. But to me, all lines look the same!

**Mother:** Let me show you, beta! Stand up straight like a soldier. Your body is making a line that goes up and down. What kind of line do you think that is?

**Child:** An up-and-down line?

**Mother:** We call it a **standing line** or **vertical line**. Now lie down flat on the floor.

**Child:** Okay, now I am flat like a sleeping line!

**Mother:** Exactly! A **sleeping line** or **horizontal line** goes from left to right, flat like the ground. Now lean against the wall like this -- at an angle.

**Child:** Like a slide in the park?

**Mother:** Yes! That is a **slanting line** or **diagonal line**. It tilts to one side. And one more -- look at the bangle on my wrist. What kind of line does it make?

**Child:** It is round... it bends! That is a **curved line**!

**Mother:** Perfect! So we have four types: standing, sleeping, slanting, and curved. The first three are all straight lines. Only the curved line is not straight.

**Child:** Oh, so standing, sleeping, and slanting are all straight -- just going in different directions!

**Mother:** Exactly right, beta!

## Lines in Letters and Nature

**Child:** Maa, can I find these lines in things around me?

**Mother:** Of course! Let us start with something you see every day -- letters of the alphabet. Look at the letter **L**. What lines does it have?

**Child:** It has a line going down... that is a standing line! And a line going to the right... that is a sleeping line!

**Mother:** Very good! What about the letter **V**?

**Child:** Both lines are slanting! They go at angles and meet at the bottom.

**Mother:** And the letter **O**?

**Child:** That is all curved! Like a circle!

**Mother:** Now let us look outside the window. See that bamboo tree? Its trunk is a...

**Child:** Standing line!

**Mother:** And the ground below it?

**Child:** A sleeping line!

**Mother:** And that hill in the distance?

**Child:** Its slope is a slanting line! And the rainbow from yesterday was a curved line!

**Mother:** You are finding lines everywhere! That is exactly what mathematicians do -- they see patterns and shapes in the world around them. Even Indian artists use these lines. Madhubani paintings from Bihar use bold straight and curved lines to draw fish and flowers.

**Child:** I want to draw like that too, Maa!

## Lines from Paper Folding

**Child:** Maa, today teacher asked us to fold paper and look at the lines. She called them creases. What is a crease?

**Mother:** A crease is the line left behind when you fold paper. Let us try it! Take this sheet of paper and fold it from top to bottom.

**Child:** Done! Now I see a line going left to right when I open it. That is a sleeping crease!

**Mother:** Now fold a new paper from left to right.

**Child:** This one has a line going up and down. A standing crease!

**Mother:** Wonderful! Now here is a tricky one. Fold the paper from one corner to the opposite corner -- diagonally.

**Child:** Oh! The crease goes at an angle. That is a slanting crease!

**Mother:** See how folding in different directions gives you different types of creases? Now here is a fun challenge -- fold a paper twice, once from top to bottom and once from left to right. How many creases do you get?

**Child:** Let me try... I see 2 crease lines! One sleeping and one standing! They cross each other in the middle like a plus sign!

**Mother:** Brilliant! Now can you get a curved crease?

**Child:** Hmm... no matter how I fold, the crease is always straight!

**Mother:** That is a great discovery! Paper folds only make straight creases -- standing, sleeping, or slanting. You cannot make a curved crease by folding!

**Child:** That makes sense because the paper goes flat against itself when I fold it!
