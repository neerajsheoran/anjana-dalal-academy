## Lines in Yoga

When we practise **yoga**, our body creates different types of lines!

- In **Tadasana** (Mountain Pose), you stand tall like a pillar. Your body makes a **standing line**.
- In **Trikonasana** (Triangle Pose), your arms and legs stretch at angles, forming **slanting lines**.
- In **Chakrasana** (Wheel Pose), your back bends into a smooth **curved line**.
- When you lie flat in **Shavasana** (Resting Pose), your body makes a **sleeping line**.

> **Fun Fact:** Yoga was born in **India** thousands of years ago. It is described in the ancient **Yoga Sutras of Patanjali** and is now practised all over the world!

## Types of Lines

There are four main types of lines we see around us:

### 1. Standing Line (Vertical Line)

A standing line goes **straight up and down**, like a temple pillar or an electric pole.

```
  |
  |
  |
  |
```

**Where you see it:** Flag poles, tree trunks, pillars, the sides of a door frame.

### 2. Sleeping Line (Horizontal Line)

A sleeping line goes **flat from left to right**, like the surface of still water in a lake.

```
  ___________
```

**Where you see it:** The edge of a table, a ruler lying flat, the horizon where sky meets earth, railway tracks.

### 3. Slanting Line (Diagonal Line)

A slanting line goes at an **angle** -- it tilts to one side. It is neither standing nor sleeping.

```
    /        \
   /          \
  /            \
```

**Where you see it:** A slide in a park, a leaning ladder, the sides of a tent, the string of a kite flying in the breeze.

### 4. Curved Line

A curved line **bends gently** without any sharp turns. It is not straight at all.

```
   ~~~~~
```

**Where you see it:** A rainbow after the rain, a bangle on a wrist, a snake moving on the ground, a river seen from above.

## Straight or Not?

Take a piece of string and hold it tightly between both hands. Stretch it out -- it becomes a **straight line**.

Now let go of one end. The string hangs down in a **curved line**. It is no longer straight!

> **Remember:** Standing lines, sleeping lines, and slanting lines are all **straight lines**. Only curved lines are not straight.

| Line Type | Straight? | Direction |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Standing (Vertical) | Yes | Up and down |
| Sleeping (Horizontal) | Yes | Left and right |
| Slanting (Diagonal) | Yes | At an angle |
| Curved | No | Bends gently |

## Lines in Letters and Nature

Pick any picture or object near you. You can find all four types of lines hiding in it!

**In a house:**
- The walls have **standing lines**.
- The floor has **sleeping lines**.
- The roof may have **slanting lines**.
- A rounded arch or a doorknob has **curved lines**.

**In nature:**
- A bamboo stalk is a **standing line**.
- The flat ground is a **sleeping line**.
- A mountain slope is a **slanting line**.
- A rainbow stretching across the sky is a lovely **curved line**.

**In letters:**
- The letter **L** has a standing line and a sleeping line.
- The letter **V** has two slanting lines.
- The letter **O** is made of curved lines.
- The letter **T** has a standing line and a sleeping line.

## Playing with Dots

You can join dots to build shapes using different lines.

- Join dots with **straight lines** (standing, sleeping, or slanting) to make triangles, squares, and rectangles.
- Join dots with **curved lines** to draw clouds, waves, flowers, and rainbows.

## Lines in Indian Art

Many traditional Indian art forms celebrate the beauty of lines:

- **Madhubani painting** from Bihar uses thick straight and curved lines to paint fish, peacocks, and lotus flowers.
- **Warli painting** from Maharashtra uses tiny triangles and circles built from straight and curved lines.
- **Kalamkari art** from Andhra Pradesh uses delicate curved lines to create detailed floral patterns on fabric.

## Lines from Paper Folding

When you fold a sheet of paper, the fold leaves a line called a **crease**.

- Fold a paper once -- you get **1 crease** (a straight line).
- Fold it again -- you get another crease.
- Different folds create **standing creases**, **sleeping creases**, or **slanting creases**.

> **Try this:** Take a square sheet of paper and fold it in different directions. Open it up and look at the creases. Can you spot standing, sleeping, and slanting lines?

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Standing line (Vertical)** | A straight line going up and down |
| **Sleeping line (Horizontal)** | A straight line going left and right |
| **Slanting line (Diagonal)** | A straight line going at an angle |
| **Curved line** | A line that bends smoothly, not straight |
| **Straight line** | A line that does not bend or curve |
| **Crease** | The line left behind when you fold paper |
