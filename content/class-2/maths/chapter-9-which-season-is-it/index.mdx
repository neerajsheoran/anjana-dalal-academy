## Seasons of India

India is a land of many colours, and each season brings its own special magic. We have **five main seasons** in a year.

### 1. Vasant (Spring)

Spring arrives and the world wakes up! **Flowers bloom** everywhere, **birds chirp** happily, and trees grow fresh green leaves. The weather is warm and pleasant — not too hot, not too cold.

**Festivals in spring:** Holi (the festival of colours), Baisakhi, Gudi Padwa, Ram Navami

### 2. Grishma (Summer)

Summer means the **hot sun** beats down all day. Days are long and nights are short. Schools close for summer holidays! We cool off with juicy mangoes, ice-cold watermelon slices, and tall glasses of lassi.

**Festivals in summer:** Buddha Purnima, Guru Purnima

### 3. Varsha (Monsoon / Rainy Season)

Dark clouds fill the sky and rain pours down! The earth smells fresh after the first shower. **Peacocks dance** in the rain, and **frogs sing** from the puddles. We carry umbrellas and wear raincoats. Everything turns lush and green.

**Festivals in monsoon:** Raksha Bandhan, Janmashtami, Independence Day (15th August)

### 4. Sharad (Autumn)

The rain stops and the sky becomes clear and blue. The air feels **cool and pleasant**. Some **leaves turn brown and fall** from trees. The nights start getting longer.

**Festivals in autumn:** Dussehra, Diwali, Sharad Purnima

### 5. Shishir (Winter)

Brrr! Winter is **cold**! We bundle up in sweaters, shawls, jackets, and woollen caps. In some parts of India, it even snows! We love eating gajak, rewri, and warm gajar ka halwa. Hot chai and soup keep us cosy.

**Festivals in winter:** Lohri, Makar Sankranti, Republic Day (26th January), Christmas

> **Did you know?** Some festivals like **Eid** and **Diwali** may fall in different seasons in different years because their dates are based on special calendars.

## Calendar (Months and Days)

A **calendar** is like a big chart that shows us every day of the year. It helps us plan birthdays, holidays, and festivals!

### Twelve Months in a Year

| Month | Days |
|-------|------|
| 1. January | 31 |
| 2. February | 28 (or 29 in a leap year) |
| 3. March | 31 |
| 4. April | 30 |
| 5. May | 31 |
| 6. June | 30 |
| 7. July | 31 |
| 8. August | 31 |
| 9. September | 30 |
| 10. October | 31 |
| 11. November | 30 |
| 12. December | 31 |

> **Trick to remember:** Months with **31 days** — January, March, May, July, August, October, December (say "Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Oct, Dec"). Months with **30 days** — April, June, September, November. And **February** is the special short month with 28 or 29 days.

### Seven Days in a Week

A week has **7 days** that repeat in a cycle:

1. **Sunday** (Ravivar)
2. **Monday** (Somvar)
3. **Tuesday** (Mangalvar)
4. **Wednesday** (Budhvar)
5. **Thursday** (Guruvar)
6. **Friday** (Shukravar)
7. **Saturday** (Shanivar)

After Saturday, Sunday comes again. The cycle never stops!

### How to Read a Calendar

Open any calendar page. Each row shows one **week**. The columns show the **days** (Sunday, Monday, etc.). The numbers are the **dates**.

To find which day a date falls on, look at the column that number is in. For example, on which day is 26th January (Republic Day)? Find 26 in the January page and check which column it is in!

## Reading a Clock and Directions

### Parts of a Clock

A clock has **two hands** that move in circles:

- The **short hand** is the **hour hand**. It moves slowly and shows which **hour** it is.
- The **long hand** is the **minute hand**. It moves faster and shows the **minutes**.

When the **long hand (minute hand)** points straight up at **12** and the short hand points to a number, we say that time as **"o'clock"**.

```
Short hand at 7, Long hand at 12  -->  7 o'clock
Short hand at 12, Long hand at 12 -->  12 o'clock
Short hand at 4, Long hand at 12  -->  4 o'clock
```

### Finding Directions

There are **four main directions**: **North, South, East, and West**.

Here is an easy way to find them:

| Direction | How to Find It |
|-----------|---------------|
| **East** | Stand outside in the morning and face the rising sun — you are looking East |
| **West** | Your back now faces West |
| **North** | Your left hand points to North |
| **South** | Your right hand points to South |

> **Memory trick:** Face the **sunrise** (East). Behind you is **West**. Stretch your arms — **left = North**, **right = South**.

### Left and Right

Your body has a **left side** and a **right side**. Most people write with their right hand. When someone says "turn left at the chai shop", they mean the direction of your left hand.

### What is a Compass?

A **compass** is a small round tool with a needle that always points to **North**. Sailors, travellers, and hikers use it to find their way. With a compass, you can figure out all four directions no matter where you are or what time it is!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Season** | A time of year with its own weather (spring, summer, monsoon, autumn, winter) |
| **Calendar** | A chart showing all the days, weeks, and months of a year |
| **Month** | One of 12 parts of a year (January to December) |
| **Week** | A group of 7 days |
| **Hour hand** | The short hand on a clock that shows the hour |
| **Minute hand** | The long hand on a clock that shows the minutes |
| **O'clock** | The time when the minute hand points to 12 |
| **Direction** | The way something faces or moves (North, South, East, West) |
| **Compass** | A tool that always points North, helping us find directions |
