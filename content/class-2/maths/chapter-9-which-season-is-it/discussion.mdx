## The Magic of Seasons

**Child:** Maa, why does it feel so cold right now? Last month it was hot!

**Mother:** That is because the **season** has changed, beta. We are now in **winter** (Shishir). A few months ago, it was **summer** (Grishma). India has five beautiful seasons!

**Child:** Five? I only know hot and cold!

**Mother:** Ha ha! Let me tell you. First comes **Vasant** — that is spring, when flowers bloom and we play Holi. Then comes **Grishma** — summer, when we eat mangoes and drink lassi. After that is **Varsha** — the rainy season, when peacocks dance and we carry umbrellas.

**Child:** I love the rain! What comes next?

**Mother:** **Sharad** — autumn. The rain stops, the sky is clear, and we celebrate Dussehra. And then comes **Shishir** — winter! We wear sweaters and eat gajak and drink hot chai.

**Child:** And then spring comes again?

**Mother:** Yes! The seasons go round and round, year after year. Like a big circle that never ends.

**Child:** Maa, which season is Diwali in?

**Mother:** Diwali usually comes in autumn, around October or November. But some festivals like Eid come in different seasons each year because they follow a different calendar.

## Days, Months, and the Calendar

**Child:** Maa, how many days until my birthday?

**Mother:** Let us check the calendar! Do you know how many months are in a year?

**Child:** Twelve!

**Mother:** Good. And how many days in a week?

**Child:** Seven — Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday!

**Mother:** Perfect. Now, some months have 31 days, some have 30, and one special month has only 28 or 29 days. Do you know which one?

**Child:** February! Teacher told us it is the shortest month.

**Mother:** Right. And in a **leap year**, February gets one extra day — 29 instead of 28. That happens once every 4 years.

**Child:** Maa, how do I know which months have 31 days?

**Mother:** Here is a trick — remember "Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Oct, Dec" — those are the 31-day months. April, June, September, November have 30 days.

**Child:** And February is the odd one out with 28!

**Mother:** Exactly. Now look at the calendar and tell me — on which day is Republic Day this year?

**Child:** Let me find 26th January... it is on a Sunday!

**Mother:** See how useful the calendar is? You can find the day for any date!

## Telling Time and Finding Your Way

**Child:** Maa, I can read the clock now! The short hand is at 6 and the long hand is at 12. So it is 6 o'clock!

**Mother:** Well done, beta! The **short hand** is the hour hand and the **long hand** is the minute hand. When the long hand points to 12, we just read the number the short hand is pointing at.

**Child:** So right now, it is 6 o'clock in the evening. That means I have been home from school for 3 hours!

**Mother:** Good thinking! You came home at 3 o'clock and now it is 6 o'clock. 6 minus 3 is 3 hours.

**Child:** Maa, teacher also taught us about directions. She said the sun rises in the East!

**Mother:** That is right. Stand facing the rising sun in the morning — that direction is **East**. Behind you is **West**. Your left hand points **North** and your right hand points **South**.

**Child:** But what if it is cloudy and I cannot see the sun?

**Mother:** Great question! Then you can use a **compass** — it is a small tool with a needle that always points to North. Once you know where North is, you can figure out all the other directions.

**Child:** Like in the pirate movies? They use a compass to find treasure!

**Mother:** Ha ha, yes! Sailors and travellers use compasses all the time. Even the maps on Maa's phone use a digital compass!

**Child:** So cool! I want a compass for my birthday, Maa!
