## Solid Shapes (3D Shapes)

**Child:** Maa, teacher said everything around us has a shape. But how do I know which shape something is?

**Mother:** That is easy, beta! Let us look around our kitchen. Pick up that steel dabba. What does it look like?

**Child:** It is round on top and bottom, and it has a curved side. Like a tube!

**Mother:** Exactly! That shape is called a **cylinder**. Now look at the bread. What shape is a slice of bread?

**Child:** It looks like a square!

**Mother:** Good! But that is a flat shape. Now pick up the whole bread loaf. Can you hold it? Does it have thickness?

**Child:** Yes! It is like a box.

**Mother:** Right. Things you can hold and feel from all sides are called **solid shapes** or **3D shapes**. A loaf of bread is shaped like a **cuboid**. A cricket ball is a **sphere**. A dice is a **cube**. A birthday cap is a **cone**.

**Child:** Oh! So a 3D shape is something I can hold in my hand, and a 2D shape is flat like a drawing!

**Mother:** Perfect understanding!

## Faces, Edges, and Corners

**Child:** Maa, teacher told us that shapes have faces, edges, and corners. But what are those?

**Mother:** Okay, pick up your tiffin box. Now touch one flat side of it. That flat side is called a **face**. How many flat sides can you count?

**Child:** Let me see... top, bottom, front, back, left, right. Six!

**Mother:** Correct! A cuboid has 6 faces. Now run your finger along the line where two sides meet. Feel that straight line?

**Child:** Yes, it is like a border!

**Mother:** That is an **edge**. And now feel the pointy spot where three edges come together...

**Child:** Oh, that sharp point at the corner!

**Mother:** That is called a **corner**! Your tiffin box has 8 corners. Now think about your cricket ball. Does it have any corners?

**Child:** No! It is perfectly smooth and round. No corners, no edges!

**Mother:** Exactly. A **sphere** has 0 corners and 0 edges. That is why it rolls so beautifully!

## Flat Shapes (2D Shapes)

**Child:** Maa, what about shapes on paper? Like when I draw a circle?

**Mother:** Those are called **flat shapes** or **2D shapes**. They only have length and width -- no thickness. You cannot pick them up from the paper!

**Child:** So a circle, a square, a triangle, and a rectangle are all 2D shapes?

**Mother:** Yes! And here is a fun way to connect 3D and 2D shapes. Take your steel glass and press its bottom onto ink, then stamp it on paper. What mark does it leave?

**Child:** A circle!

**Mother:** Right! The flat face of a cylinder is a circle. Now if you stamp a dice face, what do you get?

**Child:** A square! Because all sides of the dice face are equal!

**Mother:** And what shape is our classroom door?

**Child:** A rectangle! It has 2 long sides and 2 short sides.

**Mother:** And a samosa?

**Child:** A triangle! It has 3 sides and 3 corners!

**Mother:** See, flat shapes are hiding everywhere around you. Your one-rupee coin is a circle, your notebook is a rectangle, a floor tile is a square. Once you start looking, you will find shapes in everything!

**Child:** This is like a treasure hunt for shapes, Maa!
