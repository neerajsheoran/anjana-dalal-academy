## Measuring Things Around Us

**Child:** Maa, today teacher said we can measure things with our hands. Is that true?

**Mother:** Of course, beta! Stretch your hand out wide — from your thumb to your little finger. That distance is called a **handspan**. Try measuring the dining table with it!

**Child:** Okay... one, two, three, four, five, six! It is 6 handspans long!

**Mother:** Good! Now let me try. One, two, three, four, five. I got only 5 handspans for the same table!

**Child:** But Maa, how can the same table give different numbers?

**Mother:** Because my hands are bigger than yours, beta. One of my handspans covers more space. So I need fewer handspans to cover the same table.

**Child:** Oh! So if Dadi measures it, she might get a different answer too?

**Mother:** Exactly. That is why in the old days, there were so many mix-ups. A tailor would measure cloth with his hands, but the customer had different-sized hands! That is why people invented rulers and measuring tapes — so everyone gets the **same** answer.

## Is It Heavy or Light?

**Child:** Maa, today Rani brought a big pumpkin to class. I tried to lift it but it was so heavy!

**Mother:** Ha ha! Pumpkins are indeed very heavy. Tell me — what is heavier, a pumpkin or a green chilli?

**Child:** A pumpkin, of course! The chilli is tiny!

**Mother:** Right. Now here is a trick. Pick up a bag of rice in one hand and a packet of biscuits in the other. Which hand goes down?

**Child:** The rice hand goes down!

**Mother:** That means the rice is **heavier**. And the biscuit is **lighter**. You just compared weights using your own hands — like a human balance!

**Child:** Maa, what about a see-saw? Is that also like a balance?

**Mother:** Very smart! Yes, a see-saw works just like a balance. When Papa sits on one side and you sit on the other, his side goes down because he is heavier. To balance it, maybe two or three children need to sit on your side!

**Child:** That is funny! I want to try that in the park!

## How Much Does It Hold?

**Child:** Maa, why do you use a small cup to measure milk for chai?

**Mother:** Because **capacity** matters, beta. The cup tells me how much liquid I am using. One cup of milk for one cup of chai. If I want to make chai for all four of us, I need four cups of water and two cups of milk.

**Child:** So we just multiply everything?

**Mother:** Yes! If the recipe says 1 spoon of sugar for 1 cup, then for 4 cups I need 4 spoons of sugar.

**Child:** Maa, our matka holds so much water. How can I find out how much?

**Mother:** Fill it using a glass! Count how many glasses it takes to fill the matka completely. If it takes 10 glasses, then the matka's capacity is 10 glasses.

**Child:** And our lota would take fewer glasses?

**Mother:** Right, maybe only 2 glasses. The matka has **more capacity** than the lota. You can compare any two containers this way — just count the glasses!

**Child:** Maa, can I try making music with water glasses like teacher showed us? She called it Jal Tarang!

**Mother:** Sure! Take some steel glasses, fill them with different amounts of water, and tap them with a spoon. Each one will make a different sound. The glass with less water makes a higher sound, and the one with more water makes a deeper sound.

**Child:** That is so cool! I want to play a song!

**Mother:** Go ahead, beta. Just remember — do not waste the water. Pour it into a plant when you finish!
