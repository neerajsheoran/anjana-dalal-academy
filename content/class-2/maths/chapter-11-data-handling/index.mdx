## Collecting and Organising Data

When we gather information about things and write it down, that information is called **data**. Gathering data helps us answer interesting questions!

### Asking a Question

Suppose you want to know what **ice cream flavour** your classmates love the most. You walk around and ask each friend: "Which flavour is your favourite?"

You write down each answer:
- Chocolate, Vanilla, Mango, Chocolate, Strawberry, Mango, Chocolate, Vanilla, Mango, Mango, Chocolate, Strawberry, Vanilla, Chocolate, Mango

That is your **data**. But right now it is just a jumbled list! It is hard to see which flavour won. We need to **organise** it.

### Making a Table

The best way to sort data is to put it in a **table**. Count how many times each answer appears:

| Flavour | Number of Children |
|---------|-------------------|
| Chocolate | 5 |
| Mango | 5 |
| Vanilla | 3 |
| Strawberry | 2 |

Now everything is clear:
- **Chocolate** and **Mango** are tied for the most popular (5 children each).
- **Strawberry** is the least popular (only 2 children).
- More children like vanilla than strawberry.

### Steps to Collect Data

You can collect your own data about anything! Follow these steps:

1. **Pick a question.** Example: "What is your favourite sport?"
2. **Ask your friends.** Write down every answer.
3. **Make a table.** Count how many children chose each option.
4. **Read the table.** Find which answer is the most popular and which is the least popular.

## Reading Data Tables

Once data is in a table, we can answer all sorts of questions by reading it carefully.

### Favourite Sabzi (Vegetable)

Riya asked her classmates which sabzi they like the most:

| Sabzi | Number of Children |
|-------|-------------------|
| Aloo (Potato) | 8 |
| Palak (Spinach) | 3 |
| Gobhi (Cauliflower) | 5 |
| Matar (Peas) | 6 |
| Bhindi (Okra) | 4 |

From this table:
- The **most liked** sabzi is **aloo** (8 children). No surprise there!
- The **least liked** sabzi is **palak** (3 children).
- More children like matar than gobhi.
- Bhindi is liked more than palak but less than gobhi.

### How Do Children Come to School?

| Way of Coming | Number of Children |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Walking | 9 |
| Cycle | 4 |
| Auto | 6 |
| School bus | 7 |
| Car | 3 |

From this table:
- **Most** children come by **walking** (9 children).
- **Fewest** children come by **car** (3 children).
- More children use the school bus than the auto.

### Comparing Data

When reading tables, we use these comparing words:

| Word | What It Means |
|------|--------------|
| **More than** | A bigger number |
| **Less than / Fewer than** | A smaller number |
| **Equal to** | The same number |
| **Most** | The biggest number in the group |
| **Least / Fewest** | The smallest number in the group |

**Example:** If 8 children like aloo and 3 like palak:
- Aloo is liked by **more** children **than** palak.
- Palak is liked by **fewer** children **than** aloo.
- How many more like aloo? 8 - 3 = **5 more**.

## Pictographs

A **pictograph** is a special chart that uses **small pictures** to show data. Instead of writing numbers, we draw tiny icons — and each icon stands for **one item** (or sometimes more).

### Why Pictographs Are Useful

Pictographs make data easy to understand at a **single glance**. You just look at which row has the **most pictures** (that is the biggest group) and which has the **fewest** (that is the smallest group).

### Games Children Play

Let us show this data using a pictograph. Each star means 1 child:

| Game | Children Who Play |
|------|------------------|
| Kho-kho | 4 |
| Kabaddi | 6 |
| Cricket | 9 |
| Skipping rope | 5 |
| Carrom | 3 |

As a pictograph (each star = 1 child):

```
Kho-kho:        * * * *                 (4)
Kabaddi:        * * * * * *             (6)
Cricket:        * * * * * * * * *       (9)
Skipping rope:  * * * * *               (5)
Carrom:         * * *                   (3)
```

By looking at the rows:
- **Cricket** has the longest row — the most popular game (9 children).
- **Carrom** has the shortest row — the least popular (3 children).
- Kabaddi has more stars than skipping rope.

## Family Size Data

You can collect data about families too! Tara asked families in her mohalla how many people live in each home:

| People in Home | Number of Families |
|---------------|-------------------|
| 2 people | 2 |
| 3 people | 5 |
| 4 people | 9 |
| 5 people | 4 |
| 6 or more | 3 |

From this data:
- **Most** families have **4 people** in their home (9 families).
- The **fewest** families have only **2 people** (2 families).
- Total families Tara asked: 2 + 5 + 9 + 4 + 3 = **23 families**.

> **Activity idea:** Ask families on your street how many children they have. Make a table and see which number is the most common!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Data** | Information collected by asking questions or observing things |
| **Table** | A chart with rows and columns to organise data neatly |
| **Pictograph** | A chart that uses pictures or symbols to show data |
| **Count** | Finding how many of each type there are |
| **Most** | The biggest number in a group |
| **Least** | The smallest number in a group |
| **Compare** | Looking at numbers to see which is bigger, smaller, or equal |
| **Organise** | Arranging data in a clear and neat way |
