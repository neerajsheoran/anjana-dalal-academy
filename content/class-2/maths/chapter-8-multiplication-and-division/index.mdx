## Multiplication as Repeated Addition

Anita is helping her Maa set the dining table. She needs to put **spoons** for everyone. Each plate gets **2 spoons** (one big, one small).

If there are **5 plates**, she counts the spoons:

```
2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10
```

She added **2** five times. There is a quicker way to say this — **5 times 2 is 10**.

We write it with the **multiplication sign** (x):

```
5 x 2 = 10
```

This is called **multiplication** — a fast way to add the **same number many times**.

### More Examples from Daily Life

| Situation | Addition Way | Multiplication Way |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| 3 autos, 3 wheels each | 3 + 3 + 3 = 9 | 3 x 3 = 9 |
| 6 cars, 4 wheels each | 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 24 | 6 x 4 = 24 |
| 4 hands, 5 fingers each | 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20 | 4 x 5 = 20 |
| 2 spiders, 8 legs each | 8 + 8 = 16 | 2 x 8 = 16 |
| 5 rows of students, 10 in each row | 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 50 | 5 x 10 = 50 |

> **Tip:** Whenever you see the words "times" or "groups of", think of multiplication!

### The Order Secret

Here is something amazing about multiplication — **you can swap the numbers and still get the same answer**!

Kavya makes **3 garlands with 5 flowers each**. Neha makes **5 garlands with 3 flowers each**. Both use the same number of flowers:

```
3 x 5 = 15
5 x 3 = 15
```

This works every time:

```
4 x 6 = 24   and   6 x 4 = 24
2 x 9 = 18   and   9 x 2 = 18
```

> **Remember:** You can multiply in any order — the answer stays the same!

### Multiplication in Everyday Life

- Meena sews **3 buttons** on each frock. She has **6 frocks**. Total buttons = 6 x 3 = **18 buttons**.
- There are **5 boxes of crayons** with **10 crayons** in each box. Total crayons = 5 x 10 = **50 crayons**.
- Rohan bought **4 samosas** at Rs 5 each. Total cost = 4 x 5 = **Rs 20**.
- **3 families** go on a picnic. Each family has **4 members**. Total people = 3 x 4 = **12 people**.

## Sharing Equally (Introduction to Division)

Dadi made **12 laddoos** for her two grandchildren, Vivek and Priya. She wants to share them **equally**.

She gives one laddoo at a time to each child:
- 1 for Vivek, 1 for Priya.
- 2 for Vivek, 2 for Priya.
- 3 for Vivek, 3 for Priya.
- 4 for Vivek, 4 for Priya.
- 5 for Vivek, 5 for Priya.
- 6 for Vivek, 6 for Priya.

All 12 laddoos are shared! Each child gets **6 laddoos**.

This is called **division** — splitting a total into **equal parts**.

> **Think of it this way:** Multiplication **puts** equal groups together. Division **breaks** a total into equal groups!

### Making Equal Groups

Sita has **20 glass bangles** and wants to make gift sets with **4 bangles** in each set.

She keeps making sets until all bangles are used:
- 1st set: 4 bangles used, 16 left.
- 2nd set: 4 more used, 12 left.
- 3rd set: 4 more used, 8 left.
- 4th set: 4 more used, 4 left.
- 5th set: 4 more used, 0 left.

She made **5 gift sets** from 20 bangles!

```
20 split into groups of 4 = 5 groups
```

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Multiplication** | A quick way to add the same number many times |
| **Times (x)** | The sign used for multiplication |
| **Groups of** | Equal-sized collections of things |
| **Multiplication table** | A list of results from multiplying a number by 1 through 10 |
| **Division** | Splitting a number into equal groups |
| **Sharing equally** | Giving the same amount to every person or group |
| **Product** | The answer when you multiply two numbers |
| **Even number** | A number that ends in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 |
