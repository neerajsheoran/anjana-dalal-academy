## Counting Things Around Us

**Child:** Maa, today at the mela I tried to count all the balloons at the stall but I kept losing track! There were so many!

**Mother:** That happens, beta! When there are too many things, counting one by one gets confusing. That is why we learn to count in groups. Tell me, how does the paanwala near our house keep his supari packets?

**Child:** He keeps them in small bundles!

**Mother:** Exactly! And how many are in each bundle?

**Child:** I think ten!

**Mother:** Right! When we make groups of 10, counting becomes so much easier. Instead of counting 1, 2, 3, 4... all the way to 34, you just count 3 bundles and 4 extra. Done!

**Child:** Oh! So that is why Meena Aunty packs 10 laddoos in each box at her shop!

**Mother:** Smart thinking! Now tell me, if she has 4 full boxes and 2 extra laddoos, how many laddoos does she have?

**Child:** 4 tens is 40... plus 2... that is 42 laddoos!

**Mother:** Perfect!

## Counting in Groups of Ten

**Child:** Maa, but what about really big numbers? Like 78?

**Mother:** Let us think about it like this. Imagine you have a big pile of marbles. First you make groups of 10. How many groups of 10 can you make from 78 marbles?

**Child:** Hmm... 7 groups of 10? That is 70. And then 8 left over!

**Mother:** Wonderful! So 78 = 7 tens and 8 ones. And this works for any number. What about 55?

**Child:** 5 groups of 10 and 5 extra! Easy!

**Mother:** Now here is the fun part. What happens when you have exactly 10 groups of 10?

**Child:** 10 times 10... that is 100!

**Mother:** Yes! 100 is a very special number. It is the first number with three digits.

**Child:** Wow, so 100 is just 10 bundles of 10? That sounds simple when you say it that way!

## Comparing Numbers

**Child:** Maa, Priya said she has 47 stickers and I have 52. Who has more?

**Mother:** Good question! Let us compare step by step. First, look at the tens. How many tens does 47 have?

**Child:** 4 tens.

**Mother:** And 52?

**Child:** 5 tens! Oh, 5 is more than 4, so I have more stickers!

**Mother:** Exactly. When the tens are different, the number with more tens is the bigger number. Simple!

**Child:** But what if the tens are the same?

**Mother:** Then we look at the ones. Say you have 43 stamps and Priya has 48 stamps. Both have 4 tens. But 48 has 8 ones and 43 has only 3 ones. So 48 is more.

**Child:** So we always check tens first, and then ones. Got it, Maa!

## Ordinal Numbers -- Positions

**Child:** Maa, at sports day Amit came first in the race. Teacher said that is "1st position." What does that mean?

**Mother:** You know how we use numbers for counting -- 1, 2, 3? Well, numbers can also show the order or rank of things. The child who finishes the race first is in 1st position, the next child is in 2nd position, then 3rd, 4th, and so on.

**Child:** Oh! So it is like the line we make during assembly. Neha stands 1st, I stand 2nd, Riya is 3rd...

**Mother:** Perfect example! These are called **ordinal numbers** because they tell us the order. Remember the difference -- "3" means three things, but "3rd" means the third one in a line.

**Child:** Maa, is my house number also like that? Our house is number 14.

**Mother:** No, beta! Your house number is just a label, a name for our house. It does not mean our house is the 14th house on the street. Just like a cricketer wearing jersey number 18 -- it is not his position in the team, it is just his special number.

**Child:** Oh, so numbers can do three things -- count, show position, and be a name!

**Mother:** You said it perfectly!
