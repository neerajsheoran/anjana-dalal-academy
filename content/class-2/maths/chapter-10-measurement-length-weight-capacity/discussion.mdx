## Learning About Coins and Notes

**Child:** Maa, today Papa gave me a shiny coin. It says "5" on it. What does that mean?

**Mother:** That is a **Rs 5 coin**, beta. It means it is worth 5 rupees. In India, we use rupees to buy things.

**Child:** Are there bigger coins too?

**Mother:** Yes! We have Rs 1, Rs 2, Rs 5, Rs 10, and Rs 20 coins. And for bigger amounts, we use **notes** — like the Rs 10 note, Rs 50 note, Rs 100 note, and even the Rs 500 note!

**Child:** Dadi showed me a very old coin. She said it was a 25 paise coin. What is paise?

**Mother:** Long ago, people used smaller coins called **paise**. 100 paise made 1 rupee. But these coins are not used anymore. The smallest coin we use now is the Rs 1 coin.

**Child:** So my Rs 5 coin is like five Rs 1 coins?

**Mother:** Exactly right! Five Rs 1 coins add up to the same amount as one Rs 5 coin.

## Counting and Adding Money

**Child:** Maa, I have been saving coins in my piggy bank. How do I know how much I have?

**Mother:** Let us count together! Take out all your coins and sort them — put all Rs 1 coins together, all Rs 2 coins together, and so on. Then we add them up.

**Child:** I have three Rs 5 coins, two Rs 2 coins, and four Rs 1 coins.

**Mother:** Good. Three Rs 5 coins = 5 + 5 + 5 = Rs 15. Two Rs 2 coins = 2 + 2 = Rs 4. Four Rs 1 coins = Rs 4. Now add everything: 15 + 4 + 4 = **Rs 23**!

**Child:** Rs 23! That is a lot! Can I buy something at the mela?

**Mother:** Let us see. A balloon costs Rs 5, candy floss costs Rs 10, and a toy whistle costs Rs 8. If you buy a balloon and candy floss, how much will it cost?

**Child:** Rs 5 + Rs 10 = Rs 15!

**Mother:** And how much will you have left?

**Child:** Rs 23 minus Rs 15 = Rs 8! I can still buy the toy whistle with exactly Rs 8!

**Mother:** Perfect calculation, beta! You just used addition and subtraction with money!

## Getting Change and Being Smart with Money

**Child:** Maa, at the shop yesterday, I gave Rs 10 for a toffee that costs Rs 3. The shopkeeper gave me coins back. Why?

**Mother:** That extra money the shopkeeper gave back is called **change**. You paid Rs 10 for something that costs Rs 3, so the shopkeeper returned Rs 10 - Rs 3 = Rs 7 as change.

**Child:** Oh! So I should always check that the change is correct?

**Mother:** Yes, always count your change! If something costs Rs 22 and you give Rs 50, how much change should you get?

**Child:** Rs 50 minus Rs 22... that is Rs 28!

**Mother:** Excellent. And here is something interesting — you can pay the same amount in different ways. If I need to pay Rs 15, I can use one Rs 10 coin and one Rs 5 coin. Or I can use three Rs 5 coins. Or I can use fifteen Rs 1 coins!

**Child:** Fifteen Rs 1 coins would be so heavy, Maa!

**Mother:** Ha ha, that is true! That is why we have bigger coins and notes — to make paying easier. Now, one more thing — it is a very good habit to **save** money. Even if you get Rs 10, try to save Rs 2 or Rs 3 in your piggy bank.

**Child:** I will, Maa! I want to save up for a storybook!

**Mother:** That is a wonderful goal, beta. Every rupee you save brings you closer to your book!
