## Indian Coins and Notes

In India, we use **rupees** to buy things. The short form is **Rs**. Let us learn about the coins and notes we see every day!

### Coins We Use

| Coin | Value |
|------|-------|
| Rs 1 coin | 1 rupee |
| Rs 2 coin | 2 rupees |
| Rs 5 coin | 5 rupees |
| Rs 10 coin | 10 rupees |
| Rs 20 coin | 20 rupees |

### Notes We Use

| Note | Value |
|------|-------|
| Rs 10 note | 10 rupees |
| Rs 20 note | 20 rupees |
| Rs 50 note | 50 rupees |
| Rs 100 note | 100 rupees |
| Rs 200 note | 200 rupees |
| Rs 500 note | 500 rupees |

> **Did you know?** The Rs 500 note has a picture of the **Red Fort** of Delhi on it. And the number "500" is written on the note in many different Indian languages!

### Paise — Old Coins from Dadi's Time

Long ago, people also used **paise** along with rupees. Sonal's Dadi showed her some old coins:

- **50 paise** coin
- **25 paise** coin
- **10 paise** coin

```
100 paise = 1 rupee
```

So:
- 2 coins of 50 paise = Rs 1
- 4 coins of 25 paise = Rs 1

> Today, paise coins are no longer used. The smallest coin now is the **Rs 1 coin**.

## Adding and Counting Money

To know how much money we have, we **add up** the value of every coin and note.

### Counting Amit's Piggy Bank

Amit broke open his piggy bank. Inside he found:

One Rs 20 note + one Rs 10 coin + one Rs 5 coin + two Rs 2 coins + one Rs 1 coin

```
20 + 10 + 5 + 2 + 2 + 1 = Rs 40
```

Amit has **Rs 40** in total!

### Buying Things at a Village Mela

At the mela, different things cost different amounts:

| Item | Price |
|------|-------|
| Gulab jamun plate | Rs 20 |
| Glass bangles (set) | Rs 25 |
| Candy floss (buddhi ke baal) | Rs 10 |
| Balloon | Rs 5 |
| Ferris wheel ride | Rs 15 |

If Amit wants to buy a balloon and candy floss:
- Cost = Rs 5 + Rs 10 = **Rs 15**

If he wants gulab jamuns and a ride on the ferris wheel:
- Cost = Rs 20 + Rs 15 = **Rs 35**

### Adding Bigger Amounts

**Example 1:** Pooja bought ribbons for Rs 18 and a hair clip for Rs 32.
Total spent = Rs 18 + Rs 32 = **Rs 50**

**Example 2:** A packet of chips costs Rs 20 and a juice box costs Rs 25.
Total cost = Rs 20 + Rs 25 = **Rs 45**

## Spending and Getting Change

When we buy something, the money goes out of our pocket. The money we give is **spent**. The money that is left with us is our **savings**.

```
Money we had  -  Money we spent  =  Money left
```

### Amit at the Mela

Amit had **Rs 40**. He bought candy floss for Rs 10 and a balloon for Rs 5.

- Money spent = Rs 10 + Rs 5 = Rs 15
- Money left = Rs 40 - Rs 15 = **Rs 25**

### Finding How Much Was Spent

Sometimes you know how much you started with and how much is left. Then you can find how much was spent:

```
Money we had  -  Money left  =  Money spent
```

Pooja had Rs 50 and now she has Rs 12 left.

Money spent = Rs 50 - Rs 12 = **Rs 38**

### Getting Change from a Shop

When you give the shopkeeper **more money** than the price of the item, the shopkeeper gives back the extra. This extra money is called **change**.

- A pencil costs Rs 8. You give Rs 10. Change = Rs 10 - Rs 8 = **Rs 2**
- A notebook costs Rs 35. You give Rs 50. Change = Rs 50 - Rs 35 = **Rs 15**

> **Tip:** Always count the change the shopkeeper gives you to make sure it is correct!

### Paying the Same Amount in Different Ways

The same price can be paid using **different combinations** of coins and notes. For example, Rs 35 can be paid as:

| Way | Coins and Notes Used |
|-----|---------------------|
| Way 1 | One Rs 20 note + one Rs 10 coin + one Rs 5 coin |
| Way 2 | One Rs 20 note + three Rs 5 coins |
| Way 3 | Three Rs 10 coins + one Rs 5 coin |
| Way 4 | Seven Rs 5 coins |

There are always many ways to pay the same amount!

### Who Has More?

**Example:** Rohit has Rs 38. Sneha has Rs 15 more than Rohit.
Sneha has = Rs 38 + Rs 15 = **Rs 53**

**Example:** Anu has Rs 65. She spent Rs 27 at the bookshop.
Money left = Rs 65 - Rs 27 = **Rs 38**

## Digital Money

These days, many people pay using **mobile phones** instead of cash. Papa might tap his phone at a shop and the money goes from his account to the shopkeeper's account. This is called **digital payment** or **UPI**.

But whether we pay with coins, notes, or a phone, we always need to know how to **count, add, and subtract** money!

> **Good habit:** Try to save some money every week. A piggy bank is a great place to keep your coins. Small savings grow into big amounts over time!

## Key Words to Remember

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Rupee (Rs)** | The money used in India |
| **Paise** | Older, smaller parts of a rupee (100 paise = 1 rupee), no longer used |
| **Coin** | A round metal piece of money |
| **Note** | A paper piece of money |
| **Price / Cost** | How much money an item is worth |
| **Spent** | Money given to buy something |
| **Change** | Extra money the shopkeeper gives back |
| **Total** | The sum of all amounts added together |
| **Save** | Keeping money for later use |
