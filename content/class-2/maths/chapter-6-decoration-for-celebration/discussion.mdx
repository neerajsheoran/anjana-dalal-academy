## Addition of Two-Digit Numbers (Without Carrying)

**Child:** Maa, I made 25 paper flowers and Vikram made 34. How many do we have altogether?

**Mother:** Great question, beta! When you put two groups together, you are doing **addition**. Let us add 25 and 34. First, we add the ones. How many ones do 25 and 34 have?

**Child:** 25 has 5 ones and 34 has 4 ones. 5 + 4 = 9!

**Mother:** Now add the tens. 25 has 2 tens and 34 has 3 tens. 2 + 3 = ?

**Child:** 5! So the answer is 59! We have 59 flowers together!

**Mother:** Wonderful! See how easy it is? First ones, then tens. As long as the ones add up to less than 10, it is very smooth.

**Child:** But Maa, what if the ones add up to more than 10?

**Mother:** Ah, that is when it gets a little trickier. But we have a special trick for that called **carrying**. Let me show you next!

## Addition of Two-Digit Numbers (With Carrying)

**Child:** Maa, tell me about carrying! What happens when ones add up to more than 10?

**Mother:** Okay, let us try 28 + 35. First, add the ones: 8 + 5 = ?

**Child:** 13!

**Mother:** Right! But we cannot write 13 in the ones place -- that is too many. So we split 13 into 1 ten and 3 ones. We write the **3** in the ones place, and we **carry** the 1 ten over to the tens column.

**Child:** So the 1 goes with the tens?

**Mother:** Exactly! Now add the tens: 2 + 3 = 5, plus the 1 we carried = 6. So the answer is **63**.

**Child:** Oh, I get it! It is like when we are making flower chains. If I have 13 loose flowers, I can tie 10 of them into a new chain and keep 3 loose!

**Mother:** What a perfect way to think about it! The new chain goes to the tens column, and the 3 leftover flowers stay in the ones column.

**Child:** Let me try one! 47 + 38. Ones: 7 + 8 = 15. Write 5, carry 1. Tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. Answer is 85!

**Mother:** You nailed it, beta!

## Subtraction of Two-Digit Numbers

**Child:** Maa, a potter made 68 diyas and sold 35. How do we find how many are left?

**Mother:** When we take something away, we do **subtraction**. We write 68 - 35. Let us start with the ones: 8 - 5 = ?

**Child:** 3!

**Mother:** Now the tens: 6 - 3 = ?

**Child:** 3! So 68 - 35 = 33. He has 33 diyas left!

**Mother:** Perfect! But what if we need to subtract a bigger ones digit from a smaller one? Like 52 - 27. Can we take 7 away from 2?

**Child:** No, 2 is smaller than 7! We do not have enough.

**Mother:** Right! So we **borrow** 1 ten from the tens place. The 5 tens become 4 tens, and the 2 ones become 12 ones. Now can we take 7 from 12?

**Child:** 12 - 7 = 5! And for tens: 4 - 2 = 2. So the answer is 25!

**Mother:** Exactly! Borrowing is like opening a flower chain. If you need more loose flowers, you open one chain to get 10 extra flowers.

**Child:** Maa, I also know something cool. If 15 + 25 = 40, then 40 - 25 must be 15, and 40 - 15 must be 25!

**Mother:** That is called a **fact family**! Addition and subtraction are like two sides of the same coin. When you know one, you automatically know the other. You are becoming a maths expert, beta!
