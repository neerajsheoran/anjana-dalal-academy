## In One Paragraph

**Globalisation** is the rapid integration of economies through the freer movement of goods, services, capital, technology, and people across borders. Its modern engine is the **multinational corporation (MNC)**, which splits production across countries to find the cheapest, fastest, or most skilled location for each step — placing software work in Bengaluru, call centres in Noida, iPhone assembly in Sriperumbudur, and headquarters in California. MNCs enter foreign markets by buying local firms, forming joint ventures, placing orders, or building factories. Three forces drove modern globalisation: **technology** (shipping containers, the internet, mobile data — Jio's 2016 launch transformed India), **liberalisation** (India's 1991 reforms by P.V. Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh, triggered by a foreign exchange crisis, dismantled the licence-permit raj, cut tariffs, opened up FDI), and **rules** (the **World Trade Organization**, set up in 1995, which pushes for freer trade but is often criticised for tilting rules in favour of rich countries that heavily subsidise their farmers). The winners in India include skilled urban professionals, consumers (more variety, better quality), large Indian companies that became MNCs themselves (Tata, Reliance, Infosys, Sun Pharma), and remittance-receiving families. The losers or squeezed include small producers crushed by cheap imports, farmers exposed to global prices, informal workers pressed by MNC suppliers, and traditional artisans. The Indian government has responded with **Make in India**, **Production-Linked Incentives**, **FTAs**, and digital infrastructure (UPI, Aadhaar, ONDC) — trying to be globally connected while strengthening Indian jobs and companies. The honest verdict: globalisation has lifted huge numbers but the gains and losses are unequally shared; smart policy widens the winners and cushions the losers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Globalisation** = rapid integration of economies through freer movement of goods, services, capital, technology, ideas, and people.
- It is not the same as trade. What is new is that **production itself crosses borders** (one product, many countries) and the speed/scale of flows.
- **Multinational Corporations (MNCs)** own or control production in more than one country and place each step where it's cheapest, fastest, or most skilled.
- India's main globalisation slots: **software services, BPO/call centres, pharmaceuticals**, and increasingly **electronics assembly** (Foxconn iPhones at Sriperumbudur, Samsung at Noida).
- MNCs enter foreign markets in four ways: **buy a local firm, joint venture, place orders, build new factories** (FDI).
- **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)** = a foreign company putting money into setting up or controlling production inside another country (e.g. Maruti-Suzuki, Foxconn).
- Foreign trade leads to **integration of markets** — prices in different countries move together; a drought in Spain affects oil prices in Bengaluru.
- Three forces drove modern globalisation: **technology** (container, internet, mobile), **liberalisation** (India's 1991 reforms), and **rules** (WTO since 1995).
- The **shipping container** (1950s, widespread 1970s) collapsed shipping costs and times — boring-looking, world-changing.
- **Reliance Jio's launch (2016)** collapsed mobile data costs in India; by 2024 India had over 800 million internet users.
- Pre-1991 India followed **import substitution** — high tariffs, licence-permit raj, restricted FDI, protected (and lazy) domestic industry.
- The **1991 crisis**: Gulf War + collapsing remittances brought reserves down to ~2 weeks of imports. India shipped gold to London for an IMF loan.
- **1991 Reforms** (P.V. Narasimha Rao and Dr Manmohan Singh): cut tariffs, dismantled licences, opened FDI, devalued rupee.
- The **WTO** (1995, ~165 members) sets rules for global trade — lower tariffs, fewer subsidies, equal treatment of foreign firms. In theory fair; in practice tilted toward rich countries.
- The **subsidy problem**: USA pays farmers ~$40 billion/year; EU more. Indian farmers compete against subsidised foreign goods on supposedly "fair" terms.
- **Winners in India**: IT/MNC professionals, consumers, large competitive Indian companies (Tata bought Corus and JLR; Sun Pharma is a global generic giant), remittance-receiving families.
- **Indians abroad sent home over $125 billion in remittances in 2024** — the world's highest.
- **Losers/squeezed**: small producers crushed by Chinese imports, farmers exposed to global price swings, informal-sector workers, traditional artisans (handloom, toys).
- Indian government's recent responses: **Free Trade Agreements** (Japan, ASEAN, UAE, Australia, EFTA), **Make in India** (2014), **Production-Linked Incentives (PLI)**, strategic caution on Chinese imports, and **digital infrastructure exports** (UPI, Aadhaar, ONDC).
- The honest verdict: globalisation is neither hero nor villain. It is a powerful force whose gains are real but unequally distributed; policy's job is to maximise gains and cushion losses.

## Key Terms

- **Globalisation** — rapid integration of countries' economies through freer movement of goods, services, capital, technology, and (sometimes) people across borders.
- **Multinational Corporation (MNC)** — a company that owns or controls production in more than one country (e.g. Samsung, Coca-Cola, Tata Motors).
- **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)** — investment by a foreign company in setting up factories or buying control of firms in another country.
- **Outsourcing / Business Process Outsourcing (BPO)** — a company giving away part of its work (often back-office or customer support) to another company, often abroad.
- **Tariff** — a tax a government places on imports, making foreign goods more expensive than they would otherwise be.
- **Trade Barrier** — any restriction (tariffs, quotas, licences) that limits imports or foreign investment.
- **Liberalisation** — removing or loosening government-imposed barriers on trade and foreign investment.
- **Import Substitution** — pre-1991 Indian policy of protecting domestic industry by replacing foreign imports with locally made goods.
- **World Trade Organization (WTO)** — international body set up in 1995 that frames rules for global trade and resolves trade disputes.
- **Free Trade Agreement (FTA)** — a treaty between two or more countries that reduces tariffs and trade barriers between them.
- **Make in India** — 2014 government initiative to make India a global manufacturing hub.
- **Remittances** — money sent home by people working in another country (Indians abroad sent home over $125 billion in 2024).
