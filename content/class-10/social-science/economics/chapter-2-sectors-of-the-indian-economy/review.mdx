## In One Paragraph

All economic activity can be classified into three **sectors**: the **primary sector** (farming, fishing, mining — using nature directly), the **secondary sector** (manufacturing, construction — turning raw materials into goods), and the **tertiary sector** (services — transport, trade, banking, education, IT). India today has a striking imbalance: services produce more than **half of GDP** but employ only about **30%** of workers, while agriculture produces **just 16-17% of GDP** but employs **45% of workers**. This mismatch hides huge **disguised unemployment** in agriculture — people who appear employed but contribute nothing extra. To address rural unemployment and underemployment, Parliament passed **MGNREGA in 2005**, guaranteeing every rural household 100 days of paid work per year as a legal right. A second divide cuts across all sectors: the **organised sector** (formal contracts, PF, paid leave, job security) versus the **unorganised sector** (no contract, no benefits, no security) — and over **90% of Indian workers are unorganised**. A third lens separates the **public sector** (government-owned — Railways, RBI, SBI, ONGC) from the **private sector** (privately-owned — Tata, Reliance, Infosys, local shops). Finally, the chapter highlights what GDP misses: the enormous **unpaid work of women** in cooking, cleaning, and care — invisible in statistics, central to family life. Understanding all of these together is what it means to **see the Indian economy** clearly.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three sectors of economic activity**: **Primary** (uses nature — agriculture, fishing, mining); **Secondary** (transforms raw materials — manufacturing, construction); **Tertiary** (services — transport, banking, IT, education).
- **GDP** (Gross Domestic Product) measures the **final value** of all goods and services produced inside a country in a year. It avoids double-counting by valuing only the final product.
- **India's GDP share by sector** (roughly): Primary ~16-17%, Secondary ~27-29%, Tertiary ~53-55%. India is now a **service-dominated** economy by GDP.
- **India's employment share by sector** (roughly): Primary ~45%, Secondary ~25%, Tertiary ~30%. **Agriculture still employs the most people** even though it produces the least.
- **The central puzzle**: agriculture employs nearly half the country but earns only a sixth — meaning income per farmer is very low and disguised unemployment is widespread.
- **Three unemployment ideas to distinguish**: **Unemployment** (no work, looking); **Underemployment** (working below capacity — e.g., engineer driving a taxi); **Disguised unemployment** (extra people on a task who add nothing — common in family farms).
- **MGNREGA (2005)** — Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act. Gives every rural household a **legal right to 100 days of paid work per year**. The first national right-to-work law of this scale.
- **Organised vs Unorganised sectors**: Organised = written contract, fixed hours, salary, paid leave, PF/gratuity/ESI, job security, unions, labour-law protection. Unorganised = none of these.
- **Over 90% of Indian workers are in the unorganised sector** — farmers, agricultural labourers, construction workers, domestic helpers, street vendors, small-shop workers, autorickshaw drivers, and most gig workers.
- **Public vs Private sectors**: Public = government-owned (Railways, India Post, SBI, ONGC, AIIMS) for service, strategic, and developmental purposes. Private = privately-owned (Tata, Reliance, Infosys, local kiranas) for profit.
- **Women's unpaid work** — cooking, cleaning, childcare, eldercare, water-fetching, household management — is **excluded from GDP** even though Indian women spend 5-6 hours a day on it. This is a major hidden part of the Indian economy.
- **Jobless growth** — when GDP rises faster than employment, leaving large numbers without work even as the economy grows. A major Indian challenge.
- **Where India is headed**: more services growth, push for manufacturing under "Make in India"/PLI schemes, gradual formalisation, emergence of the **gig/platform economy** (Ola, Uber, Swiggy, Zomato, Urban Company) creating a new fourth category of work.
- **Big-picture lesson**: A growth number is one number. Behind it lie different sectors, different working conditions, different lives — including millions of invisible workers, especially women.

## Key Terms

- **Primary sector** — economic activity that directly uses natural resources (agriculture, fishing, dairy, forestry, mining).
- **Secondary sector** — economic activity that transforms raw materials into goods (manufacturing, construction, electricity, processing).
- **Tertiary sector** — economic activity that provides services rather than goods (transport, trade, banking, IT, education, healthcare).
- **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** — total value of all final goods and services produced inside a country in a year.
- **Unemployment** — being able and willing to work, looking for work, but not finding any.
- **Underemployment** — being employed but below one's capacity or for fewer days/hours than possible.
- **Disguised unemployment** — when more people are employed on a task than needed; if some left, output would not fall.
- **MGNREGA** — Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005, giving every rural household 100 days of paid work per year as a legal right.
- **Organised sector** — sector where workers have formal contracts, regular salary, fixed hours, paid leave, social-security benefits, and legal protection.
- **Unorganised sector** — sector where workers lack contracts, regular wages, paid leave, social-security, and labour-law protection. Over 90% of Indian workers are here.
- **Public sector** — economic activity owned and managed by the government, aimed at service, strategic interest, or development.
- **Private sector** — economic activity owned by individuals or companies, aimed at profit.
- **Female Labour Force Participation Rate (FLFPR)** — the percentage of women of working age who are in paid work or looking for it. India's rate is among the lowest in the world.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **More Indians work in the farms — but most of India's income comes from the offices.**

That one mismatch — high employment in agriculture, high GDP share in services — is the puzzle at the heart of the Indian economy. Almost every major problem (rural poverty, urban migration, jobless growth, the demand for "Make in India," the importance of MGNREGA) traces back to it.
