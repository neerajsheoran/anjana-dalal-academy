## In One Paragraph

Indian nationalism became a **mass movement** between 1919 and 1947 by binding together hundreds of local struggles under one shared word — **swaraj**. The **First World War** pushed up taxes, prices, and forced recruitment; famine and the 1918 flu killed 12-13 million Indians; anger was everywhere when **Gandhi** returned from South Africa with a new weapon — **satyagraha**, non-violent active resistance. After the **Rowlatt Act (1919)** and the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919)**, Gandhi joined hands with the **Khilafat** leaders and launched the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)** — boycotting schools, courts, and foreign cloth. In the countryside, **Awadh peasants** under Baba Ramchandra, **Gudem tribals** under Alluri Sitaram Raju, and **Assam plantation workers** all interpreted swaraj in their own ways. Gandhi withdrew the movement after the violence at **Chauri Chaura (February 1922)**. The **Salt March (March-April 1930)** launched the **Civil Disobedience Movement**, which mobilised rich peasants, industrialists, women, and city protesters. But it also exposed cracks — **Ambedkar** demanded separate electorates for Dalits (settled by the **Poona Pact, 1932**); **Muhammad Iqbal** spoke for separate Muslim political space; **Bhagat Singh** and the HSRA chose the path of armed revolution. The **Quit India Movement (1942)** was the final mass push. Through it all, nationalism was built not only by politics but by **culture** — **Bharat Mata**, **Vande Mataram**, folk songs, flags, and a rewritten national history.

## Key Takeaways

- The **First World War** wrecked the Indian economy: taxes doubled, prices doubled, forced recruitment in villages, famine and flu killed **12-13 million Indians** by 1921.
- **Gandhi's satyagraha** is non-violent **active resistance** — refusing to cooperate with injustice and willingly suffering the consequences to win over the wrongdoer's conscience.
- The **Rowlatt Act (1919)** allowed detention without trial. Gandhi's hartal led to the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919)** — Dyer killed hundreds in an enclosed garden to "produce a moral effect."
- The **Khilafat-Non-Cooperation alliance** brought Hindus and Muslims together briefly. **Non-Cooperation (1920-22)** boycotted government schools, councils, courts, and foreign cloth. Import of foreign cloth halved.
- Different groups meant different things by **swaraj**: Awadh peasants wanted rent reduction; Gudem tribals (under **Alluri Sitaram Raju**) wanted their forests back; Assam plantation workers wanted the right to leave their gardens.
- Gandhi withdrew Non-Cooperation after **Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922)** — 22 policemen burnt to death — saying Indians were not yet trained for disciplined non-violence.
- The **Lahore Congress (Dec 1929)** declared **Purna Swaraj** (full independence). **26 January 1930** was the first Independence Day.
- The **Salt March (March-April 1930)** — 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi — used salt as a brilliant symbol because every Indian, rich or poor, uses it. It launched the **Civil Disobedience Movement**.
- The **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)** suspended the movement; the **Second Round Table Conference** failed; Civil Disobedience was relaunched but faded by 1934. About **100,000 Indians** were arrested.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** demanded **separate electorates** for Dalits at the Second Round Table Conference. Gandhi began a **fast unto death**. They compromised in the **Poona Pact (Sept 1932)** — reserved seats but a joint electorate.
- **Women** participated in large numbers in Civil Disobedience — picketing, salt-making, jail. But the Congress and Gandhi himself remained traditional about women's social roles.
- **Bhagat Singh** and the **HSRA (1928)** chose armed revolution. Bhagat Singh was executed in 1931 at age 23. **"Inquilab Zindabad!"**
- Nationalism spread through **culture** too: **Bharat Mata** (image by Abanindranath Tagore, hymn "Vande Mataram" by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay), folk songs, the **Swaraj flag** with a spinning wheel (1921), and rewriting Indian history.
- The **Quit India Movement (1942)** — sparked by Gandhi's "Do or Die" speech — was led from below after the Congress leadership was arrested. It was the final push before Independence (15 August 1947).

## Key Terms

- **Satyagraha** — Gandhi's method of struggle: non-violent active resistance based on the power of truth; refusing to cooperate with injustice while accepting suffering oneself.
- **Swaraj** — Self-rule. Meant different things to different groups: full political independence (Congress), end of rent oppression (peasants), forest rights (tribals), end of untouchability (Dalits).
- **Khilafat** — Cause of restoring the Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa (Islamic spiritual head) after WWI; Gandhi joined hands with Khilafat leaders to unite Hindus and Muslims.
- **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)** — Mass movement based on refusing to cooperate with British rule: boycott of schools, courts, councils, foreign goods.
- **Hartal** — Mass strike with shops shut down and work suspended, used as a form of protest.
- **Rowlatt Act (1919)** — British law allowing detention of political prisoners without trial for up to two years.
- **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** — On 13 April 1919, General Dyer fired on a peaceful crowd in an enclosed garden in Amritsar, killing hundreds.
- **Begar** — Unpaid forced labour that peasants were made to do for landlords or the government.
- **Picket** — A form of protest where demonstrators block the entrance to a shop, factory, or office.
- **Boycott** — Organised refusal to buy from, deal with, or participate in something as a form of protest.
- **Khadi** — Hand-spun, hand-woven Indian cloth; symbol of self-reliance and rejection of British mill-cloth.
- **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34)** — Movement that began with the Salt March; Indians actively broke unjust colonial laws, not merely refused to cooperate.
- **Purna Swaraj** — "Complete Independence" — adopted as the goal of the Congress at the Lahore Session (December 1929).
- **Poona Pact (1932)** — Agreement between Gandhi and Ambedkar: Dalits would get reserved seats in legislatures but be elected by a joint electorate, not a separate one.
- **Harijan** — "Children of God" — Gandhi's term for the so-called untouchables; later rejected by Dalit activists as patronising.
- **Inquilab Zindabad** — "Long live the Revolution!" — slogan associated with Bhagat Singh and the HSRA.
- **Bharat Mata** — Image of India as a mother goddess; key symbol of nationalist imagination; first painted by Abanindranath Tagore (1905).
- **Vande Mataram** — "I bow to the Motherland" — hymn by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay; rallying song of the freedom movement.
