## In One Paragraph

Agriculture is still the largest single employer in India — roughly half the working population earns from the soil, and almost everything you eat or wear begins as a crop decision made by a farmer. Indian farming comes in four broad types: **primitive subsistence (jhum / slash-and-burn) farming** in the north-east and hill pockets, **intensive subsistence farming** in densely populated plains where small holdings are pushed hard for every grain, **commercial farming** with HYV seeds and modern inputs in Punjab-Haryana-style belts, and **plantation agriculture** for tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane, and bananas. The year follows three seasons — **kharif** (June–October, paddy/maize/cotton/jute), **rabi** (October–March, wheat/gram/mustard) and **zaid** (April–June, watermelon and vegetables). Rice belongs to wet eastern and southern belts; wheat to the cooler dry north-west; millets to dry inland zones; sugarcane to UP and Maharashtra; cotton to the Deccan black soils; jute to the West Bengal flood plains; tea to Assam and Darjeeling; coffee to Karnataka. The **Green Revolution** of the 1960s–70s ended hunger but cost groundwater, soil health and crop diversity. **Land reforms, MSP, FCI procurement, the PDS, Kisan Credit Cards and crop insurance** form the institutional safety net. **Globalisation** has opened export markets but also lowered prices, raised input costs, and fed a wave of farmer distress. The story is not finished — it is the unfinished political argument of modern India.

## Key Takeaways

- **Roughly half of working India** still depends on agriculture for a living, even though its share of GDP has shrunk.
- **Primitive subsistence farming** is slash-and-burn (jhumming), low-yield, monsoon-dependent, with regional names like bewar, podu, kumari, khil and kuruwa.
- **Intensive subsistence farming** dominates dense rural India — tiny holdings, heavy fertiliser and irrigation, family labour, driven by inheritance-fragmented land.
- **Commercial farming** uses HYV seeds and modern inputs; the same crop can be commercial in one state and subsistence in another (rice in Punjab vs. rice in Odisha).
- **Plantation agriculture** = single crop, large area, capital and migrant labour, processed on-site for global markets (tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane, bananas).
- India has **three cropping seasons**: kharif (monsoon), rabi (winter), zaid (short hot summer).
- **Rice**: kharif, staple, eastern and southern India + irrigated Punjab/Haryana; India is the world's 2nd-largest producer after China.
- **Wheat**: rabi, north-west plains and Deccan black soil; Punjab, Haryana, UP, MP, Bihar, Rajasthan.
- **Millets (jowar, bajra, ragi)**: nutritionally dense coarse grains for dry rain-fed zones.
- **Pulses**: India is the world's largest producer and consumer; mostly leguminous, fix nitrogen, grown in rotation.
- **Sugarcane**: long-duration tropical crop; major source of sugar, gur, khandsari, molasses; UP and Maharashtra lead.
- **Plantation specialties**: tea (Assam/Darjeeling/Kerala/Tamil Nadu), coffee (Karnataka mainly, Arabica from Yemen), rubber (Kerala), cotton (Deccan black soil), jute (West Bengal flood plains).
- **The Green Revolution** ended import dependence but left behind groundwater depletion, soil degradation, regional imbalance, and a wheat-rice bias that crowded out millets and pulses.
- **Institutional reforms** include abolition of zamindari, consolidation of holdings, MSP, FCI procurement, PDS, KCC, crop insurance, Grameen banks, and the Bhoodan-Gramdan movement led by **Vinoba Bhave**.

## Key Terms

- **Subsistence farming** — farming mainly to feed the farmer's family, with little or nothing left to sell.
- **Jhumming / shifting cultivation** — slash-and-burn farming on cleared forest patches that are abandoned after a few seasons.
- **Intensive subsistence farming** — labour-intensive small-plot farming with heavy fertiliser and irrigation, driven by population pressure on land.
- **Commercial farming** — farming primarily for the market, using HYV seeds and modern inputs.
- **Plantation agriculture** — large-area single-crop commercial farming (tea, coffee, rubber) linked tightly to industry.
- **Kharif / Rabi / Zaid** — India's monsoon, winter and short-summer cropping seasons.
- **HYV (High-Yielding Variety) seeds** — improved seed varieties at the heart of the Green Revolution.
- **MSP (Minimum Support Price)** — government-guaranteed floor price for major crops, paid through FCI procurement.
- **PDS (Public Distribution System)** — the network of ration shops delivering subsidised food grain to poor households.
- **Bhoodan-Gramdan** — Vinoba Bhave's voluntary land-donation movement; the "Blood-less Revolution".
- **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)** — government scheme giving farmers short-term credit at lower interest.
- **FPO (Farmer Producer Organisation)** — collective body of small farmers that pools produce to sell at better prices.
