## In One Paragraph

**Manufacturing** turns raw materials into higher-value finished goods — a process of **value addition** that earns wages, foreign exchange, and the technical strength a country needs to grow. India's manufacturing sector is classified by **raw material** (agro-based like cotton, jute, sugar; mineral-based like iron-and-steel, aluminium, cement), by **role** (basic industries like steel that feed others; consumer industries like soap that go straight to households), by **capital investment** (small-scale vs large-scale), by **ownership** (public, private, joint, cooperative), and by **bulk** (heavy vs light). **Cotton textiles**, India's oldest organised industry since 1854, still cluster around Mumbai and Ahmedabad because of cotton, climate, port and capital. **Jute** sits in a tight Hugli-river belt in West Bengal. **Sugar** runs through UP, Bihar and Maharashtra, with the cooperative sector dominant. **Iron and steel** concentrates in the Chhotanagpur plateau — TISCO Jamshedpur, Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bokaro, plus Vizag on the coast — because iron ore, coking coal and limestone are all there. **Aluminium smelting** is in Odisha (NALCO), Korba (BALCO) and Renukoot (HINDALCO) — close to bauxite and cheap power. **Fertilisers** are spread across Sindri, Gorakhpur, Trombay and other plants. **Cement** clusters in the Gujarat-Karnataka-Tamil Nadu belt. **Automobiles** are in NCR, Pune, Chennai, Bengaluru. **IT** centres in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune. Industries are pulled by **raw material, power, labour, market, transport, capital and government policy**. But factories also bring serious **air, water, soil and noise pollution** — the Bhopal Gas tragedy is the worst single example. Control needs three-stage **effluent treatment**, water reuse, smoke filters, low-sulphur fuels, generator silencers, and serious enforcement. **NTPC's environmental management system** shows what is possible. The challenge for India is to grow industry without poisoning the rivers and air that the same industry depends on.

## Key Takeaways

- **Manufacturing** is the secondary-sector activity of converting raw materials into higher-value finished goods — the heart of **value addition** in any economy
- Manufacturing drives development by **creating large-scale employment, modernising agriculture, earning foreign exchange, and reducing regional disparity** — the logic behind public-sector plants in backward regions
- Industries are classified by **source of raw material** (agro vs mineral), **role** (basic vs consumer), **capital** (small-scale vs large), **ownership** (public, private, joint, cooperative) and **bulk** (heavy vs light)
- **Cotton textiles** began in Mumbai (1854), Ahmedabad (1861); they concentrated in the Maharashtra-Gujarat belt because of raw cotton, moist climate, port access, labour and merchant capital; **handloom and khadi** still employ lakhs of weavers
- **Jute industry** is concentrated along the **Hugli river in West Bengal** — first mill at Rishra in 1855; **Partition in 1947** sent three-fourths of jute-growing land to East Pakistan but left the mills in India
- **Sugar industry** — India is the world's second-largest producer; mills are mostly in **UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu**; the **cooperative sector** suits it because cane is bulky and must be crushed near the field
- **Iron and steel** needs iron ore, coking coal and limestone in 4:2:1 ratio; major plants — **TISCO Jamshedpur (1907), Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bokaro, Vizag (the only coastal one), Salem, Bhadravati** — cluster in the **Chhotanagpur plateau**
- **Aluminium smelting** needs bauxite and cheap electricity; **NALCO (Odisha), BALCO (Korba, Chhattisgarh), HINDALCO (Renukoot, UP)** are the big plants
- **Chemical industry** is split into inorganic (sulphuric acid, soda ash) spread across the country and organic (petrochemicals, plastics, drugs) located near oil refineries in **Maharashtra, Gujarat, UP, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh**
- **Fertiliser industry** makes nitrogenous (urea), phosphatic (DAP) and complex N-P-K fertilisers; potash is fully imported; key plants at **Sindri, Gorakhpur, Trombay, Nangal, Rourkela**
- **Cement industry** needs bulky limestone; the **Gujarat-Karnataka-Tamil Nadu belt** dominates; first plant at Chennai in 1904; Gujarat plants export cement to Gulf countries
- **Location factors** for any industry — **raw material, power, labour, market, transport, capital and entrepreneurship, government policy** — these combined produce **clusters** (Pune for auto, Bengaluru for IT, Tirupur for knitwear)
- **Industrial pollution** has four forms — **air** (smoke, SO2, particulate matter), **water** (organic and inorganic effluents), **soil** (chemical dumping), **noise**; the **Bhopal Gas tragedy of 1984** remains the worst example
- **Pollution control** needs three-stage effluent treatment (mechanical, biological, chemical), water reuse and rainwater harvesting, smoke-stack filters (electrostatic precipitators, scrubbers), low-sulphur fuels, generator silencers; **NTPC's ISO 14001-certified Environment Management System** is the model

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Manufacturing | Production of goods in large quantities after processing raw materials into more valuable products — the secondary sector of the economy |
| Value addition | The increase in value of a product as it moves from raw material through each stage of processing to finished good |
| Agro-based industry | Industry that uses agricultural products as raw material — cotton textile, jute, sugar, vegetable oil, tea |
| Mineral-based industry | Industry that uses minerals and metals as raw material — iron-and-steel, aluminium smelting, cement, petrochemicals |
| Basic or key industry | Industry whose products are used as raw material by other industries — iron and steel, copper smelting, aluminium smelting |
| Consumer industry | Industry that produces goods for direct use by consumers — sugar, toothpaste, paper, fans, sewing machines |
| Cooperative sector | Industry owned and run jointly by producers or workers who pool resources and share profits or losses — e.g. Maharashtra sugar mills, Kerala coir |
| Coking coal | A grade of coal with low ash and high carbon used in blast furnaces to convert iron ore into steel — India imports much of its coking coal |
| TISCO | Tata Iron and Steel Company — set up by Jamsetji Tata at Jamshedpur in 1907; the first integrated steel plant in India |
| Bauxite | The bulky reddish ore from which aluminium is extracted by smelting — India's major reserves are in Odisha, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh |
| Location factor | Any condition — raw material, power, labour, market, transport, capital, policy — that pulls an industry to a particular place |
| Cluster effect | The self-reinforcing tendency for firms in the same industry to gather in one location to share workers, suppliers and customers |
| Sustainable development | Economic growth that meets present needs without harming the environment or the ability of future generations to meet theirs |
