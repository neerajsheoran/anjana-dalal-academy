## In One Paragraph

A **resource** is anything in our environment that satisfies a human need, provided we have the technology to use it and the society finds it acceptable — resources are not, they *become*. Geographers classify resources four ways: by **origin** (biotic vs abiotic), by **exhaustibility** (renewable vs non-renewable), by **ownership** (individual, community, national, international), and by **status of development** (potential, developed, stock, reserve). India's uneven endowment — coal in Jharkhand, solar potential in Rajasthan, alluvium in the Gangetic plain — makes **resource planning** essential, and Indian planning since 1951 has followed a three-stage process: inventory and surveying, building institutions and technology, and aligning use with development goals. **Land** is the most basic resource; net sown area covers about 46% of India's reporting area, but forest cover sits below the 33% target. **Soil**, the thin life-giving layer that takes a thousand years to form a centimetre, is India's quietest treasure. The six major Indian soils — **alluvial** (Gangetic plain, supports 60% of the population), **black or regur** (Deccan trap, perfect for cotton), **red and yellow** (peninsular plateau), **laterite** (Western Ghats, tea/coffee/cashew), **arid** (Rajasthan, Thar), and **forest/mountain** (Himalayas, Western Ghats) — each have distinct properties, crops, and threats. **Soil erosion** caused by deforestation, over-grazing, faulty farming, and mining is countered through **contour ploughing**, **terrace farming**, **strip cropping**, **shelter belts**, and **afforestation**. The **Brundtland Commission** of 1987 gave the world the definition of **sustainable development** as "meeting present needs without compromising future generations," and the **Rio Earth Summit of 1992** translated it into **Agenda 21**, the global blueprint signed by over 170 countries. Mahatma Gandhi had said it earlier and more bluntly: "There is enough for everybody's need but not for everybody's greed."

## Key Takeaways

- **A resource is a relationship**, not a thing — it depends on human need, available technology, and cultural acceptability (Erich Zimmermann's "resources are not, they become").
- **Origin classification**: **biotic** (from the living world — forests, fish, cattle, humans) vs **abiotic** (from the non-living world — minerals, water, air, rocks).
- **Exhaustibility classification**: **renewable** (sun, wind, water, forests) vs **non-renewable** (coal, petroleum, metallic minerals).
- **Ownership classification**: **individual** (private property), **community** (village pond, grazing land), **national** (minerals, rivers, territorial waters up to 12 nautical miles), **international** (high seas beyond 200 nautical miles, Antarctica).
- **Status classification**: **potential** (surveyed but unused — Rajasthan solar), **developed** (in active use), **stock** (exists but tech not ready — hydrogen in seawater), **reserve** (set aside for future use).
- **Resource planning in India** is a three-stage process: identification & inventory → building institutions, laws, technology → aligning with overall development plans (since the First Five Year Plan in 1951).
- **India's land use**: net sown area is about 46% of reporting area; forest cover is around 22-24%, below the National Forest Policy 1952 target of 33%.
- **Six major Indian soils**: alluvial (40% of country), black/regur, red & yellow, laterite, arid, and forest/mountain.
- **Alluvial soil** is sub-classified into **khadar** (new, fertile, annual flood plain) and **bhangar** (old, with kankar nodules); grows wheat, rice, sugarcane, jute.
- **Regur (black soil)** is formed from Deccan basalt, is moisture-retentive (cracks self-aerate), and grows cotton, jowar, bajra, sugarcane.
- **Laterite soil** forms in heavy-rain hilly regions through leaching; supports tea, coffee, cashew, and rubber when manured.
- **Soil erosion** causes: deforestation, over-grazing, faulty farming, mining, industrial effluents; forms: gully, sheet, wind, salinity, water-logging.
- **Soil conservation**: contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping, shelter belts, afforestation, joint forest management, grazing bans.
- **Sustainable development** was defined by the Brundtland Commission, 1987, in *Our Common Future*; the Rio Earth Summit (1992) produced Agenda 21 and the Climate Change and Biodiversity Conventions.

## Key Terms

- **Resource** — Anything in our environment which can be used to satisfy a human need, provided it is technologically accessible, economically feasible, and culturally acceptable.
- **Biotic Resource** — A resource obtained from the biosphere — forests, fisheries, livestock, crops, human beings.
- **Abiotic Resource** — A non-living resource — rocks, minerals, water, air, soil.
- **Renewable Resource** — A resource that can be renewed by physical, chemical, or biological processes — sunlight, wind, water, forests (when used sustainably).
- **Non-renewable Resource** — A resource that takes millions of years to form and cannot be replaced within a human timescale — coal, petroleum, natural gas, most metallic minerals.
- **Sustainable Development** — Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs (Brundtland Commission, 1987).
- **Resource Planning** — A widely accepted strategy for the judicious use of resources through a three-stage process: identification, building a planning structure, and aligning use with overall development plans.
- **Alluvial Soil** — Fertile soil deposited by Himalayan rivers in the Indo-Gangetic plain, covering about 40% of India and supporting 60% of the population; sub-types are *khadar* (new) and *bhangar* (old).
- **Black Soil (Regur)** — Dark, clayey, moisture-retentive soil formed from Deccan basalt; ideal for cotton, found in Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP, Karnataka, AP.
- **Laterite Soil** — Porous, iron-rich soil formed by intense leaching in heavy-rain hilly regions; supports tea, coffee, cashew when manured. From Latin *later* meaning brick.
- **Land Degradation** — The decline in land quality through over-use, salinity, water-logging, deforestation, or chemical contamination; affects about 130 million hectares in India.
- **Soil Erosion** — The denudation of the soil cover faster than nature can replenish it, caused by water, wind, or human activity.
- **Khadar and Bhangar** — Khadar is new alluvium deposited annually by floods; bhangar is older alluvium found above the flood plain, with kankar (lime nodules).
- **Agenda 21** — A blueprint adopted at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit by over 170 countries to achieve global sustainable development through Local Agenda 21 plans.
