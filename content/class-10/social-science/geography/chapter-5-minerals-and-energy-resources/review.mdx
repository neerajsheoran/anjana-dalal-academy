## In One Paragraph

Minerals are naturally occurring homogeneous substances found in igneous and metamorphic rocks (as veins and lodes), in sedimentary rocks (as beds), as residual decomposition deposits (like bauxite), as alluvial placer deposits, and in ocean waters. They are classified into metallic (ferrous like iron and manganese, non-ferrous like copper and bauxite), non-metallic (mica, limestone, salt), and energy minerals (coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium). India's iron ore — mostly haematite and magnetite — is concentrated in four belts: Odisha-Jharkhand, Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur, Ballari-Chitradurga-Chikkamagaluru, and Maharashtra-Goa. Copper comes from Khetri (Rajasthan), Singhbhum (Jharkhand), and Balaghat (MP); bauxite from the Amarkantak plateau and Maikal hills with Odisha (Panchpatmali) leading; mica from Jharkhand's Koderma belt and Andhra's Nellore belt. Coal — anthracite, bituminous, lignite, peat — is concentrated in the Damodar (Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro), Talcher, Korba, and Godavari valleys, while lignite comes from Neyveli. Petroleum flows from Mumbai High, Ankleshwar, Digboi, and the Krishna-Godavari basin; natural gas from Mumbai High, the K-G basin, and the Gulf of Khambhat. Non-conventional energy is rising — Tamil Nadu leads in wind, Karnataka and Rajasthan in solar parks (Pavagada, Bhadla), Jharkhand and Rajasthan in uranium, Manikaran (HP) and Puga (Ladakh) in geothermal, and Gulf of Kachchh in tidal potential. Minerals are finite; conservation through recycling, planned use, and a slow shift to renewables is the only path that protects both India's economy and its environment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Minerals** are naturally occurring homogeneous substances with definable internal structure; about 2,000 are known but only ~100 are economically important.
- **Modes of occurrence** — veins and lodes in igneous/metamorphic rocks (tin, copper, zinc, lead); beds in sedimentary rocks (coal, iron ore, gypsum); residual deposits (bauxite); placer deposits in valley sands (gold, platinum); and ocean waters (salt, magnesium).
- **Classification** — metallic (ferrous like iron ore, manganese; non-ferrous like copper, bauxite), non-metallic (mica, limestone, salt), and energy minerals (coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium).
- **Iron ore belts** — Odisha-Jharkhand (Badampahar, Gua, Noamundi), Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur (Bailadila), Ballari-Chitradurga-Chikkamagaluru (Kudremukh), and Maharashtra-Goa.
- **Haematite** (up to 70% iron) is the industrially most important ore; **magnetite** has the highest iron content and best magnetic qualities.
- **Manganese** — used in making steel (10 kg per tonne); Odisha is the largest producer.
- **Copper** — Khetri (Rajasthan), Singhbhum/Ghatsila (Jharkhand), and Balaghat (MP) are the three main producing belts.
- **Bauxite** — from the Amarkantak plateau and Maikal hills; Odisha (Panchpatmali in Koraput) is the leading producer; raw material for aluminium.
- **Mica** — Koderma–Gaya–Hazaribagh belt in Jharkhand is the largest producer; Nellore belt in Andhra Pradesh produces the best quality mica in the world.
- **Coal types** — anthracite (highest carbon), bituminous (most used), lignite (Neyveli, TN), peat (lowest); Gondwana coal in Damodar (Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro), Talcher, Korba; tertiary coal in the North-East.
- **Petroleum** — Mumbai High offshore (~63% of output), Ankleshwar (Gujarat), Digboi (Assam), Krishna-Godavari basin.
- **Natural gas** — Mumbai High, Krishna-Godavari basin, Gulf of Khambhat, Andaman & Nicobar; HVJ pipeline carries gas across India; CNG used to reduce urban air pollution.
- **Non-conventional sources** — Solar (Pavagada, Bhadla, Kurnool), Wind (Tamil Nadu leads, Nagercoil to Madurai), Biogas (gobar gas plants), Nuclear (Kalpakkam, Tarapur, Rawatbhata, Narora, Kakrapar, Kaiga) using uranium from Jharkhand-Rajasthan, Geothermal (Manikaran HP, Puga Ladakh), Tidal (Gulf of Kachchh).
- **Conservation** — minerals are finite and non-renewable; recycling (2/3 of world aluminium now from scrap), planned use, substitution, and better mining technology are essential.

## Key Terms

- **Mineral** — A naturally occurring homogeneous substance with a definable internal structure, usually found within rocks.
- **Ore** — An accumulation of any mineral mixed with other elements in a quantity that makes economic extraction worthwhile.
- **Vein / Lode** — Cracks, joints, or faults in igneous and metamorphic rocks in which minerals crystallise; veins are smaller, lodes are larger.
- **Haematite** — The most important industrial iron ore, reddish-brown, with up to 70 per cent iron content.
- **Magnetite** — The finest iron ore with very high iron content and excellent magnetic qualities; used in the electrical industry.
- **Ferrous mineral** — A metallic mineral that contains iron (iron ore, manganese, nickel, cobalt).
- **Non-ferrous mineral** — A metallic mineral that does not contain iron (copper, bauxite, lead, zinc, tin).
- **Bauxite** — Residual ore from which aluminium is extracted; formed by the decomposition of aluminium-rich surface rocks.
- **Anthracite** — The highest grade of coal with the maximum carbon content (above 80%) and least moisture.
- **Bituminous coal** — Medium-grade coal that is the most popular for commercial use; supplies metallurgical coke for the iron and steel industry.
- **Lignite** — A low-grade brown coal, soft, with high moisture content; Neyveli in Tamil Nadu is the main producing site.
- **Photovoltaic technology** — Technology that converts sunlight directly into electricity through solar cells made of silicon.
- **Biogas** — A mixture of methane and carbon dioxide produced by decomposition of organic matter (cattle dung, agricultural waste) in oxygen-free conditions; called *gobar gas* in rural India.
