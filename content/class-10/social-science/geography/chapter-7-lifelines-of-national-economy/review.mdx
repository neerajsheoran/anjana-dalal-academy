## In One Paragraph

The "lifelines" of India's economy are the transport and communication networks that move people, goods, and information across a vast and diverse country. India has the **world's second-largest road network** (about 62 lakh km), classified into Golden Quadrilateral Super Highways, National Highways, State Highways, District Roads, Other Roads, and Border Roads — the last built by the BRO in strategic frontier regions, where it has now bored the 9-km Atal Tunnel through the Pir Panjal. Indian Railways, the country's largest public-sector undertaking, runs **67,956 km of track** across **17 zones** on broad, metre, and narrow gauge. Pipelines — invisible but vital — move crude oil from Assam to Kanpur, from Salaya to Jalandhar, and natural gas from Hazira to Jagdishpur, eliminating transhipment losses. Inland waterways (14,500 km, 111 declared National Waterways, NW-1 on the Ganga and NW-2 on the Brahmaputra are the busiest) carry the cheapest cargo, while **12 major ports** along an 11,098-km coastline handle 95% of foreign trade. Airways — led by IGI Delhi and CSM Mumbai, with UDAN now connecting smaller towns — give India its fastest mode. Communication networks (world's largest postal system, one of Asia's largest telecom networks, Doordarshan, All India Radio, BharatNet, Digital India) tie the country together, while international trade — exports of gems, chemicals, software, agricultural goods, and imports of crude oil — make India part of a connected world economy.

## Key Takeaways

- Movement of goods and services is as important to development as production itself; transport, communication, and trade are complementary.
- **India has the world's second-largest road network** (~62 lakh km, 2020–21); roads preceded railways and remain dominant for short distances, door-to-door service, and last-mile connectivity.
- The six road classes are: **Golden Quadrilateral Super Highways**, **National Highways**, **State Highways**, **District Roads**, **Other Roads** (PMGSY rural roads), and **Border Roads** (BRO, established 1960).
- The Golden Quadrilateral links Delhi–Mumbai–Chennai–Kolkata; the **North-South Corridor** runs Srinagar to Kanniyakumari; the **East-West Corridor** runs Silchar to Porbandar. Implemented by **NHAI**.
- **Indian Railways** is Asia's longest network, reorganised into **17 zones**, with **67,956 km** of track on broad gauge (63,950 km), metre gauge (2,402 km), and narrow gauge (1,604 km). The first train ran Mumbai–Thane in **1853**.
- **Pipelines** carry crude oil, refined petroleum, and natural gas with low running cost and no transhipment losses; major routes include Upper Assam–Kanpur, Salaya–Jalandhar, and the 1,700-km **HVJ (Hazira–Vijaipur–Jagdishpur) gas pipeline** linking Mumbai High and Bassein to north and west India.
- India has **14,500 km of navigable inland waterways**; under the **National Waterways Act 2016**, 111 routes are National Waterways. **NW-1** is the Ganga (Prayagraj–Haldia, 1,620 km); **NW-2** is the Brahmaputra (Sadiya–Dhubri, 891 km); **NW-3** is the West Coast Canal in Kerala.
- India's **11,098 km coastline** has **12 major ports** handling **95% of foreign trade by volume** (68% by value): Deendayal (Kandla), Mumbai, JNPT, Mormugao, New Mangalore, Cochin on the west; V. O. Chidambaranar (Tuticorin), Chennai, Kamarajar (Ennore), Visakhapatnam, Paradip, Shyama Prasad Mookerjee (Kolkata), Haldia on the east.
- **Airways** carry the fastest and most prestigious traffic; **Indira Gandhi International (Delhi)** and **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International (Mumbai)** are the largest hubs. **Pawan Hans** serves ONGC offshore rigs and remote hill terrain. **UDAN** (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik) extends air service to small regional airports.
- The **Indian postal network is the largest in the world**; first-class mail (cards, envelopes) is airlifted and second-class mail (book packets, newspapers, periodicals) goes by surface. Six channels — Rajdhani, Metro, Green, Business, Bulk Mail, Periodical — speed urban delivery.
- Mass communication: **All India Radio (Akashvani)**, **Doordarshan**, newspapers in ~100 languages (largest count in Hindi, then English, then Urdu), and India is the **world's largest producer of feature films**, certified by the CBFC.
- **Balance of trade** = exports minus imports. **Favourable** when exports exceed imports; **unfavourable** when imports exceed exports. India's main exports include gems & jewellery, chemicals, agricultural products, engineering goods, and **software services**; main imports include **petroleum crude**, gems, chemicals, base metals, electronics, and machinery.
- **Tourism** is treated as a trade because it brings in foreign exchange; schemes include **Swadesh Darshan 2.0**, **PRASHAD**, **Vibrant Village Programme**, **Paryatan Mitra**. Foreign tourists come for heritage, eco, adventure, cultural, medical, and business tourism.
- **Digital India** is the umbrella programme that pushes IT (Indian Talent) + IT (Information Technology) = IT (India Tomorrow), backed by BharatNet broadband to every gram panchayat.

## Key Terms

- **Golden Quadrilateral** — six-lane super-highway linking Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata; built by NHAI to cut intercity travel time.
- **NHAI (National Highways Authority of India)** — the central agency that builds and maintains national highways and corridor projects.
- **Border Roads Organisation (BRO)** — Government of India undertaking established in 1960 to build strategic roads in northern and north-eastern frontier regions; built the Atal Tunnel.
- **Broad gauge / Metre gauge / Narrow gauge** — the three widths of railway track in India (1.676 m, 1.000 m, and 0.762/0.610 m), in descending order of total route length.
- **HVJ Pipeline** — the 1,700-km Hazira–Vijaipur–Jagdishpur cross-country natural gas pipeline linking Mumbai High and Bassein gas fields to western and northern India.
- **National Waterway (NW)** — an inland water route officially declared for development under the National Waterways Act 2016; 111 such routes exist, with NW-1 (Ganga) and NW-2 (Brahmaputra) the busiest.
- **Major port** — one of 12 large ports designated under the central government, handling roughly 95% of India's foreign trade by volume.
- **Tidal port** — a port whose water level rises and falls with the ocean tide, often requiring dredging (Kandla and Kolkata are examples).
- **Balance of trade** — the difference between a country's exports and imports; favourable when exports exceed imports, unfavourable when the reverse.
- **UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik)** — Regional Connectivity Scheme launched by the Ministry of Civil Aviation in 2016 to make air travel affordable on small regional routes.
- **Digital India** — Government of India's umbrella programme to deliver public services digitally and connect every village by broadband; relies on BharatNet, Aadhaar, and mobile platforms.
- **Pradhan Mantri Grameen Sadak Yojana (PMGSY)** — scheme to link every village with an all-weather motorable road.
