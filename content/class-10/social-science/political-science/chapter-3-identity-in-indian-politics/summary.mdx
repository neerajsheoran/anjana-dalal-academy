## In One Paragraph

Social diversity is normal in every society — Indian politics is shaped by three major social divisions: **gender, religion, and caste**. The instinct to "ignore these differences" is wrong — suppressing identity creates resentment; politically expressing it can empower the marginalised. **Gender**: patriarchy persists through sexual division of labour, with women doing unpaid domestic work and being excluded from public roles; the women's movement has won voting rights, education, employment, and political reservation (1/3 in panchayats; the **Women's Reservation Bill 2023** will reserve 1/3 of Lok Sabha and state seats once implemented). India ranks 127/146 in the Global Gender Gap Index. **Religion**: India's Constitution makes it a **secular** state with no official religion and equal respect for all faiths, but **communalism** (politicising religious identity for exclusion and division) remains a danger. **Caste**: a birth-based hierarchy that has caused great injustice, especially to **Dalits** (formerly "untouchables"); anti-caste reformers (Buddha, Bhakti saints, Phule, Periyar, Ambedkar, Gandhi) have fought for centuries; the Constitution abolishes untouchability and provides reservations for SCs (15%), STs (7.5%), and OBCs (27%). Caste persists in politics — sometimes democratising (Dalit empowerment), sometimes dividing (vote banks). The right approach is **accommodation and empowerment** of the marginalised, not suppression of difference or exploitation of division.

## Key Takeaways

- **Social diversity is normal**; political expression of difference can strengthen or damage democracy depending on how it's done.
- **Gender** is political — patriarchy, sexual division of labour, exclusion of women from public life.
- **Sexual division of labour**: women do unpaid domestic work, men do paid public work; this shapes power.
- **Women's movement**: voting rights (won at different times in different countries), education, employment, political representation.
- **Women's Reservation Bill, 2023**: will reserve 1/3 of Lok Sabha and state assembly seats for women (after 2026 delimitation).
- **Local government reservation**: 1/3 (often 50%) for women — over 1 million elected women in panchayats and municipalities.
- **India's gender gap**: ranks **127 out of 146** in Global Gender Gap Index 2023.
- **Religion and politics**: India is **secular** — no state religion, equal respect for all faiths, religious freedom.
- **Communalism**: political use of religion to divide; dangerous; causes riots and violence.
- **Healthy religious politics** can exist (religious-inspired civic action) vs **communalism** (exclusion, demonisation, vote-bank).
- **Caste**: hereditary social hierarchy unique to South Asia; four varnas + Dalits ("untouchables") + tribals + OBCs.
- Anti-caste tradition: Buddha, Bhakti saints (Kabir, Ravidas, Nanak), Sikh gurus, Jyotirao Phule, Periyar, Narayana Guru, Gandhi, **Ambedkar**.
- Constitutional provisions: **Article 17** (abolition of untouchability); Articles 14, 15, 16 (equality); reservations for SCs (15%), STs (7.5%), OBCs (27%).
- Caste is **transforming** but **not gone**: urban professional life less caste-driven; rural areas, marriages, some violence still affected.
- **Three conclusions about caste**: it persists; cannot be the SOLE basis of politics; doesn't always mean discrimination — can be democratising.
- **Right approach**: **accommodation and empowerment** of historically marginalised groups; reject suppression and exploitation.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Suppress differences and they erupt; exploit them and they destroy; recognise and accommodate them and they enrich.**

The Indian Constitution's wisdom was to choose the third path — recognising and accommodating gender, religious, and caste differences while empowering those who have been excluded. Living this vision is the work of every Indian citizen.
