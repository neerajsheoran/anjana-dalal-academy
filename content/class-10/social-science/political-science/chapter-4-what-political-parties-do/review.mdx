## In One Paragraph

A **political party** is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold political power, with agreed policies and programmes. Despite widespread cynicism ("all parties are the same"), parties are **essential to modern democracy** — they simplify voter choice, pool resources, form governments, develop policies, hold government accountable, connect citizens to politics, and provide alternatives. Three components: **leaders, active members, supporters/voters**. **Party systems**: **one-party** (authoritarian — China, Cuba), **two-party** (USA, UK), **multi-party** (India, Germany, Israel). India's huge diversity needs multi-party democracy. **Major Indian parties** — National: **Congress** (oldest, freedom movement legacy), **BJP** (currently ruling, Hindutva ideology), **AAP** (anti-corruption, newer), **CPI/CPI(M)** (left), **BSP** (Dalit), **NPP**. **Regional**: DMK/AIADMK (Tamil Nadu), TMC (Bengal), BJD (Odisha), TDP/YSRCP (AP), Shiv Sena, NCP, JDU, RJD, SP, Akali Dal, and many more. **Coalition era** since 1989 — NDA (BJP-led), UPA (Congress-led). **Major challenges** for Indian parties: dynastic politics, lack of internal democracy, money in politics, criminalisation (40% of MPs have criminal cases), identity politics, anti-defection rigidity, lack of transparency. **Reforms needed**: internal democracy, transparent finance, decriminalisation, issue-based politics. **Citizens' role**: vote thoughtfully, reject candidates with serious criminal cases, push for reforms through engagement and voting.

## Key Takeaways

- **Political party** = group competing for power with agreed policies.
- **Three components**: leaders, active members, supporters/voters.
- **Why parties matter**: simplify choice, form governments, develop policy, hold government accountable, connect citizens, aggregate interests, provide alternatives.
- **One-party system** (China, Cuba, North Korea) = not democracy.
- **Two-party system** (USA, UK, Australia) = clear choice, stable, but smaller groups underrepresented.
- **Multi-party system** (India, Germany, Israel) = more choice and diversity, but less stable.
- India needs multi-party — too diverse for two parties.
- **Major Indian national parties**: **INC** (Congress — 1885 — secularism, welfare), **BJP** (1980 — Hindutva, economic liberalism), **AAP** (2012 — anti-corruption), **CPI**/**CPI(M)** (left), **BSP** (Dalit-focused), **NPP**.
- **Regional parties** dominate many states: DMK/AIADMK (TN), TMC (WB), BJD (Odisha), TDP/YSRCP (AP), Shiv Sena variants, NCP variants, JDU, RJD, SP, Akali Dal, many others.
- India has 2,500+ registered parties; ~50-100 active; ~20-30 major.
- **Coalition era** since 1989 — most central governments have been coalitions.
- **NDA** (BJP-led) and **UPA** (Congress-led) are major coalitions.
- Major **challenges** for Indian parties: dynastic politics (Congress/Nehru-Gandhi, DMK/Karunanidhi, TMC/Banerjee, NCP/Pawar, etc.); lack of internal democracy; money in politics (corporate donations, black money, Electoral Bonds controversy); criminalisation (~40% of Lok Sabha MPs have criminal cases per ADR); identity politics; anti-defection law tradeoffs; lack of transparency.
- **Reforms needed**: internal party democracy; transparent financing; decriminalisation; issue-based politics; stronger anti-corruption; public funding of elections.
- **Citizens have power**: voters have rejected governments (1977, 1989, 2004, 2024 reduced BJP majority).

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Parties are imperfect tools; they are also indispensable.**

We can't run democracy without parties — but the parties we have are not what democracy deserves. The work of citizens is to demand better parties through votes, voice, and engagement.
