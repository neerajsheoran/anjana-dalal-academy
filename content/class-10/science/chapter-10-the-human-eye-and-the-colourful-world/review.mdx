## Key Concepts

- The eye works like a camera: **cornea** (major refraction) → **iris** (controls light via pupil) → **eye lens** (fine-tunes focus) → **retina** (light-sensitive screen)
- **Retina** has **rod cells** (dim light/peripheral vision) and **cone cells** (colour/bright light vision)
- Image on retina is **real and inverted**; the brain flips it right-side up
- **Power of accommodation**: ability of the eye lens to change focal length using **ciliary muscles**
- Near point (least distance of distinct vision) = **25 cm**; Far point = **infinity** (for normal eye)
- **Myopia** (short-sightedness): image forms **in front of** retina; corrected by **concave lens**
- **Hypermetropia** (long-sightedness): image forms **behind** retina; corrected by **convex lens**
- **Presbyopia**: age-related loss of accommodation; corrected by **bifocal lenses** (concave on top + convex at bottom)
- **Prism** bends light twice (entry + exit); angle between original and emergent ray = **angle of deviation**
- **Dispersion**: prism splits white light into 7 colours — **VIBGYOR** (Violet bends most, Red bends least)
- Newton's two-prism experiment: second inverted prism recombines the spectrum back into white light
- **Atmospheric refraction** (due to varying air density) causes: twinkling of stars, steady glow of planets, Sun visible ~2 min before actual sunrise and after actual sunset
- Stars **twinkle** (point sources, light fluctuates); planets **don't twinkle** (disc sources, fluctuations average out)
- **Tyndall effect**: scattering of light by colloidal particles (visible beam in fog/milk)
- **Rayleigh scattering**: shorter wavelengths scatter more; sky appears **blue** (blue scatters most; violet scatters even more but our eyes are less sensitive to it)
- **Sky is black in space**: no atmosphere, no scattering
- **Sun appears reddish at sunrise/sunset**: longer path through atmosphere scatters away blue; only red/orange survives

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Power of accommodation | Ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length to focus at different distances |
| Near point | Closest distance at which eye can see clearly (25 cm for normal eye) |
| Myopia | Defect where distant objects appear blurry; image forms in front of retina |
| Hypermetropia | Defect where nearby objects appear blurry; image forms behind retina |
| Presbyopia | Age-related weakening of accommodation |
| Dispersion | Splitting of white light into its component colours by a prism |
| Spectrum | Band of seven colours obtained by dispersion of white light |
| Scattering of light | Deflection of light by tiny particles in different directions |
| Tyndall effect | Scattering of light by colloidal particles making a beam visible |

## Remember These

- Myopia → concave lens; Hypermetropia → convex lens; Presbyopia → bifocal lens
- **VIBGYOR**: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red — violet bends **most**, red bends **least**
- The Sun appears ~2 minutes early at sunrise and ~2 minutes late at sunset due to atmospheric refraction
- Danger signals and brake lights are **red** because red scatters the **least** and travels the farthest
- White light is a **mixture of seven colours** — proved by Newton's prism experiment
