## Key Concepts

- A **chemical reaction** transforms reactants into products, detected by colour change, gas formation, temperature change, or precipitate formation
- A **chemical equation** uses symbols and formulae to represent a reaction; reactants on the left, products on the right of the arrow
- A **balanced equation** has equal atoms of each element on both sides, satisfying the **law of conservation of mass**
- Balance equations using the **hit-and-trial method** — adjust coefficients only, never change subscripts
- **State symbols**: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) aqueous
- **Combination reaction**: two or more substances form a single product (e.g., CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂)
- **Decomposition reaction**: one compound breaks into two or more simpler substances; needs energy input (heat/light/electricity)
- **Displacement reaction**: a more reactive element replaces a less reactive one from its compound
- **Double displacement reaction**: two compounds exchange ions; may produce a **precipitate**
- **Exothermic reactions** release heat (combustion, respiration); **endothermic reactions** absorb heat (decomposition)
- **Oxidation** = gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen; **Reduction** = loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen
- **Redox reactions**: oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously in the same reaction
- **Corrosion**: slow oxidation of metals by moisture and air (iron → rust, copper → green coating, silver → black tarnish)
- **Rancidity**: oxidation of fats/oils in food, causing bad smell; prevented by antioxidants, airtight storage, or nitrogen flushing
- Galvanisation, painting, oiling, and alloying are methods to prevent corrosion

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Chemical reaction | Process where reactants transform into new products |
| Balanced equation | Equation with equal number of each type of atom on both sides |
| Combination reaction | Two or more substances combine to form one product |
| Decomposition reaction | Single compound breaks into two or more simpler substances |
| Displacement reaction | A more reactive element replaces a less reactive one from its compound |
| Double displacement | Two compounds exchange ions to form two new compounds |
| Precipitate | Insoluble solid formed during a reaction in solution |
| Exothermic reaction | Reaction that releases heat energy |
| Endothermic reaction | Reaction that absorbs heat energy |
| Oxidation | Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen |
| Reduction | Loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen |
| Redox reaction | Reaction where oxidation and reduction occur together |
| Corrosion | Slow destruction of metals by reaction with air and moisture |
| Rancidity | Spoilage of food due to oxidation of fats and oils |

## Remember These

- **Never change subscripts** when balancing — only change coefficients
- Decomposition types: **thermal** (heat), **electrolytic** (electricity), **photolytic** (light)
- Respiration (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy) is an **exothermic** process
- In electrolysis of water, H₂ and O₂ are produced in **2:1 volume ratio**
- Rust is **hydrated iron oxide**; both air AND moisture must be present for rusting
