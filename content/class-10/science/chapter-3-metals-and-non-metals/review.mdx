## Key Concepts

- **Metals**: lustrous, hard, malleable, ductile, sonorous, good conductors of heat and electricity
- Exceptions: **mercury** is liquid; **sodium/potassium** are soft; **lead/mercury** are poor heat conductors
- **Non-metals**: brittle, dull, poor conductors; exceptions — **iodine** is lustrous, **graphite** conducts electricity, **diamond** is extremely hard
- **Bromine** is the only liquid non-metal; **mercury** is the only liquid metal
- Metal + O₂ → **Metal oxide** (basic); Al₂O₃ and ZnO are **amphoteric** (react with both acids and bases)
- Reactivity with oxygen: K > Na (stored in kerosene) > Mg > Al > Cu > Ag/Au (no reaction)
- Metal + Water → Metal oxide/hydroxide + H₂; Na/K react violently with cold water; Cu/Ag/Au do not react
- Metal + Dilute acid → Salt + H₂; metals **below hydrogen** in reactivity series do not react with dilute acids
- **Reactivity series**: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > **H** > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au
- **Aqua regia** (3 HCl : 1 HNO₃) dissolves gold and platinum
- **Ionic bonds** form by electron transfer from metals to non-metals; ionic compounds have high melting points, dissolve in water, conduct electricity only when molten or in solution
- **Extraction by reactivity**: low reactivity → heat alone; medium → carbon reduction (after roasting/calcination); high → electrolysis
- **Roasting**: heating sulphide ore in air → oxide; **Calcination**: heating carbonate ore in limited air → oxide
- **Thermit reaction**: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ (used to weld railway tracks)
- **Electrolytic refining**: anode = impure metal, cathode = pure metal, electrolyte = metal salt solution
- **Alloys**: homogeneous mixtures of metals; steel, stainless steel, brass, bronze, solder; have lower conductivity and melting points than pure metals
- **Galvanisation**: coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Malleability | Ability to be hammered into thin sheets |
| Ductility | Ability to be drawn into thin wires |
| Ore | Mineral from which a metal can be extracted economically |
| Gangue | Unwanted rocky impurities in an ore |
| Roasting | Heating sulphide ore in excess air to convert to oxide |
| Calcination | Heating carbonate ore in limited air to convert to oxide |
| Ionic compound | Compound formed by transfer of electrons between metal and non-metal |
| Amphoteric oxide | Oxide that reacts with both acids and bases (e.g., Al₂O₃, ZnO) |
| Alloy | Homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal with a non-metal |
| Amalgam | Alloy containing mercury |
| Galvanisation | Coating iron with zinc to prevent corrosion |

## Remember These

- HNO₃ does **not** liberate H₂ with metals (it oxidises H₂ to water)
- Ionic compounds conduct electricity **only when molten or dissolved**, not in solid state
- Na and K are stored in **kerosene**; they catch fire in open air
- 22-carat gold = 22 parts gold + 2 parts copper/silver (pure gold is 24-carat, too soft for jewellery)
- Copper gains a **green coating** (basic copper carbonate); silver turns **black** (silver sulphide)
