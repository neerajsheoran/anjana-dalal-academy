## Key Concepts

- **Reproduction** ensures continuation of a species; it is not essential for an individual's survival
- **DNA copying** during reproduction introduces small **variations** — raw material for evolution
- **Asexual reproduction**: one parent, offspring genetically similar to parent, minimal variation
- **Binary fission**: cell splits into two (Amoeba — any plane; Leishmania — fixed orientation)
- **Multiple fission**: cell forms many daughter cells inside a cyst (Plasmodium in RBCs)
- **Fragmentation**: organism breaks into pieces, each grows into a new individual (Spirogyra)
- **Regeneration**: regrowth of a whole organism from a body part using specialised cells (Planaria, Hydra)
- **Budding**: outgrowth develops on parent, detaches as new individual (Hydra, yeast)
- **Vegetative propagation**: new plants from stems (sugarcane), tubers (potato eyes), roots (sweet potato), leaves (Bryophyllum buds)
- **Spore formation**: spores released from **sporangium**; germinate in moist conditions (Rhizopus/bread mould)
- **Sexual reproduction**: two parents, male + female gametes fuse (**fertilisation**) to form a **zygote** — greater genetic variation
- **Flower parts**: sepals (protection), petals (attract pollinators), **stamens** (male: anther + filament), **pistil** (female: stigma + style + ovary with ovules)
- **Bisexual flower**: has both stamens and pistil; **Unisexual**: has only one (e.g., papaya)
- **Pollination**: transfer of pollen from anther to stigma; **self-pollination** vs **cross-pollination**
- After pollination, pollen tube grows through style; male gamete fuses with egg in ovule = **fertilisation**
- Post-fertilisation: zygote → embryo; ovule → **seed**; ovary → **fruit**
- **Puberty**: reproductive system matures; testosterone (boys), oestrogen (girls) trigger changes
- **Male system**: testes (in scrotum, produce sperm + testosterone), vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra, penis
- **Female system**: ovaries (produce eggs + oestrogen), fallopian tubes (fertilisation site), uterus (embryo develops), vagina
- Fertilisation occurs in the **fallopian tube**; embryo implants in uterine wall; **placenta** provides nutrients and removes waste
- **Menstruation**: monthly shedding of uterine lining when no fertilisation occurs (~28-day cycle)
- **Contraceptive methods**: barrier (condoms — also prevent STDs), hormonal (pills), IUDs (Copper-T), surgical (vasectomy/tubectomy)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Asexual reproduction | Reproduction involving a single parent; offspring are near-identical |
| Sexual reproduction | Reproduction involving fusion of male and female gametes from two parents |
| Fertilisation | Fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote |
| Pollination | Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma |
| Zygote | Cell formed by fusion of male and female gametes |
| Embryo | Early stage of development from repeated division of zygote |
| Placenta | Disc-shaped organ connecting embryo to mother's uterine wall for nutrient/waste exchange |
| Menstruation | Monthly shedding of uterine lining when pregnancy does not occur |
| Vegetative propagation | Asexual reproduction in plants using vegetative parts (stem, root, leaf) |
| Regeneration | Ability to regrow a complete organism from a body fragment |

## Remember These

- Regeneration is **not the same** as reproduction — it is a response to injury
- Only **condoms** protect against both pregnancy and **sexually transmitted diseases**
- The **placenta** allows exchange between mother and foetus, but their blood **never mixes directly**
- Sperm production requires temperature **slightly below body temperature** — hence testes are in the scrotum
- Bryophyllum reproduces from **leaf margin buds** — a classic exam example of vegetative propagation
