## Nutrition — Making and Getting Food

**Meera:** Arjun, I was looking at the photosynthesis equation last night. CO₂ plus water plus sunlight gives glucose and oxygen. NCERT writes it like one clean step, but the teacher said it is actually two stages.

**Arjun:** Yes — light reaction and dark reaction. I read it in the reference book my chachi gave me. Light reaction happens in the thylakoid membrane, splits water, makes ATP and NADPH. Dark reaction or Calvin cycle happens in the stroma and actually builds glucose using CO₂.

**Meera:** And stomata — they let CO₂ in and O₂ out. Guard cells swell to open them, shrink to close them, right?

**Arjun:** Right. Most plants open them by day, close at night. But desert plants like cactus do the opposite — open at night to grab CO₂, store it as an organic acid, then use it in the day with stomata shut. Saves water. The board does not ask CAM photosynthesis by name, but if you mention it in a value-added line, examiners notice.

**Meera:** For heterotrophs — saprophytic is digesting food outside the body, like fungi?

**Arjun:** Yaar, that one came in last year's PYQ as a 1-marker. Mushroom on dead wood — secretes enzymes onto the log, breaks it down externally, then absorbs. Amoeba uses pseudopodia to engulf and digest inside a food vacuole. Paramoecium uses cilia to sweep food into its oral groove. Diagrams of Amoeba and Paramoecium are classic — practise them till your hand draws them without thinking.

## The Human Digestive System

**Meera:** Arjun, can we trace a paratha through the whole digestive system? Like, exactly which enzyme acts where?

**Arjun:** Good idea — that exact 5-marker came in the 2024 prelim. Mouth — teeth crush, salivary amylase starts breaking starch in the atta into maltose. Tongue rolls it into a bolus.

**Meera:** Then the oesophagus, peristalsis pushes it down. Even if you were standing on your head, peristalsis would still move it.

**Arjun:** Stomach — HCl makes it acidic, pH around 1.5, kills bacteria and activates pepsin. Pepsin starts on the protein from the ghee and dal. Mucus protects the stomach wall from its own acid. Miss any of these three — acid, enzyme, mucus — and you lose marks.

**Meera:** Small intestine is where the action really happens. Bile from the liver does not contain enzymes but emulsifies fat — breaks big globules into tiny droplets like dishwashing liquid breaks oil.

**Arjun:** Exactly. Bigger surface area for lipase to act on. Pancreatic juice brings trypsin for proteins, lipase for fats, amylase for any starch left over. Intestinal juice finishes the job — everything becomes glucose, amino acids, fatty acids.

**Meera:** And villi absorb the nutrients. Each villus has a blood capillary and a lymph vessel. Six metres of small intestine, surface area comparable to a tennis court.

**Arjun:** That tennis court line is gold in answers. Examiners love it. And remember to write "finger-like projections" — that exact phrase from NCERT.

## Respiration — Breathing vs Energy Release

**Meera:** Arjun, breathing and respiration are not the same thing — but half our class still mixes them up.

**Arjun:** That confusion alone costs marks every year. Breathing is the physical act — air in, air out. Respiration is the chemical process inside cells — glucose broken down in the mitochondria to release ATP. Breathing supplies the oxygen; the actual energy release happens in the cell.

**Meera:** Aerobic respiration gives 38 ATP per glucose. Anaerobic gives only 2. Why such a huge gap?

**Arjun:** In aerobic, glucose is completely broken down into CO₂ and water — every drop of energy extracted. In anaerobic, it stops halfway. The energy stays locked in ethanol in yeast, lactic acid in our muscles.

**Meera:** That lactic acid — that is the burn I felt after the 400-metre run on sports day.

**Arjun:** Exactly. When you sprint, muscles need energy faster than blood can deliver oxygen, so they go anaerobic for a bit. Lactic acid accumulates, you feel the burn. After you stop and breathe hard, the extra oxygen clears it — that is the oxygen debt being repaid. Athletes in breath training shibirs and pranayama specifically work on raising this threshold so they can run longer before cramping.

**Meera:** I want to do MBBS eventually, maybe AIIMS. Will all this come back at the NEET level?

**Arjun:** Massively. The digestive enzymes here become a full unit on digestive physiology in Class 11. Mitochondria becomes glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron transport chain. If your Class 10 base is solid, the rest piles on top easily.

## Transportation and Excretion

**Meera:** Arjun, in the heart chapter — the left ventricle has the thickest wall. Why exactly?

**Arjun:** Because it pumps oxygenated blood to the entire body, against the highest pressure. Right ventricle only pumps to the lungs, which is a shorter, lower-pressure circuit. NCERT highlights this — it has come as a 2-marker.

**Meera:** And double circulation — pulmonary plus systemic, both in one heartbeat cycle. Why is the separation so important?

**Arjun:** Because we are warm-blooded. Constant 37°C body temperature needs continuous, undiluted oxygen supply. Frogs and lizards with three-chambered hearts let some mixing happen — they can afford to because they are cold-blooded.

**Meera:** Xylem and phloem — xylem is passive, phloem needs ATP. That distinction is a favourite PYQ.

**Arjun:** Yes. Xylem is dead cells, one-way upward, driven by root pressure from below and transpiration pull from above. Phloem is living sieve tubes with companion cells, needs ATP, and works both ways — up to growing shoots, down to storage roots. Examiners want you to mention "living vs dead" and "bidirectional" clearly.

**Meera:** Nephron — filtration in glomerulus, selective reabsorption in the tubule.

**Arjun:** A million nephrons per kidney. Bowman's capsule receives the filtrate — water, glucose, salts, urea all get squeezed out under pressure. Then as filtrate moves through the tubule, glucose, amino acids, most water, needed salts all get reabsorbed. What is left is urine. And when kidneys fail, dialysis mimics this externally. Very humane piece of biology.

## Exam Preparation

**Meera:** This chapter is huge, Arjun. Where should I focus my last revision?

**Arjun:** Four diagrams are non-negotiable. Human digestive system with every organ labelled. Heart with four chambers and arrows showing blood flow. Nephron with Bowman's capsule, glomerulus, tubule. Leaf cross-section with stomata and guard cells. These four alone can fetch 15-20 marks if you nail them.

**Meera:** What about the comparison tables?

**Arjun:** Three comparisons examiners love — aerobic vs anaerobic respiration, arteries vs veins, xylem vs phloem. Make a one-page sheet with all three side by side. Revise it the night before the exam. And for those targeting NEET later — every single sub-topic here expands in Class 11 NCERT. Building it solid now saves you months later.

**Meera:** Got it. Diagrams, comparisons, NCERT lines word-for-word.

**Arjun:** And keep a NCERT example sheet — every "for example" line NCERT gives, examiners reuse. Lock them in.
