## Key Concepts

- An **ecosystem** is a community of living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) components interacting together
- Ecosystems can be **natural** (forest, pond, ocean) or **artificial/human-made** (crop field, garden, aquarium)
- **Producers** (autotrophs): green plants that make food via photosynthesis
- **Consumers** (heterotrophs): herbivores (primary), carnivores (secondary/tertiary), omnivores, parasites
- **Decomposers**: bacteria and fungi that break down dead matter into simple inorganic substances
- A **food chain** shows the linear path of energy transfer: Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary consumer → Tertiary consumer
- Each step in a food chain is a **trophic level**
- Multiple interconnected food chains form a **food web**
- **10% rule**: only ~10% of energy passes from one trophic level to the next; the rest is lost as heat
- Green plants convert only ~**1%** of solar energy into food
- Energy flows in **one direction** (Sun → producers → consumers); never flows backward
- Food chains rarely exceed **3–4 trophic levels** (not enough energy remains)
- **Biological magnification** (biomagnification): non-biodegradable chemicals (e.g., DDT) accumulate at increasing concentrations at higher trophic levels; humans at the top face the highest burden
- **Ozone (O₃)** in the stratosphere absorbs harmful **UV radiation** from the Sun
- Ozone formation: UV splits O₂ → O + O; then O + O₂ → O₃
- **CFCs** (chlorofluorocarbons) from refrigerators/ACs destroy ozone; each Cl atom destroys thousands of O₃ molecules
- **Montreal Protocol** (1987): international agreement to phase out CFCs; ozone layer is slowly recovering
- **Biodegradable waste**: can be broken down by decomposers (fruit peels, paper, cotton)
- **Non-biodegradable waste**: cannot be broken down naturally; persists for decades/centuries (plastic, polythene, glass, pesticides)
- Waste management: **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle**; separate wet and dry waste; compost kitchen waste

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ecosystem | A system of biotic and abiotic components interacting as a functional unit |
| Producer | Organism that makes its own food through photosynthesis |
| Consumer | Organism that depends on other organisms for food |
| Decomposer | Organism that breaks down dead organic matter into simple substances |
| Food chain | Linear sequence showing energy transfer from one organism to the next |
| Food web | Network of interconnected food chains in an ecosystem |
| Trophic level | Each step/level in a food chain |
| Biological magnification | Progressive increase in concentration of non-biodegradable chemicals at higher trophic levels |
| Ozone layer | Protective layer of O₃ in the stratosphere that absorbs UV radiation |
| Biodegradable | Substances that can be decomposed by biological processes |
| Non-biodegradable | Substances that cannot be broken down by natural biological processes |

## Remember These

- Only **10%** of energy transfers to the next level — 90% is lost as heat in life processes
- **Biomagnification** means organisms at the **top** of the food chain (including humans) have the **highest** concentration of toxins
- The **ozone hole** was discovered over **Antarctica** in the 1980s
- CFCs are now banned/phased out under the **Montreal Protocol** (UNEP, 1987)
- Plastic is non-biodegradable because no natural enzyme can break down its synthetic molecular structure
