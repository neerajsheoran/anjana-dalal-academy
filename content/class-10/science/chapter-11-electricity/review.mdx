## Key Concepts

- **Electric current** (I) = Q/t; measured in **amperes (A)**; 1 A = 1 coulomb/second
- Conventional current flows from **positive to negative** terminal (opposite to electron flow)
- **Ammeter**: measures current, connected in **series**
- **Potential difference** (V) = W/Q; measured in **volts (V)**; 1 V = 1 J/C
- **Voltmeter**: measures voltage, connected in **parallel**
- **Ohm's Law**: V = IR (at constant temperature); V-I graph is a straight line through the origin
- **Resistance** (R): opposition to current flow; measured in **ohms (Ω)**
- **R = ρl/A** (ρ = resistivity of material, l = length, A = cross-sectional area)
- Longer wire → more resistance; thicker wire → less resistance
- **Resistivity** (ρ): material property; does not depend on dimensions; measured in Ω·m
- Low ρ: copper, aluminium (conductors); High ρ: nichrome, manganin (heating elements); Very high ρ: rubber, glass (insulators)

| Combination | Total Resistance | Current | Voltage |
|-------------|-----------------|---------|---------|
| **Series** | R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ | Same through all | Splits: V = V₁+V₂+V₃ |
| **Parallel** | 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ | Splits: I = I₁+I₂+I₃ | Same across all |

- Series: total R is **greater** than the largest individual R
- Parallel: total R is **smaller** than the smallest individual R
- **Heating effect**: H = I²Rt (Joule's law); also H = VIt = V²t/R
- **Nichrome** used in heaters: high resistivity, high melting point, oxidation resistant
- **Tungsten** used in bulb filaments: can withstand ~3380°C
- **Fuse**: thin wire that melts and breaks circuit when current exceeds safe limit
- **Electric power**: P = VI = I²R = V²/R; measured in **watts (W)**
- **1 kWh** = 1 unit of electricity = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
- Home appliances are wired in **parallel**: each gets full voltage, works independently, adding appliances doesn't affect others

## Important Formulas

| Formula | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| I = Q/t | Current = charge / time |
| V = IR | Ohm's Law |
| R = ρl/A | Resistance from material properties |
| H = I²Rt | Heat produced (Joule's law) |
| P = VI | Electric power |
| 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J | Commercial unit of energy |

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Electric current | Rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor |
| Potential difference | Work done per unit charge in moving charge between two points |
| Resistance | Opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of current |
| Resistivity | Resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area |
| Electric power | Rate at which electrical energy is consumed |
| Kilowatt-hour | Commercial unit of electrical energy (1 unit on electricity bill) |

## Remember These

- Ammeter = **series**; Voltmeter = **parallel** — mixing them up is a common exam mistake
- In parallel circuits, total resistance is **always less** than the smallest resistor
- Home wiring is **parallel** so each appliance gets **220 V** and can be switched independently
- **Short circuit**: live and neutral wires touch directly → very high current → fuse blows
- Alloys (not pure metals) are used in heating elements because they have **high and stable resistivity**
