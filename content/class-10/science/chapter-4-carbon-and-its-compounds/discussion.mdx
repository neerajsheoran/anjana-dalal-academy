## Why Carbon Shares Instead of Transferring

**Meera:** Arjun, carbon has 4 outer electrons. Why does it not just gain or lose 4 like sodium and chlorine do?

**Arjun:** Pure energy logic. To lose 4 electrons, carbon would need to overcome 4 increasing ionisation energies — the 4th is enormous. To gain 4, it would have to hold 10 electrons on a nucleus with only 6 protons — that nucleus simply cannot pull that many in. Sharing is the only path that does not break the energy bank.

**Meera:** So covalent bonding is not just a textbook category — it is what carbon physically must do.

**Arjun:** Exactly. NCERT explains this on page 58 — that whole paragraph is gold for the 3-mark "why is carbon tetravalent" question. The 2023 board paper asked something close: "why does carbon form covalent bonds." Half the class wrote vague answers. The energy reasoning is what fetches full marks.

**Meera:** Single, double, triple bonds — for the board exam, do I need to know the strength order?

**Arjun:** Yes. Triple stronger than double, double stronger than single. Shorter and stronger as more pairs are shared. The N≡N in nitrogen gas is so strong that nitrogen is basically unreactive at room temperature. That is why 78% of our atmosphere is just sitting there, doing nothing.

**Meera:** And the low melting point of covalent compounds — that comes from weak forces between molecules, not weak bonds within them?

**Arjun:** Right. The bonds inside one CH₄ molecule are strong. But between two CH₄ molecules, the forces are weak. Heat separates molecules easily, so methane is a gas at room temperature. Diamond is the famous exception — every atom is bonded to four others in one continuous network, so there are no separate molecules to pull apart.

## Catenation, Tetravalency, and Carbon's Diversity

**Meera:** Arjun, catenation. Carbon bonds to itself in long chains. Can any other element do this?

**Arjun:** Silicon can manage about 7-8 atoms, but those chains are unstable and reactive. Sulphur forms small rings. Carbon's C-C bond is uniquely strong because the carbon atom is small — the shared electrons are held very close to both nuclei. That bond strength is why we have polythene, DNA, proteins, cellulose, petroleum hydrocarbons — millions of compounds. NCERT page 59 covers this.

**Meera:** So organic chemistry is literally just "the chemistry of one element" — and it still has more compounds than the rest of the periodic table combined?

**Arjun:** Yes. That fact alone often comes as a 1-mark factual recall. Worth memorising the specific phrasing.

**Meera:** Saturated versus unsaturated — the cooking oil connection is what makes it click for me.

**Arjun:** That is a great anchor. Cooking oils — mustard, groundnut, sunflower — have unsaturated fatty acids with C=C double bonds, which is why they stay liquid. When factories add hydrogen across those double bonds using a nickel catalyst, the oils become saturated and solid. That is how vanaspati ghee is made. The chemistry inside a Dalda tin is a single addition reaction repeated millions of times.

**Meera:** And health-wise unsaturated is better for the heart?

**Arjun:** Generally yes. NEET biology talks about this — saturated fats raise LDL cholesterol, unsaturated fats are healthier. The 2022 NEET paper had a question on hydrogenation. Class 10 chemistry feeding into Class 12 biology.

## Naming Compounds and the Homologous Series

**Meera:** Arjun, the homologous series — each member differs by CH₂. Same general formula, gradually changing properties. How does this come in the board paper?

**Arjun:** Two common formats. One — they give you the formula of the 3rd member and ask you to write the 5th. Two — they ask "what is a homologous series, give two characteristics." Pure scoring questions if you have the framework. I memorised three series: alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, alkenes CₙH₂ₙ, alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂.

**Meera:** And IUPAC naming. Prefix for carbon count, suffix for functional group.

**Arjun:** Yes. Meth, eth, prop, but, pent, hex — for 1 to 6 carbons. Then suffix: -ane single, -ene double, -yne triple. Functional group: -ol alcohol, -al aldehyde, -one ketone, -oic acid carboxylic. And drop the terminal 'e' when the suffix starts with a vowel — that is the rule students forget.

**Meera:** So propan-1-ol — 3 carbons, alcohol, OH on carbon 1. Got it.

**Arjun:** And ethanal — 2 carbons, aldehyde. CH₃COOH is ethanoic acid in IUPAC, acetic acid in common name. Board paper wants both names sometimes, so I noted them in pairs. Methanal-formaldehyde, ethanal-acetaldehyde, ethanoic-acetic.

## Reactions of Carbon Compounds

**Meera:** Arjun, four major reaction types — combustion, oxidation, addition, substitution. Each board paper picks one or two. Which are most likely?

**Arjun:** Combustion is almost always there because it links to ethanol fuel. Oxidation of ethanol to ethanoic acid with alkaline KMnO₄ is a classic — both the equation and the colour change matter. Addition reaction comes up because of hydrogenation and vanaspati ghee. Substitution comes up with the methane plus chlorine equation needing sunlight.

**Meera:** The clean blue flame versus yellow smoky flame distinction — I remember saturated burns clean, unsaturated burns smoky.

**Arjun:** Yes. And the reason — unsaturated compounds have a higher carbon-to-hydrogen ratio, so they burn with unburnt carbon as soot. The 2023 board paper had exactly this question: "why does ethyne burn with a sooty flame?" Three-mark answer needs the C:H ratio reasoning.

**Meera:** What about ethanol-blended petrol? I see notices at petrol pumps that say E10 or E20.

**Arjun:** That is the National Biofuel Policy. India blends up to 20% ethanol with petrol now. The ethanol comes from sugarcane molasses — BPCL and IOCL distill it and blend at their refineries. Reduces crude oil imports, reduces emissions, and adds farm income. Class 10 chemistry meets national policy.

**Meera:** And esterification — acid plus alcohol gives ester plus water. That gives the fruity smell of pickles and perfumes?

**Arjun:** Yes. Acetic acid plus ethanol gives ethyl acetate, sweet fruity smell. The reverse — saponification — is how soap is made. Ester plus NaOH gives soap and glycerol. Both reactions are 3-mark equation favourites.

## Soaps and Detergents

**Meera:** Arjun, the micelle diagram — how detailed does the board paper want it?

**Arjun:** A neat circle, hydrophobic tails pointing inward, hydrophilic heads facing the water outside, grease trapped in the middle. Two arrows — one labelling water, one labelling grease. The 2024 board paper had a 5-mark question with the diagram worth 2 marks alone.

**Meera:** And why soap fails in hard water?

**Arjun:** Calcium and magnesium ions in hard water react with soap to form an insoluble scum. Soap molecules are wasted, scum sticks to clothes. The Mumbai chawl water that is borewell-fed is famously hard — many families end up using detergent instead of soap.

**Meera:** Detergents do not form scum because their structure does not react with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ the same way?

**Arjun:** Right. Sodium salts of sulphonic acids do not precipitate with hardness ions. That is why every Indian detergent — Surf, Tide, Ariel — works in any water type. And NCERT has a specific note that you cannot use detergent to test for hard water, only soap can. That is a clever 1-mark question that has come up twice.

## Exam Strategy and Beyond

**Meera:** Arjun, this is the heaviest chemistry chapter. What is your prioritisation?

**Arjun:** Five blocks. One — covalent bonding and electron-dot structures for methane, ethene, ethyne. Two — homologous series and IUPAC naming up to C5. Three — the four reaction types with one example each. Four — ethanol and ethanoic acid reactions, especially esterification and the test with sodium carbonate. Five — soaps, detergents, micelles, hard water.

**Meera:** Common mistakes?

**Arjun:** Four big ones. One — confusing molecular formula with structural formula. Always show the bonds. Two — miscounting carbons in naming. Three — forgetting to balance combustion equations. Four — mixing up addition and substitution. Addition only with unsaturated compounds, substitution only with saturated.

**Meera:** And for NEET and JEE this becomes the giant organic chemistry section?

**Arjun:** The biggest single block of marks in JEE chemistry. Class 11 introduces general organic chemistry — IUPAC, isomerism, reaction mechanisms. Class 12 has 10 chapters of organic. Every named reaction in JEE — Sandmeyer, Cannizzaro, Aldol, Wurtz — builds on what we are doing now. The strength of our Class 10 base decides whether Class 11 feels manageable or overwhelming.

**Meera:** And the Indian industry connection?

**Arjun:** Massive. Reliance and IOCL refineries run on hydrocarbon chemistry. The pharmaceutical hubs at Hyderabad, Mumbai, and Ahmedabad — Sun Pharma, Cipla, Dr. Reddy's, Aurobindo, Lupin — every drug they make is an organic compound with a specific functional group. India is the world's largest exporter of generic medicines. That entire industry rests on understanding the reactions you and I are revising tonight.

**Arjun:** Done. And I will photograph the soap-micelle diagram from NCERT page 81 — that is the version examiners expect to see.
