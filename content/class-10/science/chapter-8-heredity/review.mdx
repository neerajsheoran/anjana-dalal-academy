## Key Concepts

- **Heredity**: transmission of traits from parents to offspring
- **Variations** during reproduction (DNA copying errors + genetic recombination) drive **evolution**
- **Gregor Mendel** — Father of Genetics; studied pea plants (*Pisum sativum*)
- Pea plants chosen because: contrasting traits, self/cross-pollination possible, quick growth, large offspring numbers
- **Monohybrid cross** (one trait): TT × tt → F1 all Tt (tall); F1 × F1 → F2 ratio **3 tall : 1 dwarf**
- **Dominant trait**: expressed when at least one dominant allele is present; **Recessive trait**: expressed only when both alleles are recessive
- F2 genotypic ratio: **1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt**; phenotypic ratio: **3 : 1**
- **Genotype** = genetic makeup (TT, Tt, tt); **Phenotype** = visible appearance (tall/dwarf)
- **Homozygous** = both alleles same (TT or tt); **Heterozygous** = different alleles (Tt)
- **Dihybrid cross** (two traits): RRYY × rryy → F1 all RrYy; F2 ratio **9:3:3:1** (new combinations appear)
- **Law of Independent Assortment**: genes for different traits are inherited independently
- Genes code for **proteins** (enzymes) that determine traits
- **Genes** are located on **chromosomes** (made of DNA) in the cell nucleus
- Body cells are **diploid** (2 sets of chromosomes); gametes are **haploid** (1 set)
- Fertilisation restores the **diploid** number in the offspring
- **Sex determination in humans**: females = **XX**, males = **XY**
- All eggs carry **X**; sperms carry either **X** or **Y**
- X-sperm + egg = **XX (girl)**; Y-sperm + egg = **XY (boy)** — probability is always **50:50**
- Sex of the child is determined by the **father's sperm**, not the mother

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Gene | Unit of heredity; segment of DNA that codes for a specific protein |
| Allele | Different forms of the same gene (e.g., T and t for height) |
| Dominant trait | Trait expressed when at least one dominant allele is present |
| Recessive trait | Trait expressed only when both alleles are recessive |
| Genotype | Genetic constitution of an organism (e.g., TT, Tt, tt) |
| Phenotype | Observable physical appearance of an organism |
| Homozygous | Both alleles for a trait are identical (TT or tt) |
| Heterozygous | Two different alleles for a trait (Tt) |
| Monohybrid cross | Cross involving a single contrasting trait |
| Dihybrid cross | Cross involving two contrasting traits simultaneously |
| Sex chromosomes | Chromosomes that determine the sex of an organism (X and Y in humans) |
| Autosomes | The 22 pairs of non-sex chromosomes in humans |

## Remember These

- Humans have **23 pairs** of chromosomes: 22 pairs autosomes + 1 pair sex chromosomes
- In a monohybrid cross, **F1 shows only the dominant trait** — the recessive trait reappears in F2
- Dihybrid F2 ratio **9:3:3:1** includes **new trait combinations** not present in parents
- The father determines the sex of the child — **not** the mother (all eggs are X)
- A heterozygous organism (Tt) **looks dominant** but **carries** the recessive allele
