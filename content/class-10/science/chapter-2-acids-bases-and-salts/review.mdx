## Key Concepts

- **Acids** taste sour, turn blue litmus **red**; **bases** taste bitter, feel soapy, turn red litmus **blue**
- **Indicators**: litmus (natural), turmeric, methyl orange, phenolphthalein (synthetic); onion/vanilla are **olfactory indicators**
- Acid + Metal → **Salt + H₂** (pop sound test for hydrogen)
- Acid + Metal carbonate/hydrogencarbonate → **Salt + H₂O + CO₂** (lime water turns milky)
- Base + Acid → **Salt + Water** (neutralisation reaction)
- **Metal oxides** are basic; **non-metal oxides** are acidic
- Acids produce **H⁺ (or H₃O⁺)** ions in water; bases produce **OH⁻** ions in water
- Without water, acids and bases do not show their properties (dry HCl cannot change litmus)
- Dilution rule: always add **acid to water**, never water to acid (exothermic process)
- **pH scale**: 0–14; pH 7 = neutral; below 7 = acidic; above 7 = basic
- pH of blood: 7.0–7.8; acid rain: pH < 5.6; tooth enamel dissolves below pH 5.5
- **Antacids** (e.g., Mg(OH)₂) neutralise excess stomach acid
- **Chlor-alkali process**: electrolysis of brine → NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂
- **Baking soda** (NaHCO₃): used in baking powder, antacids, fire extinguishers
- **Washing soda** (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O): used in glass/soap making, water softening
- **Plaster of Paris** (CaSO₄·½H₂O): made by heating gypsum at 373 K; sets hard by absorbing water
- **Water of crystallisation**: fixed water molecules in a crystal (e.g., CuSO₄·5H₂O is blue; anhydrous CuSO₄ is white)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Indicator | Substance that shows different colours/odours in acidic vs basic solutions |
| Neutralisation | Reaction between acid and base producing salt and water |
| pH scale | Scale from 0–14 measuring acidity or basicity of a solution |
| Strong acid | Acid that fully ionises in water (e.g., HCl, H₂SO₄) |
| Weak acid | Acid that partially ionises in water (e.g., CH₃COOH, citric acid) |
| Alkali | A base that dissolves in water |
| Chlor-alkali process | Electrolysis of brine to produce NaOH, Cl₂, and H₂ |
| Bleaching powder | Ca(ClO)₂ — made by passing Cl₂ over dry slaked lime |
| Water of crystallisation | Fixed number of water molecules chemically bound in a crystal |
| Plaster of Paris | CaSO₄·½H₂O — sets hard when mixed with water |

## Remember These

- Salt pH depends on parent acid and base: strong acid + strong base → neutral; strong acid + weak base → acidic; weak acid + strong base → basic
- Excess CO₂ in lime water makes milkiness **disappear** (forms soluble Ca(HCO₃)₂)
- Baking powder = baking soda + tartaric acid (CO₂ makes bread fluffy)
- Plaster of Paris must be stored in **moisture-proof containers** or it hardens prematurely
- Bee sting (formic acid) is treated with **baking soda** (a mild base)
