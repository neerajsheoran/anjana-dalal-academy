## Key Concepts

- Animals use the **nervous system** (fast) and **endocrine system** (slow, sustained) for control and coordination
- Plants use **phytohormones** (no nervous system)
- **Neuron** structure: dendrites (receive signals) → cell body (processes) → axon (transmits) → axon terminals (pass signal forward)
- Signals travel as **electrical impulses** within a neuron and cross the **synapse** via **neurotransmitters** (chemical)
- **Reflex action**: ultra-fast involuntary response controlled by the **spinal cord**
- **Reflex arc**: Receptor → Sensory neuron → Spinal cord (relay neuron) → Motor neuron → Effector (muscle/gland)
- **Cerebrum** (fore-brain): thinking, memory, voluntary actions, sensory processing
- **Cerebellum** (hind-brain): balance and coordination of movements
- **Medulla oblongata**: controls involuntary functions — heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure
- **Pons**: relay station between cerebrum and cerebellum
- **Spinal cord**: relays signals between brain and body; independently handles reflexes
- **Nastic movements** in plants: not growth-related (e.g., Mimosa/chhui-mui folds due to water loss in cells)
- **Tropic movements**: growth-related, directional response to stimulus

| Tropism | Stimulus |
|---------|----------|
| Phototropism | Light |
| Geotropism | Gravity |
| Hydrotropism | Water |
| Chemotropism | Chemical |
| Thigmotropism | Touch |

- **Auxin**: cell elongation, phototropism (accumulates on shaded side)
- **Gibberellins**: stem elongation, flowering
- **Cytokinins**: cell division
- **Abscisic acid (ABA)**: inhibits growth, promotes leaf fall/dormancy
- **Ethylene**: fruit ripening (gaseous hormone)
- **Endocrine glands** secrete hormones directly into the bloodstream

| Gland | Hormone | Function |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Pituitary | Growth hormone | Body growth |
| Thyroid | Thyroxin | Metabolic rate |
| Pancreas | Insulin | Lowers blood sugar |
| Adrenal | Adrenaline | Fight-or-flight response |
| Testes | Testosterone | Male puberty changes |
| Ovaries | Oestrogen | Female puberty changes |

- **Iodine deficiency** → enlarged thyroid → **goitre**
- **Insulin deficiency** → **diabetes** (high blood sugar)
- **Feedback mechanism**: hormone levels are self-regulated (e.g., blood sugar rises → more insulin released → sugar drops → insulin reduces)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Neuron | Basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system |
| Synapse | Gap between two neurons where signal is transmitted chemically |
| Reflex action | Involuntary, rapid, automatic response controlled by the spinal cord |
| Reflex arc | Pathway of nerve impulse during a reflex action |
| Hormone | Chemical substance secreted by endocrine glands into the blood |
| Tropism | Directional growth movement of a plant in response to a stimulus |
| Nastic movement | Non-directional plant movement not related to growth |
| Feedback mechanism | Self-regulating process to maintain hormone balance |

## Remember These

- **Voluntary** actions → cerebrum; **Involuntary** actions → medulla; **Reflex** actions → spinal cord
- Auxin on the **shaded side** makes that side grow faster, bending the shoot **towards light**
- Nervous system = fast, short-lived, electrical; Endocrine system = slow, long-lasting, chemical
- Iodised salt prevents **goitre** (iodine is needed to make thyroxin)
- The brain is protected by the **cranium** (skull) and surrounded by **cerebrospinal fluid**
