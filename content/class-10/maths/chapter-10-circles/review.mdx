## Key Concepts

- A **tangent** to a circle touches it at **exactly one point** (the point of contact)
- A **secant** crosses the circle at **two points**
- A tangent is the limiting position of a secant when its two intersection points merge
- **Theorem 10.1**: The tangent at any point is **perpendicular** to the radius at that point
- **Theorem 10.2**: Two tangents from an **external point** are **equal** in length
- The line from the external point to the centre **bisects** the angle between the two tangents
- Number of tangents from a point: **inside** = 0, **on the circle** = 1, **outside** = 2
- Tangents at the two ends of a **diameter** are **parallel** to each other
- Angle between tangents from an external point + angle at centre between radii to contact points = **180 degrees** (supplementary)
- If a quadrilateral circumscribes a circle: **sum of opposite sides are equal** (PQ + RS = PS + QR)
- A **parallelogram** circumscribing a circle must be a **rhombus** (all sides equal)
- Tangent length can be found using **Pythagoras theorem**: tangent^2 + radius^2 = (distance to centre)^2

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Tangent** | Line touching a circle at exactly one point |
| **Secant** | Line intersecting a circle at two points |
| **Point of contact** | The single point where a tangent meets the circle |
| **Tangent length** | Distance from an external point to the point of contact |

## Remember These

- Tangent is perpendicular to radius -- this creates a **right angle** at the contact point, enabling Pythagoras theorem
- From an external point, **both tangents are equal** -- this is heavily tested in board exams
- For circumscribed quadrilateral problems, apply **PQ + RS = PS + QR** immediately
- The angle subtended at the centre by the two contact points and the angle between the tangents are **supplementary** (sum = 180)
- A chord of the outer concentric circle that is tangent to the inner circle is **bisected** at the point of tangency
