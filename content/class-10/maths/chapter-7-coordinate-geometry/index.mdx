## Introduction

In Class 9, you discovered that pinpointing a location on a flat surface requires a pair of **coordinate axes**. The horizontal distance of a point from the y-axis is its **x-coordinate (abscissa)**, and the vertical distance from the x-axis is its **y-coordinate (ordinate)**.

In this chapter, we build on that foundation with three powerful tools:

1. **Distance Formula** -- calculating the straight-line gap between two points
2. **Section Formula** -- locating a point that splits a line segment in a specific ratio
3. **Area of a Triangle** -- computing area directly from vertex coordinates

From Google Maps routing to satellite orbit calculations at ISRO, coordinate geometry powers the modern world!

## Distance Formula

### A Motivating Scenario

Rohit's house is 20 km east and 15 km north of Amit's house. What is the straight-line distance between their homes?

Using Pythagoras theorem: Distance = root(20^2 + 15^2) = root(400 + 225) = root(625) = **25 km**

### The General Formula

The distance between two points **P(x1, y1)** and **Q(x2, y2)** is:

**PQ = root[(x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2]**

![Right triangle formed by two points P and Q on a coordinate plane, with horizontal and vertical legs showing the distance formula derived from Pythagoras theorem](distance-formula-right-triangle.png)

This is the **distance formula** -- a direct application of the Pythagoras theorem on the coordinate plane.

### Special Cases

- Distance from a point **P(x, y) to the origin** O(0, 0): **OP = root(x^2 + y^2)**
- Distance between two points on the **x-axis**, say (x1, 0) and (x2, 0): **|x2 - x1|**
- Distance between two points on the **y-axis**, say (0, y1) and (0, y2): **|y2 - y1|**

> **Key idea:** The distance formula is the Pythagoras theorem wearing a coordinate-geometry disguise. The horizontal and vertical gaps between the points form two legs of a right triangle; the distance is the hypotenuse.

## Worked Examples on Distance Formula

### Example 1: Distance Between Two Points

Find the distance between P(5, -1) and Q(-3, 5).

PQ = root[(5 - (-3))^2 + (-1 - 5)^2]
= root[(8)^2 + (-6)^2]
= root[64 + 36]
= root(100)
= **10 units**

### Example 2: Verifying a Square

Show that the points A(0, 5), B(3, 0), C(-2, -3), D(-5, 2) form a square.

- AB = root(9 + 25) = root(34)
- BC = root(25 + 9) = root(34)
- CD = root(9 + 25) = root(34)
- DA = root(25 + 9) = root(34)
- AC = root(4 + 64) = root(68) (diagonal)
- BD = root(64 + 4) = root(68) (diagonal)

All four sides equal root(34) and both diagonals equal root(68). Hence ABCD is a **square**.

### Example 3: Testing Collinearity

Are the points A(2, 3), B(5, 6), and C(9, 10) collinear (on the same line)?

- AB = root(9 + 9) = root(18) = 3 root(2)
- BC = root(16 + 16) = root(32) = 4 root(2)
- AC = root(49 + 49) = root(98) = 7 root(2)

Since AB + BC = 3 root(2) + 4 root(2) = 7 root(2) = AC, the points are **collinear**.

> **Key idea:** Three points A, B, C are collinear when the sum of two of the pairwise distances equals the third.

### Example 4: Equidistant Point Condition

Find a relation between x and y such that (x, y) is equidistant from (5, -3) and (-1, 3).

Set the distances equal:
(x - 5)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = (x + 1)^2 + (y - 3)^2

Expanding and simplifying:
x^2 - 10x + 25 + y^2 + 6y + 9 = x^2 + 2x + 1 + y^2 - 6y + 9

-10x + 6y + 34 = 2x - 6y + 10

-12x + 12y = -24

**x - y = 2**

This equation represents the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining (5, -3) and (-1, 3).

## Applications of the Distance Formula

The distance formula helps you:

1. **Classify triangles** formed by three points (equilateral, isosceles, right-angled)
2. **Identify quadrilateral types** (square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram)
3. **Test collinearity** of three points
4. **Locate points on an axis** that are equidistant from two given points

### Identifying Quadrilaterals

| Shape | What to Verify |
|-------|---------------|
| Parallelogram | Opposite sides are equal |
| Rectangle | Opposite sides equal AND diagonals equal |
| Rhombus | All four sides equal |
| Square | All four sides equal AND diagonals equal |

## Section Formula

### A Practical Situation

A mobile tower needs to be erected between two villages A and B such that it is twice as close to A as to B. If A is at the origin and B is at (27, 12), where should the tower be placed?

The tower divides AB in the ratio 1:2.

### The General Formula

![Line segment AB with point P dividing it in ratio m1:m2, with coordinates labeled](section-formula-diagram.png)

If point **P(x, y)** divides the line segment joining **A(x1, y1)** and **B(x2, y2)** internally in the ratio **m1 : m2**, then:

**x = (m1 x x2 + m2 x x1) / (m1 + m2)**

**y = (m1 x y2 + m2 x y1) / (m1 + m2)**

This is the **section formula**.

> **Key idea:** Picture it as a weighted average. The closer the point is to B, the more B's coordinates influence the result, and vice versa.

### Solving the Tower Problem

P divides AB in ratio 1:2, where A(0, 0) and B(27, 12).

x = (1 x 27 + 2 x 0) / (1 + 2) = 27/3 = **9**
y = (1 x 12 + 2 x 0) / (1 + 2) = 12/3 = **4**

The tower should stand at **P(9, 4)**.

## Mid-Point Formula

When a point divides a segment in the ratio **1:1**, it sits at the **mid-point** -- a special case of the section formula.

The mid-point of the segment joining **A(x1, y1)** and **B(x2, y2)** is:

**M = ((x1 + x2)/2, (y1 + y2)/2)**

### Worked Example

Find the mid-point of A(6, -4) and B(-2, 10).

M = ((6 + (-2))/2, (-4 + 10)/2) = (4/2, 6/2) = **(2, 3)**

## Worked Examples on Section Formula

### Example 1

Find the coordinates of the point dividing the segment joining A(3, -4) and B(-5, 6) in the ratio 3:5.

x = (3(-5) + 5(3)) / (3 + 5) = (-15 + 15)/8 = **0**
y = (3(6) + 5(-4)) / (3 + 5) = (18 - 20)/8 = **-1/4**

The point is **(0, -1/4)**.

### Example 2

Determine the ratio in which the point (3, -2) divides the segment joining (1, 4) and (7, -14).

Let the ratio be k:1.

x-coordinate: 3 = (7k + 1) / (k + 1)
3k + 3 = 7k + 1
-4k = -2
**k = 1/2**

So the ratio is **1:2**.

Verify with y-coordinate: y = (1/2 x (-14) + 4) / (1/2 + 1) = (-7 + 4) / (3/2) = (-3) / (3/2) = -2. Correct!

### Example 3: Trisection Points

Find the trisection points of the segment joining A(1, -3) and B(-8, 6).

The trisection points P and Q divide AB into three equal parts.

- P divides AB in ratio 1:2:
  P = ((1(-8) + 2(1))/3, (1(6) + 2(-3))/3) = (-6/3, 0/3) = **(-2, 0)**

- Q divides AB in ratio 2:1:
  Q = ((2(-8) + 1(1))/3, (2(6) + 1(-3))/3) = (-15/3, 9/3) = **(-5, 3)**

## Area of a Triangle

Given the coordinates of three vertices, the area can be computed with a single formula.

### The Formula

Area of a triangle with vertices **A(x1, y1)**, **B(x2, y2)**, and **C(x3, y3)** is:

**Area = (1/2) |x1(y2 - y3) + x2(y3 - y1) + x3(y1 - y2)|**

The **absolute value** ensures the area is always positive.

> **Key idea:** If this formula yields zero, the three points are **collinear** -- they lie on the same straight line and cannot form a triangle.

### Worked Example 1

Find the area of the triangle with vertices A(2, 3), B(-1, 0), C(4, -2).

Area = (1/2) |2(0 - (-2)) + (-1)((-2) - 3) + 4(3 - 0)|
= (1/2) |2(2) + (-1)(-5) + 4(3)|
= (1/2) |4 + 5 + 12|
= (1/2) |21|
= **10.5 square units**

### Worked Example 2: Collinearity Check

Are the points (2, 1), (4, 5), and (6, 9) collinear?

Area = (1/2) |2(5 - 9) + 4(9 - 1) + 6(1 - 5)|
= (1/2) |2(-4) + 4(8) + 6(-4)|
= (1/2) |-8 + 32 - 24|
= (1/2) |0|
= 0

Since the area is zero, the points are **collinear**.

### Worked Example 3: Finding an Unknown Coordinate

Find the value of p if the points A(1, 2), B(3, p), and C(5, -2) are collinear.

For collinearity, area = 0:

0 = (1/2) |1(p - (-2)) + 3((-2) - 2) + 5(2 - p)|
0 = |1(p + 2) + 3(-4) + 5(2 - p)|
0 = |p + 2 - 12 + 10 - 5p|
0 = |-4p|
**p = 0**

## Area of a Quadrilateral

To find the area of a quadrilateral whose vertices are known, split it into two triangles using a diagonal and add their areas.

**Area of quadrilateral ABCD = Area of triangle ABC + Area of triangle ACD**

## Chapter Summary

1. **Distance Formula:** The distance between P(x1, y1) and Q(x2, y2) is:
   PQ = root[(x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2]

2. **Section Formula:** The point P dividing the segment from A(x1, y1) to B(x2, y2) in ratio m1:m2 has coordinates:
   ((m1.x2 + m2.x1)/(m1 + m2), (m1.y2 + m2.y1)/(m1 + m2))

3. **Mid-point Formula:** Mid-point of A(x1, y1) and B(x2, y2) is:
   ((x1 + x2)/2, (y1 + y2)/2)

4. **Area of a Triangle** with vertices (x1, y1), (x2, y2), (x3, y3):
   Area = (1/2)|x1(y2 - y3) + x2(y3 - y1) + x3(y1 - y2)|

5. Three points are **collinear** if the area of the triangle they form is zero.
