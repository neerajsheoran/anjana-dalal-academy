## Introduction

Picture yourself standing at India Gate in Delhi, gazing up at the very top. A right triangle forms naturally between you, the base of the monument, and its peak. Could you determine the height without climbing up with a measuring tape? Absolutely -- using **trigonometry**!

The word **trigonometry** has Greek roots:
- **tri** = three
- **gon** = sides (angles)
- **metron** = measurement

Trigonometry explores the connections between the sides and angles of triangles. Ancient civilisations in Mesopotamia, Egypt, and India all contributed to its development. The Indian mathematician **Aryabhata** (476-550 CE) pioneered the concept of "ardha-jya" (half-chord), which evolved into our modern sine function.

## Trigonometric Ratios

Take a right triangle PQR, right-angled at Q. For the acute angle P:

![Right triangle ABC with angle A labeled, showing opposite side (BC), adjacent side (AB), and hypotenuse (AC)](right-triangle-sides-labeled.png)

- The side **opposite** to angle P is **QR**
- The side **adjacent** to angle P is **PQ**
- The **hypotenuse** (longest side, facing the right angle) is **PR**

### The Six Trigonometric Ratios

| Ratio | What it measures | Formula |
|-------|-----------------|---------|
| **sin P** | opposite / hypotenuse | QR / PR |
| **cos P** | adjacent / hypotenuse | PQ / PR |
| **tan P** | opposite / adjacent | QR / PQ |
| **cosec P** | hypotenuse / opposite | PR / QR |
| **sec P** | hypotenuse / adjacent | PR / PQ |
| **cot P** | adjacent / opposite | PQ / QR |

### Reciprocal Relationships

- **cosec P = 1 / sin P**
- **sec P = 1 / cos P**
- **cot P = 1 / tan P**

### Quotient Relationships

- **tan P = sin P / cos P**
- **cot P = cos P / sin P**

> **Key idea:** The expression "sin P" is a single notation representing "the sine of angle P". Taken apart from the angle, "sin" alone is meaningless.

## Deriving All Ratios from a Single One

Knowing just one trigonometric ratio lets you calculate every other ratio, using the Pythagoras theorem.

### Worked Example

Given cos P = 5/13, find all remaining trigonometric ratios.

**Step 1:** cos P = adjacent/hypotenuse = PQ/PR = 5/13

Let PQ = 5k, then PR = 13k.

**Step 2:** Find QR using Pythagoras theorem:
QR^2 = PR^2 - PQ^2 = 169k^2 - 25k^2 = 144k^2
QR = 12k

**Step 3:** Calculate all ratios:
- sin P = QR/PR = 12k/13k = **12/13**
- tan P = QR/PQ = 12k/5k = **12/5**
- cosec P = 1/sin P = **13/12**
- sec P = 1/cos P = **13/5**
- cot P = 1/tan P = **5/12**

> **Key idea:** Trigonometric ratios depend solely on the angle, not on the triangle's size. Enlarge or shrink the right triangle keeping angle P the same, and every ratio stays unchanged (because the triangles are similar).

## Trigonometric Ratios of Specific Angles

These standard values are essential for the board exam.

### Values for 0, 30, 45, 60, and 90 Degrees

| Angle P | 0 degrees | 30 degrees | 45 degrees | 60 degrees | 90 degrees |
|---------|-----------|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **sin P** | 0 | 1/2 | 1/root(2) | root(3)/2 | 1 |
| **cos P** | 1 | root(3)/2 | 1/root(2) | 1/2 | 0 |
| **tan P** | 0 | 1/root(3) | 1 | root(3) | not defined |
| **cosec P** | not defined | 2 | root(2) | 2/root(3) | 1 |
| **sec P** | 1 | 2/root(3) | root(2) | 2 | not defined |
| **cot P** | not defined | root(3) | 1 | 1/root(3) | 0 |

### Memory Aid for sin Values

For sin 0, sin 30, sin 45, sin 60, sin 90, notice the elegant pattern:

root(0)/2, root(1)/2, root(2)/2, root(3)/2, root(4)/2

= 0, 1/2, 1/root(2), root(3)/2, 1

For **cos**, read the same sequence **backwards**.

### How to Derive 45-Degree Values

Consider an isosceles right triangle where both equal sides measure 1 unit.
Hypotenuse = root(1^2 + 1^2) = root(2).

- sin 45 = 1/root(2)
- cos 45 = 1/root(2)
- tan 45 = 1

### How to Derive 30 and 60-Degree Values

![Equilateral triangle with side 2 split into two right triangles by a perpendicular, showing 30-60-90 triangle with sides 1, root 3, and 2](equilateral-triangle-30-60-derivation.png)

Start with an equilateral triangle of side 2 units. Drop a perpendicular from one vertex to the opposite side. This splits it into two identical right triangles, each with:
- Hypotenuse = 2, base = 1, height = root(3)

For the 30-degree angle:
- sin 30 = 1/2
- cos 30 = root(3)/2

For the 60-degree angle:
- sin 60 = root(3)/2
- cos 60 = 1/2

## Important Observations

1. **sin P** always stays between **0 and 1** (inclusive) for angles from 0 to 90 degrees.
2. **cos P** also stays between **0 and 1** (inclusive) for the same range.
3. As angle P grows from 0 to 90 degrees, **sin P rises** from 0 to 1.
4. As angle P grows from 0 to 90 degrees, **cos P falls** from 1 to 0.
5. **tan P rises** from 0 and shoots towards infinity as the angle approaches 90 degrees.

## Trigonometric Ratios of Complementary Angles

Two angles are **complementary** when they add up to 90 degrees. In a right triangle with angle P, the other acute angle is (90 - P).

### The Complementary Angle Relationships

- **sin(90 - P) = cos P**
- **cos(90 - P) = sin P**
- **tan(90 - P) = cot P**
- **cot(90 - P) = tan P**
- **sec(90 - P) = cosec P**
- **cosec(90 - P) = sec P**

> **Key idea:** Each trigonometric ratio of an angle equals the co-ratio of its complement. The "co" in cosine, cotangent, and cosecant literally stands for "complement."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Evaluate sin 72 / cos 18.

Since 72 + 18 = 90, they are complementary.
sin 72 = sin(90 - 18) = cos 18

So sin 72 / cos 18 = cos 18 / cos 18 = **1**

**Example 2:** If cos P = sin Q, find P + Q.

cos P = sin Q = cos(90 - Q)
So P = 90 - Q
**P + Q = 90 degrees**

**Example 3:** Write sec 82 + tan 68 using angles between 0 and 45 degrees.

sec 82 = sec(90 - 8) = cosec 8
tan 68 = tan(90 - 22) = cot 22

So the answer is **cosec 8 + cot 22**

## Trigonometric Identities

An equation involving trigonometric ratios that holds true for **every permissible** value of the angle is called a **trigonometric identity**.

### The Three Fundamental Identities

**Identity 1:** **sin^2 P + cos^2 P = 1**

Derived directly from the Pythagoras theorem:
In triangle PQR, QR^2 + PQ^2 = PR^2
Dividing throughout by PR^2: (QR/PR)^2 + (PQ/PR)^2 = 1
Hence sin^2 P + cos^2 P = 1

**Identity 2:** **1 + tan^2 P = sec^2 P** (valid for 0 &lt;= P &lt; 90)

Divide the Pythagorean equation by PQ^2:
(QR/PQ)^2 + 1 = (PR/PQ)^2
tan^2 P + 1 = sec^2 P

**Identity 3:** **1 + cot^2 P = cosec^2 P** (valid for 0 &lt; P &lt;= 90)

Divide by QR^2:
1 + (PQ/QR)^2 = (PR/QR)^2
1 + cot^2 P = cosec^2 P

> **Key idea:** All three identities spring from a single source -- the Pythagoras theorem, divided by a different side each time. They are indispensable for simplifying expressions and proving other identities.

### Useful Rearrangements

From Identity 1:
- sin^2 P = 1 - cos^2 P
- cos^2 P = 1 - sin^2 P

From Identity 2:
- tan^2 P = sec^2 P - 1
- sec^2 P - tan^2 P = 1

From Identity 3:
- cot^2 P = cosec^2 P - 1
- cosec^2 P - cot^2 P = 1

## Proving Trigonometric Identities

### Strategy for Proofs

1. Begin with the more involved side (usually the LHS)
2. If stuck, convert everything to **sin** and **cos**
3. Apply the three fundamental identities
4. Watch for algebraic factorisations like a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)

### Worked Example 1: Prove that (1 - sin^2 P) sec^2 P = 1

LHS = (1 - sin^2 P) sec^2 P
= cos^2 P x (1/cos^2 P)  [using Identity 1]
= 1 = RHS

### Worked Example 2: Prove that (1 + cot^2 P)/(1 + tan^2 P) = cot^2 P

LHS = cosec^2 P / sec^2 P  [using Identities 2 and 3]
= (1/sin^2 P) / (1/cos^2 P)
= cos^2 P / sin^2 P
= cot^2 P = RHS

### Worked Example 3: Prove that (cos P + sec P)^2 + (sin P + cosec P)^2 = 7 + tan^2 P + cot^2 P

LHS = cos^2 P + 2 cos P sec P + sec^2 P + sin^2 P + 2 sin P cosec P + cosec^2 P
= (cos^2 P + sin^2 P) + 2(1) + sec^2 P + 2(1) + cosec^2 P
= 1 + 2 + (1 + tan^2 P) + 2 + (1 + cot^2 P)
= 7 + tan^2 P + cot^2 P = RHS

## Chapter Summary

1. **Trigonometric ratios** for angle P in a right triangle:
   - sin P = opposite/hypotenuse, cos P = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan P = opposite/adjacent

2. **Reciprocal ratios:** cosec P = 1/sin P, sec P = 1/cos P, cot P = 1/tan P

3. **Quotient relations:** tan P = sin P/cos P, cot P = cos P/sin P

4. Given one ratio, every other ratio can be found using the Pythagoras theorem.

5. **Standard values** for 0, 30, 45, 60, and 90 degrees must be committed to memory.

6. sin P and cos P never exceed 1; sec P and cosec P are always >= 1.

7. **Complementary angle relations:** sin(90-P) = cos P, cos(90-P) = sin P, etc.

8. **Three fundamental identities:**
   - sin^2 P + cos^2 P = 1
   - 1 + tan^2 P = sec^2 P
   - 1 + cot^2 P = cosec^2 P
