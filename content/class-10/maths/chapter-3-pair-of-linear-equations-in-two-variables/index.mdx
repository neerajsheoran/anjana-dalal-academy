## Introduction

Picture this: Ramesh visits a neighbourhood bakery. He buys some samosas and some pastries. The number of pastries is three less than the number of samosas. Each samosa costs Rs 15 and each pastry costs Rs 30. His total bill is Rs 180. Can you figure out how many of each he bought?

Setting up the equations:
- y = x - 3 (pastries are three less than samosas)
- 15x + 30y = 180 (total bill)

This is a **pair of linear equations in two variables**. In this chapter, we learn to solve such pairs using graphical and algebraic techniques.

### What Exactly Is a Pair of Linear Equations?

Two equations of the form:

- **a1x + b1y + c1 = 0**
- **a2x + b2y + c2 = 0**

where a1, a2, b1, b2, c1, c2 are real numbers with a1^2 + b1^2 not zero and a2^2 + b2^2 not zero.

A **solution** is a pair of values (x, y) that makes both equations true at the same time.

---

## Graphical Method of Solution

Each linear equation in two variables represents a **straight line** on the coordinate plane. Drawing both lines on the same axes reveals three possible outcomes:

### Case 1: The Lines Cross at One Point
- The system has a **unique solution** (the intersection point)
- The system is called **consistent**

### Case 2: The Lines Lie on Top of Each Other (Coincident)
- The system has **infinitely many solutions**
- Every point on the shared line works
- The system is **dependent** (and consistent)

### Case 3: The Lines Run Parallel
- The system has **no solution** at all
- The system is called **inconsistent**

> **Key idea:** Whether a system has one solution, infinitely many, or none depends entirely on how the two lines relate geometrically -- do they cross, overlap, or never meet?

---

## Comparing Ratios to Predict the Solution Type

For equations a1x + b1y + c1 = 0 and a2x + b2y + c2 = 0, you can classify the system **without plotting** by examining the ratios:

| Condition | Geometry | Solution Type |
|-----------|----------|---------------|
| a1/a2 is not equal to b1/b2 | Intersecting lines | Unique solution (consistent) |
| a1/a2 = b1/b2 = c1/c2 | Coincident lines | Infinitely many solutions (dependent) |
| a1/a2 = b1/b2 but not equal to c1/c2 | Parallel lines | No solution (inconsistent) |

### Worked Example

Classify these pairs of equations:

**(i) 2x - 5y = 0 and 3x + 7y - 31 = 0**

- a1/a2 = 2/3, b1/b2 = -5/7
- Since 2/3 is not equal to -5/7, the lines **intersect** -- unique solution (consistent)

**(ii) 3x + 4y - 12 = 0 and 6x + 8y - 24 = 0**

- a1/a2 = 3/6 = 1/2, b1/b2 = 4/8 = 1/2, c1/c2 = -12/-24 = 1/2
- All ratios equal, so the lines **coincide** -- infinitely many solutions (dependent)

**(iii) 2x + 5y - 10 = 0 and 4x + 10y - 30 = 0**

- a1/a2 = 2/4 = 1/2, b1/b2 = 5/10 = 1/2, c1/c2 = -10/-30 = 1/3
- First two ratios match but differ from the third, so the lines are **parallel** -- no solution (inconsistent)

## Solving Graphically -- Step by Step

To find the solution graphically:

1. **Compute at least two points** for each equation by plugging in values of x
2. **Plot these points** on graph paper
3. **Draw straight lines** through each pair of points
4. **Read the intersection coordinates** -- that is your answer

### Worked Example

Solve graphically: 2x + y = 8 and x - y = 1

For 2x + y = 8: when x = 0, y = 8; when x = 4, y = 0

For x - y = 1: when x = 0, y = -1; when x = 1, y = 0

Plotting and drawing the lines, they meet at **(3, 2)**.

So the solution is **x = 3, y = 2**.

## Algebraic Methods of Solving

Graphs are elegant but not always practical -- especially when solutions involve awkward fractions or decimals. Algebraic methods give exact answers. We focus on two:

1. **Substitution Method**
2. **Elimination Method**

---

## Substitution Method

### Steps

**Step 1:** Pick the simpler equation and express one variable in terms of the other.

**Step 2:** Plug this expression into the second equation. You now have a single equation in one variable.

**Step 3:** Solve for that variable.

**Step 4:** Substitute back to find the remaining variable.

### Worked Example 1

Solve: 5x - 3y = 1 ... (1) and x + 4y = 11 ... (2)

**Step 1:** From equation (2): x = 11 - 4y

**Step 2:** Substitute in equation (1):
5(11 - 4y) - 3y = 1
55 - 20y - 3y = 1
-23y = -54
**y = 54/23**

**Step 3:** Substitute back:
x = 11 - 4(54/23) = 11 - 216/23 = (253 - 216)/23 = **37/23**

**Solution: x = 37/23, y = 54/23**

### Worked Example 2: Age Problem

Deepa tells her son, "Five years ago, I was five times your age. Also, ten years from now, I will be twice your age." Find their present ages.

Let Deepa's age = p years, son's age = q years.

**Equation 1:** p - 5 = 5(q - 5), i.e., p - 5q + 20 = 0
**Equation 2:** p + 10 = 2(q + 10), i.e., p - 2q = 10

From equation (2): p = 2q + 10

Substituting in equation (1): (2q + 10) - 5q + 20 = 0
-3q + 30 = 0, so **q = 10**

Then p = 2(10) + 10 = **30**

**Deepa is 30 years old and her son is 10 years old.**

### Special Cases in Substitution

**Case 1: Infinitely many solutions**

If substitution produces a **universally true statement** like 24 = 24 (no variable left), the equations are dependent -- every point on the line is a solution.

**Example:** 3x + 5y = 15 and 6x + 10y = 30. The second is simply double the first. Substitution yields 30 = 30.

**Case 2: No solution**

If substitution yields a **false statement** like 7 = 0, the equations are inconsistent -- no solution exists.

**Example:** 2x + 3y - 6 = 0 and 4x + 6y - 18 = 0. Substitution yields 12 - 18 = 0, i.e., -6 = 0, which is impossible.

## Elimination Method

Here we eliminate one variable by making its coefficients match in both equations, then adding or subtracting.

### Steps

**Step 1:** Multiply one or both equations by suitable constants so that one variable has **equal coefficients** (in absolute value).

**Step 2:** **Add or subtract** the modified equations to remove that variable.

**Step 3:** Solve the resulting single-variable equation.

**Step 4:** Substitute back to find the other variable.

### Worked Example 1: Savings Problem

Two friends have monthly incomes in the ratio 7:5 and monthly expenditures in the ratio 3:2. Each saves Rs 1500 per month. Find their monthly incomes.

Let incomes be Rs 7x and Rs 5x, expenditures be Rs 3y and Rs 2y.

**Equation 1:** 7x - 3y = 1500
**Equation 2:** 5x - 2y = 1500

**Step 1:** Multiply equation (1) by 2 and equation (2) by 3:
- 14x - 6y = 3000 ... (3)
- 15x - 6y = 4500 ... (4)

**Step 2:** Subtract (3) from (4):
15x - 14x = 4500 - 3000
**x = 1500**

**Step 3:** Substitute in equation (1):
7(1500) - 3y = 1500
10500 - 3y = 1500
3y = 9000
**y = 3000**

**Monthly incomes: Rs 10,500 and Rs 7,500**

**Verification:** 10500:7500 = 7:5. Expenditures: 9000:6000 = 3:2. Each saves Rs 1500. Correct!

### Worked Example 2: Reversing Digits

The sum of a two-digit number and the number formed by swapping its digits is 110. The difference between the digits is 4. Find the number.

Let tens digit = x, units digit = y.

Original number = 10x + y. Swapped number = 10y + x.

**Equation 1:** (10x + y) + (10y + x) = 110, giving 11(x + y) = 110, so **x + y = 10**

**Equation 2:** x - y = 4 (or y - x = 4)

**Case 1:** x + y = 10 and x - y = 4
Adding: 2x = 14, so x = 7, y = 3. Number = **73**

**Case 2:** x + y = 10 and y - x = 4
Adding: 2y = 14, so y = 7, x = 3. Number = **37**

Both **73 and 37** are valid. Verification: 73 + 37 = 110 and 7 - 3 = 4.

## Word Problems -- Setting Up Equations

Common problem types and how to translate them:

### Type 1: Age Problems
- "M was k times as old as N, p years ago" translates to: (M - p) = k(N - p)

### Type 2: Number Problems
- Two-digit number with tens digit x and units digit y = 10x + y
- Reversing gives: 10y + x

### Type 3: Cost Problems
- "m items at Rs a each and n items at Rs b each cost Rs T" becomes: ma + nb = T

### Type 4: Fraction Problems
- If the fraction is x/y, then "adding 3 to numerator and subtracting 1 from denominator gives 7/8" means: (x + 3)/(y - 1) = 7/8

### Type 5: Speed, Distance, Time Problems
- Distance = Speed x Time
- Upstream speed = boat speed - current speed
- Downstream speed = boat speed + current speed

> **Key idea:** The art of word problems lies in translating sentences into equations. Define variables clearly, set up equations systematically, and the algebra takes care of the rest.

## Equations Reducible to Linear Form

Some equations look non-linear but can be **converted** by smart substitution.

**Example:** Solve 3/x + 2/y = 9 and 7/x - 3/y = -1

These are not linear in x and y. But substituting **u = 1/x** and **v = 1/y** gives:

- 3u + 2v = 9
- 7u - 3v = -1

Solve this linear system for u and v, then recover x = 1/u and y = 1/v.

## Chapter Summary

1. A pair of linear equations in two variables can be solved by:
   - **Graphical method** (plotting and finding where lines meet)
   - **Substitution method** (express one variable, plug into the other equation)
   - **Elimination method** (equalise coefficients and add/subtract)

2. **Graphically**, two lines can be:
   - **Intersecting** -- unique solution (consistent)
   - **Coincident** -- infinitely many solutions (dependent, consistent)
   - **Parallel** -- no solution (inconsistent)

3. **Quick ratio test** for a1x + b1y + c1 = 0 and a2x + b2y + c2 = 0:
   - a1/a2 not equal to b1/b2 -- unique solution
   - a1/a2 = b1/b2 = c1/c2 -- infinitely many solutions
   - a1/a2 = b1/b2 but not c1/c2 -- no solution

4. Many practical situations involving ages, costs, digits, speeds, and fractions can be modelled as pairs of linear equations and solved using these methods.
