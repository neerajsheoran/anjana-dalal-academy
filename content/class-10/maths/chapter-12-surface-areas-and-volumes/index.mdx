## Introduction

In previous classes, you learnt to calculate the surface areas and volumes of individual solids -- cuboids, cylinders, cones, and spheres. But look around you: real-world objects are rarely simple shapes. They are **combinations** of basic solids joined together.

Consider these everyday examples:
- A **matka** (earthen pot) resembles a sphere with a cylindrical neck
- A **kulfi mould** is a cone with a cylindrical handle
- A **capsule** of medicine is a cylinder capped with hemispheres on both ends
- A **temple gopuram** can be modelled as a series of frustums stacked together

![Examples of combined solids: truck container (cylinder with hemispheres), test tube (cylinder with hemisphere), ice cream cone (cone with hemisphere)](combined-solids-examples.png)

This chapter teaches you how to compute surface areas and volumes of such **composite solids**.

## Recap: Formulas for Basic Solids

### Cuboid (length l, breadth b, height h)
- **TSA** = 2(lb + bh + hl)
- **Volume** = l x b x h

### Cylinder (radius r, height h)
- **CSA** = 2 pi r h
- **TSA** = 2 pi r(r + h)
- **Volume** = pi r^2 h

### Cone (radius r, height h, slant height l)
- **CSA** = pi r l
- **TSA** = pi r(r + l)
- **Volume** = (1/3) pi r^2 h
- **Slant height** l = root(r^2 + h^2)

### Sphere (radius r)
- **SA** = 4 pi r^2
- **Volume** = (4/3) pi r^3

### Hemisphere (radius r)
- **CSA** = 2 pi r^2
- **TSA** = 3 pi r^2
- **Volume** = (2/3) pi r^3

> **Core insight:** Memorise these formulas thoroughly. Every problem in this chapter applies them in combination.

## Surface Area of Combination of Solids

When basic solids are joined to form a composite shape, the total surface area equals the **sum of only the exposed surfaces**.

### The Hidden Surface Rule

At the junction where two solids are fused, the touching surfaces become **internal** and are not part of the exterior. These hidden surfaces must be **excluded** from the total surface area calculation.

### Worked Example 1: Spinning Top (Lattu)

A wooden spinning top is shaped like a hemisphere topped with a cone. The overall height is 7 cm and the diameter is 4 cm.

**Step 1:** Identify visible surfaces.
- CSA of the cone (the pointed body)
- CSA of the hemisphere (the rounded top)
- The flat circular face at the junction is **hidden**

**Step 2:** Calculate.
- Radius r = 4/2 = 2 cm
- Height of hemisphere = r = 2 cm
- Height of cone = 7 - 2 = 5 cm
- Slant height of cone l = root(2^2 + 5^2) = root(4 + 25) = root(29) = 5.39 cm (approx.)
- CSA of hemisphere = 2 pi r^2 = 2 x (22/7) x 4 = 25.14 cm^2 (approx.)
- CSA of cone = pi r l = (22/7) x 2 x 5.39 = 33.87 cm^2 (approx.)

**Total surface area = 25.14 + 33.87 = 59.01 cm^2** (approx.)

### Worked Example 2: Vitamin Capsule

A capsule is a cylinder with hemispherical ends. Total length = 18 mm, diameter = 6 mm.

- Radius r = 3 mm
- Length of cylindrical part = 18 - 2(3) = 12 mm
- CSA of cylinder = 2 pi r h = 2 x pi x 3 x 12 = 72 pi
- CSA of 2 hemispheres = 2 x 2 pi r^2 = 4 pi x 9 = 36 pi

Total surface area = 72 pi + 36 pi = **108 pi mm^2** = 339.43 mm^2 (approx.)

## Volume of Combination of Solids

The volume of a composite solid is simply the **sum of the volumes** of its component parts. Unlike surface area, there are no hidden volumes to subtract.

### Worked Example 1: Toy (Cone on Hemisphere)

A toy consists of a cone mounted on a hemisphere. Radius = 2 cm, height of cone = 3 cm.

Volume = Volume of cone + Volume of hemisphere
= (1/3) pi r^2 h + (2/3) pi r^3
= (1/3) pi (4)(3) + (2/3) pi (8)
= 4 pi + (16/3) pi
= **(28/3) pi cm^3** (= 29.33 cm^3 approx.)

### Worked Example 2: Laddu Shaped Sweet

A laddu-shaped sweet is modelled as a cylinder (height 3 cm, diameter 3 cm) with hemispherical ends.

- Radius r = 1.5 cm
- Cylindrical height = 3 cm

Volume = pi r^2 h + 2 x (2/3) pi r^3
= pi(1.5)^2(3) + (4/3) pi (1.5)^3
= pi x 2.25 x 3 + (4/3) pi x 3.375
= 6.75 pi + 4.5 pi
= **11.25 pi cm^3** (= 35.36 cm^3 approx.)

For 50 such sweets dipped in sugar syrup at 25% of volume:
= 50 x 35.36 x 0.25 = **442 cm^3** (approx.)

## Conversion of One Solid into Another

When a solid is **melted** and **reshaped**, the **volume stays constant**. Only the form changes.

> **Core insight:** In recasting problems, set the volume of the original solid equal to the volume of the new solid and solve for the unknown dimension.

### Worked Example 1: Sphere to Cylinder

A metallic sphere of radius 6.3 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 9 cm. Find the cylinder's height.

Volume of sphere = Volume of cylinder
(4/3) pi (6.3)^3 = pi (9)^2 h
(4/3)(250.047) = 81h
333.396 = 81h
**h = 4.116 cm** (approx.)

### Worked Example 2: How Many Buttons from a Metal Block?

A metal block measuring 22 cm x 8 cm x 5 cm is melted into circular buttons of diameter 2 cm and thickness 0.25 cm.

Volume of block = 22 x 8 x 5 = 880 cm^3

Volume of one button = pi x (1)^2 x 0.25 = (22/7) x 0.25 = 0.7857 cm^3

Number of buttons = 880 / 0.7857 = **1120 buttons** (approx.)

### Worked Example 3: Water Flow Through a Pipe

Water flows through a cylindrical pipe (internal diameter 3 cm) at 1.2 m/s into a cylindrical tank (diameter 60 cm). How long to raise the water level by 30 cm?

Volume needed = pi x (30)^2 x 30 = 27000 pi cm^3
Volume per second = pi x (1.5)^2 x 120 = 270 pi cm^3 (1.2 m = 120 cm)

Time = 27000 pi / 270 pi = **100 seconds**

## Frustum of a Cone

![Cone being cut by a plane parallel to its base, producing a frustum with labeled dimensions r1 (larger radius), r2 (smaller radius), h (height), and l (slant height)](frustum-of-cone-labeled.png)

When a cone is sliced by a plane parallel to its base, the portion between the base and the cut is called a **frustum**.

Everyday frustums include a steel bucket, a flower pot (gamla), and the shade of a table lamp.

### Dimensions of a Frustum

A frustum has:
- **r1** = radius of the larger base (bottom)
- **r2** = radius of the smaller base (top)
- **h** = perpendicular height
- **l** = slant height = root(h^2 + (r1 - r2)^2)

### Formulas for a Frustum

- **CSA** = pi (r1 + r2) l
- **TSA** = pi (r1 + r2) l + pi r1^2 + pi r2^2
- **Volume** = (1/3) pi h (r1^2 + r2^2 + r1 x r2)

### Worked Example: A Steel Bucket

A bucket shaped like a frustum has top diameter 36 cm, bottom diameter 24 cm, and height 28 cm. Find its capacity.

r1 = 18 cm, r2 = 12 cm, h = 28 cm

Volume = (1/3) x (22/7) x 28 x (18^2 + 12^2 + 18 x 12)
= (1/3) x (22/7) x 28 x (324 + 144 + 216)
= (1/3) x (22/7) x 28 x 684
= (22 x 4 x 684) / 3
= **20064 cm^3** (approx.)

Converting to litres: 20064 / 1000 = **20.064 litres** (approx.)

## Tips for Board Exam Problems

1. **Sketch the solid** and label every dimension clearly.

2. **Identify which basic solids** form the combination.

3. For **surface area**: sum only the **exposed** surfaces (exclude joined faces).

4. For **volume**: sum the volumes of all components.

5. For **recasting problems**: equate volume of original = volume of new shape.

6. **Check units**: 1 litre = 1000 cm^3, 1 m^3 = 1000 litres, 1 cm^3 = 1 ml.

7. Always use the value of **pi** specified in the question.

## Chapter Summary

1. The **surface area** of a composite solid is found by summing the visible surface areas of its parts.

2. The **volume** of a composite solid is the sum of volumes of its parts.

3. When a solid is melted and recast, **volume is conserved**.

4. **Frustum of a cone** formulas:
   - CSA = pi(r1 + r2)l
   - TSA = pi(r1 + r2)l + pi r1^2 + pi r2^2
   - Volume = (1/3) pi h(r1^2 + r2^2 + r1 r2)
   - Slant height l = root(h^2 + (r1 - r2)^2)
