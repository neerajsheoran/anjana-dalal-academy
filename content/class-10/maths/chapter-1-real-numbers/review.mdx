## Key Concepts

- The **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic** states that every composite number has a **unique prime factorisation** (ignoring order of factors)
- A **factor tree** is a visual method to break a number into its prime factors
- **HCF** = product of the **lowest powers** of all **common** prime factors
- **LCM** = product of the **highest powers** of all prime factors appearing in **either** number
- For two numbers a and b: **HCF(a, b) x LCM(a, b) = a x b**
- This HCF x LCM formula does **NOT** work for three or more numbers
- If p is a prime and p divides a^2, then **p divides a** (Theorem 1.2)
- **Proof by contradiction** is used to prove irrationality: assume rational, derive a contradiction
- To prove root p is irrational (p prime): assume root p = a/b (coprime), show p divides both a and b, contradicting coprimality
- The **sum or difference** of a rational and an irrational number is always **irrational**
- The **product or quotient** of a non-zero rational and an irrational number is always **irrational**
- To check if n^k can end with digit 0, verify that both 2 and 5 appear in the prime factorisation of n
- 8^n and 12^n can **never** end with digit zero (no factor of 5)

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Prime factorisation** | Expressing a number as a product of prime numbers |
| **HCF** | Highest Common Factor -- largest number dividing both given numbers |
| **LCM** | Least Common Multiple -- smallest number divisible by both given numbers |
| **Irrational number** | A number that cannot be expressed as p/q (q not zero, p and q integers) |
| **Coprime** | Two numbers sharing no common factor except 1 |
| **Composite number** | A number with more than two factors |

## Remember These

- HCF x LCM = product of the two numbers -- use this to find one from the other quickly
- For **three numbers**, HCF x LCM does NOT equal the product -- this is a common exam trap
- When proving irrationality of expressions like 7 + root 5, isolate the irrational part and show it equals a rational expression -- contradiction
- Always verify HCF and LCM answers: HCF must divide both numbers, LCM must be divisible by both
