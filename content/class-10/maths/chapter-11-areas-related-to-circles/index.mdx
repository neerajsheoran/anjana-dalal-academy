## Introduction

You already know the fundamental formulas: circumference = 2 pi r and area = pi r^2. This chapter takes you further into calculating areas and lengths associated with **portions** of a circle -- namely **sectors** and **segments**.

These ideas appear everywhere in daily life: the region swept by a windshield wiper, the grazing field of a goat tethered to a pole, the area covered by a rotating sprinkler, and even the design of rangoli patterns during Diwali.

---

## Recap: Fundamental Circle Formulas

Let us quickly recall:

- **Circumference** of a circle = 2 pi r
- **Area** of a circle = pi r^2

where **r** is the radius.

Unless the problem states otherwise, use **pi = 22/7** for calculations.

---

## Sector and Segment of a Circle

### What Is a Sector?

A **sector** is the region bounded by **two radii** and the **arc** connecting their endpoints. Visualise it as a slice from a round paratha.

![Circle divided into minor sector (shaded smaller region) and major sector (larger region) with two radii and angle theta labeled](sector-minor-major-labeled.png)

- **Minor sector:** the smaller slice (central angle less than 180 degrees)
- **Major sector:** the larger slice (central angle greater than 180 degrees)

The central angle is called the **angle of the sector** (theta).

Note: Angle of major sector = 360 - theta.

### What Is a Segment?

![Circle with a chord dividing it into minor segment (smaller shaded region between chord and arc) and major segment (larger region)](segment-minor-major-labeled.png)

A **segment** is the region trapped between a **chord** and the **arc** it intercepts.

- **Minor segment:** the smaller region (between the chord and the minor arc)
- **Major segment:** the larger region (between the chord and the major arc)

> **Core insight:** A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc (like a paratha slice). A segment is bounded by a chord and an arc (no radii involved in the boundary).

---

## Area of a Sector

A full circle spans 360 degrees and has area pi r^2. A sector with central angle theta is a fraction of the full circle:

**Area of sector = (theta / 360) x pi r^2**

### How to Remember

The ratio **(theta / 360)** represents what fraction of the entire circle the sector covers. Multiply this fraction by the total area of the circle.

### Worked Example 1

Find the area of a sector with radius 7 cm and angle 90 degrees.

Area = (90/360) x (22/7) x 7^2
= (1/4) x (22/7) x 49
= (1/4) x 154
= **38.5 cm^2**

### Worked Example 2

The minute hand of a wall clock is 21 cm long. What area does it sweep in 10 minutes?

The minute hand completes 360 degrees in 60 minutes.
In 10 minutes, it sweeps: (10/60) x 360 = 60 degrees.

Area = (60/360) x (22/7) x 21^2
= (1/6) x (22/7) x 441
= (1/6) x 1386
= **231 cm^2**

---

## Length of an Arc

The arc length for a sector of angle theta is:

**Length of arc = (theta / 360) x 2 pi r**

### Worked Example

A circle has radius 14 cm and an arc subtends 90 degrees at the centre. Find the arc length.

Arc length = (90/360) x 2 x (22/7) x 14
= (1/4) x 88
= **22 cm**

## Area of a Segment

A segment is the region between a chord and its arc. To compute its area:

**Area of segment = Area of sector - Area of triangle**

![Diagram showing segment area as sector area minus triangle area, with sector and triangle clearly labeled](segment-equals-sector-minus-triangle.png)

The triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord.

> **Core insight:** Segment = Sector minus Triangle. This single formula handles all segment problems.

### Computing the Triangle's Area

For a sector with angle theta and radius r, the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord has area:

**Area of triangle = (1/2) r^2 sin(theta)**

For commonly tested angles:
- theta = 60 degrees: Area of triangle = (root(3)/4) r^2 (equilateral triangle)
- theta = 90 degrees: Area of triangle = (1/2) r^2 (right-angled isosceles triangle)
- theta = 120 degrees: Area of triangle = (root(3)/4) r^2

### Worked Example: 90-Degree Segment

A chord in a circle of radius 14 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. Find the minor segment area. (Use pi = 22/7)

**Step 1: Area of sector**
= (90/360) x (22/7) x 14^2
= (1/4) x (22/7) x 196
= (1/4) x 616
= 154 cm^2

**Step 2: Area of triangle**
= (1/2) x 14 x 14 = 98 cm^2

**Step 3: Area of segment**
= 154 - 98 = **56 cm^2**

### Worked Example: 60-Degree Segment

A chord in a circle of radius 42 cm subtends 60 degrees at the centre. Find the minor segment area. (Use pi = 22/7, root(3) = 1.73)

**Step 1: Area of sector**
= (60/360) x (22/7) x 42^2
= (1/6) x (22/7) x 1764
= (1/6) x 5544
= 924 cm^2

**Step 2: Area of triangle**
Since the angle is 60 degrees and the two sides are equal radii, the triangle is equilateral.
Area = (root(3)/4) x 42^2 = (1.73/4) x 1764 = 763.23 cm^2

**Step 3: Area of segment**
= 924 - 763.23 = **160.77 cm^2** (approx.)

---

## Area of Major Sector and Major Segment

- **Area of major sector** = Area of circle - Area of minor sector
  = pi r^2 - (theta/360) x pi r^2

- **Area of major segment** = Area of circle - Area of minor segment

## Real-Life Application Problems

### Goat Tied to a Corner

A goat is tethered with a 7 m rope to a corner of a rectangular field whose sides are longer than 7 m. The angle at each corner of a rectangle is 90 degrees, so the goat can graze a **quarter circle**.

Grazing area = (90/360) x (22/7) x 7^2 = (1/4) x 154 = **38.5 m^2**

If the rope is extended to 14 m:
New area = (1/4) x (22/7) x 196 = (1/4) x 616 = 154 m^2
Additional area = 154 - 38.5 = **115.5 m^2**

### Windshield Wiper

A car's wiper blade is 35 cm long and sweeps through an angle of 120 degrees. The area cleaned by one blade:

Area = (120/360) x (22/7) x 35^2
= (1/3) x (22/7) x 1225
= (1/3) x 3850
= **1283.33 cm^2** (approx.)

For two blades: 2 x 1283.33 = **2566.67 cm^2** (approx.)

### Clock Hand Sweep

The minute hand of a clock is 21 cm long. Find the area swept between 3:00 and 3:15 (15 minutes).

Angle swept in 15 min = (15/60) x 360 = 90 degrees
Area = (90/360) x (22/7) x 21^2 = (1/4) x 1386 = **346.5 cm^2**

## Combination of Figures

Many examination problems require computing areas of regions formed by overlapping or combining sectors, segments, and polygons. The general approach:

1. **Decompose the figure** into recognisable shapes (sectors, triangles, rectangles, semicircles)
2. **Calculate each area** individually
3. **Add or subtract** as the problem demands

## Chapter Summary

1. **Length of an arc** of a sector with angle theta and radius r:
   = (theta/360) x 2 pi r

2. **Area of a sector** with angle theta and radius r:
   = (theta/360) x pi r^2

3. **Area of a segment** = Area of corresponding sector - Area of corresponding triangle

4. **Area of major sector** = pi r^2 - Area of minor sector

5. **Area of major segment** = pi r^2 - Area of minor segment
