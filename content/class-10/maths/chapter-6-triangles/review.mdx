## Key Concepts

- **Similar figures**: same shape, possibly different size; all congruent figures are similar but not vice versa
- Two polygons are similar if (i) corresponding angles are equal AND (ii) corresponding sides are proportional
- For **triangles**, either condition alone guarantees the other
- **Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT / Thales' Theorem)**: a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides in **equal ratio**
- **Converse of BPT**: if a line divides two sides proportionally, it is parallel to the third side
- **AA criterion**: two pairs of equal angles --> triangles are similar (most commonly used)
- **SSS criterion**: all three pairs of sides in the same ratio --> similar
- **SAS criterion**: one pair of equal angles with including sides proportional --> similar
- In similarity statement triangle PQR ~ triangle XYZ, vertex order indicates correspondence: P<->X, Q<->Y, R<->Z
- **Area ratio theorem**: Area(triangle 1) / Area(triangle 2) = **(side ratio)^2** for similar triangles
- This square-of-ratio rule also applies to altitudes, medians, and angle bisectors
- **Pythagoras Theorem**: In a right triangle, **hypotenuse^2 = sum of squares of other two sides**
- **Converse of Pythagoras**: if one side^2 = sum of other two sides^2, the triangle is right-angled

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Similar figures** | Same shape, not necessarily same size |
| **BPT (Thales' Theorem)** | Line parallel to one side divides other two sides proportionally |
| **AA Similarity** | Two equal angle pairs guarantee similarity |
| **Hypotenuse** | Longest side of a right triangle, opposite the right angle |

## Remember These

- **AA is the go-to test** for similarity in most problems -- you only need two angle pairs
- When writing similarity statements, the **vertex order matters** and determines all corresponding ratios
- Areas of similar triangles are in the ratio of the **square** of corresponding sides (sides 3:5 --> areas 9:25)
- To test if a triangle is right-angled: check if the square of the longest side = sum of squares of the other two
- BPT is your first instinct whenever you see a line parallel to one side of a triangle
