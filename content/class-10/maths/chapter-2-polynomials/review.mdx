## Key Concepts

- A **polynomial** has whole-number exponents only -- expressions with x in the denominator or under a root are NOT polynomials
- **Degree** = highest power of the variable: degree 1 (linear), degree 2 (quadratic), degree 3 (cubic)
- A **zero** of polynomial p(x) is a value k where p(k) = 0
- Geometrically, zeroes are the **x-coordinates** where the graph of y = p(x) crosses the x-axis
- A polynomial of degree n has **at most n** zeroes
- Graph of a quadratic is a **parabola**: opens up if a > 0, opens down if a < 0
- A quadratic can have 2 distinct zeroes, 1 repeated zero, or no real zeroes
- For quadratic ax^2 + bx + c with zeroes alpha, beta: **alpha + beta = -b/a** and **alpha x beta = c/a**
- For cubic ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d with zeroes alpha, beta, gamma:
  - alpha + beta + gamma = **-b/a**
  - alpha.beta + beta.gamma + gamma.alpha = **c/a**
  - alpha.beta.gamma = **-d/a**
- To find a quadratic from given zeroes: **p(x) = x^2 - (sum)x + (product)**
- **Splitting the middle term**: for ax^2 + bx + c, find two numbers with product = ac and sum = b
- Always **verify** zeroes using the sum and product formulas after finding them

## Important Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Zero / Root** | Value of x that makes the polynomial equal to zero |
| **Linear polynomial** | Degree 1, form ax + b, has exactly 1 zero |
| **Quadratic polynomial** | Degree 2, form ax^2 + bx + c, at most 2 zeroes |
| **Cubic polynomial** | Degree 3, form ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d, at most 3 zeroes |
| **Parabola** | U-shaped curve; graph of a quadratic polynomial |

## Remember These

- The signs in the cubic zero-coefficient relationship alternate: **-b/a, +c/a, -d/a**
- Zero of a linear polynomial ax + b is simply **-b/a**
- Verifying the sum and product relationship after finding zeroes earns full marks in board exams
- A quadratic with **no real zeroes** means the parabola does not touch the x-axis at all
