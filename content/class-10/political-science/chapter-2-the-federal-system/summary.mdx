## In One Paragraph

**Federalism** is a system where **power is divided between a central authority and constituent units (states), with this division protected by the constitution**. Federations form in two ways: **"coming together"** (independent states unite — USA, Switzerland) or **"holding together"** (a large country devolves power to units — India, Spain, Belgium). India is a "holding together" federation designed to manage immense diversity. The Constitution's **Seventh Schedule** divides subjects into three lists: **Union List** (Centre only — 97 subjects like defence, foreign affairs, currency), **State List** (States only — 66 subjects like police, public health, agriculture), and **Concurrent List** (both — 47 subjects like education, criminal law). **Residuary powers** (anything not in any list) go to the Centre, making India a **"quasi-federal"** system with a strong centre. India accommodates diversity through **linguistic states** (since 1956), **22 official languages** in the Eighth Schedule, and the **three-language formula** in schools. The **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** added a third tier — **local government** (panchayats and municipalities) — with reservations for women, SC, ST. Federal tensions exist (Centre vs opposition states, north vs south, river disputes, GST, special status debates), but Indian federalism has succeeded in keeping a vast diverse country united for 75+ years.

## Key Takeaways

- **Federalism** = power-sharing between Centre and states, constitutionally protected.
- Two types of federations: **"coming together"** (USA, Switzerland) and **"holding together"** (India, Spain, Belgium).
- India is a **"holding together"** federation with **strong central features** — sometimes called **quasi-federal**.
- The **Seventh Schedule** divides subjects into 3 lists:
  - **Union List** (Centre — 97 subjects): defence, foreign affairs, currency, railways, etc.
  - **State List** (States — 66 subjects): police, public health, agriculture, local govt.
  - **Concurrent List** (both — 47 subjects): education, criminal law, marriage, forests.
- **Residuary subjects** go to **Centre** (in India), unlike USA where they go to states.
- **Linguistic states** were created starting **1956** with the States Reorganisation Act.
- India has **22 official languages** in the Eighth Schedule; **no national language**; Hindi + English at Union level.
- **Three-language formula**: in non-Hindi states, students learn their language + Hindi + English (or another language).
- **Article 356** (President's Rule) — used over 100 times; **Bommai case (1994)** restricted misuse.
- **GST (Goods and Services Tax, 2017)** — major federal reform; **GST Council** with Centre + all state finance ministers.
- **73rd Amendment (1992)** — three-tier rural local government (Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad).
- **74th Amendment (1992)** — urban local bodies (Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council, Nagar Panchayat).
- Reservations in local government: **1/3 for women** (many states raised to 50%); SC/ST based on population.
- **Over 1 million elected women representatives** in Indian local government — more than entire western Europe.
- **Finance Commission** (constitutional body) decides Centre-State revenue sharing every 5 years.
- Federal tensions: Centre vs opposition-ruled states, north vs south, river water disputes, GST autonomy, special status (e.g., Article 370 revocation 2019).
- Indian federalism has kept the country **united despite extraordinary diversity** for 75+ years.

## A Phrase to Remember

> **Federalism is unity in diversity, written into law.**

India is too diverse to be governed by a single power. Federalism gives each region a stake in the country while keeping the country one. When you defend federalism, you defend the very arrangement that has held India together for three quarters of a century.
